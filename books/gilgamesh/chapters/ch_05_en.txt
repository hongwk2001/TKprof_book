# Chapter 5: The Search for Immortality

Heartbroken and filled with a terrible fear of his own death, Gilgamesh threw away his royal robes. He clothed himself in wild animal skins and wandered through the deserts, crying out, "Will I die like Enkidu? I cannot rest. The fear of death eats my heart. I must find Utnapishtim, the only mortal who survived the Great Flood and received eternal life from the gods. He will tell me how to live forever."

Gilgamesh traveled across wide deserts and climbed rugged mountains, searching for the way to the land of Utnapishtim. At last, he reached the edge of the world, where the twin peaks of Mount Mashu rise. These mountains guard the rising and setting sun, and their foundations reach deep into the Underworld. 

Guarding the entrance to the dark tunnel beneath the mountains were the giant Scorpion-Men. Their looks were terrifying, and their glance was death. When the Scorpion-Man saw Gilgamesh approaching, he turned to his wife and said, "He who comes is of royal blood. Look at his face and his stride."

Seeing Gilgamesh's royal bearing, the Scorpion-Man asked, "Why have you traveled so far to this forbidden land, O king?"

Gilgamesh replied, "I search for Utnapishtim, my ancestor, who stands in the assembly of the gods and possesses eternal life. I wish to ask him how he conquered death."

The Scorpion-Man warned him, "No mortal has ever walked through this path, Gilgamesh. The tunnel is twelve leagues of absolute darkness. If you enter, you will see no light, and the cold will freeze your heart."

"Though there be darkness and sorrow," Gilgamesh said, "I must go forward."

Seeing his determination, the Scorpion-Man opened the gate. "Go in safety, Gilgamesh. May the mountains allow you to pass."

Gilgamesh entered the dark tunnel. For the first league, the darkness was thick, and he could see nothing before or behind him. He walked for many hours, the air growing colder and heavier. By the eighth league, he was panting with exhaustion, but he pushed forward. By the eleventh league, a faint wind began to blow, and at the twelfth league, he finally emerged into the bright sunshine. 

He found himself in a beautiful garden of gemstones by the sea. The trees bore precious stones instead of fruit, with clusters of lapis lazuli and carnelian shining in the sun. 

By the shore lived Siduri, the wise tavern keeper. She sat by the sea, watching the horizon. When she saw Gilgamesh walking toward her, she was alarmed. He wore wild animal skins, his face was hollow, and his body was worn from his long journey. Fearing he was a robber, she barred the heavy wooden door of her house. 

Gilgamesh called out to her, "Tavern keeper, why do you bar your door? I am Gilgamesh, the King of Uruk, who killed the guardian of the Cedar Forest and slew the Bull of Heaven!"

Siduri opened her window and looked at him. "If you are indeed Gilgamesh, why are your cheeks so hollow? Why is your face so sad, and why do you wear the skins of wild beasts?"

Gilgamesh replied, "Why should my cheeks not be hollow? My brother Enkidu, whom I loved so dearly, has died. I wept for him for seven days, hoping he would wake, but he has turned to dust. And now, I fear my own death. Tell me, wise woman, how do I find Utnapishtim? How do I cross the Waters of Death?"

Siduri looked at the weary king with pity and spoke to him gently, "Gilgamesh, why do you chase the wind? The gods kept eternal life for themselves and gave death to mankind. Fill your belly with sweet food, dress in clean clothes, wash your head, and bathe in cool water. Rejoice with the wife of your bosom, and hold the hand of your little child. That is the true joy of life. Do not search for a way to live forever."

But Gilgamesh refused to stop. "My brother is dead, and I cannot rest. Tell me, how do I cross the sea?"

Siduri sighed and said, "Only the sun god Shamash has ever crossed this sea. But by the shore lives Urshanabi, the ferryman of Utnapishtim. Go to him. If it is possible, he will take you across. If not, you must turn back."

Gilgamesh found Urshanabi. The ferryman told him, "The Waters of Death are deadly to touch, Gilgamesh. Go into the forest and cut down one hundred and twenty giant wooden poles. We will use them to push our boat, using a new pole for each stroke so that our hands never touch the lethal waters."

Gilgamesh cut down the wooden poles. They pushed the boat into the sea, and for many days they sailed. Using the poles one by one, they crossed the Waters of Death without touching the water. At last, they reached the distant shore where Utnapishtim lived, and Gilgamesh stepped onto the land of the immortals.
