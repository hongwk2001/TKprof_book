Book 12

1. Everything you are trying to reach through a long, roundabout path can be yours right now, if you only stop holding yourself back. This means leaving the past behind, trusting the future to fate, and focusing entirely on living the present moment with reverence and justice. Live with reverence, so you can accept your assigned path, because Nature designed it for you and you for it. Live with justice, so you can speak the truth freely and without deception. Do what is right and proper. Do not let the wickedness, opinions, or gossip of others stop you. Do not let the sensations of your physical body hold you back, let the body worry about its own feelings. 
If you are nearing the end of your life and choose to focus solely on your guiding mind and the divine spark within you, leaving everything else behind; if you stop fearing the end of life and instead fear never truly beginning to live in harmony with Nature, then you will be a man worthy of the universe that created you. You will stop acting like a stranger in your own world, staring in shock at everyday events, and getting distracted by this or that trivial thing.

2. God sees all souls stripped of their physical shells, husks, and garbage. With his intelligence alone, he touches only what he placed inside us, the part that flowed directly from him. If you train yourself to do the same, you will spare yourself a great deal of suffering. A person who ignores their flesh will not waste time worrying about clothing, houses, reputation, or any other external trappings and showpieces.

3. You are made of three parts: body, breath, and mind. The first two belong to you only in the sense that you must care for them, but the third is your true self. If you separate your mind from the actions and words of others, from your own past actions and words, and from your fears about the future; if you detach yourself from the uncontrollable events affecting your body and breath, and from the chaotic storms of the outside world, your pure, untangled mind can live freely. It can live justly, accept what happens, and speak the truth. I repeat, if you clear your mind of excessive attachment to the past and the future, you will become like Empedocles's sphere:

Perfectly round and rejoicing in its endless rest.

Focus on living the only life you actually have, the present. Then you can spend whatever time remains in noble tranquility, completely at peace with your inner spirit.

4. I have always found it strange that everyone loves themselves more than anyone else, yet values their own opinion of themselves less than the opinions of others. If a god or a wise teacher ordered a man to instantly speak aloud every thought that popped into his head, he wouldn't last a single day. This shows how we fear what our neighbors think of us far more than we respect our own thoughts.

5. How is it possible that the gods, who organized everything perfectly for humanity's benefit, overlooked just one detail? Namely, that some of the best people, those closest to the divine, who spent their lives doing good works and honoring the gods, die and simply vanish, never to return. If this is truly the case, you can be absolutely certain that if things should have been different, the gods would have made them different. If it were right, it would have been possible. If it were in harmony with Nature, Nature would have made it happen. Because it is not this way, assuming it really isn't, you must trust that it wasn't meant to be.
Notice that by debating this, you are arguing about justice with the gods. We wouldn't argue with them if they weren't completely good and just. And if they are entirely good and just, they wouldn't have carelessly or unfairly neglected any part of the universe's design.

6. Try doing even the things you think you are terrible at. Notice how your left hand is mostly useless because it lacks practice, yet it holds the reins of a horse more firmly than your right hand, because that is what it was trained to do.

7. Think about the state your body and soul should be in when death finally catches you. Remember how short life is, the infinite stretches of time before and after you, and how fragile all material things are.

8. Look at the core causes of things, stripped of all disguises. Examine the true nature of pain, pleasure, death, and fame. Realize how often people cause their own endless anxiety. Recognize that no one can truly stop you, and everything is simply a matter of your own perspective.

9. When using your core principles, be like a wrestler, not a gladiator with a sword. If the gladiator drops his weapon, he is doomed. But the wrestler always has his hands attached and only needs to use them.

10. Look closely at the true nature of things. Break them down into their material, their cause, and their purpose.

11. What a glorious power humans possess! We have the ability to only take actions that God would approve of, and to welcome whatever fate God assigns us.

12. We should never blame the gods for natural events, because they do no wrong, intentionally or otherwise. We shouldn't blame people either, because they do wrong out of ignorance. Therefore, there is no one to blame.

13. How ridiculous and out of touch it is to be surprised by anything that happens in life!

14. The universe operates by one of three rules: an unchangeable fate, a caring Providence that we can pray to, or blind chaos without a leader. If it is an unchangeable fate, why fight it? If it is a caring Providence, make yourself worthy of divine help. If it is aimless chaos, find comfort in knowing you have an intelligent mind to guide you through the storm. If the storm sweeps you away, let it take your body and your breath, because it can never take your mind.

15. A lamp's light continues to shine and keeps its brightness until it is put out. Will you let your own truth, justice, and self-control be extinguished before you are?

16. When you believe someone has done wrong, ask yourself: "How do I know for sure this is a sin?" And if they really did do wrong, realize that they are their own judge and jury, like someone tearing at their own face in frustration.
Wishing that bad people wouldn't do bad things is like wishing a fig tree wouldn't produce juice, babies wouldn't cry, or horses wouldn't neigh. It is wishing for the impossible. What else can a bad person do with a bad character? If it bothers you so much, help them cure it.

17. If it is not right, do not do it. If it is not true, do not say it.

18. Always try to understand exactly what is triggering your reactions. Break it apart and analyze the cause, the material, its relation to other things, and the specific time it will inevitably end.

19. Finally realize that you have something better and more divine inside you than the simple instincts pulling your strings toward pleasure or pain. What is dominating your mind right now? Is it fear? Suspicion? Lust? Or some other wild emotion?

20. First, never act randomly or without a clear purpose. Second, make sure your purpose always serves the common good.

21. In a short while, you will be gone. Nothing you see right now will remain, and no one alive today will still be here. It is the nature of everything to change, transform, and decay, so that new things can be born from the ashes.

22. Remember that everything is simply a matter of perspective, and you control your perspective. Delete your negative opinions whenever you want. You will feel like a sailor who has just rounded a stormy cape and sailed into a calm, peaceful bay without a single wave.

23. Any natural action that finishes at its proper time suffers no harm by ending. The person who performed the action suffers no harm either. In the same way, if the entire series of actions we call "life" ends at its proper time, it suffers no harm by stopping. The person completing that life cycle is not harmed either. Nature sets the right time and limit. Sometimes it is your own nature, like dying of old age, but it is always the universal Nature keeping the whole system fresh and blooming through this constant cycle of change. 
Whatever benefits the universe is always good and timely. Therefore, the end of life cannot be bad for any individual. There is nothing shameful about it, since we do not control it and it does not harm the common good. In fact, it is a good thing, because it is timely, helpful, and in perfect rhythm with the universe. A person is truly led by God when they walk the same path as God, moving in the exact same direction.

24. Keep these three thoughts ready at all times: First, never act carelessly, and never act differently than Justice herself would. As for outside events, they happen by either chance or divine plan, and you have no right to complain about either. 
Second, examine everything from the moment it is a seed to the moment it gains life, and from life to death. Look at what it is made of, and what it will eventually break down into. 
Third, imagine you could float high above the earth and look down at all human affairs. You would see incredible variety, and you would see all the spiritual beings surrounding us. But no matter how many times you floated up there, you would always see the exact same things, all uniform and short-lived. Is this really something to be proud of?

25. Throw away your opinions, and you are saved. Who is stopping you from throwing them away?

26. When you get angry about anything, you have forgotten that everything happens exactly as the universe intends, and that the wrong was committed by someone else. You have forgotten that whatever happens has always happened, will always happen, and is happening everywhere right now. You have forgotten the massive connection between you and the entire human race, a connection not of blood or genetics, but of a shared mind. You have forgotten that everyone's mind is divine and comes directly from God. You have forgotten that you own absolutely nothing: your children, your body, and your very life are on loan from God. You have forgotten that everything is a matter of perspective, and that you only ever live, and lose, the exact present moment.

27. Keep bringing to mind the people who complained too much, or those who achieved the absolute peaks of fame, tragedy, bitter rivalries, or extreme luck. Then stop and ask: Where are they all now? Smoke, ashes, and an old story, or maybe entirely forgotten. Think of Fabius Catullinus relaxing in the country, Lucius Lupus in his gardens, Stertinius at Baiae, Tiberius at Capreae, Velius Rufus, and anyone else who chased high status and popularity. How cheap are the things people eagerly hunt for! 
It is much better for a philosopher to quietly do their job in their small corner of the world. Be just, exercise self-control, and follow the gods with simplicity. The absolute worst thing is being proud of your own fake humility.

28. If someone asks you, "Where have you seen the gods, and how are you so sure they exist that you worship them?" answer them like this: First, you can see their work with your own eyes. Second, I have never seen my own soul, yet I honor it. The same goes for the gods, I constantly feel their power, so I know they exist, and I worship them.

29. The secret to a safe, steady life is to look closely at the complete nature of everything. Break down its material and its purpose. Practice justice with all your heart, and speak the truth. What is left to do but enjoy life, linking one good deed to the next until there isn't a single gap?

30. There is only one light from the sun, even though it breaks across walls, hills, and a million other objects. There is only one shared material substance, even though it is split among ten thousand bodies with endless different traits. There is only one soul, even though it is divided among countless different living creatures. There is only one intelligent mind, even though it seems cut up into pieces. 
The physical parts of these things, like breath and matter, have no feeling or connection to one another, but they are still held together by gravity and that one guiding spirit. Intelligence, however, naturally reaches out to connect with its own kind, and that shared feeling can never be broken.

31. What do you really want? To just keep living? Is it to feel things, or to chase desires? To grow and then decay? To speak or think? Which of these is actually worth wanting? If they are all worthless, move on to the final goal: following reason and following God. If you truly respect reason and God, you won't cry over losing those other worthless things in death.

32. Think about how tiny our slice of the endless timeline really is, and how quickly it vanishes into eternity! We get such a microscopic amount of the universe's material and spirit. We crawl on a tiny speck of dust in the vast universe. Keeping all this in mind, realize that the only truly great thing is to act as your nature directs, and gracefully accept whatever the universe's Nature throws your way.

33. How is your guiding mind doing? Everything depends on this. Every single other thing, whether you control it or not, is nothing but dead weight, dust, and smoke.

34. This thought should really help you stop fearing death: Even the people who thought pleasure was the only good and pain was the only evil didn't fear death.

35. The person who only cares about doing the right thing at the right time doesn't worry about death. They don't care if they get to perform more actions aligned with reason or fewer. They don't care if they get to look at the universe for a long time or a short time, death simply holds no terror for them.

36. Man, you have lived as a citizen in this great city of the universe. What does it matter if you were here for five years or three? What the law dictates is fair to everyone. So why is it a tragedy if you are sent out of the city? You are not being banished by a dictator or a corrupt judge, but by the very Nature that brought you here in the first place. It is exactly like a director who hires an actor and then dismisses them from the stage. 
"But I've only performed three acts, not the full five!" you might say. That is true, but in your life, three acts make up the entire play. The length of the play is decided by the one who wrote it and the one who is now ending it. You didn't control either part. So make your exit gracefully, because the one dismissing you is full of grace.

THE END.
