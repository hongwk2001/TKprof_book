Book 4

1. When the power that rules within us is in tune with nature, it adapts to any situation with ease. It doesn’t need specific material to work with. Instead, it takes whatever obstacles come its way and uses them to fuel its own purpose. Think of a fire consuming everything thrown into it. A small flame might be smothered, but a roaring blaze quickly devours whatever you toss in, burning even higher and brighter because of it.

2. Never do anything at random, or without a solid guiding principle behind it.

3. People constantly look for retreats in the country, by the ocean, or in the mountains, and you often crave these escapes yourself. But this is foolish, because you can retreat into yourself whenever you want. There is no place more peaceful and free from interruptions than your own soul, especially when you have inner resources that instantly bring you peace. I believe peace is nothing more than perfect order within the soul. So, give yourself this retreat constantly, and use it to recharge. Keep a few brief, fundamental truths close at hand. When you remember them, they will shut out the noise of the world and send you back to your work without frustration.

What are you frustrated about? The wickedness of people? Remember that rational beings are made for each other. Tolerating them is a part of justice, and they often do wrong out of ignorance. Think of all the people who spent their lives filled with hatred, suspicion, and conflict, only to end up as ashes. Remember that, and stop complaining.

Are you upset about your assigned role in the world's destiny? Calm down and remember the alternative: either a guiding intelligence directs the universe, or it is just a random collection of atoms. Remember the many proofs that the universe is like a single, unified city.

Do physical pains still bother you? Realize that your mind, once it withdraws and recognizes its own power, is entirely separate from the rough or smooth workings of your breathing body. Remember everything you have learned and accepted about pain and pleasure.

Are you distracted by the illusion of fame? Think about how quickly everything is forgotten. Look at the infinite abyss of time on either side of our brief existence. Realize how empty the noise of praise really is. Notice how fickle and superficial those who praise us can be, and how small the world actually is. The entire earth is just a tiny point in the universe. How small is the corner we inhabit, and how few people are here to praise us, and what is their praise even worth?

Remember, you always have the option to retreat into the little field within yourself. Above all, do not stress or strain yourself. Keep your freedom. Face everything with courage, as a human being, a citizen, and a mortal. Keep these two core principles ready at all times: First, external things cannot touch the soul; they remain outside, and all your trouble comes from your own internal judgments. Second, everything you see changes in an instant and is gone forever. Think of all the changes you have already witnessed in your life. The world is constant change, and our life is shaped by our thoughts.

4. If we all share a mind, then we also share the reason that makes us rational beings. We share the power that tells us what to do and what not to do. This means we all live under a common law, making us fellow citizens of a shared community. The universe itself must be a kind of city, because what other community could possibly include all of humanity? It is from this common city that we get our intelligence, our reason, and our sense of law. Where else could they come from? Just as my earthy parts come from the earth, my fluids from water, and my breath and heat from their own sources, my intellect must also have a source. Nothing comes from nothing, and nothing returns to nothing.

5. Death, like birth, is a mystery of nature. One is a coming together of elements, and the other is a breaking down of those same elements. There is nothing shameful about it. It is not against the nature of a rational being, nor does it violate the laws of our creation.

6. It is a fact of nature that certain types of people will act in certain ways. Expecting otherwise is like expecting a fig tree not to produce juice. Remember this: in a very short time, both you and the person who wronged you will be dead, and soon after, even your names will be forgotten.

7. Remove the thought, "I am hurt," and the feeling of being hurt disappears. Remove the feeling of being hurt, and the injury itself is gone.

8. If something doesn't make you a worse person, it doesn't make your life worse, and it cannot harm you inside or out.

9. The law of nature must operate this way for the common good.

10. Everything that happens, happens exactly as it should. If you pay close attention, you will see this is true. I don't just mean it follows a natural order, but that it follows a sense of justice, as if someone is handing out exactly what each person deserves. Keep observing this, and in whatever you do, aim for true goodness. Hold onto this standard in every action.

11. Do not view things the way your insulter sees them, or the way they want you to see them. See things exactly as they truly are.

12. Always be ready for two things. First, only do what your reason—the ruling power within you—suggests is best for humanity. Second, be willing to change your mind if someone can correct you or offer a better perspective. But make sure you change your mind because you genuinely believe it serves justice or the common good, not because it brings you pleasure or popularity.

13. Do you have a rational mind? Yes. Then why aren't you using it? If your mind is doing its job, what more do you need?

14. You exist as a small part of a vast whole. You will eventually disappear back into the source that created you, or rather, you will be transformed and absorbed back into the creative intelligence of the universe.

15. Many grains of incense are placed on the same altar. One burns up first, another later. It makes no difference.

16. Within ten days, if you return to living by moral principles and reason, the same people who now see you as a wild beast or an ape will treat you like a god.

17. Don't live as if you have ten thousand years left. Death is always hanging over you. While you are alive, and while you still have the power, choose to be good.

18. You gain so much free time and peace when you stop worrying about what your neighbors say, do, or think. Focus only on making sure your own actions are just and pure. As Agathon said, don't look around at the moral character of others. Run straight toward your own goal without getting distracted.

19. Anyone obsessing over the legacy they will leave behind forgets that the people remembering them will also die very soon. And the generation after that will die, until every memory of them, passed down by fleeting admirers, fades entirely. Even if you and the people remembering you lived forever, what does fame actually do for you? It means nothing to the dead, and for the living, what real value does praise hold, aside from maybe some practical advantage? You are ignoring the power nature gave you today just to obsess over what people will say about you tomorrow.

20. Anything that is truly beautiful is beautiful all on its own. Its beauty ends right there, and praise has absolutely nothing to do with it. Nothing gets better or worse just because someone praises it. This applies to everyday things too, like physical objects or artwork. True beauty doesn't need anything added to it, just like the law, truth, kindness, or honor. Does any of these gain a single ounce of grace from praise, or lose anything from criticism? Does an emerald lose its value if no one praises it? Does a lack of compliments ruin gold, ivory, a flower, or a shrub?

21. If our souls survive death, you might ask, how has the air held them all for eternity? I would ask in return: how has the earth held so many buried bodies for so long? Just as corpses break down and make room for others, souls released into the air remain for a while before they are transformed, diffused, and reabsorbed into the universal spirit, making room for new souls. This is the answer, assuming souls do survive. But we shouldn't just think about buried bodies. Think of all the plants and animals we consume every day. We act as living graves for countless creatures, yet there is always room for more, because everything is broken down into blood, air, and fire. What is the key to understanding this? It is the difference between the physical matter and the life force that shapes it.

22. Do not wander off your path. Give every action the justice it deserves, and make sure you truly understand everything you think about.

23. I am in tune with everything that fits your harmony, O Nature. Nothing is too early or too late for me if it comes on your schedule. Everything your seasons bring is fruit to me. Everything comes from you, exists within you, and returns to you. The poet says, "Dear city of Athens!" Why shouldn't I say, "Dear city of the Universe!"

24. "Do a few things if you want peace," says the philosopher. A better rule is: "Do only what is necessary." Do what the reason of a social being requires, and do it with the right spirit. This brings the peace of doing good, along with the peace of having less to do. Most of the things we say and do are unnecessary. Cut them out, and you will have more free time and less stress. In every situation, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary?" And don't just eliminate unnecessary actions—eliminate unnecessary thoughts, too. That way, pointless actions won't follow.

25. Try living the life of a good person and see how it works for you. Be content with the fate the universe has assigned you, and find satisfaction in the justice of your own actions and the kindness of your heart.

26. You have seen the chaotic way of living; now try this one. Avoid overcomplicating things and seek simplicity. Has someone done wrong? The burden of that wrong is on them. Has something happened to you? That is fine. Everything that happens was destined for you from the very beginning. In short, life is brief. Use the present moment to act with reason and justice. Stay clear-headed, even when you relax.

27. The universe is either an orderly system or a chaotic mess. But it is definitely an orderly system. How could there be order within you, but only chaos in the universe, especially when everything—even though it seems separate—is so deeply connected?

28. Look at how ugly these character traits are: malicious, cowardly, savage, beastly, childish, stupid, fake, crude, deceitful, and tyrannical.

29. Anyone who doesn't understand the universe they live in is a stranger to it, and so is anyone who ignores what is happening right in front of them. You are a deserter if you run away from the reason that governs our world. You are blind if you close your mind's eye. You are a beggar if you rely on others and cannot find what you need within yourself. You are an outcast from the universe if you rebel against nature and complain about your fate. The same nature that created this moment also created you. You are a traitor to the community if you separate your own soul from the shared soul of all rational beings.

30. One man lives as a philosopher without a coat, another without books, and a third half-naked. One says, "I have no bread, but I stick to reason." Another says, "I have no education, but I still hold on to it."

31. Love the trade you have learned, no matter how humble, and find your peace in it. Spend the rest of your life as someone who has entrusted everything to the gods, acting neither as a tyrant nor a slave to anyone.

32. Think back to the time of Emperor Vespasian. It looked exactly like our time. People were marrying, raising children, getting sick, and dying. They were fighting, feasting, trading, and farming. They flattered each other, acted stubborn, suspected each other, plotted, wished for the death of rivals, complained about their luck, chased affairs, hoarded money, and hunted for power and kingdoms. All of those lives are gone now. Move forward to the time of Trajan. It was the exact same story, and that generation is dead, too. Look at the history of other eras and nations. See how they rushed around in a frenzy, only to fall and return to the earth. Most importantly, remember the people you knew personally who wasted their lives chasing empty things instead of following their own true nature and finding peace. Remember to only care about things in proportion to their actual value. If you keep this in mind, you won't get burned out by caring too much about things that don't matter.

33. The popular phrases of the past are now obsolete, and the names of once-famous people sound strange to us today. Camillus, Caeso, Volesus, Leonnatus—then Scipio and Cato, and eventually Augustus, Hadrian, and Antoninus—they are all forgotten. Everything rushes toward an end, quickly becomes a myth, and is buried in oblivion. And this is just for the people who achieved massive fame. For the rest of us, the moment we die, we are entirely unknown and forgotten. What does it even mean to be remembered forever? It is completely empty. So, what should we actually care about? Only this: a just mind, unselfish actions, honest words, and a spirit that embraces everything that happens as natural, inevitable, and flowing from the same source as we do.

34. Willingly hand yourself over to fate, letting it spin whatever life it chooses for you.

35. Everything is temporary—both the person remembering and the thing being remembered.

36. Constantly observe that everything exists in a state of change. Keep this thought with you: Nature loves nothing more than changing what exists and creating new things out of it. Everything that exists right now is just the seed of what is coming next. Don't make the mistake of thinking seeds only grow in dirt or a womb.

37. You are going to die soon, and you still haven't found simplicity or peace. You still haven't stopped worrying about external things hurting you. You still haven't learned to be kind to everyone, or realized that wisdom simply means acting justly.

38. Look closely at the minds of the people in charge. See what they care about, what they chase, and what they run away from.

39. The things you consider evil don't exist in someone else's mind, nor do they exist in the physical changes of the world around you. So where is the evil? It exists entirely in your own judgment of what is bad. Stop judging it as bad, and everything is fine. Even if your body is cut, burned, or infected, your mind can remain perfectly calm. As long as a situation can happen to both a good person and a bad person, it cannot truly be considered good or evil. Anything that happens to everyone—regardless of how they live—is simply a natural event.

40. Always think of the universe as one living creature, with one physical body and one soul. Notice how everything connects back to the single mind of this creature. See how everything moves together in a single motion, how everything causes everything else, and how it is all intricately woven together.

41. "You are just a little soul carrying a corpse," as Epictetus used to say.

42. There is nothing bad about undergoing change, just as there is nothing inherently good about staying the same.

43. Time is a rushing river, a violent flood of events. As soon as something appears, it is swept away, replaced by the next thing, which will also be swept away.

44. Everything that happens is as normal and expected as a rose in spring or fruit in summer. That includes sickness, death, slander, betrayal, and everything else that makes foolish people overly happy or miserable.

45. What comes next is always deeply connected to what came before. It isn’t just a random list of disconnected events forced together. It is a rational, harmonious relationship. Just as the things that exist right now fit together perfectly, the things that are yet to come will unfold in a beautiful, logical order, not just one after another.

46. Always remember the words of Heraclitus: the death of earth becomes water, the death of water becomes air, the death of air becomes fire, and the cycle reverses. Remember what happens to the man who forgets where the road leads. People often fight against the very reason that guides the universe, and they act surprised by the things they see every single day. We shouldn’t act or speak as if we are sleepwalking, even though our minds are active in our sleep. And we shouldn’t just blindly accept everything our parents tell us like naive children.

47. If a god told you that you were going to die tomorrow, or at the latest, the day after, you wouldn’t really care which day it was—unless you were incredibly cowardly. What is the difference, really? In the same way, don't think it makes much of a difference whether you die in many years or tomorrow.

48. Think constantly about all the doctors who furrowed their brows over their patients, only to die themselves. Think of the astrologers who arrogantly predicted the deaths of others, the philosophers who wrote endless books on death and immortality, the generals who slaughtered thousands, and the tyrants who abused their power over life and death as if they themselves were immortal. Think of entire cities that have died: Helice, Pompeii, Herculaneum, and countless others. Next, go through all the people you have known personally. One person buried a friend, then died and was buried by someone else, who was then buried by another. And it all happens so fast.

Look at human affairs and see how short-lived and cheap they really are—mucus yesterday, and tomorrow, ashes or rotting flesh. Spend the brief time you have left living in tune with nature, and leave this life gracefully. Be like a ripe olive falling from the tree, blessing the earth that grew it and thankful to the branch that held it.

49. Be like a cliff that the waves crash against. It stands firm while the furious water settles down around it.

"I am so unlucky that this happened to me," you say.
No. Instead say, "I am lucky that, even though this happened, I am still unbothered. I am not broken by the present, and I am not afraid of the future."

This situation could have happened to anyone, but not everyone could have faced it without falling apart. Why focus on the bad luck of the event rather than the good luck of your own mental strength? Can you really call something a misfortune if it doesn't damage your human nature? And does it damage your nature if it doesn't violate your purpose? You know what your purpose is. Does this event stop you from being just, generous, sensible, wise, careful, truthful, honorable, or free? Does it stop you from using any of the qualities that make you fully human?

Remember this rule for the future whenever you face sorrow: This event is not a misfortune, but handling it bravely is incredibly good luck.

50. It might seem like a simple thought, but it is highly effective for overcoming the fear of death: think about the people who clung to life the longest. Did they gain anything more than those who died young? They are all lying dead somewhere. Think of long-lived men like Cadicianus, Fabius, Julian, and Lepidus. They watched countless funerals, only to be carried to the grave themselves. The difference in time is incredibly small, and look at the trouble, the terrible company, and the failing bodies they had to endure during those extra years. Don't value it so highly. Look back at the eternity behind you, and forward into the infinite abyss ahead. Compared to infinity, what is the difference between living for three days and living for three generations like Nestor?

51. Always take the shortest path. The shortest path is the one that aligns with nature. Speak and act according to the healthiest, most rational rules. This simple choice will free you from stress, conflict, exhaustion, and the need to show off.
