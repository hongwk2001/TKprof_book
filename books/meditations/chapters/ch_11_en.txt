Book 11

1. These are the traits of the rational soul. It sees itself. It shapes and regulates every part of itself. It enjoys the fruit it creates, unlike plants or animals whose products are enjoyed by others. It achieves its own goal, no matter when life comes to an end. If a dance or a play is interrupted, the whole performance is ruined. But the rational soul is different. No matter what it is doing, or when death finally comes, it makes its own part complete and fully sufficient. It can confidently say, "I have received what is mine." The rational soul also explores the entire universe and the empty space around it, understanding its design. It reaches out into limitless eternity. It grasps the endless cycles of rebirth, seeing clearly that those who come after us will see nothing new. Likewise, those who came before us saw nothing more than we do now. In fact, anyone who has reached forty years old and has a basic understanding of the world has already seen everything that ever was and ever will be, because things constantly repeat themselves. The rational soul is also defined by love for everyone around us, truth, modesty, and above all else, self-respect. This self-respect is a trait shared by the universal law. Therefore, there is no conflict between right reason and the reason of justice.

2. You will quickly lose interest in a beautiful song, a dance, or a gymnastics routine if you break them down. If you separate the melody into single notes and ask yourself, "Does this one note impress me?", you will be embarrassed to admit it does not. The same happens if you break a dance down into individual movements and poses, or do the same for a gymnastic display. As a general rule, with the exception of virtue and virtuous actions, remember to look at the individual parts of things. By taking them apart, you will see how unimpressive they really are. Apply this habit to life as a whole.

3. How happy is the soul that is ready to leave the body when the time comes. Whether it is meant to be extinguished, scattered into the universe, or to survive in some other form. But this readiness must come from your own clear judgment. It should not come from simple stubbornness, as we see with the Christians. It must be deliberate and dignified, without any dramatic or tragic flair. That way, your calm acceptance can inspire others to feel the same.

4. Have I done anything for the common good? If so, then I have already benefited myself. Keep this thought with you always, and never stop acting on it.

5. What is your profession? To be good. And how else can you achieve this if not by holding sound principles? Principles about the universe as a whole, and about human nature in particular.

6. First, tragic plays were created to remind us of reality. They show us that certain events simply happen, and they are fated to happen exactly as they do. They teach us that the things which entertain us on stage should not break our hearts on the greater stage of the real world. You see that these struggles must be endured. Even the characters who cried out in agony had to bear their burdens. Our tragic playwrights have spoken some excellent truths, especially lines like these, "If the Gods neglect me and my children, it is for some good reason." Or again, "It is useless to be angry at external things." And also, "We must reap our lives like ears of ripened corn."
After tragedy came Old Comedy. It spoke with the harsh freedom of a strict teacher. By using plain, direct language, it did a great job of teaching humility. Diogenes used a very similar method. Next, consider the purpose of Middle Comedy. Finally, think about why New Comedy was introduced. It eventually decayed into nothing more than clever but empty mimicry. It is true that the writers of New Comedy still said a few useful things. But what real purpose did they have for all that poetry and playmaking?

7. It is incredibly clear that no other path in life is better suited for the practice of philosophy than the one you are on right now.

8. A branch cut off from the branch next to it is completely separated from the entire tree. In the same way, a person who turns away from a single fellow human has fallen away from the whole community. A branch is cut off by someone else. But a person separates himself from his neighbor by his own actions, through hatred and avoidance. He does not realize that by doing this, he has ripped himself away from the universal community. 
Yet, the founder of this great community has given us a remarkable gift. We always have the power to graft ourselves back onto our kind. We can once again become natural parts that complete the whole. However, if this separation happens too often, it becomes harder and harder to reunite and restore the broken piece. As gardeners will tell you, a branch that has always grown with the tree and stayed connected is not the same as one that was cut off and grafted back on. They might grow on the same tree, but they no longer share the same mind.

9. When people oppose you on the path of right reason, they have no power to stop you from doing what is right. In the exact same way, do not let them stop you from treating them with kindness. Be equally vigilant in two areas. First, stick to your steady judgment and right actions. Second, stay gentle toward those who try to stop or bother you. Getting angry at them is just as weak as giving up and accepting defeat. Both reactions make you a deserter. You run away if you give up in fear, and you run away if you refuse to stand by your fellow humans as friends and family.

10. Nature can never be inferior to art, because all art is just an imitation of nature. If that is true, then nature, being the most perfect and comprehensive force of all, cannot be less capable than human skill. All arts take lesser materials and use them for higher purposes. The universe does the exact same thing. This is where justice comes from, and all other virtues grow out of justice. But we cannot preserve justice if we care too much about things that do not truly matter. We will lose it if we allow ourselves to be easily fooled, act recklessly, or constantly change our minds.

11. The things that trouble you, the ones you desperately chase or try to avoid, do not actually come to you. Instead, you are the one going to them. Let your judgment about them find peace. Then they will remain perfectly still, and you will no longer be caught in the endless cycle of chasing them or running away.

12. The soul takes on a perfect, sphere-like shape when it stops reaching out for external things and stops shrinking back into itself. It should neither collapse inward nor stretch out aggressively. Instead, it should shine with a steady light. By this light, it can see the truth in all things, both out in the world and deep within itself.

13. Does anyone despise me? That is his problem. My only concern is to make sure I do not say or do anything that deserves to be despised. Does anyone hate me? That is his business. I will remain kind and good-natured to everyone. I will be ready to show the person who hates me where he went wrong. I will not do this to scold him or to show off my own patience. I will do it out of genuine goodness, just like Phocion used to do, assuming his goodness was truly sincere. 
Your inner character should be so pure that the divine powers never see you angry or complaining about anything. Why should it feel like a hardship to simply act in harmony with your own nature? Why struggle to accept what the universe decides is right for this moment? After all, you are a human being, placed here to serve the common good.

14. People despise one another, yet they still flatter each other. They constantly try to climb over one another, yet they grovel at each other's feet.

15. How fake and rotten is the man who says, "I am going to be totally honest with you." What are you doing, my friend? There is no need to announce it. Your honesty should speak for itself. True sincerity should be written clearly on your forehead. A person's character should shine right out of their eyes, just as someone who is loved can instantly feel it in the gaze of the person who loves them. 
A genuinely good and honest person should be like someone with a strong smell. A passerby notices them the second they get close, whether they want to or not. Making a big show of your honesty is just hiding a dagger under your cloak. There is nothing worse than the fake friendship of a wolf. Avoid it above all else. A truly good, honest, and kind person shows all these traits in their eyes, and you simply cannot miss it.

16. The soul has the power to live the best possible life, as long as it remains indifferent to things that do not truly matter. You can achieve this indifference by looking at everything carefully, both in its parts and as a whole. Remember that no external object can force you to have an opinion about it, nor can it force its way into your mind. Things stand completely still outside of us. We are the ones who create judgments about them, effectively writing these opinions into our own hearts. We have the power to stop writing them down. And if a false judgment sneaks in by accident, we can wipe it out instantly. 
We only need to keep up this guard for a short time, and then life will be over. Why should we view this effort as difficult? If an event aligns with nature, embrace it happily, and dealing with it will become easy. If it goes against nature, figure out what actually fits your true self and pursue that fiercely. Do this even if it brings you no fame or glory. Everyone is forgiven for simply seeking their own true good.

17. Look closely at where every object came from and what materials it is made of. Think about what it will eventually transform into, and what state it will be in after that change. Remember that through all of this, it will suffer no harm.

18. When people offend me, I must keep these things in mind.

First. Consider how I am connected to all of humanity. We were made for one another. In another sense, as a leader, I was placed over them like a ram over a flock or a bull over a herd. But look even deeper. The universe is either a random chaotic crash of atoms, or it is governed by an intelligent nature. If it is guided by intelligence, then lower beings were created for the sake of the higher, and the higher beings were created to help one another.

Secondly. Look at how these people act at the dinner table, in bed, and everywhere else. Pay special attention to the core principles they blindly follow, and notice how much arrogant pride they take in their own actions.

Thirdly. If they are doing the right thing, I have no reason to be upset. If they are doing the wrong thing, it is obvious they are doing it by mistake and out of ignorance. No soul willingly chooses to be deprived of the truth, nor do they willingly fail to treat others fairly. People are deeply hurt when they are called unjust, ungrateful, greedy, or bad neighbors.

Fourthly. You make mistakes all the time. You are no better than anyone else. Even if you manage to avoid certain bad behaviors, you still carry the deep urge to commit them. You are only holding back out of cowardice, a fear for your reputation, or some other selfish reason.

Fifthly. You cannot even be absolutely sure they have done anything wrong. Many actions are done with good reasons that are hidden from you. Generally speaking, you need to learn a lot more about a person's motives before you can fairly judge their behavior.

Sixthly. When you are exhausted and overflowing with anger, remind yourself that human life lasts only for a fleeting moment. Very soon, we will all be laid to rest in the grave.

Seventhly. It is never the actions of other people that disturb us. Their actions belong entirely to their own minds. It is only our own opinions about those actions that cause us pain. Throw away those judgments! Decide right now to stop viewing the situation as a personal disaster, and your anger will vanish. How do you remove those judgments? By reminding yourself that what they did does not actually damage your character. If you do not believe that moral shame is the only true evil, you will end up committing terrible crimes yourself. You could easily become a thief or a total villain.

Eighthly. Think about how much more damage we suffer from our own anger and grief than from the things that made us angry in the first place.

Ninthly. True gentleness is invincible, as long as it is genuine and not a fake, mocking smile. What can the most insulting person in the world do to you if you consistently treat him with kindness? If you get the chance, gently correct him right at the exact moment he is trying to harm you. You can say, "No, my son, we were born for better things. You cannot hurt me, but you are hurting yourself." Calmly and delicately show him the broader truth. Remind him that bees and other social animals do not attack their own kind. Do this without any sarcasm or bitterness in your voice. Do not lecture him or try to show off to anyone listening. Speak directly to him, even if there is a crowd around you.

Remember these nine lessons as if they were gifts from the Muses, and start acting like a true human being for the rest of your life. Be equally careful to avoid getting angry at people and to avoid flattering them. Both are destructive to society and lead straight to harm. Whenever you feel your temper rising, remind yourself that flying into a rage is not manly. Gentleness and calm behavior are far more human, and therefore much more masculine. It is the mild and gentle person who possesses real strength, nerve, and courage, not the angry and impatient one. The closer a person gets to mastering their emotions, the closer they get to true power. Breaking down in grief is a sign of weakness, and so is losing your temper. In both cases, you have been injured, and you have surrendered.

If you want a tenth gift from the leader of the Muses, take this one. Expecting bad people not to do bad things is pure madness. It is demanding the impossible. But allowing them to abuse other people while selfishly demanding they never cross you is the mark of a foolish tyrant.

19. There are four states of the mind you must constantly guard against. When you catch them slipping in, you must wipe them out immediately by telling yourself the following. First, "This thought is completely unnecessary." Second, "This thought destroys our connection to other people." Third, "You cannot say this from your true heart, and speaking anything other than your deep truth is the most absurd way to live." The fourth state is anything that causes you to feel ashamed of yourself. This shame means the divine and reasoning part of your soul has surrendered to the lowest, physical, and mortal part of your body.

20. Your body is made of airy and fiery elements that naturally want to rise. However, they obey the universal law and stay grounded to keep you whole. You are also made of earthy and watery elements that naturally want to sink. Yet they are held up and remain in place, even though that is not their natural position. The physical elements always obey the grand design, staying exactly where a higher power put them, waiting for the final signal to dissolve. 
Isn't it tragic that your mind is the only part of you that rebels and complains about its job? No one is forcing your mind to do anything against its own nature. But it still grows impatient and fights back. Every time you lean toward injustice, wild indulgence, anger, sorrow, or fear, you are abandoning nature. When your soul complains about anything that happens to you, it is abandoning its post. Your soul was built for reverence and devotion to the divine, just as much as it was built for justice. In fact, honoring the universe is an even older and deeper part of social goodness than the practice of justice itself.

21. A person who does not have a single, constant goal in life cannot remain the same person throughout their life. But simply having one goal is not enough. You must also figure out what that goal should actually be. Most people cannot agree on what is good, except when it comes to things that benefit the public. Therefore, your single goal should be to serve society and your fellow citizens. The person who points all of his personal goals toward the common good will keep his actions steady. By doing this, he will always remain the same steadfast man.

22. Remember the story of the country mouse and the city mouse. Think about how the city mouse lived in constant fear and trembling.

23. Socrates used to say that the beliefs of the common crowd are just monsters and ghost stories meant to scare children.

24. When the Spartans hosted public shows, they always placed the seats for strangers in the shade. But they themselves sat wherever they could find room.

25. When Perdiccas invited Socrates to his court, Socrates refused. He gave this excuse, "I do not want to suffer the worst fate possible, which is to receive favors I can never repay."

26. In the writings of the ancient Ephesians, there is a helpful rule. They tell us to constantly remember the great people of the past who lived lives of deep virtue.

27. The Pythagoreans advised us to look up at the morning sky. It reminds us of the stars and planets that do their proper work endlessly and without failure. It reminds us of their perfect order, purity, and naked simplicity. After all, a star wears no disguise.

28. Think of Socrates wrapping himself in an animal skin after his wife Xanthippe took his cloak and left the house. Remember what he said to his friends when they were embarrassed and tried to run away from him because he was dressed so strangely.

29. You have to be taught how to read and write before you can teach anyone else. This is even more true when it comes to learning how to live.

30. Since you are still a slave to your own desires, your words can never truly be free.

31. And my heart laughed inside me.

32. They attack virtue itself with the harshest words imaginable.

33. Searching for fresh figs in the dead of winter is pure madness. It is just as crazy to cling to a child who is no longer meant to be yours.

34. Epictetus said that when you kiss your child goodnight, you should whisper to yourself, "Tomorrow, he may die." People would respond, "Those are terrible, unlucky words!" But Epictetus replied, "They are not unlucky at all. They simply describe a natural event. Is it unlucky to say that the corn has been harvested?"

35. The unripe grape, the ripe cluster, and the dried raisin are all just stages of change. They do not turn into nothingness. They simply transform into something that does not yet exist.

36. As Epictetus once said, no one on earth can steal your freedom of choice.

37. Epictetus also told us we need to learn the true art of giving our agreement to things. When it comes to our desires, he said we must carefully restrain them. They must be guided by caution, a focus on the common good, and a strong sense of proportion. He also warned us to completely avoid wild sensual passions, and to stop fighting against the things we simply cannot control.

38. He also said that this struggle is not about some random, unimportant issue. The fight is to decide whether we are going to be insane or sane.

39. Socrates asks, "What do you want? Do you want the souls of rational beings or irrational animals?" You answer, "Rational beings." Socrates continues, "What kind of rational beings? Virtuous or vicious?" You say, "Virtuous." Then he asks, "So why aren't you out there seeking them?" You reply, "Because we already have them." And Socrates delivers the final blow, "Then why are you all constantly fighting and arguing with each other?"
