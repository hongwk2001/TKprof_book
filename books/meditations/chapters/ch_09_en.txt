Book 9

1. Anyone who commits an injustice commits a crime against nature. Universal nature designed rational creatures to help each other. We are meant to be useful to one another based on our individual merits, and never to cause harm. Anyone who goes against this design is clearly disrespecting the oldest and most venerable of the gods.

Lying is also a crime against the same divine force. The nature of the universe is the nature of all things that exist, and everything that exists is connected to what has come before it. Nature itself is truth, the original source of all true things. Therefore, anyone who lies intentionally commits a crime against nature because they cause harm through deception. Even someone who lies unintentionally commits a crime, because they are out of tune with universal nature and create disorder in the universe by fighting its design. Anyone who opposes the truth is at war with nature. They have neglected the tools nature gave them and can no longer tell the difference between what is true and what is false.

Anyone who chases pleasure as a good thing, or avoids pain as an evil thing, is also committing a crime against nature. Such a person will inevitably end up blaming nature for giving bad and good people the wrong rewards. Bad people often enjoy pleasures and have the means to get them, while good people often experience pain and the things that cause it. Furthermore, a person who fears pain will eventually fear something that is a natural part of the world order, and this is disrespectful to nature. Someone who chases pleasure will eventually commit an injustice to get it, which is another clear crime. When it comes to things that nature treats equally, those who want to follow nature should also treat them equally. After all, nature would not have created both pain and pleasure if it favored one over the other. Anyone who is not indifferent to pain and pleasure, life and death, or fame and disgrace, is clearly going against nature, because universal nature uses all of these things equally. When I say nature uses them equally, I mean they happen naturally to all beings that exist. They follow one after another in the great chain of events that started with the very first impulse of providence. Guided by this first impulse, providence set the universe in motion from a specific starting point. It laid out the plan for everything that would ever exist, assigning specific powers to create all the different substances, changes, and cycles of life.

2. It would be ideal to leave this world without ever having experienced deceit, hypocrisy, luxury, or vanity. The next best thing would be to die once you are completely sick of these vices. Have you chosen to keep living in this corruption, or has experience not yet convinced you to run away from the plague? A corrupted mind is far more dangerous than any disease or polluted air. A physical disease is only a plague to animals because they are animals, but a corrupted mind is a plague to human beings because they are human.

3. Do not despise death. Accept it gladly as one of the things nature wants for you. Growing young, growing old, and reaching adulthood are all natural processes. So are growing teeth, growing a beard, turning gray, having children, and giving birth. Death is just like all the other natural changes that the seasons of life bring. A wise person shouldn't face death with carelessness, impatience, or arrogance. You should wait for it as just another operation of nature. Just as you wait for the day your wife gives birth to a child, you should wait for the hour when your soul will finally drop its physical shell. If you want a more common source of comfort, here is a thought that strikes right at the heart. You will be completely at peace with death if you think about the things you are leaving behind and the morals of the confused crowd your soul will finally be free from. You shouldn't be offended by these people. In fact, it is your duty to care for them and tolerate them gently. But remember that when you leave, you won't be leaving people who share your principles. The only thing that could possibly make you want to stay in this life would be living with people who share your beliefs. As things stand, you can see how exhausting it is to live among people who clash with you. It might even make you cry out, "Hurry up, death, before I forget myself too!"

4. The person who sins, sins against themselves. The wrongdoer hurts themselves by making their own character evil.

5. People are often unjust because of what they fail to do, not just because of what they do.

6. Be satisfied with your current opinion, as long as it is based on certainty. Be satisfied with your current actions, as long as they serve the common good. Be satisfied with your current mood, as long as you are content with whatever happens to you from the outside world.

7. Wipe away your false impressions. Stop your impulsive urges. Put out the fire of your desires. Keep your rational mind in complete control of itself.

8. Irrational animals all share a single kind of soul. Rational beings, on the other hand, all share a single reasoning intelligence. In the same way, there is one earth for all earthly things. All of us who can see and breathe share one light to see by and one air to breathe.

9. All things that share a common trait are strongly drawn to others of their kind. Earthy things are pulled toward the earth. Fluids flow together, and gases do the same. Only external force can keep them apart. Fire rises upward to join the elemental fire above. It is so eager to burn with all the other fire in the world that any dry material will easily catch fire, simply because the elements resisting the flame are too weak to stop it. In the exact same way, anything that shares the common intellectual nature is drawn toward its own kind, perhaps even more strongly. The more advanced a nature is, the stronger its drive to mix and connect with its equals. Even among irrational creatures, we see swarms of bees, herds of cattle, parents caring for their young, and a basic form of love. Animals have a soul, and in these more advanced creatures, you can see a mutual attraction that doesn't exist in plants, stones, or wood. Moving up to rational animals, you find societies, friendships, families, and assemblies. Even in war, there are treaties and truces. Among even higher beings, like the stars, there is a certain kind of unity even though they are incredibly far apart. Moving up the scale of existence creates sympathy even across vast distances. But look at what happens among us humans. We are the only intellectual beings who forget we are supposed to care for one another and unite. Here alone, that natural coming together is missing. Yet, even when people try to escape it, they are still surrounded and held in place by it. Nature always wins in the end. Look closely and you will see what I mean. You are more likely to find a piece of earth disconnected from the rest of the earth than to find a human being completely separated from all other humans.

10. Humanity, God, and the universe all bear fruit, each at the proper time. We usually only use the word fruit for vines and trees, but that doesn't matter. Reason produces its own kind of fruit, both for all of humanity and for itself. It creates things that are just like reason itself.

11. If you can, teach people a better way. If you can't, remember that you were given the virtue of kindness for exactly this reason. Even the gods are patient with people. The gods are so gracious that they even help bad people achieve health, wealth, and fame. You can do the same. What is stopping you?

12. Endure hard work and pain without acting miserable. Don't do it to get pity or admiration. You should only want one thing: to act or to hold back exactly as social wisdom demands.

13. Today I escaped from all my troubles. Or rather, I threw all my troubles out. They weren't outside of me to begin with. They were inside, living in my own opinions.

14. In our experience, all things are exactly the same. They only last for a single day, and their physical substance is cheap. Everything right now is exactly the same as it was when we buried the people who came before us.

15. Things exist completely outside of us. They don't know anything about themselves, and they don't explain themselves to us. So what is it that judges them? Your own reasoning mind.

16. The good and evil of a rational, social creature doesn't come from what it feels. It comes from what it does. In the same way, a person's virtue or vice is found in their actions, not their emotions.

17. When you throw a stone into the air, it is not a bad thing for the stone to fall, and it is not a good thing for it to rise.

18. Look deep into the souls of other people. You will see what kind of judges you are so afraid of, and how harshly they judge themselves.

19. Everything is constantly changing. You yourself are going through a continuous transformation and, in a way, decaying. The entire universe is doing the same.

20. When someone else makes a mistake, you must leave it with them.

21. When an action ends, or when an intense desire or opinion fades away, it is essentially a death for those things. This is not a bad thing. Think about the different stages of your own life: childhood, youth, adulthood, and old age. Every time you moved from one stage to the next, the previous stage died. Was there anything to be afraid of? Now think about your life when your grandfather was alive, then your mother, and then your father. You will find many other changes, endings, and transitions. Ask yourself: Was there anything to be afraid of? In the exact same way, there is nothing to fear when your entire life stops, ends, and changes.

22. Quickly examine your own mind, the mind of the universe, and the mind of the person who offended you. Look at your own mind so you can guide it toward justice. Look at the mind of the universe so you can remember what you are a part of. Look at the offender's mind so you can figure out whether they acted out of ignorance or on purpose, and to remember that they are your relative.

23. You yourself are a necessary part of a complete social system. Therefore, every action you take should also be a part of that social life. If one of your actions doesn't connect, directly or indirectly, to the common good, it ruins your life and destroys its unity. It is an act of rebellion. It is exactly like a citizen in a society who does everything in his power to tear himself away from the harmony of the community.

24. Children fighting! Silly games! Poor souls carrying around dead bodies! That is what our life is like. The play called The Masque of the Dead makes a lot more sense in comparison.

25. Look at the core quality of a cause. Separate it from its physical material and study it on its own. Then, figure out how long a thing with that specific quality can naturally exist at most.

26. You have suffered countless times because you weren't satisfied with letting your rational mind do what it was designed to do. That is enough.

27. When someone insults you, hates you, or says anything bad about you, look into their soul. Step inside and see what kind of person they really are. You will realize that you don't need to stress over making them think well or poorly of you. Even so, you should still be kind to them, because nature made them your friends. The gods help them too, through dreams and prophecies, even in the worldly things they care about so much.

28. The course of the world is always the same. It is a continuous rotation, up and down, from one age to the next. Either the universal mind controls every single event, in which case you should accept whatever it gives you, or it set things in motion once, and everything else follows in a necessary chain of events. Or maybe the origin of everything is just atoms and indivisible particles. The point is this: if there is a God, everything is fine. If everything is just random chance, you don't have to act randomly yourself.

Soon enough, the earth will cover all of us. Then the earth itself will change into other forms, and those forms will change again, endlessly. If you think about how incredibly fast these changes happen, rolling in like waves one after another, you will stop caring about mortal things.

29. The universal cause is like a rushing winter flood. It drags everything along with it. How worthless are those foolish politicians who pretend to understand matters of state, thinking they are both philosophers and leaders! They are completely full of hot air. Do what nature demands of you right now, my friend. Start working on it if you can. Don't look around to see if anyone is watching, and don't expect to build Plato's perfect Republic. Be satisfied if even the smallest thing goes right, and realize that even a small success is no minor achievement. After all, who can really change the way people think? Without changing their minds, what are they but slaves who complain while pretending to be obedient? Go ahead and talk about Alexander the Great, Philip of Macedon, and Demetrius of Phalerum. They know whether they actually understood what nature wanted from them, and whether they disciplined themselves to follow it. But if they just wanted to play the role of a tragic hero, no one is forcing me to do the same. The work of philosophy is simple and modest. Don't let anyone drag you off course to chase arrogant political power.

30. Look down from high above at the countless herds of people. Watch their solemn religious festivals and their endless travels in storms and calm weather. Watch the different phases of those being born, those joining together in life, and those dying in the end. Think about the lives people lived long ago, the lives they will live after you are gone, and the lives of people in foreign lands right now. How many of them don't even know your name? How many will forget it instantly? How many might praise you today but insult you tomorrow? Realize that being remembered after you die is completely worthless. Present glory means nothing. None of it matters at all.

31. Don't let anything outside of your own control disturb your peace of mind. Be just in everything your active mind decides to do. Focus your willpower and your actions entirely on the common good, because that is what you were designed for.

32. You can wipe out many of the unnecessary troubles that bother you, because they exist entirely in your own imagination. You will give yourself plenty of space and peace if you do this. You can achieve this by looking at the entire universe in your mind, and by thinking about eternity. Think about how quickly individual things change. Think about how incredibly short the time is between their birth and their destruction, how massive the ages were before they existed, and how infinite the eternity will be after they are gone.

33. Everything you see will quickly be destroyed. The people who watch those things get destroyed will also die very soon. The person who lives to be extremely old will end up in the exact same place as the person who dies young.

34. What kind of souls do these people have? What is the goal of all their hard work? Why do they love and respect the things they do? Strip away everything and imagine their souls naked in front of you. When they think their insults hurt us, or their praise helps us, their ego is truly unbelievable!

35. Loss is nothing more than change. Universal nature finds joy in change, and nature makes sure everything is ordered perfectly. Things have been created this way since the beginning of time, and they will continue this way for all of eternity. How can you say that everything has always been bad, and will always be bad? Are you saying that among so many gods, there is no power to fix things, and the universe is just cursed to carry a burden of endless misery?

36. Look at how corrupt the physical matter of everything really is. It is just water, dust, bones, and filth! Marble is just hardened dirt from the earth. Gold and silver are just heavy sediment. Our clothes are just animal hair, and purple dye is just blood. Everything else is exactly the same. Even the breath in your lungs is the same way, constantly changing from one state to another.

37. Enough of this miserable life. Enough complaining and acting foolishly. Why are you so upset? Are any of these problems new? What is getting you so worked up? Is it the cause of the problem? Take a good look at it. Is it the physical matter? Take a good look at that, too. Outside of those two things, there is nothing else. From now on, just act with more simplicity and goodness toward the gods. Whether you watch this show for a hundred years or for three years, it is exactly the same.

38. If someone has done wrong, the harm is on them. But then again, maybe they didn't do anything wrong at all.

39. Either everything comes from one single intelligence and connects into one unified body, in which case the individual part shouldn't complain about what happens for the good of the whole, or everything is just random atoms, a confusing mess of things mixing together and falling apart. So why are you so disturbed? Say to your soul: "You are dead. You are rotting. You have turned into an animal, joined the herd, and you are eating right alongside them."

40. Either the gods have power, or they don't. If they don't have power, why are you praying to them? If they do have power, why don't you pray for the strength not to fear things, not to desire things, and not to be sad about things outside your control? Why do you just pray for those external things to come or go? If the gods can help people at all, they can certainly help with your mindset. You might say, "The gods put those things in my own control." Well then, isn't it better to use what you actually control and keep your freedom, rather than obsessing over things you can't control and becoming a pathetic slave? Who told you the gods don't help us with the things that are inside our control? Start praying about those things and see what happens. One man prays, "Please let me sleep with that woman!" You should pray, "Please take away my desire to sleep with her!" Another man prays, "Please save me from this person!" You should pray, "Please take away my need to be saved from him!" A third person cries, "Please don't let me lose my child!" You should pray, "Please take away my fear of losing him!" Turn your prayers in this direction, and watch what happens.

41. Epicurus said: "When I was sick, I didn't talk about the diseases of my poor body, and I didn't discuss that kind of stuff with my visitors. I kept talking about natural philosophy just like before. I focused on how the mind can stay calm and guard its own inner strength, even when the physical body is in pain. I didn't let the doctors act arrogant or brag as if they were doing something incredibly important. My life just stayed pleasant and happy." You should do exactly what he did if you get sick or face any other disaster. Every school of philosophy agrees on this principle: never abandon your philosophy no matter what happens to you, and never join in the foolishness of ignorant people. Focus your mind entirely on the task in front of you and the tools you need to get it done.

42. When someone's shameless behavior offends you, immediately ask yourself: "Can the world exist without shameless people?" It cannot. So don't demand the impossible. Your enemy is just one of those shameless people who inevitably has to exist in the universe. Keep this same question ready when you are shocked by deceit, betrayal, or any other wrong. As soon as you remember that these types of people have to exist, you will be much kinder to them individually. It is also helpful to ask yourself: "What virtue did nature give humans to fight this specific flaw?" Nature gave us gentleness as an antidote for irrationality, and other specific strengths for other weaknesses. You always have the freedom to correct someone who has lost their way, because everyone who does wrong is simply missing their target and wandering off course. Besides, how have you actually been harmed? You will find that none of the people making you angry have done anything that could actually damage your rational mind. True harm and injury can only happen inside your own mind, nowhere else. Why is it surprising or bad that an uneducated person acts like an uneducated person? You should really blame yourself for not expecting them to make those mistakes. Your own reasoning gave you the ability to predict that they would probably do wrong. You forgot, and now you are surprised that they did it. Above all, when you blame someone for being unfaithful or ungrateful, look at yourself. The fault is obviously yours if you trusted a person with that kind of character to keep their word. The fault is also yours if you did a favor and expected something in return, instead of realizing that doing the good deed was its own reward. What more do you want when you do someone a kindness? Isn't it enough that you acted in line with your own nature? Do you really need to be paid for it? That is like your eyes demanding to be paid for seeing, or your feet demanding a reward for walking. These body parts were created for a specific purpose, and when they do their job, they achieve their proper goal. In the exact same way, humans were created by nature to be kind to each other. Whenever a person acts kindly, or does anything else for the common good, they have fulfilled the purpose they were created for, and they have gained what truly belongs to them.

End of Book 9
