Chapter II. WAGING WAR

1. Sun Tzu said: "In the operations of war, deploying a thousand fast chariots, a thousand heavy chariots, and a hundred thousand armored troops, with provisions to carry them a thousand miles, means massive expenses at home and on the front line. Factoring in diplomatic entertainment, supplies like glue and paint, and the maintenance of chariots and armor, the cost will run to a thousand ounces of silver per day. Such is the price of raising an army of 100,000 men."

2. "In active combat, if victory is delayed, your weapons will dull and your troops' morale will plummet. If you besiege a city, you will exhaust your strength."

3. "Furthermore, a prolonged campaign will drain the state’s resources to the breaking point."

4. "When your weapons are dull, your morale broken, your strength exhausted, and your funds depleted, rival warlords will capitalize on your vulnerability. At that point, not even the wisest strategist can reverse the ensuing disaster."

5. "While we have seen reckless speed in war, we have never seen true brilliance tied to prolonged delays."

6. "No nation has ever benefited from an endless war."

7. "Only a leader who deeply understands the catastrophic costs of war can truly grasp the most effective ways to wage it."

8. "A master commander does not conscript troops a second time, nor does he demand multiple resupplies from the homeland."

9. "Bring your military equipment from home, but forage for food in enemy territory. This ensures your army is always well-fed."

10. "An impoverished national treasury forces an army to be sustained by long-distance supply chains. Forcing citizens to maintain these distant supply lines drives them into poverty."

11. "Conversely, the physical presence of an army drives up local prices, and skyrocketing prices drain the people’s wealth."

12. "When their wealth is drained, the peasantry is crushed by heavy taxation."

13, 14. "With this devastating loss of wealth and exhaustion of strength, civilian homes will be stripped bare, and seventy percent of the people's income will vanish. Meanwhile, government expenditures to replace broken chariots, exhausted horses, shattered armor, and depleted weaponry will consume forty percent of the state’s total revenue."

15. "Therefore, a brilliant general makes it a priority to forage off the enemy. One cartload of enemy provisions is worth twenty of your own, and one unit of enemy feed is worth twenty from your own storehouses."

16. "To kill the enemy, your men must be driven by fierce anger; to strip the enemy of their resources, your troops must be incentivized with the spoils."

17. "In chariot warfare, when you capture ten or more enemy chariots, immediately reward the troops who captured the first one. Replace the enemy's flags with your own, and integrate the captured chariots into your own formations. Treat captured soldiers well and conscript them into your ranks."

18. "This is what it means to use the conquered foe to amplify your own strength."

19. "In war, let your supreme objective be swift victory, not a drawn-out campaign."

20. "Understand this: the commander of armies is the master of the people’s fate—the single individual upon whom the safety or ruin of the entire nation depends."
