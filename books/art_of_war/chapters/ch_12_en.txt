# Chapter XII. The Attack by Fire

**1. Sun Tzu said:** There are five methods of attacking with fire:
1. Burning the soldiers in their camp.
2. Burning their supply stores.
3. Burning their baggage trains.
4. Burning their arsenals and magazines.
5. Hurling fire into the enemy lines.

> **Du Mu:** Supply stores include provisions, fuel, and fodder. Arsenals and magazines hold weapons, tools, valuables, and clothing.
> 
> **Li Quan:** The tactic is to set the camp ablaze and eliminate the soldiers as they flee the flames. As the Han dynasty general Ban Chao stated during his western expedition: *"Nothing ventured, nothing gained. Our only option is a night assault by fire. Unable to see our true numbers, the barbarians will panic, allowing us to exterminate them."*
> 
> **Du Yu:** "Hurling fire" refers to dipping arrowheads into a brazier to ignite them, then firing these flaming arrows from powerful crossbows into enemy ranks.

**2.** To execute a fire attack, you must have the necessary means available. Combustible materials must always be kept ready.

> **Cao Cao:** You must rely on traitors within the enemy's camp to help execute this.
> 
> **Jia Lin:** You must take advantage of dry weather and strong winds.
> 
> **Du Mu:** Essential fire-starting materials include dry vegetation, reeds, brushwood, straw, grease, and oil.
> 
> **Zhang Yu:** This means preparing vessels to carry fire and kindling to ignite it.

**3.** There are proper seasons for fire attacks, and specific days for setting the blaze.

**4.** The proper season is during extreme dry spells. The specific days are when the moon aligns with the constellations of the Sieve, the Wall, the Wing, or the Cross-bar—these are days when winds are guaranteed to rise.

**5.** When attacking with fire, you must be prepared to respond to five possible developments:

**6.** (1) If fire breaks out inside the enemy's camp, immediately follow up with a coordinated assault from the outside.

**7.** (2) If the fire breaks out, but the enemy soldiers remain calm and hold their ground, bide your time. Do not attack.

**8.** (3) When the flames reach their absolute peak, strike if it is strategically sound. If not, hold your position.

> **Cao Cao:** If an opening presents itself, advance. If the resistance is too strong, retreat.

**9.** (4) If you can launch a fire attack from the outside, do not wait for a fire to start inside. Deliver your attack at the most favorable moment.

> **Du Mu:** If the enemy camps in a wasteland filled with dry grass, or in any position vulnerable to fire, seize the opportunity to burn them out immediately. Do not wait for internal sabotage. If you delay, the enemy might preemptively burn the surrounding vegetation themselves, neutralizing your advantage.

**10.** (5) When setting a fire, always stay upwind. Never attack from downwind.

> **Zhang Yu:** The enemy will naturally retreat away from the flames. If you attack them head-on from the leeward side, they will be forced to fight desperately for their lives, which will cost you dearly.
> 
> **Du Mu:** If an east wind blows, ignite the fire on the east side of the enemy and attack from the east. If you set the fire on the east but attack from the west, you will be caught in your own flames just like the enemy.

**11.** A wind that rises during the day will blow for a long time, but a night breeze will soon die down.

> **Mei Yaochen:** Generally, daytime winds subside at dusk, and nighttime winds fade at dawn.

**12.** An army must understand these five tactical developments of fire. They must calculate the movements of the stars and diligently watch for the proper days.

> **Du Mu:** We must track the stars to predict when the winds will rise before launching a fire attack.
> 
> **Zhang Yu:** We must not only know how to attack with fire, but also how to defend against it.

**13.** Those who use fire to aid their attacks show supreme intelligence. Those who use water to aid their attacks gain immense strength.

**14.** Water can be used to cut off an enemy, but it cannot destroy all their supplies.

> **Cao Cao:** Water can obstruct the enemy's path or split their forces in two, but it will not incinerate their stockpiles.
> 
> **Zhang Yu:** Water is highly effective, but it lacks the terrifying, all-consuming destructive power of fire.
> 
> **Wu Qi:** If an army camps in low-lying marshlands during heavy rain, they can be drowned by a flood. If they camp in overgrown, weed-choked plains battered by gales, they can be exterminated by fire.

**15.** It is tragic to win battles and successfully conquer objectives without cultivating the spirit of enterprise. The result is wasted time and general stagnation.

> **Cao Cao:** Rewards for outstanding service must never be delayed by even a single day.
> 
> **Du Mu:** If you fail to capitalize on momentum and reward those who deserve it, your subordinates will lose motivation, commands will be ignored, and disaster will follow.
> 
> **Mei Yaochen:** To ensure continuous victory, you must seize favorable moments and boldly employ decisive measures like fire and water. The most fatal mistake is to sit still and passively cling to past gains.

**16.** Hence the maxim: The enlightened ruler plans far ahead; the brilliant general cultivates his resources.

> **Du Mu:** A wise leader governs their troops through steadfast authority, unites them through unshakable trust, and motivates them with fair rewards. If trust erodes, the army shatters. If rewards are lacking, commands lose all meaning.

**17.** Do not move unless you see a clear advantage. Do not deploy your troops unless there is a tangible objective to gain. Do not fight unless the situation is absolutely critical.

**18.** No ruler should march an army into war merely to satisfy their own anger. No general should fight a battle purely out of spite.

**19.** If the move is advantageous, advance. If not, hold your ground.

**20.** Anger can eventually turn into joy; frustration can be replaced by satisfaction.

**21.** But a kingdom once destroyed can never be restored; the dead can never be brought back to life.

**22.** Therefore, the enlightened ruler exercises extreme caution, and the brilliant general moves with intense care. This is the only way to keep a nation at peace and an army intact.
