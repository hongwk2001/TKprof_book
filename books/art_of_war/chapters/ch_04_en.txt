Chapter IV. TACTICAL DISPOSITIONS

1. Sun Tzu said: "The great warriors of the past first made themselves invincible, then waited for the enemy's vulnerability to emerge."
> Cao Cao: "To make yourself invincible is to adopt a defensive posture. To wait for the enemy's vulnerability is to prepare for the offensive."

2. "Securing ourselves against defeat is in our own hands, but the opportunity to defeat the enemy is provided by the enemy himself."
> Du Mu: "By hiding our dispositions and remaining quiet, we secure ourselves. The enemy provides the opportunity by making a mistake or exposing a flaw."

3. "Therefore, the skilled warrior can guarantee his own invulnerability, but cannot guarantee the enemy's defeat."
> Mei Yao-ch'en: "We can ensure that we are not defeated, but we cannot force the enemy to make a mistake."

4. "Hence the saying: One may know how to conquer without being able to do it."

5. "Invincibility lies in defense; the possibility of victory in attack."

6. "Taking a defensive posture indicates insufficient strength; attacking indicates overwhelming strength."
> Zhang Yu: "When our strength is not yet sufficient to conquer the enemy, we must defend. When it is more than sufficient, we can attack."

7. "The master of defense hides in the deepest depths of the earth; the master of attack strikes from the highest heights of heaven. Thus, they can both protect themselves and achieve total victory."
> Du Mu: "Those who hide in the earth conceal their tracks so well that the enemy cannot find them. Those who strike from heaven attack so swiftly that the enemy cannot defend against them."

8. "To foresee a victory that any ordinary person could see is not the pinnacle of excellence."

9. "Neither is it the pinnacle of excellence to fight and conquer while the whole empire cheers."

10. "Lifting an autumn hair does not signify great strength; seeing the sun and moon does not signify sharp vision; hearing thunder does not signify sharp hearing."
> Cao Cao: "These are things that everyone can do. True excellence lies in seeing and hearing what others cannot."

11. "What the ancients called a master of war is one who not only wins, but excels in winning effortlessly."
> Du Mu: "He plans so well that the enemy is defeated before the battle even begins."

12. "Consequently, his victories earn him no reputation for brilliance, nor credit for courage."

13. "He wins his battles by making no mistakes. Making no mistakes establishes the certainty of victory, for it means defeating an enemy already beaten."
> Mei Yao-ch'en: "He leaves nothing to chance, ensuring that the enemy is in a position where they must lose."

14. "Therefore, the skilled commander positions himself where defeat is impossible, and never misses the moment to crush the enemy."

15. "Thus, the victorious strategist only seeks battle after the victory has been won, whereas the destined loser first fights and then hopes for victory."
> Zhang Yu: "The wise commander ensures all conditions for victory are met before engaging. The foolish commander rushes into battle and relies on luck."

16. "The supreme commander cultivates the moral law and strictly enforces discipline and method; thus, he holds the power to guarantee success."

17. "In military strategy, there are five essential elements: first, Measurement; second, Estimation; third, Calculation; fourth, Comparison; and fifth, Victory."

18. "Earth dictates Measurement; Measurement dictates Estimation; Estimation dictates Calculation; Calculation dictates Comparison; and Comparison dictates Victory."
> Cao Cao: "The terrain determines the distance. Distance determines the amount of resources. Resources determine the size of the force. The forces are compared, and victory is decided."

19. "A victorious army facing a defeated one is like a heavy weight placed in the scale against a single grain of sand."

20. "The devastating assault of a victorious force is like the bursting of pent-up waters into a chasm a thousand fathoms deep. These are the principles of tactical dispositions."
> Du Mu: "Water accumulates power when it is dammed up. When released, its force is unstoppable. Such is the power of a perfectly positioned army."
