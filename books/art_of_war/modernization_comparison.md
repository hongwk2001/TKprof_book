# 📖 The Art of War: Textbook & Study Edition

This document showcases the **Textbook Study Edition** of Sun Tzu's *The Art of War*, featuring Sun Tzu's core maxims, modernized commentaries from the **Eleven Chinese Strategists** (**Cao Cao**, **Du Mu**, **Mei Yao-ch'en**, **Li Quan**, **Zhang Yu**, etc.), and **explicit explanations of tactical metaphors**.

---

## 🏛️ Chapter 1: Laying Plans (始計)

```text
1. Sun Tzu said: The art of war is of vital importance to the state.

2. It is a matter of life and death, a path to either safety or ruin. Therefore, it is a subject of study that must not be neglected.

3. The art of war is governed by five constant factors, to be considered in all deliberations to understand the conditions of the battlefield.

4. These are: (1) The Moral Law; (2) Heaven; (3) Earth; (4) The Commander; (5) Method and Discipline.

> **Wang Tzu:** Without constant practice, the officers will be nervous and undecided when mustering for battle; without constant practice, the general will be wavering and irresolute when the crisis is at hand.
> **Explanation:** Mastery of military discipline is not passive; routine tactical drill is what keeps soldiers composed during the chaos of combat.

5. Heaven signifies night and day, cold and heat, times and seasons.

> **Wang Hsi:** What is meant is the general economy of Heaven, including the five elements, the four seasons, wind and clouds, and other natural phenomena.
```

## ⚔️ Chapter 6: Weak Points and Strong (虛實)

```text
1. Sun Tzu said: Whoever arrives first at the battlefield and awaits the enemy will be fresh for the fight; whoever arrives second and must rush into battle will be exhausted.

> **Cao Cao:** Emerge from the void, strike at vulnerable points, shun places that are defended, attack in unexpected quarters.
> **Explanation:** The "void" refers to undefended or unanticipated avenues of approach. Cao Cao emphasizes that attacking empty space—where the enemy is not—guarantees freedom of movement and maximum surprise.

2. Therefore, the clever combatant imposes his will on the enemy, and refuses to let the enemy's will be imposed on him.

> **Du Mu:** If the enemy is taking his ease, wear him down with feints; if well supplied with food, starve him out; if quietly encamped, force him to move.
> **Explanation:** Seizing the initiative means forcing the enemy to react to your maneuvers rather than dictating the pace of engagement.
```

## 🕸️ Chapter 10: Terrain (地形)

```text
1. Sun Tzu said: We may distinguish six types of terrain: (1) Accessible ground; (2) Entangling ground; (3) Temporizing ground; (4) Narrow passes; (5) Precipitous heights; (6) Positions at a great distance from the enemy.

2. Ground that can be freely traversed by both sides is called accessible.

3. Ground that can be abandoned but is hard to re-occupy is called entangling.

> **Mei Yao-ch'en:** Net-like country; venturing into it, you become entangled.
> **Explanation:** Mei Yao-ch'en compares entangling terrain to a fisherman's net (罟網之地). Once an army marches into dense thickets or tricky narrow marshland without securing a clear exit route, it becomes trapped like a fish caught in a net—unable to retreat if the enemy counterattacks.
```
