August 19. - Joy, joy, joy! Although it's not entirely all joyful news. I finally heard from Jonathan. The poor dear has been very ill. That's why he couldn't send a letter. Now that I know the reason, I'm not afraid to think or talk about it. Mr. Peter Hawkins passed the letter to me, and oh, how sweetly he wrote to me directly. I plan to leave first thing tomorrow morning to nurse Jonathan by his side and bring him safely home. Mr. Hawkins suggested it might not be a bad idea to have the wedding right there while we're at it. I held the nun's warm letter to my chest and cried my eyes out. Since it's a letter with news of Jonathan, I must keep it closest to my heart. For my heart is completely filled with Jonathan. All the travel plans are made, and my bags are packed. I only packed one outfit, and Lucy will take my trunk to London and keep it for me until I contact her. Perhaps... I should stop here. I need to save the things I want to say for Jonathan, my future husband. This letter, which he must have touched and looked at, will comfort me until we meet again.

Letter from Sister Agatha of the Hospital of St. Joseph and Ste. Mary, Buda-Pesth, to Miss Wilhelmina Murray.

"August 12.

"Dear Madam,

"I write at the request of Mr. Jonathan Harker, who has not yet recovered enough strength to take up a pen himself, but by the grace of God, St. Joseph, and the Virgin Mary, is showing steady improvement. He has been staying at our hospital and receiving treatment for severe brain fever for nearly six weeks. He asked me to convey his love to you, and also asked to write to Mr. Peter Hawkins in Exeter through this mail. With his respectful regards, he asked to apologize for the delay in his work and inform him that all the tasks entrusted to him have been completed. He plans to rest for a few more weeks at our sanatorium located in the mountains before returning. Mr. Harker also asked me to convey that even though he does not have much money on hand right now, he definitely wants to pay the expenses of his stay here so that others in need like him will not suffer hardship.

"Yours faithfully,

"With deep compassion and blessings,

"Sister Agatha.

"P.S. - I reopen the envelope while the patient is asleep to add a few more lines. Mr. Harker confided in me everything about you, and the fact that you two are soon to be married. May your future be full of blessings! According to the attending doctor, he seems to have suffered a terrible shock. When he was delirious with fever and having severe seizures, he spat out horrifying nonsense about wolves, poison, blood, ghosts, and demons—things too dreadful to even mention. Please be especially careful that there is nothing to provoke him with such matters for the time being. The traces of the illness he suffered are not so easily erased. We should have written to you sooner, but we knew absolutely nothing about his acquaintances, and we could not find any clues among his belongings. He came by train from Klausenburg, and the train conductor heard from the stationmaster there that Mr. Harker ran into the station and yelled at them to blindly give him a ticket to go home. Seeing his violent behavior, the station staff realized he was English and gave him a ticket to the furthest station they could toward his home.

"Please be assured that he is being well cared for here. Mr. Harker has already captivated everyone's heart with his uniquely sweet and gentle nature. He is recovering really quickly, so without a doubt, he will completely regain his former self within a few weeks. Even so, please take good care of him by his side just in case. I pray to God, St. Joseph, and the Virgin Mary that countless dazzlingly happy days unfold in your future."

Dr. Seward's Diary

August 19. - A sudden and bizarre change appeared in Renfield last night. Around 8 o'clock, he became highly excited and sniffed around everywhere like a hound looking for prey. The attendant was surprised by this sight and, knowing I was very interested in him, kept trying to talk to him. He, who was usually obedient and sometimes even groveling to the attendant, acted very arrogantly last night. He refused to even speak to the attendant, spitting out only this.

"I don't want to talk to you. You are nothing now. The 'Master' is near."

The attendant judged that he was gripped by sudden religious mania. If true, we must prepare for any contingencies. A man of monstrous strength with a combination of homicidal impulses and religious mania is extremely dangerous. When these two combine, it is a truly horrific disaster. At 9 o'clock, I went to see him myself. His attitude towards me was no different from how he treated the attendant. Wrapped up in sublime narcissism, the difference between me and the attendant seemed to mean nothing to him. It definitely looks like religious mania, and soon he will delude himself into thinking he is God. To such an omnipotent being, minor differences among humans would seem trivial. See how easily these madmen reveal their true colors! The true God watches even a single sparrow fall to the ground, but a god born of human vanity cannot even distinguish between an eagle and a sparrow. Oh, if only people realized this truth!

For over thirty minutes, Renfield became increasingly and violently excited. I didn't make it obvious I was watching him, but I didn't let my guard down for a single moment. Suddenly, the cunning gleam that madmen often have when they are fixated on something flashed in his eyes, followed by the characteristic nervous twitching of his head and back that mental hospital attendants are sickeningly familiar with. Then Renfield became unbelievably docile, sitting on the edge of the bed as if resigned, staring blankly into the air with dull eyes. To test whether his lethargy was real or an act, I casually brought up the topic of his pets (bugs), a cheat code that had never failed. At first he kept his mouth shut, but finally snapped in irritation.

"It's all annoying! I don't care the slightest bit about those things."

"What?" I asked back. "You don't mean you've lost interest in spiders, do you?" (His recent hobby was raising spiders, and his notebook was filled with tables crammed with tiny numbers.) To this, he threw an enigmatic answer.

"The bridesmaids delight the eyes while waiting for the bride. But when the bride approaches, those bridesmaids lose their light in eyes that are already filled."

He refused to explain further, and stubbornly just sat on the bed the whole time I was there.

Tonight I am terribly exhausted and depressed. I can't shake off thoughts of Lucy, and my mind is full of thoughts about what would have happened if things had turned out differently. If I can't fall asleep right away, chloral (sleeping pill)—C₂HCl₃O·H₂O, the modern-day Morpheus (God of Dreams)! I must be careful not to get addicted to it. No, I will not take anything tonight! Since I am thinking of Lucy, I will not desecrate her by mixing sleeping pills. If worse comes to worst, I will stay up all night with my eyes wide open....
