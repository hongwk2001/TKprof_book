"Where are you now?" The answer was still dreamy, but there was a purposeful tone to her voice. It was as if she was trying to interpret a sensation. It was the kind of tone Mina used when rereading notes she had written in shorthand.

"I don't know. Everything feels unfamiliar."

"What do you see?"

"I can't see anything. It's pitch black all around."

"What do you hear?" I could sense the extreme tension laced in the professor's patient voice.

"The sound of water. Rippling waves passing by, and the sound of small waves crashing. It's coming from outside."

"Then are you on a ship right now?" We looked at each other with fearful hearts, trying to read each other's thoughts. We barely even dared to think about what that meant. The answer returned immediately.

"Yes, that's right!"

"Do you hear any other sounds besides that?"

"I hear people running around heavily stamping their feet above my head. And a loud rattling sound of a machine winding up an anchor, along with the creaking of iron chains."

"And what are you doing?"

"I am still... very still. Like someone dead!" Her voice slowly faded into a heavy sigh of someone falling into a deep sleep, and her opened eyes closed again.

The sun had already fully risen, filling the room with the bright light of day. Dr. Van Helsing placed a hand on Mina's shoulder and gently laid her head on the pillow. For a moment, Mina lay peacefully like a sleeping child, and then woke up with a long sigh. She looked around at us in confusion and said. "Was I talking in my sleep?" Without any memory of it, she seemed to instinctively grasp the situation and was desperate to know what she had said. When the professor repeated their conversation exactly, Mina immediately said.

"Then there is no time to lose. It might not be too late yet!" Mr. Morris and Lord Godalming hurried toward the door, but the professor's calm voice stopped them.

"Wait, my friends. Wherever that ship is, it was just weighing anchor while Madam was speaking. At this very moment, in the vast Port of London, dozens of ships must be weighing anchor. Which one of those ships do you intend to find? Thank God, we have finally found a clue, though we don't yet know where it will lead us. We have been blind all this time. Blind like mere mortal men. Looking back now, the things we should have seen and realized beforehand are so terribly clear! Ah, I'm sorry. Was I rambling too much? I finally understand why the Count bothered to grab that money even while his life was threatened by Jonathan's fierce attack. He intended to flee. Listen well, flee! He has only one box of earth left, and he realizes that this London, where so many men are chasing him like hounds after a fox, is no longer safe for him. He has loaded his last box of earth onto a ship and is leaving England. He may believe he can escape, but he is dead wrong! We will track him to the very end. Just as Arthur shouts in his red coat when hunting, 'Tally ho!' The old fox is very cunning. Oh! So cunning. Therefore, we must become just as cunning as he is. Since I am also a cunning man, I can roughly guess his next plan. For now, you may rest comfortably. Because a vast ocean lies between us that he never wishes to cross, and cannot cross even if he wanted to. Unless the ship docks at land, and unless it is high or low tide, he cannot even disembark from the ship. So you see. The sun has just risen, and we have the whole day until sunset. Let's wash up, change our clothes, and have a hearty breakfast, which we all desperately need. Now that we know he is no longer on this land, we can eat with peace of mind." Mina looked at him with earnest eyes and asked.

"But if he has already left us, why must we continue to chase him?" The professor took Mina's hand, gently patted it, and replied.

"Please do not ask anything yet. I will answer all your questions after breakfast." The professor said nothing more, so we scattered to change our clothes.

After breakfast, Mina asked the same question again. The professor stared at her seriously for about a minute and then spoke in a very sad voice.

"Because, my dear Mrs. Mina, even if we have to follow him to the very gates of hell, we must find him more desperately than ever before!" Mina's face turned pale as she asked in a trembling voice.

"Why?"
