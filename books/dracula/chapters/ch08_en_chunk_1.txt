August 17. - I haven't been able to write in my journal for two days. I just haven't had the energy for it. It feels as if dark clouds are hanging heavily over our happiness. There is still no news from Jonathan, Lucy is growing weaker by the day, and her mother's life has entered a countdown. I have no idea why Lucy is withering away like that. She has a hearty appetite, sleeps well, and gets fresh air, but the color in her cheeks is fading, and she becomes more lethargic and listless every day. At night, I hear her panting heavily, as if she can hardly breathe. Every night I tie the room key tightly to my wrist, but she still gets up, paces the room, and slumps by the open window. Last night, I woke up terrified to find her leaning halfway out the window. I tried shaking her awake, but she didn't budge. She was completely unconscious. By the time I managed to revive her, she was limp as wet cotton, silently pouring out tears while gasping for breath painfully. When I asked why she went to the window, she shook her head and avoided my eyes. I only pray that the wound from that safety pin hasn't become infected. When I checked her neck while she was sleeping a little while ago, the wound hadn't healed at all; in fact, it was still open and seemed larger than before. The skin around it was pale white, making it look exactly as if there were two red spots side by side in the middle of a white patch. I intend to watch her for another day or two, and if it doesn't get better, I'll have a doctor examine her immediately.

Letter from the law firm of Samuel F. Billington in Whitby to Carter, Paterson & Co. transport company in London.

"August 17.

"Dear Sirs,

"Enclosed please find the invoice for the goods dispatched via the Great Northern Railway. Please transport these goods to the Carfax estate near Purfleet as soon as they arrive at the King's Cross freight station. The house is currently empty, but I am sending a tagged set of keys along with this letter.

"This shipment, consisting of 50 boxes in total, should be unloaded in the partially ruined building on the estate, marked 'A' on the enclosed sketch. It was used as a chapel long ago, so your staff should be able to find it easily. The freight will leave on the 9:30 PM train tonight and is scheduled to arrive at King's Cross station at 4:30 PM tomorrow. Since the client desires the fastest possible delivery, I would appreciate it if you would have wagons and men ready at the arrival time to transport it straight to the destination. To prevent any delay caused by your departmental payment procedures, I am enclosing a check for ten pounds (£10); please acknowledge receipt. If the final transportation cost is less than this, please return the difference, and if it exceeds it, I will immediately send a check for the balance. When you finish the job and leave, please leave the keys in the central entrance hall. The owner will enter with a duplicate key and retrieve them.

"Please do not consider it rude of me to repeatedly urge such swift processing.

"Yours faithfully,

"Samuel F. Billington Law Firm."

Letter from Carter, Paterson & Co. transport company in London to the Samuel F. Billington Law Firm in Whitby.

"August 21.

"Dear Sirs,

"We have safely received the 10 pounds, and we are returning the excess amount of 1 pound 17 shillings 9 pence by check, as detailed in the enclosed receipt. The goods were delivered exactly as instructed, and the keys were packaged in a parcel and left in the central entrance hall.

"Respectfully yours,

"Carter, Paterson & Co. Representative."

Mina Murray's Journal

August 18. - I am extremely happy today. I am sitting on a bench in the churchyard writing this. Lucy has become remarkably healthier. Last night, she slept soundly without waking me up once. She still looks pitifully pale and gaunt, but a rosy vitality has already begun to return to her cheeks. She is now cheerfully regaining her energy and sparkling with vitality. She seems to have completely washed away her recent morbid reticence. She even playfully brought up what happened that night—when I found her sleeping on this very bench—a memory I don't even need to dwell on. Lucy tapped the stone slab with the heel of her shoe and added.

"My poor little feet didn't make much noise back then! If old Mr. Swales were alive, he probably would have said I did it because I didn't want to wake Geordie (the owner of this grave)." Since Lucy was in such a talkative mood, I asked her what she dreamt about that night. She frowned before answering, an adorable expression that Arthur reportedly loves. I can completely understand why he fell for her. Soon after, she spoke in a half-dreamy tone, as if trying to drag the memory out of her mind.

"It was all too vivid to be a dream. I just wanted to be right here. I don't know why. I was afraid of something. Even without knowing what it was. I remember passing through the streets and crossing the bridge while asleep. A fish jumped up and I leaned over to look, and as I climbed the stairs, I heard all the dogs in the neighborhood barking. Then, just like I saw at sunset, a long, dark figure with flashing red eyes vaguely appeared, and a bittersweet feeling suddenly embraced me. After that, I felt like I was sinking deep into dark blue water, and that ringing sound people supposedly hear when they are drowning echoed in my ears. Then everything seemed to fade away. It felt like my soul left my body and was floating in the air. I think I also saw the West Lighthouse beneath my feet. Then, a pain hit me as if there were an earthquake, and when I regained my senses, you were shaking my body. I saw you shaking me before I even felt it on my body."

Then Lucy burst into laughter. I felt somewhat terrified and listened to her words with bated breath. Since I was rather reluctant and felt it wasn't good to keep clinging to that topic, I changed the subject, and Lucy returned to her bright old self. By the time we arrived home, the fresh wind had revived her spirits, and her pale cheeks were tinged with a rosy hue. Her mother was also very delighted to see Lucy like that, and we all spent a very happy evening together.
