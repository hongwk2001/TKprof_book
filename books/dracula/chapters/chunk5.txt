Dr. Seward's Diary.

September 20.—Only firm will and habit make me sit at my desk tonight and write this diary. I am so miserable, devastated, and completely exhausted with this world and everything in my life. Even if the angel of death flapped its wings behind my back right now, I feel like I could accept it calmly. In fact, recently that angel has been hovering around us with some ominous purpose, flapping its creepy wings. Lucy's mother, Arthur's father, and now... sigh, I'd better just record what I need to do.

I handed over the care of Lucy to Van Helsing and safely changed shifts. We advised Arthur to get some shut-eye as well, but he stubbornly refused. It was only when I repeatedly persuaded him that he must help us so Lucy wouldn't suffer during the day, and that we shouldn't all collapse at once from lack of sleep, that he barely nodded. Van Helsing treated him very affectionately. "Come here, my child," he said. "Come with me. You are very sick and weak right now. Haven't you endured not only physical fatigue but also terrible sorrow and mental agony that we cannot fathom? You shouldn't be left alone. Because the moment you are left alone, fear and anxiety will swallow you up. Let's go to my sitting room where a fire is burning warmly and there are two soft sofas. You lie on one and I will lie on the other, and even if we don't exchange words, or even if you fall asleep first, just being in the same space will be a great comfort to each other." Arthur looked back sorrowfully at Lucy's face, which had turned paler than a pillow, and followed Van Helsing out. Lucy was lying without even a slight movement, and I slowly looked around the room to check if everything was treated properly. I could tell at a glance that the professor had firmly placed garlic in this room just like the other rooms. A strong smell of garlic vibrated around all the window frames, and a roughly woven wreath of garlic flowers was placed over the silk handkerchief Van Helsing had wrapped around her. Lucy was breathing heavily, and with her mouth open revealing her pale gums, the sight was terribly wretched. In the faintly flickering dim light, her teeth looked strangely longer and sharper than when I saw them in the morning. Perhaps it was a trick of the lighting, but especially her canine teeth looked pointed enough to give me goosebumps. When I sat by the bed, she soon tossed and turned, showing signs of unease. At that very moment, a dull flapping sound of something striking the glass was heard from the window. I muffled my footsteps, approached, and peeked outside through the gap in the blinds. Under the brightly shining full moon, I saw a giant bat circling and flapping its wings. The creature, seemingly drawn to the faint light leaking from the room, occasionally tapped the window with its fluttering wings. When I returned to the bedside, Lucy had torn the garlic flower wreath from her neck while tossing and turning. I hurriedly put the wreath back on and sat in the chair, watching her all night.

Before long, Lucy opened her eyes, and I fed her the light liquid food Van Helsing prescribed. She moistened her lips just a little, with great difficulty. That tenacious vitality that had desperately struggled for life even in unconsciousness until now seemed completely extinguished. The surprising thing is that the moment she regained consciousness, she urgently pulled the garlic flowers and embraced them to her chest. While breathing heavily and slipping into a coma, she would unconsciously tear the flowers off roughly, but when she regained consciousness, she repeatedly did this bizarre action of desperately holding onto the flowers all night long.

At 6 a.m., Van Helsing came in to take over. Arthur was still sound asleep, oblivious to the world, and the professor mercifully let him rest without waking him. I heard a suppressed gasp from the professor as he examined Lucy's face. He whispered sharply. "Pull up the blinds! I need light!" Then he bent down closely until his face almost touched Lucy's nose and carefully examined her condition. The moment he removed the garlic flower wreath and lifted the silk handkerchief from Lucy's neck, he flinched back and let out a suppressed moan. "My God (Mein Gott)!" I also bent down and examined the nape of her neck, and the moment I saw it with my own eyes, a chilling shudder that reached my bones wrapped around my entire body.

The two bite marks that had clearly remained on her neck had vanished completely.

Van Helsing stood frozen like a stone in that spot for a full five minutes, staring down at her intently. Eventually, he turned to me and said in a grave voice.

"She is dying. There is not much time left now. It makes a tremendous difference whether she closes her eyes in her right mind or dies in this terrible coma. Keep that in mind. Hurry and wake the poor boy up so he can be with her at the end. We promised him, and he trusts us."

I went across to the dining room and woke Arthur. He was bewildered in his sleep, and when he saw the morning sunlight leaking through the shutters, he was startled, wondering if he was too late. While comforting him that Lucy was still sound asleep, I conveyed as carefully as possible that both Van Helsing and I had an intuition that the end was near. Arthur knelt down on the floor by the sofa with his face buried in his hands, and laying there for about a minute, prayed with bitter tears, his shoulders heaving. I held his hand tightly and helped him up. "Now, my old friend," I said. "Gather all your indomitable courage. Because that is the only way to make Lucy's journey peaceful."

When we entered Lucy's room, Van Helsing, meticulously and carefully tidying up the surroundings as usual, had made an effort to make her look as comfortable and beautiful as possible. He even combed Lucy's disheveled hair, smoothing it so that her signature dazzling golden waves naturally flowed down over the pillow. When we approached, she slowly opened her eyes and whispered affectionately, looking at Arthur.

"Arthur! Oh, my love, I'm so glad you came!" As Arthur bent down to kiss her forehead, Van Helsing hurriedly stopped him. "No!" he whispered firmly. "Not yet! Just hold her hand tightly. For now, that will be comfort enough."

Arthur knelt down and tightly held her small, pale hand. Lying on the sickbed, Lucy looked infinitely sublime and beautiful, as if an angel had descended. Before long, her two eyes gently closed and she fell into a deep sleep. Like an exhausted child, her thin chest rose and fell slightly along with her wheezing breath.

But that bizarre change I had witnessed last night raised its head again in her unconsciousness. Her breathing grew rough like a beast, her lips parted, and her pale gums rolled back, revealing teeth that were sharper and more gleaming than ever before. Opening her dim, unfocused eyes like a sleepwalker, she whispered softly in a chillingly sensual and sticky voice that I had never heard before.

"Arthur! Oh, my love, I'm so glad you came! Kiss me!" Arthur, as if possessed, leaned in to kiss her. But Van Helsing, appalled by her voice as much as I was, threw himself like a beast and grabbed Arthur's nape with both hands. It's hard to tell where such strength sprang from his old body, but Van Helsing ruthlessly yanked him back and flung him into a corner of the room.

"Your life depends on it!" The professor shouted, the veins in his neck bulging. "Not only for your soul, but for her soul too, absolutely not!" Van Helsing blocked their path, glaring murderously like a beast that had lost its young.

Arthur stood blankly, completely out of his mind with shock. In the fleeting moment he was swept by a violent urge to resist, facing the terrible reality, he just froze in place with bated breath.

Van Helsing and I fixed our eyes on Lucy. We clearly saw the shadow of uncontrollable anger sweep across her haggard face like a spasm. The sound of sharp teeth clicking together echoed chillingly. Then, her eyes closed as if rolling back, and she breathed heavily as if tearing at her chest.

Before long, Lucy opened her eyes again with her original gentle and kind eyes. Reaching out her slender, pale hand, she pulled Van Helsing's large, rough hand and kissed it. "My true friend," she pleaded in a faint voice filled with indescribable sorrow. "My true friend, and Arthur's friend! Please protect him, and grant me rest!"

"I swear!" The professor knelt by her sickbed and solemnly replied, raising one hand high as if swearing to God. Then he turned around and said to Arthur. "Come here, my child. Hold her hand tightly with your hand, and kiss her forehead. Just once."

Instead of their lips, their deep and sad eyes met, and thus the two bid their eternal farewell.

As Lucy's eyes completely closed, Van Helsing, who had been watching with bated breath, firmly grabbed Arthur's arm and made him step back.

Lucy's breath, which had grown rough and shallow again, suddenly stopped as if it snapped.

"It's all over," Van Helsing said in a low voice. "She's gone!"

I supported the devastated Arthur and took him to the living room. Collapsing onto the sofa, he covered his face with both hands and sobbed uncontrollably as if the world was falling apart, his back shaking.

When I returned to the room, Van Helsing was looking down at the corpse with a graver and stiffer face than ever before. But a bizarre change had occurred to her body. Death was rather returning the beauty it had taken from her. Her sunken cheeks and forehead regained their soft curves, and even her lips, which had been pale and bloodless, took on a lively color. It was as if the remaining blood heading to the heart was trying to cover the cold and cruel traces of death with a little warmth.

"We only thought she was asleep, but in fact she was dying, and only after she breathed her last does she finally look like she is peacefully asleep."

I approached Van Helsing's side and said in a low voice.

"Ah, poor girl. At last all her suffering is over and she has found rest. Now it is all over!"

Then the professor turned his head, looked straight at me, and answered in a grim and chilling voice.

"That is not so. Ah! It is certainly not so. This is merely the beginning!"

When I asked what he meant, he just shook his head heavily and added briefly.

"There is still nothing we can do. We just have to wait and see."
