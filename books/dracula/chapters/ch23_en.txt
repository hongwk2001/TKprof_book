[P001] Chapter 23

[P002] Dr. Seward's Diary

[P003a] October 3.— The time spent waiting for Godalming and Quincey Morris felt terribly long. Professor Van Helsing constantly talked to us, trying to keep our attention so we wouldn't be distracted by other thoughts. From the occasional side glances he cast toward Jonathan Harker, I could read his kind intentions. Poor Jonathan was crushed under a despair so deep that it was hard to bear looking at him. Just last night, he was a young, cheerful man full of vitality with dark brown hair. But today, he had become a completely different person.

[P003b] His hollow, burning eyes, deeply carved wrinkles, and hair that had turned completely white made him look like an emaciated, aged old man. Fortunately, his spirit had not yet been entirely broken. No, rather, he was like a blazing fire. If everything went well, this fierce energy might be his salvation. This flame would pull him out of the swamp of despair and allow him to face reality once again. Poor friend. I thought my own pain was hard to endure, but in the face of his suffering, I shouldn't even dare to complain!

[P003c] The professor also knew this very well, so he was doing his best to somehow keep Jonathan's mind grounded. Meanwhile, the story the professor told us was fascinating enough to put all our nerves on edge even in such a desperate situation. If I recall correctly, it went roughly like this.

[P004a] "Ever since the papers concerning this monster came into my hands, I have studied them over and over again. And the more I studied, the more I became convinced that we must completely uproot him. Evidence that the monster is evolving is scattered everywhere. It means that he is not just realizing his own strength, but he is also learning how to control it. According to the research of my friend Arminius in Budapest, Hungary, he was truly a phenomenal figure even when he was a human.

[P004b] He was a soldier and a politician, as well as an alchemist, which was the peak of scientific knowledge at the time. He possessed an extraordinary brain and unmatched scholarly knowledge, and he had a heart that knew neither fear nor remorse. It is said that there was no field of knowledge he didn't touch back then, going so far as to enter the legendary school of Scholomance to learn magic from the devil. Even after his physical body died, his remarkable intellect survived. Although his memory doesn't seem to be fully intact, as I said.

[P004c] Right now, some of his mental abilities are merely those of a child. But he is constantly growing, and the parts that were once like a child's have now taken on a rather adult form. He is brilliantly continuing his experiments. If we hadn't stood in his way, or rather, if we fail this time, he will become the founder of a new race born through death, not life."

[P005] Harker groaned and said, "So you mean all of this is aimed at my wife! But what on earth is that monster experimenting on? If we knew that, it would help us defeat him!"

[P006a] "From the moment he first arrived here, he has been slowly but surely testing his powers. That giant child's brain has been working hard. The one fortunate thing for us is that his brain is still at a child's level. If he had acted boldly from the beginning, he would have already become so powerful that we would have been helpless. But he fully intends to succeed, and since he is someone who will live for hundreds of years, he has the luxury of waiting patiently and advancing his plans slowly. Make haste slowly.

[P006b] This Latin proverb must be his motto."

[P007] "I don't quite understand." Harker said in an exhausted voice. "Ah, please explain it a bit more clearly! My head seems to have grown dull from sorrow and agony."

[P008] The professor gently placed his hand on Harker's shoulder and said.

[P009a] "Oh, my poor friend, I will explain it simply. You saw it too, didn't you? How this monster has recently been expanding his knowledge through experiments. Think about how he infiltrated our friend John's house using the madman Renfield, who eats animals. A vampire can freely come and go anytime once it has entered a place, but for the very first time, it must be invited by someone living in the house. But these things are nothing compared to his important experiments.

[P009b] Do you remember how he initially had all those numerous boxes of earth moved by the hands of others? Back then, he thought he had to do it that way. But as that giant child's brain grew, he began to wonder if he could move the boxes himself. So at first, he helped out, and when he realized that was fine, he later tried to move the boxes entirely on his own. As he developed in this way, he scattered his graves here and there, and those hidden locations became known to no one but him.

[P009c] He might have intended to bury those boxes deep underground. Doing so would have made them perfect for use only at night or when he could freely change his shape, and no one would have known that they were his hideouts!

[P009d] But, my boy, do not despair. He realized that knowledge just a hair too late! All but one of his hideouts have already been neutralized by us placing the holy Host, and before sunset, even that remaining one will be taken care of. Then he will have nowhere to move to and nowhere to hide. The reason I delayed our actions this morning was to make absolutely sure of all this. Isn't what we have at stake much greater than his? So it stands to reason that we must be more careful than he is.

[P009e] It is already exactly one o'clock by my watch, so if nothing has happened, our friends Arthur and Quincey should be on their way here right now. Today is our day, and we must proceed slowly but surely, making sure not to miss any opportunities. You see! When those who are returning arrive, there will be five of us."

[P010] As the professor was speaking, the urgent knock of a post office telegraph delivery boy rang at the front door, and we were all startled. As if we had promised, we headed to the front hall in unison, and Professor Van Helsing raised his hand to signal us to be quiet, then approached the door and opened it. The boy handed over a telegram. The professor closed the door again, checked the address on the envelope, tore open the telegram, and read it aloud.

[P011] "Beware of Count Dracula. He just left Carfax at 12:45 and headed south in a hurry. He seems to be patrolling and might try to meet you. Yours, Mina."

[P012] Jonathan Harker's voice broke the silence.

[P013] "Now, thank God. We will soon meet him!" Van Helsing quickly turned to him and said.

[P014] "God will act in His own way and at His own time. Do not be afraid yet, nor rejoice. What we desire right now might actually lead us to our doom."

[P015] "It doesn't matter anymore." Harker replied in an agitated voice. "If only I can wipe that beast off the face of this earth. I would even sell my soul to do so!"

[P016a] "Oh, hush, be quiet, my boy!" said Van Helsing. "God does not buy souls in such a manner, and the devil may buy a soul but he does not keep his promises. However, God is merciful and just, so He knows all of your pain and devotion to your beloved wife, Mina. Think about how much her pain would multiply if she heard you speaking such harsh words. None of us should be afraid. We are all devoted to this cause, and today we will finally see the end of it. The time to act is approaching.

[P016b] Today, this vampire is limited to the level of human abilities, and he cannot change his shape until the sun goes down. It will take some time for him to get here. You see, it is now one-twenty. No matter how fast he comes, he still has some time left before he arrives. What we must hope for is that Lord Arthur and Quincey arrive first."

[P017a] About thirty minutes after receiving Mrs. Harker's telegram, there was a quiet but firm knock at the front door. It was an ordinary knock commonly used by many gentlemen, but it made my heart and the professor's beat heavily. We looked at each other and then went out to the front hall together, each of us holding the various weapons we had prepared—a spiritual weapon, the crucifix and the Host, in our left hands, and a physical weapon in our right hands, ready to use them at any moment.

[P017b] Van Helsing pulled back the bolt and stepped back, leaving the door half-open and positioning himself to have both hands free. When we saw Lord Godalming and Quincey Morris standing on the steps right in front of the door, our faces must have clearly shown joy. The two men quickly came inside and closed the door, and Godalming said as he walked down the hallway.

[P018] "Everything went well. We found both places. There were six boxes in each, and we destroyed them all!"

[P019] "Destroyed them?" the professor asked.

[P020] "So that he can't use them!" We stayed silent for about a minute, and then Quincey spoke.

[P021] "Now all that's left is to wait here. But if he doesn't show up by five o'clock, we must make a move first. It's too dangerous to leave Mrs. Harker alone after sunset."

[P022a] "He will arrive soon," Van Helsing said, looking at his notebook. "Listen carefully. According to Madam's telegram, he went south from Carfax. That means he tried to cross the river, and since he could only cross when the water was calm, it must have been shortly before one o'clock. His heading south is significant to us. He is still only suspicious. So he left Carfax and went to the hideout where he was least likely to be disturbed first. You must have arrived in Bermondsey just slightly ahead of him.

[P022b] The fact that he hasn't shown up here yet means he headed to Mile End next. It would have taken some time because he had to cross the river somehow to get there. Trust me, my friends, we won't have to wait long now. We must plan our attack immediately so we don't miss our chance. Quiet, there's no time now. Everyone, draw your weapons! Get ready!" When we all heard the sound of a key being quietly inserted into the front door's keyhole, the professor raised his hand to warn us.

[P023a] Even in such a desperate moment, I couldn't help but admire the way a born leader takes charge of a situation. Whenever we hunted or went on adventures around the world, it was always Quincey Morris who devised the plans, and Arthur and I would unconsciously follow his directions. Now, that old habit revived instinctively. Quincey quickly scanned the room and, without a single word, set up our ambush by assigning us our positions with mere hand gestures.

[P023b] Van Helsing, Harker, and I stood close behind the door, ready to cut off the intruder's escape route while the professor blocked the entrance. Godalming and Quincey hid out of sight near the front window, taking charge of that side. We waited anxiously as time ticked by slowly like a nightmare. Soon, cautious and deliberate footsteps echoed down the hallway. It was clear that the Count anticipated an ambush, or at least was highly on guard.

[P024a] Suddenly, the Count leaped into the room in a single breath, tearing through us before we could even react. His swift, panther-like, and almost inhuman movement snapped us all out of our shock. The first to move was Harker. He quickly threw himself forward to block the door leading to the front room. Upon spotting us, the Count twisted his face horribly and growled, bearing his long, pointed fangs. But that wicked smile soon turned into a cold, arrogant glare like a lion's. When we all rushed at him at once, his expression changed once again.

[P024b] Looking back, we should have planned our attack more thoroughly. In that moment, even I didn't know what to do or if our ordinary weapons would even work on him. But Harker was fully prepared to test that. He gripped his large Kukri dagger tightly and swung it fiercely and suddenly at the Count. It was an incredibly powerful strike, and if the Count hadn't dodged backward with demon-like speed, he would have been killed. A second later, and that sharp blade would have pierced his heart.

[P024c] Barely missing, the tip of the blade only tore a wide hole in the Count's coat, from which wads of bills and gold coins poured onto the floor.

[P025_033] The expression on the Count's face was so demonic that, even as I watched Harker raise that terrifying blade for another strike, I feared for Harker's life. I instinctively stepped forward, thrusting out the crucifix and Host in my left hand to protect Harker. I felt an immense holy power flow through my arm, and at the same time, I saw the monster flinch and step back as all my friends held out their sacred items as if we had planned it. The hatred, thwarted malice, and hellish fury that flashed across the Count's face were beyond words.

[P033b] In contrast to his burning eyes, his pale skin turned a sickly greenish-yellow, and the red scar on his forehead throbbed like a gruesome wound against his pale flesh. The next moment, the Count bent his body like a snake and slipped under Harker's arm before the next blade could fall. He grabbed a handful of the money scattered on the floor, dashed across the room, and threw himself at the window. With a loud crash, the glass shattered, and he fell onto the stone-paved courtyard below.

[P033c] Over the sound of showering broken glass, we heard the clinking of a few gold coins hitting the stone floor.

[P033d] We ran to the window and saw him spring up from the ground without a single injury. He bounded up the steps, crossed the stone-paved yard, and roughly pushed open the stable door. There, he turned around and shouted up at us.

[P034] "Do you think you can stop me? Lined up with pale faces like a flock of sheep in a slaughterhouse! Every single one of you will regret this! You may think you have destroyed all my resting places, but I still have more hideouts. My revenge has only just begun! I will take my revenge over hundreds of years, and time is on my side. The women you love are already mine. And through them, you and all other humans will eventually belong to me as well.

[P035_037] You will become my minions, obeying my commands and bringing me prey whenever I desire! Hah!" The Count let out a mocking sneer and quickly disappeared through the door, and we heard the rusty bolt creaking as he locked it. Then, the sound of another door opening and closing in the distance echoed. Realizing it would be too difficult to chase him through the stable, we turned back toward the front hall, and the professor was the first to speak.

[P037b] "We have learned many things—so many things! He spoke arrogantly, but he is afraid of us. He fears time, and he fears losing what he has! Why else would he have rushed so much? If my ears did not deceive me, his voice itself betrayed his fear. And why on earth did he grab that money? Hurry and go after him. You are all beast hunters, so you can track his trail.

[P038_039] Meanwhile, I will make absolutely sure that he cannot use anything here even if he returns." As soon as the professor finished speaking, he swept the remaining money on the floor into his pocket, threw the bundle of property deeds and remaining documents Harker had dropped into the fireplace, and struck a match to set them on fire.

[P039b] Godalming and Morris ran out into the yard, and Harker jumped out the window to chase the Count. But the Count had already bolted the stable door from the inside, and by the time they forced the door open, they couldn't even find a trace of him. Van Helsing and I inspected the alley behind the house, but it was empty, and no one had seen the Count leave.

[P039c] It was already late afternoon, and sunset was not far away. We had to admit the fact that we had lost him for now. With heavy hearts, we agreed with the professor's next words.

[P039d] "Let us return to the lovely and poor Mrs. Mina. We have done everything we can here. At least we can protect her by her side. But there's no need to despair. There is still one box of earth left, and we must find it. If we just find that box, we can still reverse everything." I could tell that the professor was trying hard to act brave to comfort Harker. That poor friend was completely broken. Occasionally, unable to hold it in, he let out a low groan; his mind was entirely consumed by thoughts of his wife.

[P039e] We returned to the house with devastated hearts, but surprisingly, Mrs. Harker was waiting for us with a bright face. It was a truly noble demeanor born from her courage and selflessness. However, she turned as pale as a ghost as soon as she saw our faces. She closed her eyes for a second or two as if praying in her heart, and then she spoke cheerfully again.

[P040_041] "I don't know how to thank you all. Oh, my poor love!" She held her husband's graying head with both hands and kissed him. "Rest your weary head here. Everything will be all right, my dear! If it is God's good will, He will surely protect us." Poor Harker groaned. No words could offer comfort in the face of his sublime sorrow.

[P041b] We hastily had dinner together, and I think it brought us some comfort. Perhaps it was the simple warmth that food gives to starving people who hadn't eaten since morning, or maybe the camaraderie of being together helped. Anyway, we were able to shake off our miserable feelings a little and face tomorrow with a heart that wasn't entirely hopeless. As promised, we told Mrs. Harker everything that had happened today.

[P041c] She turned as white as snow when she heard that her husband was in danger, and flushed bright red when she heard of his devoted actions toward her, but she listened to the story bravely and calmly until the end. When it came to the part where Harker recklessly charged at the Count, she clung tightly to her husband's arm. It was as if she believed she could protect him from all dangers just by holding on tight. But she said nothing until we finished our story and explained our current situation.

[P041d] After the story ended, she stood up among us, still holding her husband's hand. Oh, how can I describe that scene in words. This sweet and good woman, standing in the beauty of radiant youth and vitality, bearing the conscious red scar on her forehead (which we ground our teeth looking at, knowing how it got there). Her lovely kindness countering our cold hatred, her affectionate faith countering all our fears and doubts. Despite all her goodness, purity, and faith, it broke our hearts to know that, symbolically, she was already forsaken by God.

[P041e] "Jonathan," she called. The name sounded like music, as if it was filled with love and affection. "My dear Jonathan, and my true friends, there is something I want you to keep in mind even in this terrible time. I know you must fight. Just as you had to destroy the monstrous Lucy so that the true Lucy could find peace in the afterlife, you must destroy him too. But this is not something to be done out of hatred. That poor soul who caused all this misery is the saddest being of all.

[P041f] Think of the immense joy he will feel when his wicked part is destroyed and his better part achieves spiritual immortality. Although you cannot stop the hands that will defeat him, you must have pity on him."

[P041g] As she spoke, I could see Harker's face darkening and hardening. It was as if the anger within him was burning his soul from the inside out. Harker squeezed his wife's hand so tightly that his knuckles turned white. Mina must have been in pain, but she didn't flinch and looked at her husband with more pleading eyes than ever. As soon as she finished speaking, Harker jumped up from his seat, almost shaking off his wife's hand, and shouted.

[P041h] "If only God would deliver him into my hands for even a moment, so that we can end his cursed earthly life! If only I could send his soul beyond that flesh into the eternally burning hell, I would gladly do so!"

[P041i] "Oh, please hush! In the name of the good Lord, please be quiet. Do not say such things, my husband Jonathan. Your words crush me with fear and terror. Think about it, dear. I have been thinking about this all day today. Maybe... someday... I might need that pity too. And someone like you, who has every justified reason to be angry, might not show me that pity! Oh, my dear! My husband, if there had been any other way, I would have spared you from having such terrible thoughts.

[P041j] But I pray that God will not hold these harsh words against you, and will merely see them as the heartbreaking wail of a deeply wounded man who loves dearly. Oh, God, let this graying hair be the proof of how much this man has suffered, having endured so many sorrows without doing any wrong in his entire life."

[P041k] We men were all shedding tears. We couldn't hold it back at all, and we wept aloud. Mina also cried when she saw that her gentle advice had moved our hearts. Harker knelt beside his wife, wrapped his arms around her waist, buried his face in the folds of her dress, and sobbed. Van Helsing gave us a look, and we quietly left the room so that the two loving souls could be alone with their God.

[P042_043] Before the two went to bed, the professor set up wards in the room to prevent the vampire from breaking in and reassured Mrs. Harker that it was safe to sleep. She tried to believe him, and clearly made an effort to appear peaceful to reassure her husband. It was a courageous struggle, and I firmly believe it was rewarded in its own small way. Van Helsing left a bell beside them so they could ring it anytime in an emergency.

[P044_046] After they retired, Quincey, Godalming, and I agreed to take turns staying awake through the night to ensure the poor lady's safety. Quincey took the first watch, so the rest of us should go to bed as soon as possible. Godalming is on the second watch, so he has already gone to bed. Now that my work is done, I should go to sleep too.

[P046b] Jonathan Harker's Diary

[P047_050] October 3-4, around midnight.— Yesterday felt like it would never end. I desperately wanted to sleep, clinging to the vague belief that when I woke up, everything would have changed, and that the change would surely be for the better. Before we parted ways, we discussed our next plan, but we came to no conclusion. The only thing we know for certain is that there is only one box of earth left, and only the Count knows where it is. If he decides to stay hidden, he could evade us for years. And during that time...!

[P050b] Just the thought of it is so terrifying that I barely dare to even think about it right now. But one thing is certain: if there is a perfect woman in this world, it is my poor wife who is suffering so unjustly. The affectionate pity she showed last night, the pure pity that made even my hatred for the monster feel despicable, made me love her a thousand times more. Surely, God will not allow the world to lose such a beautiful soul. This is my only hope.

[P050c] We are currently drifting toward a reef, and faith is our only anchor. Thank God! Mina is sleeping soundly without any nightmares. I am terrified to even imagine what kind of horrible nightmares the terrible memories she has endured might create. She hasn't looked this peaceful since the sun went down. For a while, a calm like a warm spring after a raging March storm lingered on her face.

[P050d] At first, I thought she looked that way because the red glow of the sunset gently enveloped her face, but for some reason, I feel there is a deeper meaning to that expression now. I am exhausted to the bone, but sleep won't come. Still, I must try to get some sleep. I have to prepare for tomorrow, and there will be no true rest for me until this matter is resolved...

[P050e] A little later.— I must have dozed off for a moment. Mina sat straight up in bed and woke me with a startled expression. I hadn't made the room completely dark, so I could clearly see her figure. She pressed her hand firmly over my mouth as a warning and whispered in my ear.

[P051_056] "Hush! There's someone in the hallway!" I got up silently, crossed the room, and carefully opened the door.

[P057a] Mr. Morris was lying wide awake on a mattress placed in the hallway right outside the door. He immediately raised his hand to signal me to be quiet and whispered.

[P057b] "Hush! Go back to bed and sleep. It's okay. One of us will be here all night. We don't intend to risk any danger!"

[P057c] Seeing his resolute expression and gesture, it was clear he didn't want to talk any further, so I returned and told Mina about this. Mina let out a sigh of relief, and a faint but clear smile spread across her pale face. She pulled me into a hug and whispered softly.

[P058a] "Oh, you good and brave men. Thank God!" She let out a sigh and fell back asleep. I should try to sleep again, but I'm completely awake now, so I'm writing in my diary.

[P058b] October 4, Morning.— Mina woke me up again during the night. This time, it was after we both had quite a good sleep. The pale light of the approaching dawn was turning the window into a distinct rectangle, and the gas flame looked like a faint speck rather than a bright disk. She spoke to me urgently.

[P058c] "Go and call the professor. I need to see him right now."

[P058d] "Why?" I asked.

[P058e] "I've realized something. Perhaps it took shape in my mind during the night without me knowing. The professor must hypnotize me before dawn. Then I'll be able to speak freely. Hurry, dear. We don't have much time." I went to the door. Dr. Seward, who was resting on the mattress in the hallway, saw me and sprang to his feet.

[P058f] "Is something wrong?" he asked in surprise.

[P058g] "No," I replied. "But Mina wants to see Professor Van Helsing right away."

[P058h] "I will go fetch him," he said, and hurried toward the professor's room.

[P058i] Two or three minutes later, Professor Van Helsing entered the room in his dressing gown, while Mr. Morris and Lord Godalming stood at the door, asking Dr. Seward what was going on. When the professor saw Mina smiling, his worried face brightened completely, and he rubbed his hands together with joy.

[P058j] "Oh, my dear Mrs. Mina, what a remarkable change. Look! Friend Jonathan, the Mrs. Mina of old has finally returned to us today!" He turned back to Mina and asked affectionately, "Now, how can I help you? You wouldn't have called me at this early hour for no reason."

[P059] "Hypnotize me!" Mina said. "Do it right now before dawn. By then, I think I will be able to speak freely without being suppressed. Hurry, there's no time!" The professor silently motioned for Mina to sit up straight in bed.

[P060a] The professor stared intently into her eyes and began making slow hand gestures, alternating his hands from the top of her head downwards. Mina gazed at him without looking away for a few minutes, and during that time, my heart pounded like a hammer. I instinctively felt that some crucial moment was approaching. Slowly, her eyes closed, and Mina sat perfectly still as if she had turned to stone. Only her quietly rising and falling chest indicated that she was alive.

[P060b] The professor made a few more gestures and then stopped, and I saw thick beads of sweat on his forehead. Mina opened her eyes, but she seemed like a completely different person from her usual self. Her unfocused, blank eyes stared into the empty air, and her voice held a dreamy and sorrowful echo that I had never heard before. The professor raised his hand to signal us to be quiet, and then motioned for me to let the others into the room. They tiptoed in carefully, closed the door, stood at the foot of the bed, and watched silently.

[P060c] Mina didn't seem to notice they had entered at all. Professor Van Helsing broke the silence in a low, calm voice. It was an uninflected, tranquil voice, intended not to disrupt the flow of her thoughts.

[P061_063] "Where are you now?" A dry, emotionless answer returned.

[P063b] "I don't know. Sleep has no fixed place." A heavy silence hung for a few minutes. Mina remained completely stiff, and the professor stared intently at her. The rest of us were careful not to even make a sound breathing. It was getting brighter outside. Dr. Van Helsing motioned for me to raise the blind without taking his eyes off Mina's face. When I raised the blind, dawn was already breaking. Streaks of red light rose over the horizon, tinting the room with a rosy hue. At that moment, the professor asked again.

[P063c] "Where are you now?" The answer was still dreamy, but there was a purposeful tone to her voice. It was as if she was trying to interpret a sensation. It was the kind of tone Mina used when rereading notes she had written in shorthand.

[P064] "I don't know. Everything feels unfamiliar."

[P065] "What do you see?"

[P066] "I can't see anything. It's pitch black all around."

[P067] "What do you hear?" I could sense the extreme tension laced in the professor's patient voice.

[P068] "The sound of water. Rippling waves passing by, and the sound of small waves crashing. It's coming from outside."

[P069a] "Then are you on a ship right now?" We looked at each other with fearful hearts, trying to read each other's thoughts. We barely even dared to think about what that meant. The answer returned immediately.

[P069b] "Yes, that's right!"

[P069c] "Do you hear any other sounds besides that?"

[P070_072] "I hear people running around heavily stamping their feet above my head. And a loud rattling sound of a machine winding up an anchor, along with the creaking of iron chains."

[P073] "And what are you doing?"

[P074] "I am still... very still. Like someone dead!" Her voice slowly faded into a heavy sigh of someone falling into a deep sleep, and her opened eyes closed again.

[P075] The sun had already fully risen, filling the room with the bright light of day. Dr. Van Helsing placed a hand on Mina's shoulder and gently laid her head on the pillow. For a moment, Mina lay peacefully like a sleeping child, and then woke up with a long sigh. She looked around at us in confusion and said. "Was I talking in my sleep?" Without any memory of it, she seemed to instinctively grasp the situation and was desperate to know what she had said. When the professor repeated their conversation exactly, Mina immediately said.

[P076] "Then there is no time to lose. It might not be too late yet!" Mr. Morris and Lord Godalming hurried toward the door, but the professor's calm voice stopped them.

[P077a] "Wait, my friends. Wherever that ship is, it was just weighing anchor while Madam was speaking. At this very moment, in the vast Port of London, dozens of ships must be weighing anchor. Which one of those ships do you intend to find? Thank God, we have finally found a clue, though we don't yet know where it will lead us. We have been blind all this time. Blind like mere mortal men. Looking back now, the things we should have seen and realized beforehand are so terribly clear! Ah, I'm sorry. Was I rambling too much?

[P077b] I finally understand why the Count bothered to grab that money even while his life was threatened by Jonathan's fierce attack. He intended to flee. Listen well, flee! He has only one box of earth left, and he realizes that this London, where so many men are chasing him like hounds after a fox, is no longer safe for him. He has loaded his last box of earth onto a ship and is leaving England. He may believe he can escape, but he is dead wrong! We will track him to the very end.

[P077c] Just as Arthur shouts in his red coat when hunting, 'Tally ho!' The old fox is very cunning. Oh! So cunning. Therefore, we must become just as cunning as he is. Since I am also a cunning man, I can roughly guess his next plan. For now, you may rest comfortably. Because a vast ocean lies between us that he never wishes to cross, and cannot cross even if he wanted to. Unless the ship docks at land, and unless it is high or low tide, he cannot even disembark from the ship. So you see.

[P077d] The sun has just risen, and we have the whole day until sunset. Let's wash up, change our clothes, and have a hearty breakfast, which we all desperately need. Now that we know he is no longer on this land, we can eat with peace of mind." Mina looked at him with earnest eyes and asked.

[P078] "But if he has already left us, why must we continue to chase him?" The professor took Mina's hand, gently patted it, and replied.
