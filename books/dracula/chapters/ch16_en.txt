[P001] CHAPTER XVI

[P002] DR. SEWARD’S DIARY—continued

[P003a] It was just 11:45 PM when we climbed over the low wall and entered the churchyard. The night was dark, with occasional patches of moonlight breaking through the heavy clouds racing across the sky. Van Helsing led the way, and the rest of us stayed closely huddled together. As we approached the family tomb, I kept a close eye on Arthur, worried that returning to a place filled with such tragic memories would overwhelm him, but he held up well. I figured the sheer bizarre nature of our mission was acting as a distraction from his grief.

[P003b] The Professor unlocked the door. Noticing our understandable hesitation to enter, he broke the ice by walking in first. We followed, and he shut the door behind us. Then he turned on his directional lantern and pointed it at the coffin. Arthur stepped forward hesitantly, and Van Helsing asked me:

[P004] "You were here with me yesterday. Was Miss Lucy's body in that coffin?"

[P005] "It was." The Professor then turned to the others and said:

[P006] "You hear that. And yet, some of you still doubt me." He took his screwdriver and pried the coffin lid off again. Arthur watched, incredibly pale but silent. When the lid was removed, he stepped closer. He clearly hadn't known—or at least hadn't remembered—that there was a sealed lead coffin inside the wooden one. When he saw the torn lead, his face flushed red for a second before draining entirely, leaving him deathly pale once again. He remained quiet. Van Helsing pried back the lead edge, and we all looked inside and recoiled in shock.

[P007] The coffin was empty!

[P008] For several minutes, nobody said a word. Quincey Morris finally broke the silence:

[P009] "Professor, I vouched for you. I trust your word completely. I normally wouldn't ask something like this—I wouldn't insult you by doubting you—but this bizarre situation goes way beyond manners and respect. Did you do this?"

[P010] "I swear by everything I hold sacred that I haven't moved or touched her. Here is what happened: Two nights ago, my friend Seward and I came here—with good intentions, I promise you. I opened this coffin, which was still fully sealed at the time, and found it completely empty, just like it is now. We waited and saw a white figure moving through the trees. When we came back the next day during sunlight, she was lying right here. Isn't that right, John?"

[P011] "Yes."

[P012a] "We arrived just in time that night. Another little child had gone missing, and thank God, we found the kid unharmed among the graves. Yesterday, I came here before sunset, because these vampires can only roam after the sun goes down. I waited all night until sunrise, but nothing happened. It was most likely because I had rubbed garlic—which these monsters can't stand—and other deterrents all over the door seams. She didn't leave the tomb last night, so today, before sunset, I removed the garlic and everything else. And that is why we are now looking at an empty coffin.

[P012b] But please, bear with me. Everything so far has been incredibly strange, but if you wait outside with me, hidden and quiet, you're going to see things that are far stranger. So,"—he slid the shutter on his lantern closed—"let's head outside." He opened the door and we filed out. He came out last and locked the tomb behind him.

[P013a] Oh, the night air felt so fresh and clean compared to the suffocating terror inside that tomb! It was a relief to watch the clouds race by and see the moonlight flickering through them—reminding me of the shifting joys and sorrows of human life. Breathing in air that wasn't tainted with the stench of death felt incredibly sweet. Seeing the reddish glow of the city lights over the hill and hearing the distant, muffled sounds of London made us feel human again. Each of us was overwhelmed in our own way.

[P013b] Arthur was completely silent, clearly trying to mentally process the meaning behind all this madness. I was surprisingly calm, finding myself ready to let go of my final doubts and fully believe Van Helsing's theories. Quincey Morris remained completely stoic, reacting like a man who accepts whatever life throws at him with cold bravery, ready to risk it all. Since he couldn't smoke, he cut off a chunk of chewing tobacco and popped it in his mouth. Meanwhile, Van Helsing was hard at work.

[P013c] First, he pulled a stack of something that looked like thin wafers wrapped in a white napkin from his bag. Then, he grabbed two handfuls of a white, doughy substance that looked like putty. He crushed the wafers into fine crumbs and kneaded them into the putty. After rolling the mixture into long, thin strips, he began pressing them into the cracks between the tomb's door and its frame. Confused by this, I stepped closer and asked him what he was doing. Arthur and Quincey, equally curious, gathered around as well. He replied:

[P014] "I am sealing the tomb so that the vampire cannot get back inside."

[P015a] "And is that stuff you just put there really going to stop it?" Quincey asked. "Good grief! Are you pulling our legs?"

[P015b] "I am not."

[P017] "What exactly are you using?" This time it was Arthur who asked. Van Helsing respectfully took off his hat as he answered:

[P018a] "The Communion Host. I brought it from Amsterdam. I received a special dispensation for it." It was an answer that stunned even the most skeptical among us. We all felt that we could no longer doubt the Professor; his intentions were so fiercely genuine that he was willing to utilize the most sacred object he knew. In respectful silence, we moved into our assigned positions—close to the tomb, but hidden from the view of anyone walking up to it. I felt terrible for the others, especially Arthur.

[P018b] I had somewhat built up a tolerance to this watching nightmare during my previous visits. Yet, even though I had rejected the evidence just an hour prior, I still felt my heart drop into my stomach. The tombs had never looked so terrifyingly pale. The cypress, yew, and juniper trees had never looked so much like the very essence of death and mourning. The grass and trees had never rustled so ominously, and the branches had never creaked in such a mysterious way. The distant howling of dogs had never felt like such a tragic warning cutting through the night.

[P019a] A long, agonizingly empty silence dragged on. Then, the Professor let out a sharp "S-s-s-s!" He pointed down the path lined with yew trees, and we saw a white figure approaching—a hazy white silhouette holding something dark against its chest. The figure stopped. Just then, a break in the heavy clouds let a beam of moonlight through, brightly illuminating a dark-haired woman wearing grave clothes. We couldn't see her face because she was looking down at what we realized was a blonde child.

[P019b] There was a pause, followed by a sharp, tiny cry, like a child whimpering in its sleep, or a dog dreaming by the fireplace. We instinctively started to step forward, but the Professor, standing behind a yew tree, held up a hand to stop us. As we watched, the white figure began moving forward again. It was close enough for us to see clearly now, and the moonlight held steady. My blood ran cold, and I heard Arthur gasp as we recognized the face of Lucy Westenra. It was Lucy, but completely transformed.

[P019c] Her sweetness had been replaced by a cold, heartless cruelty, and her purity had twisted into a sensual wickedness. Van Helsing stepped out from behind the tree, and at his signal, we moved forward as well, forming a line of four in front of the tomb door. He raised his lantern and opened the shutter. In the focused beam of light hitting Lucy's face, we could see her lips were stained crimson with fresh blood, which had dripped down her chin and ruined the pristine white of her linen burial gown.

[P020] We shuddered in sheer horror. By the shaking light of the lantern, I could tell that even Van Helsing's iron composure had cracked. Arthur was standing right next to me, and if I hadn't grabbed his arm to hold him up, he would have collapsed onto the ground.

[P021a] When Lucy—I'm calling the thing in front of us Lucy because it looked like her—saw us, she recoiled with an angry hiss, like a cat caught off guard, and her eyes scanned us. They were Lucy’s eyes in shape and color, but instead of being pure and gentle, they were filthy and blazing with hellfire. In that moment, any leftover love I had for her completely vanished, replaced entirely by hatred and disgust. If I had been forced to kill her then and there, I would have done it with savage pleasure.

[P021b] As she glared at us, her eyes glowed with a demonic light, and a sensual smile spread across her face. Good God, just looking at it made me shudder! With a casual, utterly callous motion, she threw the child she had been clutching tightly to her chest onto the ground, growling over it like a dog guarding a bone. The child let out a sharp cry and lay there whimpering. The sheer cold-bloodedness of the act drew a pained groan from Arthur.

[P021c] When she walked toward him with her arms open and a lustful smile on her face, he stumbled backward and buried his face in his hands.

[P022] She kept coming, however, and said with a lazy, seductive grace:

[P023] "Come to me, Arthur. Leave these other men and come to me. My arms are starving for you. Come, and we can rest together. Come, my husband, come!"

[P024] There was something diabolically sweet about her voice—a ringing sound like struck glass—that echoed in the minds of even those of us who weren't the target of her words. Arthur looked completely mesmerized. Pulling his hands away from his face, he opened his arms wide. She was just about to leap into them when Van Helsing lunged forward and thrust his small golden crucifix right between them. She flinched away from it, her face suddenly twisting into a mask of pure rage, and sprinted past him as if trying to dive back into the tomb.

[P025a] When she got within a foot or two of the door, however, she jerked to a halt, as if stopped by an invisible brick wall. She spun around, and her face was fully illuminated by the bright moonlight and Van Helsing's steady lantern. I have never seen such a look of frustrated, malicious hatred, and I pray to God I never see anything like it again. Her beautiful complexion turned a sickly gray, her eyes literally seemed to shoot sparks of hellfire, and her brow furrowed so deeply it looked like a tangled nest of Medusa’s snakes.

[P025b] Her lovely, bloodstained mouth stretched open into a wide square, resembling a dramatic Japanese theater mask. If a look could literally kill, we were staring straight at it.

[P026] For a full thirty seconds, which felt like an eternity, she was trapped between the raised crucifix and the religiously sealed door of her tomb. Van Helsing broke the silence, asking Arthur:

[P027] "Answer me, my friend! Should I finish what I came here to do?"

[P028] Arthur dropped to his knees, burying his face in his hands again, and answered:

[P029a] "Do whatever you have to do, my friend. Just do it. Nothing could ever be more horrifying than this." He let out a heartbroken groan. Quincey and I moved to his side at the same time and held him by the arms. We heard the click of the lantern shutter closing as Van Helsing lowered it. Stepping up to the tomb, he began scraping away some of the sacred dough he had packed into the door's seams.

[P029b] As soon as he stepped back, we watched in absolute, horrified amazement as the woman—who just a moment ago had a solid, physical body just like ours—squeezed through a crack so thin you could barely fit a knife blade into it. A wave of immense relief washed over all of us when we saw the Professor calmly reapplying the strips of putty to reseal the edges of the door.

[P030] When he finished, he picked up the child and said:

[P031a] "Come along now, my friends; there is nothing more we can do until tomorrow. There is a funeral here at noon, so we will return shortly after that. The mourners will all be gone by two o'clock, and when the caretaker locks the gates, we will stay behind. We have more work to do then, but nothing like what we saw tonight. As for this little one, he isn’t badly hurt, and by tomorrow night he should be fully recovered.

[P031b] We will leave him where the police will easily find him, just like the other night, and then we will go home." Stepping closer to Arthur, he said:

[P032] "My friend Arthur, you have been put through a terrible ordeal; but later, when you look back on this, you will understand why it was necessary. You are drinking from bitter waters right now, my boy. But by this time tomorrow, God willing, you will have pushed through the worst of it and will drink sweet waters again. So please, try not to grieve too much. Until then, I won’t even ask for your forgiveness."

[P033] Arthur and Quincey came back to my house with me, and we tried our best to comfort each other on the way. We had left the child in a safe spot, and we were all exhausted, so we managed to get some sleep, however fitful it may have been.

[P034] *       *       *       *       *

[P035a] September 29, night.—Just before midnight, the three of us—Arthur, Quincey Morris, and myself—went to pick up the Professor. Oddly enough, without discussing it beforehand, we had all dressed in black. Arthur was in deep mourning, so that made sense, but the rest of us had just done it on instinct. We reached the churchyard by 1:30 AM and kept a low profile, dodging the cemetery staff. Once the gravediggers finished their shift and the groundskeeper locked the gates—assuming everyone had left—we had the entire place to ourselves.

[P035b] Van Helsing didn't have his usual small black medical bag; instead, he brought a long leather bag, similar to a cricket equipment bag, and it was obviously heavy.

[P036a] Once we were alone and the sound of the last footsteps faded away up the road, we silently followed the Professor to the tomb, as if marching on orders. He unlocked the door, we stepped inside, and closed it behind us. He pulled his lantern from his bag and lit it, along with two wax candles. He melted the bottoms of the candles and stuck them onto the other coffins to give us enough light to work by. When he lifted the lid off Lucy’s coffin again, we all looked inside. Arthur was shaking like a leaf.

[P036b] We saw the body lying there, looking perfectly beautiful even in death. But I felt no love in my heart anymore—only absolute disgust for the filthy monster that had stolen Lucy’s face without her soul. I saw Arthur’s face harden with resolve as he looked at her. Finally, he asked Van Helsing:

[P037] "Is this really Lucy’s body, or is it just a demon wearing her face?"

[P038] "It is her body, but it is not truly her. Just wait a little while, and you will all see her exactly as she was, and as she is meant to be."

[P039a] As she lay there, she looked like a nightmarish parody of Lucy. The pointed teeth, the bloodstained, sensual mouth—which made me shudder just to look at it—and her completely carnal, soulless appearance felt like a demonic mockery of Lucy’s former sweet purity. Working with his usual methodical efficiency, Van Helsing began pulling various items from his bag and laying them out. First, he took out a soldering iron and some plumbing solder, followed by a small oil lamp. When lit in the corner of the tomb, the lamp produced a fierce blue flame.

[P039b] Next came his surgical knives, which he laid out within easy reach. Finally, he pulled out a round wooden stake, about two and a half to three inches thick and roughly three feet long. One end had been hardened in a fire and sharpened to a fine point. He paired the stake with a heavy hammer, the kind normally used in household cellars to break apart large lumps of coal. As a doctor, watching preparations for a serious procedure usually sharpens my focus, but seeing these specific tools laid out caused Arthur and Quincey visible distress.

[P039c] However, both of them held their nerve and stayed completely silent.

[P040] When everything was ready, Van Helsing said:

[P041a] "Before we do anything, let me explain this to you; it comes from the folklore and the experience of those who have studied the powers of the vampires over centuries. When someone becomes a vampire, they are cursed with immortality. They cannot die naturally; they must go on, century after century, claiming new victims and multiplying evil in the world. Anyone killed by a vampire becomes a vampire themselves, destined to prey on their own kind. And so, the circle constantly widens, like ripples from a stone tossed into water.

[P041b] My friend Arthur, if you had received that kiss from poor Lucy before she died, or if you had kissed her last night when you opened your arms to her, you eventually would have become what they call a *nosferatu* in Eastern Europe. You would have spent eternity making more of these terrifying monsters.

[P041c] The tragic career of this dear lady has only just begun. Those children she bit are not yet fully lost. But if she continues to live as a vampire, she will drain more of their blood, her power over them will draw them back to her, and she will feed on them with that wicked mouth. But if she truly dies tonight, it all stops.

[P041d] The tiny bite marks on their throats will heal, and they will go back to playing, completely unaware of what almost happened to them. Best of all, when this vampire is finally put to a true rest, the soul of the poor lady we all love will be set free. Instead of committing evil at night and growing more corrupted by it during the day, she will take her rightful place among the angels. So, my friend, whoever strikes the blow that sets her free will be doing a truly blessed deed.

[P041e] I am willing to do it myself, but isn't there someone here who has a better right? Won't it bring joy to you in the future, during those sleepless nights, to think: 'It was my hand that sent her to heaven; it was the hand of the man who loved her best; the hand she would have chosen herself, if she had been able to choose?' Tell me if there is such a man among us."

[P042] We all turned to look at Arthur. He realized, just as we all did, the profound kindness behind Van Helsing's suggestion—that Arthur should be the one to restore Lucy's memory as something holy, rather than something evil. He stepped forward bravely, though his hands were shaking and his face was as pale as snow, and said:

[P043] "My true friend, from the bottom of my broken heart, thank you. Tell me what I have to do, and I will not hesitate!" Van Helsing placed a comforting hand on his shoulder and said:

[P044] "Brave boy! It only takes a moment of courage, and then it is over. You must drive this stake through her heart. It will be a horrific ordeal—I won't lie to you about that—but it will only take a short time. Afterward, your joy will outweigh your pain; you will walk out of this grim tomb feeling as light as air. But once you start, you cannot stop. Just remember that we, your true friends, are standing right beside you, praying for you the entire time."

[P045] "Go on," Arthur said, his voice hoarse. "Tell me exactly what to do."

[P046] "Hold this stake in your left hand, and line the point up over her heart. Hold the hammer in your right hand. When we begin praying for the dead—I have a prayer book here and will read from it, and the others will follow along—strike in the name of God. Do this so that the woman we love can find peace and the monster can be destroyed."

[P047] Arthur took the stake and the hammer. Once he locked his mind onto the task, his hands stopped trembling completely. Van Helsing opened his prayer book and began to read, and Quincey and I followed along as best we could. Arthur positioned the point of the stake directly over her heart; from where I stood, I could see it indenting her white flesh. Then, he swung with all his might.

[P048a] The Thing in the coffin writhed in agony, letting out a hideous, blood-curdling screech from its open red lips. The body violently shook, twisted, and convulsed. Its sharp white teeth snapped together repeatedly until its own lips were torn and its mouth was smeared with a bloody, crimson foam. But Arthur never hesitated. He looked like the god Thor as his steady arm rose and fell, driving the stake of mercy deeper and deeper. Blood welled up from the pierced heart, spurting up and around the wood. His face was set in stone, glowing with a profound sense of duty.

[P048b] Seeing him like that gave us the courage to raise our voices, and our prayers seemed to echo forcefully throughout the small tomb.

[P049] And then the writhing and convulsing slowed down, the gnashing of the teeth weakened, and the trembling in the face faded. Finally, the body lay completely still. The horrific task was finished.

[P050a] The hammer dropped from Arthur’s hands. He swayed on his feet, and if we hadn't caught him, he would have collapsed. Huge drops of sweat beaded on his forehead, and he was gasping for air. It had been an unimaginable emotional strain on him; if it hadn't been for a sense of duty that went beyond ordinary human limits, he never would have been able to go through with it. For a few minutes, we were so focused on tending to him that we didn't even look at the coffin.

[P050b] When we finally did, a murmur of shocked surprise rippled through us. We were staring so intently that Arthur, who had been sitting on the floor, stood up and came over to look as well. Then, a strange, joyful light broke across his face, completely washing away the dark horror that had covered it just moments before.

[P051a] Lying in the coffin was no longer the foul, demonic creature we had dreaded and hated so much that we treated her destruction as a privilege given to the man who loved her most. Instead, it was Lucy, exactly as she had looked in life, with a face of unequaled sweetness and purity. Yes, the traces of worry, illness, and pain were still visible on her face, just as they had been before she died, but those marks were precious to us because they proved this was the real Lucy we knew.

[P051b] We all felt that the holy calm resting over her thin face and body like sunshine was an earthly symbol of the eternal peace she had now finally found.

[P052] Van Helsing walked over, put his hand on Arthur’s shoulder, and asked:

[P053] "And now, Arthur my friend, my dear boy, am I forgiven?"

[P054] The release of all that terrible tension finally hit Arthur. He took the old man's hand, pressed it to his lips, and said:

[P055] "Forgiven! God bless you for giving my darling her soul back, and for giving me peace." He put his hands on the Professor’s shoulders, rested his head against Van Helsing's chest, and cried silently for a few minutes while the rest of us stood completely still. When he lifted his head, Van Helsing told him:

[P056] "And now, my child, you may kiss her. Kiss her dead lips if you want to. It is what she would have wanted you to do, if she could choose. Because she is no longer a grinning devil—no longer a foul monster cursed for all eternity. She is no longer the devil’s vampire. She is God’s true dead, and her soul is with Him!"

[P057] Arthur leaned down and kissed her. After that, we sent him and Quincey outside. The Professor and I sawed off the top of the wooden stake, leaving the pointed end buried in her chest. Then, we cut off her head and stuffed her mouth with garlic. We soldered the lead coffin shut, screwed the wooden lid back on, gathered our tools, and left the tomb. Once the Professor locked the door, he handed the key to Arthur.

[P058] Outside, the air felt sweet, the sun was shining brightly, and the birds were singing. It felt as if all of nature had shifted to a happier, brighter tune. There was a sense of joy, lightness, and peace everywhere, because we had finally found closure on this one tragic chapter, and we felt a deep—if somewhat solemn—gladness.

[P059] Before we walked away, Van Helsing said:

[P060] "Now, my friends, one phase of our work is finished—the phase that was the most heartbreaking for us. But an even greater task remains: to track down the monster responsible for all our sorrow and destroy him. I have clues we can follow, but it will be a long, difficult mission, filled with danger and pain. Will you all help me? We have all learned to believe the unbelievable—isn't that true? And since we believe, don't we see what our duty is? Yes! So, do we promise to see this through to the bitter end?"

[P061] One by one, we took his hand and made the promise. Then, as we started walking away, the Professor added:

[P062a] "Two nights from now, I want you all to meet me for dinner at seven o'clock at John's house. I will invite two other people—two people you haven't met yet—and I will be ready to lay out all my research and reveal our plan. Friend John, you need to come home with me now, because I have a lot to discuss with you, and I need your help. I am leaving for Amsterdam tonight, but I will be back tomorrow evening. And then, our great hunt begins.

[P062b] But first, I have a lot to explain so that you understand exactly what we have to do, and exactly what we are up against. After that, we will renew our promise to each other. We have a terrifying task ahead of us, and once we put our hands to the plow, there is no turning back."
