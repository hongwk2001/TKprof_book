As he finished speaking and turned the doorknob, the door wouldn't budge. We threw our bodies hard against the door, and with a loud crash, the door smashed open and we almost tumbled into the room. The professor had actually fallen to the floor, and as he gathered himself on his stomach, I saw the sight in the room beyond him. What I saw was far too horrific. The hair on the back of my neck seemed to stand on end, and my heart seemed to stop.

The moonlight was so bright that we could see clearly inside the room just from the light piercing through the thick yellow blinds. On the bed near the window, Jonathan Harker lay with a flushed red face, breathing roughly as if in a deep coma. And on the edge of the bed near us, his wife in a white nightgown was kneeling facing outward. Beside her stood a tall, thin man wrapped in black clothes. He had turned his face away from us, but we instantly recognized from his every feature, even the scar on his forehead, that he was the Count. The Count was firmly gripping Mrs. Harker's two hands with his left hand, pressing them down so her arms were pulled taut, and with his right hand, he grasped the back of her neck, forcefully shoving her face against his chest. The woman's white nightgown was stained with blood, and a thin stream of blood flowed down the man's bare chest where his shirt was torn open. Their posture was chillingly similar to a child forcing a kitten's nose into a saucer of milk to make it drink. As we burst into the room, the Count turned his face, and that hellish expression I had only heard about in stories was nakedly revealed on his face. His two eyes burned red with demonic lust, and the large nostrils of his pale, hooked nose flared wide and quivered at the edges. Behind thick, dripping lips, his white, sharp teeth clattered together like a wild beast. He roughly flung his victim onto the bed as if tossing her from a high place, then turned and lunged at us. But by then, the professor had already risen from his spot and was holding up an envelope containing the Sacred Wafer towards him. Then, just as poor Lucy had done outside her tomb, the Count suddenly stopped his movements, flinched, and backed away. As we approached holding crosses high, he backed away more and more. At that moment, a massive dark cloud crossed the sky and the moonlight instantly vanished. When Quincey struck a match and turned on the gas lamp, all we saw was a faint mist. As we watched, that mist slid out under the door that had been forced open and then closed back to its original position from the rebound. Van Helsing, Arthur, and I approached Mrs. Harker. By then catching her breath, she let out an incredibly rough, piercing, and despairing scream that seemed to tear the eardrums. That scream will likely haunt my ears until the day I die. For a few seconds, she lay defenseless with her clothes disheveled. Because of the blood on her lips, cheeks, and chin, her pale face looked even more chilling, a thin stream of blood was flowing down her neck, and her two eyes were madly contorted with terror. Soon, she covered her face with her two pitiful, crushed hands, on which the red marks caused by the Count's terrible grip remained vivid against her pale skin. And behind those hands leaked a low, agonizing wail. Listening to it, even that horrific scream she had just let out felt like nothing more than a very brief outpouring of endless sorrow. Dr. Van Helsing approached and gently covered her body with a blanket, and Arthur watched her face with a despairing look for a moment before running out of the room. Dr. Van Helsing whispered to me.

"Jonathan is in a deep hypnosis that only a vampire can induce. Right now, there is nothing we can do for poor Madam Mina until she calms herself down. First, we must wake Jonathan!" He moistened the edge of a towel with cold water and began to gently pat Harker's face. Meanwhile, the woman buried her face in her hands and sobbed bitterly, as if her heart were tearing apart. I pulled back the blinds and looked out the window. The moonlight was quite bright, and I saw Quincey Morris running across the lawn and hiding in the shadow of a large yew tree. Just as I tilted my head, wondering what on earth he was doing, Harker let out a short scream as he regained consciousness, and I hurriedly turned my head toward the bed. Naturally, his face was filled with extreme shock. After staring blankly for a few seconds, he suddenly sat up, as if completely coming to his senses. His wife turned her head, startled by her husband's sudden movement, and reached out her arms as if to embrace him. But she immediately withdrew her arms, brought her elbows together, covered her face with both hands, and trembled so violently that the bed shook.

"Good God, what on earth is happening?" Harker cried out. "Dr. Seward, Dr. Van Helsing, what is it? What has happened? What went wrong? Honey, Mina, what's wrong? What is all that blood? Oh my God, oh my God! Has it finally come to this!" He fell to his knees and struck his hands together wildly in the air. "Oh Lord, look upon us! Save my wife! Please help!" He quickly jumped off the bed and frantically began to put on his clothes. The instinct that he had to move immediately seemed to fully awaken his hazy mind. "What happened? Tell me everything!" he shouted without taking a breath. "Dr. Van Helsing, I know how much you care for our Mina. Please do something to save my wife. It might not be too late. I will go find that bastard, so please protect my wife in the meantime!" Even amidst immense terror, shock, and pain, his wife sensed that her husband would be in terrible danger. She instantly cast aside her sorrow, grabbed her husband tightly, and screamed.

"No! No! Jonathan, you must not leave me. I have suffered enough tonight. Heaven knows it. I do not want to tremble in fear that the monster might harm you too. Stay by my side. Stay here with these friends who will protect you!" Her pleading expression was desperate, like a half-mad person. When her husband yielded to her stubbornness, she pulled him to sit on the edge of the bed and hugged him so tightly it seemed he might break.

Van Helsing and I tried to calm the two. The professor raised a small golden cross high and spoke in an incredibly calm and peaceful voice.

"Do not be afraid, Madam. We are here. As long as this is by your side, no evil thing will dare approach. Tonight is safe. So we must calmly discuss what to do next." She trembled violently and then leaned her head against her husband's chest without a word. When she raised her head a moment later, her husband's white nightgown was stained entirely red where her lips had touched and where blood had dripped from the thin wound on her neck. As soon as she saw that, she wailed agonizingly and backed away, sobbing breathlessly as she whispered.

"Unclean, I am defiled! I must not touch or kiss you anymore. Oh, to think that I have now become your most terrible enemy, that the one you should fear most is me." At those words, her husband replied firmly.

"That's nonsense, Mina. I am only ashamed of myself to hear such words from your mouth. I would absolutely not tolerate anyone else saying such things to you, but I especially do not want to hear you say them yourself. If any action or will of mine should cause anything to come between us, I hope God holds me accountable and punishes me with an agony more bitter than this very moment!" He opened his arms and hugged his wife tightly to his chest. She stayed in her husband's embrace for a while, sobbing bitterly. Harker looked at us over his wife's bowed head. Above his twitching nostrils, his moist eyes blinked, and his lips were tightly shut like steel. A moment later, as his wife's sobbing subsided, he spoke to me. In a voice deliberately feigning calmness, I could feel that he was squeezing out every ounce of his nerve and patience.

"Now, Dr. Seward. Please tell me everything. I already feel the terrible consequences to my bones. Please tell me exactly what has happened in the meantime." I told him everything that had occurred without omitting anything, and he seemed to listen without outward agitation. But whenever I described how the Count's merciless grip had suppressed his wife in such a horrific and chilling posture, forcing her mouth against the open wound on his chest, his nostrils twitched and his two eyes burned fiercely. What was surprising even in the midst of that horror was that, unlike his pale face convulsing with rage, only his hands were stroking his bowed wife's disheveled hair with utmost tenderness and affection. Just as my story ended, Quincey and Godalming knocked on the door. They entered the room when we called them. Dr. Van Helsing sent me a look as if asking for my opinion. I immediately realized the look meant, 'How about using the visit of these two as an excuse to take the unhappy couple's minds off their worry and self-blame, even just a little?' As I nodded in agreement, the doctor asked the two men what they had seen and done outside. Lord Godalming answered.

"We couldn't find the bastard anywhere in the corridor or our rooms. We searched the study as well, but although the traces of his visit were clear, he had already vanished. But that bastard—" He suddenly shut his mouth as he looked at the poor woman collapsed limply on the bed. Dr. Van Helsing spoke solemnly.

"Keep speaking, my friend Arthur. We no longer wish to hide anything. We must know everything completely for there to be hope. Speak openly and tell us everything!" So Arthur continued.

"Although he could only have stayed in the study for a few short seconds, he completely wrecked the place. He burned all the manuscripts, and blue flames were flickering among the white ashes. He also threw all of the doctor's phonograph cylinders into the fire, and the flames had grown uncontrollably large because of the wax." Here I interrupted. "Thank goodness there is another copy in the safe!" His face brightened for a moment, but then hardened again as he went on. "Then I ran downstairs, but there was not a trace of the bastard. I looked inside Renfield's room, but there wasn't a trace there either. Except—!" He paused once more. "Please continue." Harker urged in a hoarse voice. Then Arthur lowered his head, moistened his lips with his tongue, and added, "Except that the poor man had passed away." Mrs. Harker raised her head, looked at us one by one, and said solemnly.

"May it be as God wills!" I could not shake the feeling that Arthur was hiding something. But I assumed he had his reasons, so I kept my mouth shut. Dr. Van Helsing turned to Morris and asked.

"What about you, my friend Quincey. Did you find out anything?"

"A little," he answered. "It might turn out to be a very important clue in the end, but I can't be certain right now. I thought it would be best to keep an eye on where the Count goes when he leaves this house. I didn't see the bastard myself, but I clearly saw a bat fly up from the window of Renfield's room and head west. I guessed he would return to the Carfax mansion in some form, but it seems he went looking for another hideout. The eastern sky is turning red and dawn is breaking, so the bastard will not return tonight. We must move immediately tomorrow!"

He spat out the last words through clenched teeth. Silence flowed for about two minutes, and I even had the illusion that I could hear everyone's heartbeats. Then, Dr. Van Helsing placed his hand very affectionately on Mrs. Harker's head and spoke.

"Now, Madam Mina—poor, lovely, and dear Madam Mina—please tell us exactly what happened. Heaven knows my heart does not wish to cause you pain. But we must know everything. Because right now, we must handle all these matters more swiftly and thoroughly than ever before, risking our lives in earnest. The fateful day when we must sever the links of all these evil ties, if possible, has arrived before our eyes. And now is the last chance for us to survive and gain clues."

Our poor, precious lady trembled all over. Seeing her hug her husband even tighter and bury her face deeper and deeper into his chest, I could tell how extremely tense she was. Then, lifting her head proudly, she reached out a hand toward Dr. Van Helsing. The doctor took that hand, bowed reverently, kissed it, and then held it firmly. Her other hand was tightly gripped by her husband, who wrapped his other arm around her protectively. Pausing for a moment as if to gather her thoughts, she finally opened her mouth.

"I took the sleeping pill the doctor gave me, but it didn't take effect for quite a long time. Instead, it felt like my sleep was fleeing, and countless horrific imaginations began to flood my mind. Things about death and vampires, blood and pain, and tribulations." At his wife's words, her husband let out a groan in spite of himself, and she looked back at him and said affectionately. "Don't worry too much, honey. You must be brave and strong. You have to help me overcome this terrible ordeal well. If you knew what agonizing effort it takes for me to even speak of these terrifying things, you would understand how desperately I am hoping for your help. Anyway, I decided I must force myself to sleep to let the medicine take effect. Sure enough, I must have fallen asleep soon after. I have no memory after that. I didn't wake up when Jonathan entered the room, and my next memory is from when he was lying next to me. The same hazy mist I had seen before was laid out in the room. I don't know if I mentioned that mist, but you'll know later if I show you my diary. Along with the exact same vague terror I felt before, I sensed a presence that someone was in the room. I turned over to wake Jonathan, but he was in a deep sleep as if he had taken the sleeping pill himself. No matter how much I shook him, he showed no sign of waking up. Only then was I suddenly frightened, and I looked around in terror. At that moment, my heart sank. Beside the bed stood a tall, thin man in black clothes, as if he had walked out of the mist, or rather, as if he was made from the transformed mist—because the mist had completely vanished. I instantly recognized him from hearing the others' descriptions. His pale, wax-like face, his hooked nose where a thin streak of white light settled, his parted red lips revealing sharp, pure white teeth, and his red eyes like the sunset reflecting on the windows of St. Mary's Church in Whitby. I could even clearly recognize the red scar on his forehead from where Jonathan had struck him. For a moment my heart seemed to stop and I wanted to scream, but my whole body was paralyzed and I couldn't move a single finger. After a moment of silence, he pointed his finger at Jonathan and whispered in a piercing, sharp voice."
