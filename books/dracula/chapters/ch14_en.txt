[P001] Chapter 14

[P002] Mina Harker's Journal

[P003a] September 23.—Jonathan is much better after a restless night. I am glad he has so much work to do, for it distracts him from those terrible memories. I am also truly happy that he is free from the pressure of his new partnership. I always believed he would not lose himself, and I am so proud of how perfectly my Jonathan handles his new duties, which befit his promotion. He said he couldn't come home for lunch today, so he will be working late.

[P003b] Since I have finished all the housework, I plan to lock myself in my room and read the journal he wrote in Transylvania....

[P004a] September 24.—I could not bring myself to write last night. Jonathan's terrifying account shook me too deeply. My poor, precious husband! Whether all of it was real or mere illusion, I cannot even begin to imagine how much he must have suffered. Is there even a hint of truth in that story? Did he suffer from such extreme stress that he caught a fever and wrote down those horrifying delusions, or did those things actually happen? I may never know, because I do not have the courage to ask him directly....

[P004b] But who on earth was that man we ran into yesterday! Jonathan was absolutely certain it was him.... Poor man! Perhaps Mr. Hawkins's funeral upset him so much that his past trauma resurfaced.... He believes all of it is entirely true. I remember what he told me on our wedding day. "Whether sleeping or waking, whether mad or sane, unless a solemn duty calls me back to that painful time." There seems to be a consistent thread in all of this.... That the terrible Count was coming to London....

[P004c] If that is true, and if he arrived in London with a multitude of followers.... Then perhaps there is a solemn duty waiting for us. If that moment comes, we must never run away.... I will make preparations. I should bring the typewriter at once and copy his journal. That way, others can read it if needed. And if such a thing happens, making these preparations might prevent poor Jonathan from suffering another shock. I will explain the situation on his behalf and ensure he is never tormented or worried again.

[P004d] Someday, if Jonathan fully recovers from his nervous breakdown, he might want to confide in me. When that time comes, I can ask questions, properly understand what happened, and then think about how to comfort him.

[P021b] Mina Harker's Journal.

[P021c] September 25.—I am very excited about Dr. Van Helsing's upcoming visit. Perhaps it is because of the expectation that he might provide a clue to the terrible things Jonathan went through. Moreover, since he cared for our poor Lucy until she passed away, I am sure he will tell me about her in detail. He must be coming because of Lucy's sleepwalking, not because of Jonathan's matter. Does that mean the real truth of what happened to Jonathan will remain forever unknown!

[P021d] I am so foolish. The contents of that terrifying journal have completely captured my imagination, painting everything in dark colors. Of course, he is coming about Lucy. Lucy's sleepwalking returned, and she must have fallen ill because of that dreadful night on the cliff. I was so absorbed in my own affairs that I completely forgot how ill Lucy had been since then. Lucy must have told the doctor about her sleepwalking incident on the cliff and that I knew all about it.

[P022_026] So now, the doctor wants to hear what I know to better understand Lucy's symptoms. I hope keeping it a secret from Mrs. Westenra was the right choice. If my silence harmed poor Lucy in any way, I would never forgive myself. I hope Dr. Van Helsing does not blame me. With so many difficult and anxious things lately, I do not have the energy to handle any more.

[P026b] Sometimes, a good cry seems to clear the mind, just like the sky after a rain. It is probably because reading Jonathan's journal yesterday upset me, and Jonathan left early this morning for the whole day. It is the first time we have been apart since we got married. I just hope my precious husband stays safe and that nothing happens to upset him. It is already 2 o'clock. The doctor will be here soon. I should not mention Jonathan's journal until he asks first. I am so glad I typed out my journal.

[P026c] If the doctor asks about Lucy, I can just hand this over to him. It will save a lot of trouble answering every single question.

[P026d] * * * * *

[P027_030] Later.—He came and went. Oh, what a strange encounter! My head is spinning! It feels like a dream. Could all of this, or even just a little bit of it, be true? If I hadn't read Jonathan's journal first, I wouldn't have believed it at all. Poor, so poor our Jonathan! How much terrible suffering he must have endured. Please, God, do not let this haunt him again. I will do my best to protect him too, but perhaps it might bring him greater comfort and help.

[P030b] Even if the result is something terrible and frightening, knowing for certain that his eyes, ears, and reason were not deceived, but that everything was real, might be better. Perhaps what torments him the most is his own self-doubt. If that doubt disappears, if it is revealed that what he experienced was not an illusion but reality, he might be able to gather his courage and prepare to overcome the shock. Seeing that Arthur and Dr. Seward brought Dr. Van Helsing all the way from distant Holland to care for Lucy, he must be a highly skilled and excellent person.

[P030c] Meeting him in person, I felt his kind, good, and noble character. When he comes again tomorrow, I must ask him about Jonathan. God willing, all this sorrow and anxiety might lead to a good conclusion. I once wanted to practice doing interviews. Jonathan's friend at the 'Exeter News' told me that memory is everything in an interview, and even if it's refined later, every single word of the conversation must be recorded exactly. Since this was a rare opportunity, I will try to record today's conversation without leaving out a single word.

[P030d] The clock pointed to half past two when there was a knock. I gathered my courage and waited. A few minutes later, Mary opened the door and announced, "Dr. Van Helsing is here."

[P031_032] As I stood up to greet him, he approached. He was of medium height and sturdy build, with broad shoulders above a wide, deep chest. His neck was thick and firmly supported a well-balanced head. Just by looking at him holding his head high, I could immediately feel deep thought and strong power. His broad, large head behind his ears exuded a noble dignity. His cleanly shaven face featured a strong, angular jaw, a resolute and expressive large mouth, and an appropriately large, straight nose.

[P033a] The sensitive nostrils of his nose seemed to widen whenever he closed his mouth tightly and furrowed his thick eyebrows. His broad, straight forehead stretched straight up before tilting back over two prominent ridges. Thanks to that forehead, his reddish hair naturally fell to the sides and back without covering his brow. His large, dark blue eyes, set wide apart, seemed capable of changing from gentle to stern depending on his mood. He said to me.

[P033b] "Are you Mrs. Harker?" I nodded.

[P033c] "And you were Miss Mina Murray?" I nodded again.

[P034_039] "It is Miss Mina Murray that I have come to see. The friend of that poor child, Lucy Westenra. Madam Mina, I have come on behalf of the dead."

[P039b] I replied, "Sir, the mere fact that you were a friend and benefactor of Lucy Westenra is enough for me to gladly spare my time." I held out my hand, and he took it, saying gently:

[P039c] "Oh, Madam Mina, I knew that the friend of that poor, pure child must be a good person, but there is something I did not yet know..." He concluded with a polite bow. I asked him what brought him to see me, and he got straight to the point.

[P039d] "I read the letters you sent to Miss Lucy. Please forgive me; I did not know where to begin my investigation, and there was no one else I could ask. I know you stayed with Lucy in Whitby. Lucy used to write in her journal sometimes. Do not be surprised, Madam Mina. After you left, she started doing it, imitating your habit. In that journal, there were accounts of her sleepwalking, and she wrote that you had saved her.

[P039e] I have come with a confused mind, so I beg you, in your deep mercy, to tell me everything you remember from that time."

[P039f] "I believe I can tell you everything, Dr. Van Helsing."

[P039g] "Ah, you remember facts and details well? That is quite uncommon among young ladies."

[P039h] "No, Doctor. I recorded everything when it happened. I can show it to you if you wish."

[P040_045] "Oh, Madam Mina, I would be truly grateful if you did. You would be doing me a great favor." A touch of mischief arose within me—perhaps everyone has a little bit of that left in them—and I handed him my shorthand journal. He bowed in gratitude, took the journal, and asked:

[P045b] "May I read it?"

[P046_047] "If you wish," I answered as politely as possible. He opened the journal, and for a moment, his face stiffened. Then he stood up and bowed.

[P047b] "Oh, you are truly a clever person!" he said. "I have long known that Mr. Jonathan is a man with much to be thankful for, but I did not know his wife possessed all these wonderful qualities. If you wouldn't mind, would you read this for me? Alas, I do not know shorthand." At this point, I felt a little ashamed, thinking my joke had gone too far. I took out a typewritten copy from my sewing basket and handed it to him.

[P047c] "Forgive me," I said. "I couldn't help but play a little joke. But I guessed you would definitely ask about our Lucy, and to save you time—not for my sake, but because I know your time is precious—I typed it out."

[P047d] His eyes sparkled as he took it. "You are very kind," he said. "May I read it right now? I might have some questions for you after I finish."

[P047e] "Of course," I said. "You can read it while I prepare lunch. Then you can ask questions while we eat." He bowed, sat in a chair with his back to the light, and became deeply engrossed in the papers. Meanwhile, I went out to get lunch ready, though in truth, I just left the room so he wouldn't be disturbed. When I returned, he was pacing the room hurriedly, his face flushed with excitement. He rushed over and grabbed both of my hands.

[P048a] "Oh, Madam Mina," he said. "How can I ever express in words how indebted I am to you? These papers are like a ray of sunshine. They have opened a door for me. I am dazzled—the light is so bright that I cannot even see the dark clouds always gathering behind it. But you cannot understand what I mean by this. Oh, you clever woman, thank you so much. Madam," he added with a very serious expression. "If there is ever anything I, Abraham Van Helsing, can do for you or your family, please tell me at any time.

[P048b] Helping you as a friend would be my great joy and honor. And not just as a simple friend. I swear to use all the knowledge I have gained and every ability I possess for you and those you love. There is darkness and light in life. You are one of those lights. You will enjoy a happy and good life, and your husband too will be blessed because of you."

[P049] "But, Doctor, you overestimate me too much. Besides... besides, you hardly know me."

[P050_058] "I do not know you? I, an old man who has studied men and women all my life? I, who have specialized in the brain and everything that comes from it! I have read this journal you so kindly prepared for me, and I saw truth breathing in every sentence. And I also read that affectionate letter you wrote to poor Lucy about the deep trust between you and your husband—and still, you say I do not know you!

[P058b] Oh, Madam Mina, good women reveal the truth of their lives every day, every hour, every minute, so clearly that even angels can read it. And for men like us who try to understand, a little of that angelic vision resides within us too. Your husband is a noble man, and so are you. You possess a firm trust, and such trust cannot exist where there is a cruel or mean heart. Now, tell me about your husband. Has he fully recovered?

[P058c] Is his fever completely gone, and is he healthy and strong again?" I thought this was the perfect opportunity to ask about Jonathan, so I replied:

[P058d] "He has almost completely recovered, but he suffered a great shock when Mr. Hawkins passed away." He interrupted me.

[P058e] "Oh, yes, I know. I read the last two letters you sent." I continued.

[P058f] "I think that incident was a huge shock to Jonathan. When he went to town last Thursday, he had another episode."

[P059_064] "An episode? So soon after recovering from brain fever! That is not a good sign. What kind of episode was it?"

[P064b] "He believed he saw someone who reminded him of something truly terrifying—the very cause that gave him brain fever in the first place." At that moment, a flood of emotions overwhelmed me all at once. Pity for Jonathan, the terrible fear he had experienced, the horrifying mysteries written in his journal, and the dread that had weighed heavily on my mind ever since—all of this crashed over me like a giant wave. I must have lost my reason. I fell to my knees, reaching out my hands to him, begging him to please make my husband well again.

[P064c] He took my hands, gently helped me up, and seated me on the sofa. Then he sat beside me, held my hand tightly, and spoke in an incredibly kind voice.

[P064d] "My life is dry and lonely. Buried in work, I have not had much time for friendship. But since my friend John Seward called me here, I have met many truly wonderful people and witnessed their nobility. As I grow older, the loneliness of my life feels even greater. So please believe me when I say that I approached you with deep respect. You have given me hope—perhaps not hope for the matter I am investigating, but hope that there are still wonderful women left in this world who make life happy.

[P064e] Women with a life and truth that will serve as a great example to children in the future. I am so glad that I can be of some small help to you. If your husband is suffering, that is the very field I have studied and experienced my entire life. I promise you, I will gladly help him with all my power. I will make his life strong and sturdy again, and bring happiness back to your life. Now, please eat first. You are exhausted and overwhelmed with anxiety. Mr. Jonathan would not want to see your pale face, either.

[P065_069] It is not good for him to see the person he loves sad. So, for his sake, you must eat and smile. You have already told me everything about Lucy, so I won't bring it up for a while so as not to cause you pain. I plan to stay in Exeter tonight. I need to carefully think over the things you have told me. After giving it enough thought, with your permission, I will ask you a few questions. Then, please tell me everything you know about your husband Jonathan's condition—but not now.

[P069b] First, eat, and then you can tell me everything."

[P070a] When we returned to the living room after lunch, he said.

[P070b] "Now, please tell me everything about him." When the moment came to speak to such a learned and excellent man, I was suddenly seized with fear that he might think of me as a weak fool and Jonathan as a madman—because the contents of that journal were so bizarre. I hesitated, unable to open my mouth. But he was so kind and gentle, and he promised to help me. I trusted him and began to speak.

[P070c] "Dr. Van Helsing, even if what I am about to tell you sounds extremely strange, please do not laugh at me or my husband. Since yesterday, I have been suffering from a deep doubt, as if I had caught a fever. Please take pity on me, and do not think me foolish for half-believing these bizarre stories." He reassured me with a warm voice and tone.

[P070d] "Oh, Madam, if you knew how strange the matter I am currently investigating is, you would rather laugh at me. I have learned that no matter how bizarre a story may seem, one should not lightly dismiss another's belief. I have always tried to keep an open mind. What closes a person's mind is not ordinary daily life, but strange and bizarre things—things that make you doubt your own sanity."

[P071_074] "Thank you, I want to thank you a thousand times! You have lifted a heavy burden from my heart. If you will allow it, I would like to give you a document to read, Doctor. It is quite long, but I typed it out for you. This will explain the suffering Jonathan and I have gone through. This is a copy of a journal meticulously recording everything that happened while he was abroad. I dare not add a single word to its contents. Please read it yourself and judge.

[P074b] And the next time we meet, I hope you will share your thoughts with me."

[P074c] He promised as he took the papers. "I promise. If you permit me, I will come to see you and your husband as early as possible tomorrow morning."

[P075_076] "Jonathan will be back at 11:30. Come then, have lunch with us, and say hello to Jonathan. Then, if you catch the 3:34 PM express train, you can arrive at Paddington Station before 8 o'clock." He seemed very surprised as I recited the train schedule perfectly. He didn't know that I had memorized all the train schedules to and from Exeter to help Jonathan whenever he had to leave in a hurry.

[P076b] So he left with the papers, and I am sitting here now lost in thought—not even knowing what I am thinking about.

[P088_090] Jonathan Harker's Journal.

[P091_094] September 26.—I never thought I would write in this journal again, but the time has finally come. When I returned home last night, Mina was waiting with dinner ready. After we ate, she told me everything about Dr. Van Helsing's visit, giving him a copy of our journal, and how much she had worried about me. Then she showed me the doctor's letter stating that everything I had written in my journal was true. That news seems to have made me a completely new person.

[P094b] What had broken me all this time was the terrible doubt of not knowing what was real and what was an illusion. I had been lost in thick darkness, suffering from helplessness and paranoia. But now that I know the truth, I am no longer afraid—even if it is the Count himself. In the end, his plan to come to London succeeded, and the man I saw really was the Count. He had grown younger, but how on earth could that be? If Dr.

[P095_099] Van Helsing is as wonderful as Mina says, he must be the exact person we need to uncover the Count's identity and hunt him down. Mina and I stayed awake late discussing all of this. While Mina gets ready to go out, I plan to go to the hotel in a few minutes to fetch the doctor....

[P099b] The doctor seemed inwardly surprised to see me. When I entered the room and greeted him, he grasped both my shoulders, turned my face toward the light, stared at me intently, and said.

[P100a] "But Madam Mina told me you were ill. That you had suffered a great shock." Hearing this kind, sharply-featured old gentleman call my wife "Madam Mina" somehow made me smile. I smiled and replied.

[P100b] "It is true that I was bedridden and suffered a great shock, but you have already cured my illness, Doctor."

[P100c] "How do you mean I cured you?"

[P100d] "Thanks to the letter you sent Mina last night. I was lost in deep doubt, feeling as if my entire life was unreal. I didn't know what to believe—I couldn't even trust my own senses. Because I didn't know what to believe, I didn't know what to do. So I simply buried myself in work, trying to escape into my old ordinary routine. But even that routine didn't work for me, and I ended up losing even my belief in myself. Doctor, you probably don't know what it feels like to doubt everything about yourself, even your sanity.

[P100e] No, you could never know. Someone with strong eyebrows like yours would never have experienced such a thing." He chuckled, seemingly pleased by my words, and said.

[P100f] "Aha! So you know a bit of physiognomy (reading character from faces). I learn more every hour I am here. I am very much looking forward to having breakfast with you today. And Mr. Jonathan, forgive this old man's compliment, but you are truly blessed to have such a wife." I nodded and listened silently, for I could gladly listen all day to the doctor praising Mina.

[P101_102] "Your wife is one of the most perfect creatures God has ever made. She is a person God created Himself to show men—and other women as well—that a heaven we can enter truly exists, and that its light can shine right here on earth. She is so true, kind, and noble, yet utterly without selfishness—a rare thing indeed in this suspicious and selfish age. And as for you—I read all the letters you sent to poor Miss Lucy, and some of the letters Lucy left behind also mentioned you.

[P102b] So I had known about you through the words of others for a few days. But it was only through reading your journal last night that I finally saw the real you. Shall we shake hands? Let us be lifelong friends."

[P102c] We shook hands, and his genuine, kind demeanor brought a lump to my throat.

[P102d] "Now then," he continued. "May I ask you for one more favor? I have an enormous task before me, and the first step is to gather information. You could help me. Could you tell me what happened before you left for Transylvania? I might ask for other kinds of help later, but right now, this is what I need."

[P103_104] "Listen, Doctor," I said. "Does your task happen to involve the Count?"

[P105_109] "It does," he answered solemnly.

[P109b] "In that case, I am with you with all my heart. Since you have to catch the 10:30 train, you won't have time to read my records right now. But I will pack a bundle of papers for you, so you can read them on the train."

[P109c] After breakfast, I saw him off at the station. As we exchanged goodbyes, he said.

[P109d] "If I send someone, could you come to London? With Madam Mina, too."

[P109_113] "If you call, both of us will come anytime," I replied.
