Chapter 24

Dr. Seward's Phonograph Diary, spoken by Van Helsing

Left for Jonathan Harker.

You must stay by your beloved Mina's side. We are going out to search and confirm. We are just going to verify what we already know, so it might not even be called a search. Anyway, you must stay by her side today and protect her. That is your most sacred and important duty. Today, he won't be able to find her either. You have the right to know, so I will tell you what the four of us already know. Our enemy has left. He has returned to his castle in Transylvania. This is as clear as if a giant flame had carved the words on a wall. He had prepared for this situation from the start, and to regain his strength, he has even finished preparing to send his last box of his homeland's soil on a ship. That is why he took the money, and why he rushed so much at the last moment, fearing we would catch him before sunset. He tried to hide in the tomb of poor Lucy, who became a creature like him, but he ran completely out of time and his last hope was shattered. Since his plan went wrong, he went straight for his last resort, his final remaining fortress of earth. He is truly frighteningly cunning! Realizing that his game in London was over, he turned his steps back to his homeland without any lingering attachment. He found and boarded a ship that would retrace the route he came from. We are now going to find out which ship it is and where it is heading. As soon as we find out, we will return and tell you everything. Then you and poor Madam Mina will also find new hope and comfort. If you think about it carefully, you will realize that this situation itself is a hope. Because we haven't lost everything yet. It took hundreds of years for this monster we are chasing to reach London. But we drove him out in just one day after figuring out his true identity. Of course, he is powerful enough to cause immense harm and doesn't feel pain like we do, but in the end, he is also just a finite being. On the other hand, we stand firm in our own steadfast purposes, and when we unite, we unleash a much greater power. So, strong husband of Madam Mina, take heart once again. This fight has only just begun, and in the end, the final victors will be us. That is as certain as the fact that God is looking down on His children from above. So please stay strong and find peace until we return.

Van Helsing.

Jonathan Harker's Journal.

October 4.—When I played Professor Van Helsing's message recorded on the phonograph for Mina, my poor wife's complexion brightened noticeably. The mere assurance that the Count had left this country gave her great comfort, and that comfort immediately became her strength to live on. As for me, with the terrible threat vanishing from before my eyes, the past events completely ceased to feel like reality. Even that hellish experience at Dracula's castle felt like a distant, forgotten dream. Standing under the dazzling sunlight while breathing this clear autumn air made it feel even more so.

Ah, but how could I not believe it! Lost in thought, my gaze ended up resting on the red burn mark scarred onto the pale forehead of my endlessly pitiful and lovely wife. As long as that terrible scar, caused by contact with a vampire, remains, I can never deny reality. In the future, the memories tied to that scar alone will make our faith shine as clear as crystal. Terrified of just sitting idly by, Mina and I reread the diaries we had written from beginning to end, over and over again. It is truly strange. The more we read, the more vivid the sense of reality became, yet the pain and fear seemed to rather fade away. Throughout the diaries, a clear sense of purpose guiding us on the right path was present, providing quite a bit of comfort. Mina said that we might be tools meant to achieve an ultimate good. Perhaps we are! I, too, will try to share her will. We haven't spoken a single word about the future yet. It would be better to wait until the Professor and the others return from their investigation.

The day is flying by at an incredible speed that I thought would never come again in my life. It is already 3 PM.

Mina Harker's Journal.

October 5, 5 PM.—Reporting meeting. Attendees: Professor Van Helsing, Lord Godalming, Dr. Seward, Quincey Morris, Jonathan Harker, Mina Harker.

Dr. Van Helsing explained what he had done during the day today to find out which ship Count Dracula took and where he fled to.

"First of all, I kept in mind the fact that he was trying to return to Transylvania. It was clear that he would retrace the route around the mouth of the Danube River or the Black Sea that he used when he came here. But when we actually tried to start tracking, it felt like hitting a brick wall. Like the old Latin proverb 'Everything unknown seems grand,' we set out with very heavy hearts to inquire about what ship left for the Black Sea last night. Since Madam Mina saw a sail being raised, I assumed it must be a sailing ship. Such small ships do not make it onto the 'Times' ship departure list. So, following Lord Godalming's suggestion, we headed to the Lloyd's shipping information office, which meticulously keeps records of all departures, no matter how small the ship. There, we found out that there was only one ship that rode the tide out to the Black Sea. The ship's name was the 'Czarina Catherine.' Its itinerary was to leave Doolittle Wharf, pass through Varna, and travel inland via the Danube River. 'Aha, this is the very ship the Count is on!' I slapped my knee. We rushed straight to Doolittle Wharf and met a man working in a shabby wooden office that looked smaller than a person. We asked him about the whereabouts of the 'Czarina Catherine.' He was a man who, despite his face turning red and raising his voice to spew all sorts of curses, wasn't actually bad-hearted. When Quincey rustled some bills out of his pocket and shoved them deep into the man's clothes pocket, the man bowed and became much more agreeable. He took us to the rough, hot-tempered dock workers and asked around for us. Those workers also became quite docile after having a refreshing drink to wet their whistles. Although they mixed in hard-to-understand rough slang like blood and curses, we could roughly understand what they meant. Anyway, they willingly poured out all the information we wanted to know."

"According to the men, a man visited around 5 PM yesterday, rushing terribly as if being chased by something. They said he was a tall man with a skinny, pale face, a prominent nose, exceptionally white teeth, and eyes that seemed to burn fiercely. He was dressed in black from head to toe, and entirely out of place with his appearance or the late afternoon time, he was wearing a random straw hat. He was throwing money around like water, urgently asking everyone what ship was going to the Black Sea and where its destination was. Someone guided him through the office to where the ship was, but the man didn't board; he just stopped at the end of the gangway and barked for the captain to be called out. At the mention of a hefty reward, the captain sauntered out, and although he initially hurled all sorts of foul language, he eventually agreed to the man's terms. Then the skinny man left abruptly, went to the place someone told him about, rented a horse and carriage, and appeared a little later carrying a large box. When loading it onto the cart to put it on the ship, several burly men had to stick together and struggle with it, but surprisingly, when unloading it from the carriage, the man effortlessly lifted it off alone as if handling a light piece of luggage. He held onto the captain and went on and on for a long time about where and how to place the box. But the captain, greatly annoyed by this interference, fired back using a colorful array of swear words from all over the world, threatening that if he was so untrusting, he should come and check the spot where the box would be placed himself. The man flatly refused, saying 'No,' and added that he had a mountain of things to do and couldn't go right now. Then the captain spat out thick curses again, telling him to scramble back early on his own because they would set sail immediately before the high tide ended. The skinny man gave a sinister smile and relaxed, saying that he would naturally come at the appropriate time, but if they set sail that early, they would be quite surprised. As the captain jumped up and down and cursed again in all sorts of languages, the man just bowed politely and left the parting words that he was sorry to cause them the trouble of him boarding the ship before departure. The captain, whose face turned bright red with anger, yelled at the top of his lungs, mixing unspeakable curses and slang, that he would not let anyone on his ship except unlucky Englishmen. In the end, the man asked where he could buy shipping documents nearby and left the place without lingering."

"The people at the dock didn't care one bit about where that man went. Because immediately after, they were distracted by something much more absurd. The men explained the situation at that time, mixing in curses again. Because it became obvious that the 'Czarina Catherine' wouldn't be able to set sail as scheduled. A thin fog started to creep up over the river water, quickly thickened, and soon completely blanketed the ship and its surroundings like a stuffy cotton quilt. The captain mobilized all kinds of languages to hurl sticky curses, but there was nothing he could do. The water level rose terrifyingly, and the captain was going crazy with anxiety that he might completely miss the tide. When it reached high tide and the captain's anger reached its peak, the skinny man walked up the gangway again and demanded to be shown where his box was loaded. The captain cursed at him, telling both him and his worn-out box to drop into hellfire. But the man didn't bat an eye; he followed the mate down, leisurely checked the place where the box was placed, and came back up, standing like a ghost on the thick fog-covered deck for a long time. Then it seemed he got off the ship alone without anyone knowing. No one saw him leave. In fact, when the fog lifted and the surroundings cleared, everyone was so spaced out that they didn't have time to pay attention to the man. My friends, spewing thick curses in their drunken state, chuckled and told the next story. When they asked other sailors going up and down the river at the time, not a single person had seen the fog except around the dock! Upon learning that fact, the captain poured out a spectacular barrage of curses that far exceeded his usual level of multilingual swearing, almost approaching the level of art. Anyway, the ship left the dock riding the ebb tide, and by morning, it must have easily cleared the river mouth. By the time the men told us this story, the ship was probably already running far away in the middle of the ocean."

"So, dear Madam Mina, right now, while the Count is piercing through the fog he controls and racing over the sea toward the mouth of the Danube River, we have no choice but to catch our breath and wait for a while. No matter how much a ship runs on the wind, sailing always takes a long time. If we chase closely over land, we can arrive in Varna much faster and confront him. The best scenario is to ambush him during the daytime when the sun is up and he is helplessly trapped in his box. At that time, he won't have the strength to resist, so we can easily cut off his breath. Fortunately, we have a few days left to set up a meticulous plan. We have already met the ship owner and thoroughly checked the invoices and documents, so we can clearly see his destination. The box we are chasing is scheduled to arrive in Varna and be handed over to an agent named Ristics, and when that agent presents his credentials, the merchant friend's mission will also end. The ship owner made a fuss, asking if there was any problem and offering to send a telegram to Varna immediately to request an investigation, but we flatly declined, saying it was okay. Because if the police or customs step in rashly, it could ruin everything. This task must be finished only by our hands, in our own way."

When Dr. Van Helsing's words ended, I carefully asked if he was certain the Count was still on the ship. The Professor answered firmly. "Of course. Isn't there the most solid evidence? Namely, the testimony you gave yourself while in a hypnotic state this very morning." Since I was so terribly afraid of Jonathan leaving me—and knowing all too well that if the others went, my husband would insist on following them—I asked once again if we really had to track the Count down to the end. The Professor at first spoke calmly as if to comfort me. But as the story grew longer, his voice carried intense emotion and resolute anger, and we eventually couldn't help but deeply realize why he had been able to remain a great leader who guided countless people for such a long time.

"That is correct. Chasing the Count is necessary—absolutely, unconditionally necessary! First is to save your life, Madam, and second is to save all of humanity. This terrible monster has already caused unspeakable harm even during the short time he wandered in the dark, ignorant of the world, leaving the cramped area where he had been confined. I clearly told the others all these facts. Dear Madam Mina, you will also gradually learn of this fact through John's or your husband's phonograph records. I told the party: I said that for hundreds of years, he has been plotting this terrible plan to abandon his barren homeland where even the smell of blood is hard to catch, and cross over to a new land densely packed with life like a cornfield. If another vampire like the Count had tried to commit this crazy act, probably no amount of long years in the past or future of the world would have been able to help him. Only this bastard has miraculously accomplished this task by gathering profound, powerful, and mysterious forces of nature. The cursed place where he lived as an undead for hundreds of years is itself a very bizarre place geologically and chemically. Deep caves and earth fissures of unknown depths yawn open, and from the volcanic craters, water and gases with strange properties that steal or revive life still spout out. These mysterious forces must have strangely intertwined to cause some action that extended his physical life. Moreover, he was inherently born with outstanding qualities. In the bloody era of war, he was praised as someone with nerves of steel, a sharp brain, and indomitable courage more than anyone else in the world. The terrible vitality inside him boiled up bizarrely and reached its peak, and as his body grew and prospered, his brain also grew terrifyingly. Surprisingly, all this process is an achievement he accomplished entirely on his own without colluding with the devil. For the power of the devil eventually has no choice but to fall like autumn leaves before the great power that symbolizes good. Yet this monster is now holding our breath and shaking it. He infected you, Madam—oh, please forgive this old man for bringing up such heartbreaking words. But I am saying all this to save you. The Count has very cunningly planted a curse in your body. Even if he doesn't lift a finger anymore and leaves you alone, you will just live affectionately and beautifully as before, and then the moment you face death according to God's providence... you will terribly wake up as a monster just like him. We cannot just let such a tragedy happen! We swore in blood that we would never see such a sight. We are messengers acting on God's will. It is His noble will that this beautiful world and humans, which the Son of God saved by shedding blood, must not fall into the hands of filthy monsters that trample His name. God has allowed us to save one poor soul already, and like the old knights carrying the cross, we march out to the battlefield to save more souls. Like knights, we will advance toward the east where the sun rises, and even if we might fall, we will willingly lay down our lives for a good cause." As the Professor caught his breath for a moment, I asked in a trembling voice.

"But the Count has also suffered a bitter failure this time, so wouldn't he be scared off beforehand? Having been miserably chased out of English soil, wouldn't he never dare covet England again, just like a tiger chased by a hunter wouldn't come anywhere near a village?"
