[P001] Chapter 2

[P002] Jonathan Harker's Journal - Continued

[P003] May 5th - I must have fallen asleep. If I had been completely awake, I would have definitely noticed arriving at such a remarkable place. In the dark, the courtyard seemed quite large, and it might have appeared even bigger because several dark paths branched off under massive round arches. I haven't had a chance to see it in the daylight yet.

[P004] When the carriage stopped, the driver jumped down and offered his hand to help me out. Once again, I couldn't help but notice his incredible strength. His hand felt like a steel vise that could have easily crushed mine if he wanted to. Then, he unloaded my luggage and placed it on the ground next to me. I was standing near a massive old door studded with large iron nails, set into a projecting stone doorway. Even in the dim light, I could see that the stone was elaborately carved, though the details were heavily worn by time and weather. While I stood there, the driver climbed back into his seat and shook the reins. The horses lunged forward, and the carriage disappeared down one of the dark tunnels.

[P005] I stood there in silence, unsure of what to do. There was no sign of a bell or a knocker, and it was highly unlikely that my voice could penetrate those intimidating walls and dark window openings. The wait felt endless, and doubts and fears began to overwhelm me. What kind of place had I come to, and what sort of people lived here? What kind of terrifying adventure had I gotten myself into? Was this a normal situation for a law firm clerk sent to explain the purchase of a London property to a foreigner? A clerk! My fiancée, Mina, wouldn't like that title. I'm a lawyer now—just before leaving London, I received word that I passed my exams! I started rubbing my eyes and pinching myself to see if I was awake. It all felt like a terrible nightmare, and I half-expected to suddenly wake up in my own bed with the morning light shining through the window, just as I sometimes did after a long day of overworking. But the pinch hurt, and my eyes weren't playing tricks on me. I was definitely awake, standing in the middle of the Carpathian Mountains. All I could do now was be patient and wait for morning.

[P006] Just as I accepted this, I heard heavy footsteps approaching from behind the massive door and saw the glow of an approaching light through the cracks. Then came the sound of rattling chains and the clanking of heavy bolts being pulled back. A key turned with a loud, harsh screeching sound that suggested it hadn't been used in a long time, and the great door swung open.

[P007] Inside stood a tall older man, clean-shaven except for a long white mustache. He was dressed entirely in black, without a single spot of color on him. In his hand, he held an antique silver lamp. The flame burned without a glass cover, casting long, quivering shadows as it flickered in the draft from the open door. With a polite gesture, the old man waved his right hand to invite me inside and spoke in excellent English, though with an unusual accent:

[P008] "Welcome to my house! Enter freely and by your own choice!" He didn't take a single step forward to greet me. Instead, he stood as still as a statue, as if his welcoming gesture had turned him to stone. However, the moment I stepped over the threshold, he suddenly moved forward and grabbed my hand with a grip so strong it made me wince. The shock was only made worse by the fact that his hand felt as cold as ice—more like a corpse's hand than a living man's. He spoke again:

[P009] "Welcome to my house. Make yourself at home. Leave safely, and leave some of the happiness you bring behind!" The strength of his handshake was so similar to the driver's, whose face I had never seen, that for a second I wondered if they were the same person. Just to be sure, I asked:

[P010] "Count Dracula?" He offered a polite bow and replied:

[P011] "I am Dracula. Welcome to my house, Mr. Harker. Come in; the night air is cold, and you must need food and rest." As he spoke, he placed the lamp on a wall bracket and stepped outside to grab my luggage. He carried it inside before I could stop him. I tried to protest, but he insisted:

[P012] "No, sir, you are my guest. It's late, and my servants are asleep. Let me personally see to your comfort." He insisted on carrying my luggage down the long hallway, up a massive spiral staircase, and down another wide corridor, where our footsteps echoed heavily on the stone floor. At the end of the hall, he pushed open a heavy door, and I was thrilled to see a brightly lit room. A table was set for dinner, and a large fire, recently fueled with fresh logs, roared in the massive fireplace.

[P013] The Count stopped, set my bags down, closed the door, and walked across the room to open another door. This led into a small, octagonal room lit by a single lamp, which seemed to have no windows at all. Passing through this room, he opened another door and gestured for me to go inside. It was a wonderful sight: a large bedroom, brightly lit and warmed by another log fire. The top logs were fresh, indicating this fire had also just been built up, and it roared loudly up the wide chimney. The Count left my luggage inside the room and stepped back. Before closing the door, he said:

[P014] "You will need to freshen up and regain your strength after your journey. I hope you find everything you need. When you are ready, come into the other room, where your dinner will be waiting."

[P015] The light, the warmth, and the Count's polite welcome seemed to melt away all my doubts and fears. Once I felt like myself again, I realized I was absolutely starving. So, I quickly washed up and dressed, then headed into the other room.

[P016] I found dinner already served. My host was standing by the massive fireplace, leaning against the stonework. He gracefully gestured toward the table and said:

[P017] "Please, sit down and eat as much as you like. I hope you will excuse me for not joining you; I've already eaten, and I don't have supper."

[P018] I handed him the sealed letter Mr. Hawkins had given me. He opened it and read it carefully, then handed it to me with a charming smile. At least one part of it made me very happy to read:

[P019] "I deeply regret that a bout of gout, a condition I suffer from constantly, makes it impossible for me to travel for the time being. However, I am pleased to send a highly capable replacement, someone I trust completely. He is a young man, full of energy and talent in his field, and he is extremely loyal. He is discreet and quiet, and he has grown into a fine young man while working for me. He will be available to assist you whenever you need him during his stay, and he will follow your instructions in all matters."

[P020] The Count stepped forward and lifted the cover off a dish, and I eagerly dug into a delicious roast chicken. That, along with some cheese, a salad, and a bottle of aged Tokaji wine—of which I drank two glasses—made up my dinner. While I ate, the Count asked me many questions about my trip, and I gradually told him everything I had experienced.

[P021] By then, I had finished eating. Following my host's suggestion, I pulled a chair up to the fire and began smoking a cigar he offered me, though he apologized and said he didn't smoke himself. This gave me a chance to really look at him, and I noticed his facial features were very striking.

[P022] He had a very strong, prominent nose with a high bridge and unusually shaped nostrils. His forehead was high and rounded, and while his hair was thinning around his temples, it was very thick everywhere else. His eyebrows were extremely bushy, almost meeting over his nose, and the hair seemed to curl wildly. Beneath his heavy mustache, his mouth looked rigid and somewhat cruel, revealing peculiarly sharp, white teeth. These teeth protruded over his lips, which were surprisingly red—showing an astonishing amount of vitality for a man his age. His ears were pale and very pointed at the tops. He had a broad, strong chin and firm, thin cheeks. Overall, his complexion was incredibly pale.

[P023] Earlier, I had only seen the backs of his hands resting on his knees in the firelight, and they looked rather pale and delicate. But seeing them up close now, I couldn't help but notice that they were actually quite rough, broad, and with stubby fingers. Strangely, there was hair growing in the center of his palms. His nails were long, well-kept, and filed to a sharp point. When the Count leaned toward me and his hands touched mine, I couldn't stop a shudder. It might have been his awful breath, but a terrible wave of nausea washed over me, and despite my best efforts, I couldn't hide it. The Count clearly noticed. He pulled back, and with a grim smile that revealed even more of his protruding teeth, he sat back down on his side of the fireplace. We sat in silence for a while. As I looked toward the window, I saw the first faint light of dawn. Everything felt strangely quiet, but when I listened closely, I heard the howling of many wolves echoing up from the valley below. The Count's eyes lit up, and he said:

[P024] "Listen to them—the children of the night. What beautiful music they make!" I guess he saw a confused look on my face, because he added:

[P025] "Ah, sir, you city folks just can't understand the heart of a hunter." Then he stood up and said:

[P026] "You must be exhausted. Your bedroom is ready, and you can sleep as late as you want tomorrow. I have to be out until the afternoon, so sleep well and have good dreams!" With a polite bow, he personally opened the door to the octagonal room for me, and I went into my bedroom.

[P027] I am completely overwhelmed by all these strange things. I'm filled with doubt and fear, and my mind is racing with bizarre thoughts that I'm too scared to even admit to myself. May God protect me, if only for the sake of the people I love!

[P028_029] May 7th - It's early morning again, but I've rested and really enjoyed the past 24 hours. I slept late into the day and woke up naturally. After getting dressed, I went into the room where we had dinner and found a cold breakfast waiting for me, with a pot of coffee kept warm on the hearth. There was a card on the table that read:

[P030] "I have to be away for a while. Don't wait up for me. - D." I sat down and enjoyed a hearty meal. When I finished, I looked for a bell to let the servants know I was done, but I couldn't find one. There are definitely some strange things missing in this house, especially considering the incredible wealth surrounding me. The tableware is made of solid gold and is so beautifully crafted that it must be worth a fortune. The curtains, the upholstery on the chairs and sofas, and the bed hangings are made of the most expensive and beautiful fabrics. They must have cost an unbelievable amount when they were made, as they are centuries old but still in perfect condition. I saw similar fabrics at Hampton Court Palace, but those were worn, frayed, and moth-eaten. Yet, there isn't a single mirror in any of the rooms. There isn't even a vanity mirror on my table, so I had to pull a small shaving mirror out of my bag just to shave and brush my hair. I haven't seen a single servant anywhere, nor have I heard any sounds near the castle except for the howling wolves. Sometime after I finished eating—I'm not sure if I should call it breakfast or dinner, since it was between 5:00 and 6:00 PM—I looked around for something to read because I didn't want to wander around the castle without the Count's permission. There was absolutely nothing in the room—no books, newspapers, or even writing supplies. So, I opened another door in the room and discovered a sort of library. I tried the door across from mine, but it was locked.

[P031] In the library, I was thrilled to find a huge number of English books filling entire shelves, along with bound volumes of magazines and newspapers. The table in the center was covered with English magazines and newspapers, though none of them were very recent. The books covered a wide variety of subjects—history, geography, politics, economics, botany, geology, and law—all related to England and its customs and lifestyle. There were even reference books like the London Directory, the "Red" and "Blue" government books, Whitaker's Almanac, the Army and Navy Lists, and—which somehow made me very happy to see—the Law List.

[P032] While I was looking at the books, the door opened and the Count walked in. He greeted me warmly and said he hoped I had slept well. Then he continued:

[P033] "I'm glad you found your way in here. I'm sure there's a lot that will interest you. These companions"—he rested his hand on some of the books—"have been good friends to me. For the past few years, ever since I decided to go to London, they've given me many, many hours of pleasure. Through them, I've come to know your great country; and to know England is to love her. I am eager to walk through the crowded streets of massive London, to be right in the middle of the rushing crowds, to share in its life, its changes, its death, and everything that makes it what it is. But sadly, I only know your language through books. I am counting on you, my friend, to help me learn how to actually speak it."

[P034] "But, Count," I said, "you know and speak English perfectly!" He bowed seriously.

[P035] "Thank you, my friend, for your very generous compliment, but I'm afraid I still have a long way to go. It's true that I know the grammar and the vocabulary, but I still don't know how to speak naturally."

[P036] "No, really," I said, "you speak excellently."

[P037] "Not quite," he replied. "I know that if I were to walk and talk in London right now, everyone would instantly recognize me as a foreigner. That's not good enough for me. Here, I am a nobleman; I am a boyar. The common people know me, and I am the master. But a foreigner in a strange land is nobody. People don't know him, and if they don't know him, they don't care about him. I'll be satisfied when I blend in with everyone else, so that no one stops to stare when they see me, or pauses their conversation when they hear me speak, just to say, 'Ha! A foreigner!' I've been a master for so long that I want to remain one—or at least ensure that no one else is my master. You're not just here as the agent of my friend Peter Hawkins of Exeter to tell me about my new London estate. I hope you'll stay here with me for a while, so that by talking together, I can learn the English accent. And I want you to correct me whenever I make a mistake, no matter how small. I apologize for being away for so long today, but I know you'll forgive someone who has so many important matters to handle."

[P038] Naturally, I told him I was more than happy to help, and I asked if I could use the library whenever I wanted. He answered, "Yes, absolutely," and added:

[P039] "You can go anywhere you want in the castle, except for the locked doors, which you obviously won't want to open anyway. There are reasons why everything is the way it is. If you could see things from my perspective and know what I know, you might understand better." I said I was sure he was right, and he continued:

[P040] "We are in Transylvania, and Transylvania is not England. Our customs are not yours, and you will undoubtedly encounter many strange things. In fact, based on what you've already told me about your trip, you've already gotten a taste of how strange things can be."

[P041] This sparked a long conversation. Since it was obvious he just wanted to talk for the sake of talking, I asked him a lot of questions about the things I had already experienced or noticed. Sometimes he dodged the topic or changed the subject by pretending not to understand, but mostly he answered my questions very openly. As time passed and I grew a bit more confident, I asked him about some of the bizarre events from the night before, like why the driver stopped at the places where he saw the blue flames. He explained that according to local legend, on one specific night of the year—last night, actually, when all evil spirits are believed to roam free—a blue flame appears over any spot where treasure is buried. "There's very little doubt that treasure is hidden in the area you traveled through last night," he continued. "That land was fought over for centuries by Wallachians, Saxons, and Turks. Honestly, there's barely an inch of soil in this entire region that hasn't been soaked in the blood of men, whether they were patriots defending their home or invaders trying to conquer it. In the old days, there were chaotic times when massive armies of Austrians and Hungarians marched in. The patriots—men, women, the elderly, and even children—went out to fight them. They waited on the rocks above the mountain passes so they could trigger artificial avalanches and crush the invaders. Even when the invaders won, they found very little loot because everything of value had been safely buried in the earth."

[P042] "But how," I asked, "could it stay hidden for so long when there's such an obvious clue for anyone willing to look?" The Count smiled, and as his lips curled back, his long, sharp canine teeth were unsettlingly visible. He answered:

[P043] "Because the peasants around here are cowards and fools at heart! Those flames only show up on one night of the year, and on that night, no local man will step outside his house unless absolutely necessary. And even if they did go out, my dear sir, they wouldn't know what to do. The very peasant you mentioned, who marked the spot where he saw the flame, wouldn't be able to find his own markings in the daylight. I'd bet my life that even you wouldn't be able to find those places again."

[P044] "You're right about that," I said. "I wouldn't have the slightest idea where to look." After that, we started talking about other things.

[P045] "Come on," he finally said, "tell me about London and the house you bought for me." I apologized for my forgetfulness and went to my room to grab the paperwork from my bag. While I was organizing the documents, I heard the sound of china and silver clinking in the next room. As I walked through, I noticed the table had been cleared and the lamp was lit, as it was now pitch black outside. The lamps in the study were also lit, and I found the Count lying on the sofa reading, of all things, an English train schedule. When I walked in, he cleared the books and papers off the table, and we dove into the blueprints, deeds, and all the financial details. He was fascinated by everything and asked me countless questions about the property and its surroundings. It was clear he had studied everything he could find about the area beforehand, because by the end of our discussion, he definitely knew much more about it than I did. When I pointed this out, he replied:

[P046] "Well, my friend, isn't it necessary that I do? When I move there, I'll be completely alone. My friend Harker Jonathan—ah, excuse me, I slipped into my country's habit of saying the family name first—my friend Jonathan Harker won't be there to help me and correct my mistakes. He'll be miles away in Exeter, probably working on legal documents with my other friend, Peter Hawkins. Right?"

[P047] We went over every detail regarding the purchase of the Purfleet estate. After I explained all the facts, got his signature on the necessary documents, and wrote a letter to Mr. Hawkins to go along with them, he asked me how I managed to find such a perfect location. I read him the notes I took at the time, which I am copying down here:

[P048] "While driving down a side road in Purfleet, I stumbled upon exactly the kind of property we were looking for. There was a worn-out 'For Sale' sign out front. It's surrounded by a high, very old wall made of massive stones that hasn't been repaired in years. The heavy gates are made of old oak and iron, and they're completely rusted shut.

[P049] "The estate is called Carfax, which is probably a corruption of the old French term 'Quatre Face,' since the house has four sides that align perfectly with the four points of the compass. The property covers about twenty acres and is completely enclosed by the solid stone wall I mentioned earlier. There are lots of trees, which make certain areas feel quite dark and gloomy. There's also a deep, dark pond or small lake; it's clearly fed by underground springs because the water is clear and flows out into a decent-sized stream. The house itself is massive and features architectural styles from many different eras, going all the way back to medieval times. One section is built from incredibly thick stone and only has a few small windows placed very high up, heavily barred with iron. It looks like part of an old castle keep and sits right next to an old chapel or church. I couldn't get inside the chapel because I didn't have the key to the door connecting it to the main house, but I took several photos of the exterior from different angles with my Kodak camera. The house has been added onto over the years, but in a very disorganized, sprawling way, so I can only guess how much ground it actually covers—it must be huge. There are very few other houses nearby. The closest one is a massive building that was recently expanded and turned into a private psychiatric hospital. However, you can't see it from the Carfax grounds."

[P050] When I finished reading, he said:

[P051] "I'm glad it's large and old. I come from an ancient family myself, and living in a brand-new house would kill me. You can't turn a house into a real home overnight; after all, a century is made up of so few days. I'm also thrilled that there's an ancient chapel on the property. We Transylvanian nobles don't like the idea of our bones resting among commoners. I'm not looking for fun or joy, nor do I want the bright, vibrant pleasures of sunshine and sparkling water that appeal to the young and carefree. I am no longer young, and after enduring years of exhausting grief for the dead, my heart isn't built for happiness anymore. Besides, the walls of my castle are crumbling, there are shadows everywhere, and the cold wind blows through the broken battlements and windows. I love the shade and the shadows, and I prefer to be alone with my thoughts whenever possible." Somehow, his words didn't quite match his expression, or perhaps it was just the shape of his face that made his smile look so malicious and gloomy.

[P052] A little while later, he excused himself, asking me to gather all my papers. He was gone for quite some time, so I started looking through some of the books around me. One was an atlas, and when I opened it, it naturally fell open to the map of England, as if that specific page had been heavily used. Looking closely, I noticed small circles drawn in certain spots. When I examined them, I saw that one circle was located just east of London, right where his new estate is. The other two circles were over Exeter and Whitby, a town on the Yorkshire coast.

[P053] Almost an hour passed before the Count returned. "Aha!" he said. "Still looking at your books? Good! But you shouldn't work all the time. Come, I've been told your supper is ready." He took my arm, and we walked into the next room, where a wonderful dinner was waiting on the table. The Count apologized again, saying he had eaten while he was out. But just like the night before, he sat with me and talked while I ate. After dinner, I smoked a cigar like the previous evening, and the Count stayed with me, chatting and asking questions about every topic imaginable for hours on end. I knew it was getting incredibly late, but I didn't say anything because I felt obligated to do whatever my host wanted. I wasn't sleepy since my long nap yesterday had energized me, but I couldn't ignore the sudden chill that always hits just before dawn—it's somewhat like the shifting of the tide. People say that those who are near death usually pass away right at the break of dawn or when the tide turns; anyone who has ever stayed awake all night, feeling exhausted and stuck in one place, can easily believe that after experiencing this sudden change in the air. Suddenly, the sharp, almost supernaturally loud crow of a rooster echoed through the crisp morning air. Count Dracula jumped to his feet and said:

[P054] "Well, morning is here again! I'm so inconsiderate for keeping you up this late. You need to make your stories about my beloved new country of England a little less fascinating, so I don't lose track of time!" With a polite bow, he quickly left the room.

[P055] I went into my own room and closed the curtains, but there wasn't much to see anyway. My window looked out into the courtyard, and all I could see was the sky slowly turning a warm, bright gray. So, I closed the curtains again and wrote down everything that happened today.

[P056_057] May 8th - While writing in this journal, I started to worry that I was including too much detail. But now, I'm incredibly glad I documented everything from the very beginning. There is something so profoundly bizarre about this place and everything in it that I can't help but feel terrified. I wish I were safely away from here, or that I had never come at all. Maybe this strange nocturnal lifestyle is just messing with my head; I wish that was the only problem! If I just had one person to talk to, I think I could handle it, but there's absolutely no one. I only have the Count to talk to, and him!—I'm terrified that I might be the only actual living soul in this entire place. Let me try to be as rational and factual as possible; it will help me keep my sanity, and I cannot let my imagination take over. If I lose control of my mind, I'm doomed. Let me clearly state exactly what my situation is—or at least what it appears to be.

[P058] I only slept for a few hours before waking up, feeling like I couldn't rest any longer. I hung my small shaving mirror by the window and was just about to start shaving. Suddenly, I felt a hand on my shoulder and heard the Count's voice say, "Good morning." I jumped, completely shocked that I hadn't seen him coming, especially since the mirror reflected the entire room behind me. My sudden movement caused me to nick myself slightly with the razor, but I didn't notice it right away. After returning the Count's greeting, I looked back at the mirror to figure out how I could have missed him. This time, there was absolutely no mistake: the man was standing right next to me, and I could clearly see him over my shoulder. But he had no reflection in the mirror! The entire room behind me was visible, but there was no sign of a man in it, except for me. This was terrifying. Coming right after so many other bizarre events, it really intensified the vague sense of dread I always feel whenever the Count is nearby. But right then, I noticed that my shaving cut was bleeding slightly, and blood was trickling down my chin. I put the razor down and turned halfway around to look for some bandages. When the Count saw my face, his eyes flared with an almost demonic rage, and he suddenly lunged for my throat. I yanked myself backward, and his hand brushed against the string of beads holding my crucifix. The cross had an immediate effect on him; his rage vanished so instantly that I could barely believe it had ever been there.

[P059] "Be careful," he warned, "be very careful not to cut yourself. It's much more dangerous in this country than you realize." Then, he grabbed the shaving mirror and continued: "And this awful thing is what caused the problem. It's a disgusting symbol of human vanity. Get rid of it!" With one pull of his terrifyingly strong hand, he yanked open the heavy window and hurled the mirror outside. It shattered into a thousand pieces on the courtyard stones far below. Then, he left the room without another word. This is incredibly frustrating, because I have no idea how I'm supposed to shave now, unless I use the reflection in my watch case or the bottom of my shaving mug, which is luckily made of metal.

[P060] When I went into the dining room, breakfast was waiting, but the Count was nowhere to be found. So, I ate alone. It's really weird that I still haven't seen the Count eat or drink anything. He must be a very strange man! After breakfast, I decided to do a little exploring around the castle. I went out to the staircase and found a room facing south. The view was absolutely spectacular, and the room offered the perfect vantage point to appreciate it. The castle is built right on the edge of a massive cliff. If you dropped a stone from this window, it would fall a thousand feet before hitting the ground! As far as I could see, there was an endless ocean of green treetops, broken occasionally by deep ravines or chasms. Here and there, I could see rivers shining like silver threads as they wound their way through deep gorges in the forests.

[P061] But I'm not in the mood to talk about beautiful scenery. After enjoying the view, I tried to explore further. I found doors, doors, and more doors everywhere, but every single one was locked and bolted. Aside from the windows in the castle walls, there is absolutely no way out of here.

[P062] This castle is a literal prison, and I am its prisoner!
