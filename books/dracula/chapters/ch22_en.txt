[P001] Chapter 22

[P002] Jonathan Harker's Journal

[P003a] October 3rd.—I am writing this diary because I feel I might go mad if I don't do something. It is currently six o'clock, and we are scheduled to gather in the study in half an hour to grab a bite to eat. Dr. Van Helsing and Dr. Seward have agreed that we cannot work at our best if we skip a meal. And heaven knows, we will absolutely need to be at our best today.

[P003b] I must keep writing at every available opportunity, because if I allow myself time to pause and think, I fear I will be overwhelmed by dread. I need to record everything, whether it seems trivial or crucial. In the end, it might just be those small, trivial details that provide us with the greatest insights. Regardless of whether those insights are big or small, they could not possibly drag Mina and me into a worse situation than the one we are currently facing. Nevertheless, we must hold on to our trust and hope.

[P003c] Poor Mina, with tears streaming down her lovely cheeks, just told me that it is precisely during such hardships and trials that our faith is put to the test. She said we must keep believing and that God will support us until the very end. The end! Oh my God! What kind of end could she mean?... Well, back to work! I must work!

[P004] When Dr. Van Helsing and Dr. Seward returned from checking on poor Renfield (the asylum patient), we had a grave discussion about our next steps. First, Dr. Seward informed us that when he and Dr. Van Helsing went down to the room below, they found Renfield crumpled on the floor. His face was entirely bruised and crushed, and his neck bones were broken.

[P005a] Dr. Seward asked the orderly who was on duty in the hallway if he had heard anything. The orderly confessed that he had been sitting down and dozing off, but was awakened by loud voices in the room. He then heard Renfield loudly shout, "God! God! God!" several times. After that, there was the sound of a heavy fall, and when he entered the room, he found Renfield lying face down on the floor, exactly as the doctors had discovered him. When Dr. Van Helsing asked if he had heard multiple "voices" or just a single "voice," the orderly replied that he couldn't be certain.

[P005b] Initially, it sounded like two people, but since no one else was in the room, he assumed it must have been only one. He stated he could swear under oath, if necessary, that the patient was the one who spoke the word "God." Once we were alone, Dr. Seward told us that he did not want to investigate the matter any further.

[P005c] He had to consider the potential for a coroner's inquest, and presenting a truth that no one would believe would be entirely unhelpful. Given the circumstances, he believed he could issue a death certificate citing accidental death from falling out of bed, based on the orderly's testimony. Even if the coroner demanded a formal inquest, it would inevitably lead to the same conclusion.

[P006a] When we began to discuss our next steps, our very first decision was that Mina (Jonathan's wife) must be kept fully informed. We agreed that absolutely nothing should be hidden from her, no matter how painful it might be. She herself agreed that this was the wisest course of action. It was truly heartbreaking to see her displaying such bravery while simultaneously drowning in deep sorrow and despair. "There must be no more secrets," she said. "Alas! We have already been through too much.

[P006b] Furthermore, there is nothing in this world that could inflict more pain upon me than what I have already endured—and what I am suffering right now! Whatever happens next, it must bring me either new hope or new courage!" Dr. Van Helsing stared at her intently as she spoke, and then said, suddenly but softly:

[P007] "But my dear Madam Mina, aren't you afraid? Not for yourself, but of the impact you might have on others because of you, after that terrible incident (having her blood drained by the vampire)?" Her facial expression hardened, but her eyes shone with the devotion of a martyr as she replied:

[P008] "No! Because my mind is made up!"

[P009] "Made up to do what?" he asked gently into the dead silence of the room. Each of us had a vague idea of what she meant. Her answer came straightforwardly and plainly, as if she were merely stating a fact:

[P010] "Because if I ever detect within myself—and I will be watching very closely for it—any sign that I might harm someone I love, I will die!"

[P011] "You wouldn't kill yourself, would you?" he asked, his voice hoarse.

[P012] "I would, if I didn't have a friend who loved me enough to spare me such terrible pain and such a desperate effort!" She looked at him with deep meaning as she spoke. He had been sitting, but he now stood up, approached her, and placed his hand solemnly on her head.

[P013] "My child, there is such a person, if it were truly for your own good. For my part, I could consider it my duty before God to provide you with such euthanasia, even right at this moment, if it were the best option. Or rather, if it were safe! But my child——" He choked up for a moment, swallowing a great sob before continuing:

[P014a] "There are people here who would stand between you and death. You must not die. You must not die by anyone's hand, and especially not by your own. You must not die until the monster who defiled your pure life is truly dead. Because as long as he remains a living 'Undead' (vampire), your death would simply turn you into the exact same creature he is. No, you must live! You must fight and struggle to survive, even if death seems like an indescribable blessing.

[P014b] You must fight Death itself, whether it comes to you in pain or joy, by day or night, in safety or in peril! By your living soul, I command you not to die—and not even to think of death—until this great evil has passed." Poor, lovely Mina turned pale as a ghost, trembling in shock like quicksand shuddering under the incoming tide. We were all stunned into silence, entirely helpless. Eventually, she regained some composure, turned to him, and reached out her hand, speaking sweetly but oh so sorrowfully:

[P015a] "I promise you, my dear friend. If God allows me to live, I will fight to do so, until this terrifying nightmare finally passes from me in His own good time." Because she was so good and brave, we all felt our resolve strengthen to work and endure for her sake, and we began to discuss our next moves.

[P015b] I told her that she should take charge of all the papers in the safe, as well as any documents, diaries, or phonograph records (voice recordings) we might use in the future, and continue to keep the records as she had before. She was glad to have a task to focus on—if the word "glad" can even be applied to such a grim situation.

[P016] As always, Dr. Van Helsing was thinking ahead of everyone else, and had prepared a precise sequence of actions for our task.

[P017a] "It is perhaps a stroke of luck," he said, "that we decided not to do anything with the dirt boxes stored at Carfax during our meeting after we visited the estate. If we had touched them, the Count would undoubtedly have guessed our plans and would have taken preemptive measures to block us from doing the same to his other boxes. But as it stands, he is unaware of our intentions. Furthermore, he most likely doesn't even know that we possess the ability to 'sterilize' his hideouts using the Sacred Wafer, rendering them useless to him.

[P017b] Now that we have a much better understanding of how these boxes are distributed, once we investigate the house in Piccadilly, we should be able to track down every last one of them. Therefore, today is our day; our hopes rely entirely on what we achieve today. The sun that rose above our sorrow this morning will protect us as it crosses the sky. Until it sets tonight, that monster is forced to remain in whatever form he currently occupies. He is restricted by his physical limitations. He cannot vanish into thin air or slip through cracks, holes, or crevices.

[P017c] If he wishes to pass through a doorway, he has to open the door just like a regular human being. This gives us today to locate all of his lairs and sterilize them. In doing so, even if we cannot catch and destroy him just yet, we will corner him, driving him into a situation where we can finally catch him and finish him off for good."

[P017d] At that moment, I leaped up, unable to contain my frustration. The thought that these precious minutes and seconds, laden with Mina's life and happiness, were slipping away while we simply sat and talked without taking action was unbearable. However, Van Helsing raised his hand in a warning gesture. "No, friend Jonathan," he said. "As your proverb says, sometimes 'the longest way round is the shortest way home.' Now is not the time to rush. When the time is right, we will act with desperate and furious speed.

[P017e] But think about it—the key to this entire situation is most likely in that house in Piccadilly. The Count might own multiple houses. He will have the purchase deeds, the keys, and other related items for them. He will have his writing paper and his checkbook. There are many personal belongings he must store somewhere, and what better place than a house that is centrally located, quiet, and accessible by the front or back doors at any hour without drawing attention, due to the heavy traffic? We need to go there and search the house.

[P017f] Once we discover what's inside, we will execute what our friend Arthur (Lord Godalming) refers to in hunting terms as 'stopping the earths,' effectively smoking the old fox out of his den. Don't you agree?"

[P018] "Then let's go immediately," I cried. "We are wasting precious, precious time!" The Professor, however, did not budge and simply asked:

[P019] "And exactly how are we supposed to get into that house in Piccadilly?"

[P020] "Any way we can!" I shouted. "We'll break in if we have to."

[P021] "And what about your police? Where will they be, and what will they say?"

[P022] I was taken aback, but I realized that if he wanted to delay, he certainly had a valid reason for it. I tried to speak as calmly as possible:

[P023] "Please don't delay any longer than necessary. You must know the absolute torture I am enduring."

[P024] "Ah, my child, I do know. I have absolutely no desire to add to your agony. But think about it—what can we realistically do until the rest of the world wakes up and gets moving? Our time will come then. I have thought long and hard about this, and it seems to me that the simplest approach is the best one. Right now, we want to enter the house, but we don't have a key, correct?" I nodded.

[P025] "Now, imagine you actually owned that house but couldn't get in. Assume you felt no guilt whatsoever about breaking in. What would you do?"

[P026] "I would hire a reputable locksmith and have him pick the lock for me."

[P027] "And your police—would they interfere?"

[P028] "Oh, no! Not if they believed the man was legitimately hired for the job."

[P029a] "Then," he said, looking at me sharply, "the only thing in question is the conscience of the employer, and whether the police believe that employer has good or bad intentions. Your police must be incredibly zealous and clever—oh, so clever!—at reading hearts, if they trouble themselves with such matters. No, no, my friend Jonathan. If you go and remove the locks from a hundred empty houses in this London of yours, or in any city in the world, as long as you do it in the customary manner and at the appropriate time of day, no one will interfere.

[P029b] I once read about a gentleman who owned a magnificent house in London. He went to Switzerland for a summer holiday for a few months and locked up his house. While he was away, a burglar broke a back window and got inside. The burglar then opened the front shutters wide and walked in and out the front door, right under the noses of the police. He then held an auction in the house, advertised it, and put up large signs. When the day came, he hired a prominent auctioneer and sold off all the actual owner's belongings.

[P029c] After that, he went to a builder and sold him the house itself, signing a contract stating the builder had to demolish it and clear away the debris within a certain timeframe. And your police and local authorities helped him every step of the way. When the real owner returned from his Swiss holiday, he found nothing but an empty hole where his house used to be. All of this happened because it was done 'en règle' (according to procedure). And we will follow procedure in our work, too.

[P029d] We will not go too early, when the police have little else to do and might find it suspicious. Instead, we will go after ten o'clock, when the streets are busy, and when such actions would seem perfectly normal if we were, indeed, the owners of the house."

[P030] I couldn't deny that he was absolutely right, and I noticed the terrible despair on Mina's face ease slightly. His wise counsel offered a glimmer of hope. Van Helsing continued:

[P031] "Once we are inside that house, we might uncover more clues. At the very least, some of us can stay there while the others track down the locations of the remaining dirt boxes in Bermondsey and Mile End."

[P032] Lord Godalming stood up. "I can be of assistance here," he offered. "I will wire my staff to have horses and carriages ready at the most convenient locations."

[P033] "Look here, old fellow," Morris interjected. "Having everything ready in case we need to ride is a brilliant idea. But don't you think a flashy carriage bearing your family crest would attract a bit too much attention in the backstreets of Walworth or Mile End? I think we'd be better off taking cabs when we head south or east, and we should probably leave them some distance away from the actual neighborhoods we are investigating."

[P034] "Friend Quincey is exactly right!" the Professor agreed. "His head is, as you say, 'level with the horizon' (practical and realistic). What we are attempting is very difficult, and we want to avoid drawing any attention to ourselves if possible."

[P035a] Mina began to take a growing interest in everything, and I was pleased to see that the urgency of our situation was helping her momentarily forget the terrible events of the previous night. She was incredibly pale—almost ghostly white—and she was so thin that her lips were drawn back, making her teeth appear somewhat prominent.

[P035b] I didn't mention this last detail to her, fearing it would cause her unnecessary distress, but it made my blood run cold to remember what had happened to poor Lucy when the Count had sucked her blood (she had transformed into a vampire and her canines grew). There was no sign yet of Mina's teeth growing sharper, but very little time had passed, and there was still plenty of time for fear.

[P036] When we began discussing the sequence of our tasks and how to deploy our group, new uncertainties arose. We eventually agreed that before heading to Piccadilly, we should first destroy the Count's nearby lair (the dirt boxes at Carfax). This way, even if he discovered our actions too soon, we would still be a step ahead in our mission to destroy his safe havens. Furthermore, encountering him in his purely physical form, when he was at his weakest, might provide us with new clues.

[P037a] Regarding our deployment, the Professor suggested that after visiting Carfax, we should all enter the house in Piccadilly. The two doctors and I would stay there, while Lord Godalming and Quincey tracked down and destroyed the lairs in Walworth and Mile End. The Professor argued that it was possible, if not entirely probable, that the Count might show up in Piccadilly during the day, giving us a chance to confront him then and there. At the very least, we could pursue him together. I strongly objected to this plan regarding my own participation.

[P037b] I stated that my mind was firmly made up: I intended to stay behind and protect Mina. However, Mina refused to listen to my objections. She pointed out that there might be legal matters where my expertise could be useful, or that among the Count's papers, I might find clues I could decipher based on my experiences in Transylvania. Furthermore, she argued, we needed every ounce of strength we could muster to face the Count's extraordinary power. I had to yield because Mina's resolve was absolute. She declared that her last hope was for us all to work together.

[P037c] "As for me," she said, "I am not afraid. Things are already as bad as they can possibly be, and whatever happens next must surely carry some element of hope or comfort. Go, my husband! If God wishes it, He can protect me just as well when I am alone as when someone is with me." Hearing this, I jumped up and cried, "Then, in God's name, let's go at once! We are losing time. The Count might arrive in Piccadilly earlier than we expect."

[P038] "Not at all!" Van Helsing said, raising his hand to stop me.

[P039] "But why?" I asked.

[P040] "Have you forgotten," he replied, actually smiling, "that he feasted heavily last night and will be sleeping late?"

[P041a] How could I forget! How could I—how could any of us—ever forget that horrifying scene! Mina struggled valiantly to maintain a brave face, but the pain overwhelmed her. She covered her face with her hands, shuddering and moaning. Van Helsing hadn't meant to remind her of the terrifying ordeal. He had simply been so absorbed in his intellectual reasoning that he momentarily forgot about her presence and her trauma. When he realized what he had said, he was appalled by his thoughtlessness and rushed to comfort her. "Oh, Madam Mina," he said, "dear, dear Madam Mina, alas!

[P041b] I, who revere you so deeply, have spoken so thoughtlessly. These foolish old lips and this stupid old head of mine don't deserve forgiveness, but you will forget it, won't you?" He leaned down beside her as he spoke. She took his hand, looked at him through her tears, and said hoarsely:

[P042] "No, I won't forget, because it is right that I remember. And alongside it, I have so many sweet memories of your kindness that I accept it all together. Now, you must all leave soon. Breakfast is ready, and we must all eat so that we have the strength we need."

[P043] Breakfast was an awkward affair for all of us. We tried our best to be cheerful and encourage one another, and surprisingly, Mina was the brightest and most upbeat among us. Once we finished, Van Helsing stood up and said:

[P044a] "Now, my dear friends, we embark on our terrible mission. Are we all armed, just as we were on the night we first infiltrated our enemy's lair? Armed against spiritual attacks as well as physical ones?" We all assured him that we were. "Excellent. Now, Madam Mina, you will be absolutely safe here until sunset, no matter what happens. And we will return before then—if... No, we shall return! But before we leave, I must ensure you are protected against any personal attack.

[P044b] While you were downstairs, I prepared your room by placing items we know are effective (like garlic and crucifixes) so that He cannot enter. Now, let me protect you personally. In the name of the Father, the Son, and... I touch this piece of the Sacred Wafer to your forehead."

[P045a] A horrifying scream erupted, nearly freezing our hearts. The moment he pressed the Wafer to Mina's forehead, it seared her flesh—burning into it as if it were a piece of white-hot metal. My poor darling's brain comprehended the meaning of this (that she was tainted by the vampire's evil) just as quickly as her nerves registered the searing pain. The agonizing combination of the two overwhelmed her fragile state, bursting forth in that dreadful scream.

[P045b] But she quickly processed the reality of it; the echo of her scream was still hanging in the air when she reacted, collapsing to her knees on the floor in profound humiliation and despair. Pulling her beautiful hair over her face, much like a leper hiding behind a cloak in ancient times, she wailed:

[P046] "Unclean! I am unclean! Even the Almighty rejects my polluted flesh! I must carry this mark of shame on my forehead until Judgment Day." We all froze. I threw myself down beside her, overwhelmed by helpless grief, wrapped my arms around her, and held her tightly. For a few minutes, our sorrowful hearts beat as one, while the friends surrounding us looked away, weeping silently. Then Van Helsing turned and spoke with such profound gravity that I couldn't help but feel he was somehow divinely inspired, speaking truths beyond his normal understanding:

[P047a] "It may be that you must bear this mark until God Himself sees fit to remove it—as He most certainly will on Judgment Day, when He rights all the wrongs of the world and of His children. And oh, Madam Mina, my dear, dear child, may those of us who love you be there to witness the moment when that red scar—the sign that God knows what you have endured—fades away, leaving your forehead as pure as the heart we know you possess.

[P047b] As surely as we live, that scar will vanish when God decides it is right to lift this heavy burden from us. Until then, we must bear our cross, just as His Son did in obedience to His Will. Perhaps we are chosen instruments of His divine purpose, ascending to fulfill His commands through suffering and shame, through tears and blood, through doubts and fears, and through everything that separates God from man."

[P048a] His words offered hope and comfort, guiding us toward a peaceful resignation. Mina and I both felt it.

[P048b] Simultaneously, we each took one of the old man's hands, bowed our heads, and kissed it. Then, without a word, we all knelt down together, joined hands, and swore to remain true to one another. We men pledged to lift the veil of sorrow from the woman we each loved in our own way, and we prayed for help and guidance in the terrifying task that lay ahead.

[P049] It was time to go. I bid farewell to Mina—a parting neither of us will forget until our dying days—and we set out.

[P050] I had firmly resolved one thing in my mind: if we ultimately discovered that Mina was doomed to become a vampire, she would not enter that dark and terrible unknown alone. I suppose this is how, in ancient times, one vampire created many. Just as their grotesque bodies could only find rest in sacred soil, the purest, holiest love acted as the recruiting sergeant for their macabre ranks.

[P051a] We entered Carfax without any trouble and found everything exactly as it was during our first visit. It was difficult to believe that such a terrifying threat could exist amid such mundane surroundings, filled only with neglect, dust, and decay. If our minds hadn't been made up, and if we weren't spurred on by horrific memories, we would scarcely have been able to proceed. We found no documents or any signs that the house was being used, and the massive boxes in the old chapel looked identical to when we last saw them. Dr. Van Helsing spoke to us solemnly as we stood before them:

[P052a] "And now, my friends, we have a duty to fulfill here. The Count brought this earth, which is steeped in sacred memories, from a distant land for a foul purpose. We must sterilize it. He chose this earth specifically because it was holy. Therefore, we will defeat him with his own weapon by making it even holier. It was originally sanctified for human use, but now we shall sanctify it for God." As he spoke, he pulled a screwdriver and a wrench from his bag, and we quickly pried open the top of one of the crates.

[P052b] The earth smelled stale and musty, but we hardly noticed, as our attention was entirely focused on the Professor. Taking a piece of the Sacred Wafer from his box, he placed it reverently onto the soil. Then, with our help, he closed the lid and began screwing it back in place.

[P053a] One by one, we treated each of the enormous boxes in the exact same manner.

[P053b] We left them looking exactly as we had found them, but now, every single one contained a piece of the Host.

[P054] When we closed the door behind us, the Professor said solemnly:

[P055] "A great deal is already accomplished. If we can be just as successful with the remaining boxes, then perhaps the sunset this evening will shine on Madam Mina's forehead, leaving it as white as ivory and completely free of any stain!"

[P056a] As we walked across the lawn toward the station to catch our train, we could see the front of the asylum. I looked up eagerly and spotted Mina in the window of my room. I waved to her and nodded to signal that our mission there had been a success. She nodded back to show she understood. The last thing I saw was her waving goodbye. It was with a heavy heart that we headed to the station and barely managed to catch the train, which was steaming into the platform just as we arrived.

[P056b] I am writing this diary on the train.

[P057] *       *       *       *       *

[P059] Piccadilly, 12:30 p.m.—Just before we arrived at Fenchurch Street Station, Lord Godalming turned to me and said:

[P060a] "Quincey and I will go find a locksmith. It would be best if you didn't come with us, just in case there's any trouble. Under the circumstances, breaking into an empty house doesn't seem like a big deal for us, but you are a solicitor. The Incorporated Law Society might argue that you should have known better and held yourself to a higher standard." I protested, insisting that I didn't want to avoid sharing the danger, or even the potential disgrace, but he continued: "Besides, a smaller group will attract much less attention.

[P060b] My title (Lord Godalming) will easily reassure the locksmith, and any policeman who might happen to stroll by. You should take Jack (Dr. Seward) and the Professor to Green Park. Find a spot where you can keep an eye on the house. Once you see the door open and the locksmith leave, all three of you can cross the street. We'll be watching for you and will let you in."

[P061a] "That is excellent advice!" Van Helsing agreed, so we didn't argue further. Godalming and Morris quickly hailed a cab and hurried off, while the rest of us followed in another. We got out at the corner of Arlington Street and casually strolled into Green Park. My heart began to pound as I saw the house upon which so much of our hope depended, standing grim, silent, and apparently deserted among its more vibrant and well-kept neighbors. We found a bench with a clear view and sat down, lighting up cigars to blend in and attract as little attention as possible.

[P061b] As we waited for the others to arrive, the minutes seemed to drag by with agonizing slowness.

[P062a] Finally, we spotted a four-wheeled cab pull up. Lord Godalming and Morris stepped out in a relaxed, leisurely manner, followed by a stocky, working-class man who climbed down from the driver's box carrying a woven rush basket full of tools. Morris paid the cab driver, who touched his hat in thanks and drove off. Godalming and the locksmith walked up the steps together, and Godalming pointed out the lock he needed opened.

[P062b] The workman casually took off his coat and hung it on one of the iron spikes of the railing, exchanging a few words with a policeman who happened to be strolling past at that exact moment. The policeman nodded in agreement, and the man knelt down, placing his tool bag beside him. Rummaging through it, he selected a few tools and laid them out neatly on the ground. He then stood up, peered into the keyhole, blew into it to clear out any dust, and turned back to Godalming and Morris to make a comment.

[P062c] Lord Godalming smiled, and the locksmith held up a large ring of keys. Selecting one, he inserted it into the lock, probing and feeling his way around inside. After fumbling with it for a moment, he tried a second key, and then a third. Suddenly, with a gentle push, the door swung open, and all three men stepped into the hallway. We remained seated on our bench. I was puffing furiously on my cigar out of sheer nervous tension, but Van Helsing's had gone completely out.

[P062d] We waited patiently until we saw the locksmith emerge and bring his tool bag inside. A moment later, he reappeared, holding the door ajar and steadying it with his knee while he fitted a newly cut key into the lock. He handed the finished key to Lord Godalming, who pulled out his purse and paid him. The man touched his hat respectfully, gathered his tools, put his coat back on, and walked away. Not a single person on the street paid the slightest bit of attention to the entire routine transaction.

[P063] Once the locksmith was completely out of sight, the three of us crossed the street and knocked on the door. It was opened immediately by Quincey Morris, with Lord Godalming standing right beside him, lighting a fresh cigar.

[P064a] "The place smells absolutely vile," Lord Godalming remarked as we stepped inside. It truly did smell disgusting—exactly like the decaying stench of the old chapel at Carfax—and based on our previous experiences, it was glaringly obvious that the Count had been using the place frequently. We began to explore the house, making sure to stick together in case of a sudden attack. We knew we were dealing with a powerful and cunning enemy, and we still had no idea if the Count was currently inside.

[P064b] In the dining room, located at the back of the hallway, we discovered eight boxes of earth. Only eight boxes out of the nine we were searching for! Our mission was not complete, and it never would be until we located that missing box. First, we opened the window shutters. The window looked out over a narrow, stone-paved yard toward the blank, windowless wall of a stable, which had been designed with a pointed roof to look like the front of a miniature house. Because the stable had no windows, we didn't have to worry about anyone spying on us.

[P064c] We wasted no time inspecting the crates. Using the tools we had brought, we pried them open one by one and treated them exactly as we had treated the ones in the old chapel (placing a piece of the Sacred Wafer inside). It was now clear that the Count was not in the house, so we began searching for any of his personal belongings.

[P065a] After quickly checking the rest of the rooms, from the basement up to the attic, we concluded that the dining room was the only place containing anything that might belong to the Count, so we returned to examine those items very carefully. They were spread out in a state of organized clutter across the large dining room table. There was a large bundle containing the title deeds for the Piccadilly house, as well as the purchase deeds for the houses in Mile End and Bermondsey. There was also writing paper, envelopes, pens, and ink.

[P065b] All of these were covered with a thin sheet of wrapping paper to protect them from the dust. In addition, we found a clothes brush, a hairbrush and comb, and a jug alongside a washbasin. The basin contained dirty water that was tinged red, as if stained with blood. Finally, there was a small pile of various keys of all shapes and sizes, which likely belonged to the other properties. After examining this final discovery, Lord Godalming and Quincey Morris carefully copied down the addresses for the houses in the East and South (Mile End and Bermondsey).

[P065c] They took the large bunch of keys and set off to destroy the earth boxes at those locations. The rest of us are waiting here, with as much patience as we can muster, for their return—or for the arrival of the Count.
