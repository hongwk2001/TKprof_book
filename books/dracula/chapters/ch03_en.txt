[P001] ﻿CHAPTER III

[P002] JONATHAN HARKER'S JOURNAL—continued

[P003a] When I realized I was a prisoner, a wave of sheer panic washed over me. I ran up and down the stairs, trying every door and looking out every window I could find. After a while, however, the stark reality of my helplessness overwhelmed all my other emotions. Looking back on it hours later, I think I must have lost my mind for a brief time, because I acted exactly like a rat caught in a trap.

[P003b] But once I truly accepted that I was powerless, I sat down as quietly as I have ever done in my life and began to figure out my best course of action. I am still thinking, and I haven't reached a solid conclusion yet. Only one thing is certain: there is no point in letting the Count know what I am thinking. He knows perfectly well that I am locked in, and since he orchestrated it himself—undoubtedly for his own reasons—he would only lie to me if I confronted him with the truth.

[P003c] As far as I can tell, my only strategy is to keep my knowledge and my fears strictly to myself while staying extremely observant. I know I am either letting my own fears trick me like a child, or I am in a genuinely desperate situation. If it is the latter, I will need every ounce of my wits to survive.

[P004a] I had barely settled on this plan when I heard the massive front door slam shut below, signaling that the Count had returned. He didn't come directly to the library, so I crept to my own room and caught him making the bed. This was strange, but it merely confirmed what I had suspected all along: there are absolutely no servants in this house. Later, when I peeked through the hinge crack of the dining room door and saw him setting the table himself, my suspicions were validated.

[P004b] If he is doing all these menial chores himself, it proves there is no one else around to do them. This realization terrified me. If the castle is empty, the Count himself must have driven the carriage that brought me here. That is a horrifying thought, because if he was the driver, how was he able to control a pack of wild wolves just by silently raising his hand? Why were all the people at Bistritz and on the coach so terrified for my safety?

[P004c] What was the real meaning behind their gifts of a crucifix, garlic, wild rose, and mountain ash—items meant to ward off evil? Bless the kind woman who hung this crucifix around my neck! It gives me incredible comfort and strength whenever I hold it. It is strange that an object I was raised to view as inappropriate idolatry has become such a lifeline in a time of deep loneliness and distress. Is there genuine power within the object itself, or does it simply act as a physical anchor for memories of human sympathy and comfort?

[P004d] If I get the chance, I need to look into this and make sense of it. In the meantime, I have to learn everything I can about Count Dracula to better understand my situation. If I steer the conversation correctly tonight, he might talk about himself. I just have to be incredibly careful not to raise his suspicions.

[P004e] * * * * *

[P005_006] Midnight.—I just had a lengthy conversation with the Count. I asked him a few questions about Transylvanian history, and he became incredibly passionate about the topic. When discussing historical events, people, and especially battles, he spoke as if he had personally witnessed them all. He later explained this by saying that for an Eastern European nobleman, the pride, glory, and fate of his family name are inseparable from his own. Whenever he talked about his family, he always used the word "we," speaking almost exclusively in the plural, like royalty.

[P006b] I wish I could transcribe his exact words because I found it absolutely captivating. His stories felt like the entire history of the country bundled together. As he spoke, he grew increasingly animated, pacing the room, pulling at his large white mustache, and gripping the furniture so tightly it seemed he might crush it. I will try to write down one specific speech as accurately as I can remember, because it effectively summarizes the history of his bloodline:

[P007a] "We Szekelys have every right to be proud, because the blood of many fierce, conquering races flows through our veins. Here, in the mixing pot of European cultures, the Ugric tribes brought down from Iceland the warrior spirit bestowed upon them by Thor and Odin. Their Berserkers unleashed such devastating fury across the coasts of Europe, Asia, and Africa that people believed actual werewolves had arrived. When they settled here, they encountered the Huns, whose savage warfare had burned across the earth like a living fire.

[P007b] The slaughtered nations believed the Huns carried the blood of ancient witches from Scythia who had bred with desert demons. Fools, all of them! What devil or witch could ever match the greatness of Attila the Hun, whose very blood runs in these veins?" He raised his arms dramatically. "Is it any surprise that we were a race of conquerors? Is it surprising that we proudly drove back the massive armies of Magyars, Lombards, Avars, Bulgars, and Turks when they flooded our borders?

[P007c] It is no wonder that when the Hungarian hero Arpad and his legions marched across the land, he found us already holding the frontier. When the Hungarians pushed eastward, the victorious Magyars recognized the Szekelys as their kin, entrusting us for centuries with the defense of the Turkish border. It was a relentless duty, because as the Turks say, 'Water may sleep, but the enemy never does.' Among the Four Nations, who was faster to answer the call to war or embrace the bloody sword of battle than us?

[P007d] When the flags of Wallachia and Hungary fell to the Ottoman Empire at the tragic battle of Kosovo, who avenged our nation's greatest shame? It was a leader of my own bloodline who crossed the Danube and defeated the Turks on their own soil! He was a true Dracula! It was a tragedy that after he fell, his unworthy brother sold our people out to the Turks, bringing upon us the humiliation of slavery. And wasn't it this same Dracula who inspired his descendant, centuries later, to repeatedly launch armies across the great river into Turkish territory?

[P007e] Even when he was beaten back and forced to return alone from battlefields where his men were slaughtered, he kept attacking because he knew he was the only one capable of ultimate victory! People claimed he was selfish. Bah! What good are peasants without a leader? How can a war end without the brains and heart to direct it? Later, when we finally threw off Hungarian rule after the Battle of Mohács, the Dracula bloodline stood among the leaders, because our spirit simply refused to be subjugated.

[P007f] Ah, young man, the Szekelys—with the Draculas acting as their heart, brain, and sword—have a legacy that upstart families like the Hapsburgs and Romanoffs could never dream of matching. But the days of war are over. Blood is far too precious in these days of shameful peace, and the glory of the great warrior races is nothing more than a story from the past."

[P008a] It was nearly morning by this time, and we went to bed. (Note to self: this diary feels terrifyingly like the start of an "Arabian Nights" tale, where every story must abruptly stop at dawn—or like the ghost of Hamlet's father vanishing at cockcrow.)

[P008b] * * * * *

[P009_010] 12 May.—Let me start with the facts—plain, undeniable facts backed up by books and documents. I must be careful not to mix them up with personal experiences, which rely entirely on my own fallible observation or memory. Yesterday evening, when the Count emerged from his room, he started firing questions at me about legal matters and specific business practices. I had spent a mind-numbing day reading and, just to keep my brain active, had reviewed some of the legal precedents I studied back at Lincoln's Inn.

[P010b] There was a clear, methodical pattern to the Count's questioning, so I am going to try to record it in order; this information might prove useful to me at some point.

[P011a] First, he asked if a person in England could legally retain two or more solicitors. I told him he could have a dozen if he wanted, but that it was foolish to have more than one solicitor handle a single transaction. Only one could act at a time, and switching between them would inevitably damage his own interests. He seemed to grasp this completely.

[P011b] Then he asked if it would be practical to have one solicitor handle banking and a completely different one manage shipping, especially if local expertise was needed in a port city far from the banking solicitor's office. I asked him to clarify his scenario so I wouldn't accidentally give him bad advice, and he explained:

[P012a] "Let me illustrate. Our mutual friend, Mr. Peter Hawkins, who lives under the shadow of the beautiful cathedral in Exeter—quite far from London—purchased my London estate for me through you. Excellent! Now, let me be frank so you don't find it strange that I hired someone so far from London instead of a local resident. My reasoning was simple: I wanted to ensure that no local biases or interests were served, only my own.

[P012b] A London solicitor might have personal connections or friends to favor, so I looked further afield to find an agent whose sole focus would be my business. Now, suppose I—a man with many ongoing ventures—want to ship goods to ports like Newcastle, Durham, Harwich, or Dover. Wouldn't it be much easier to simply consign the shipments directly to an agent working in those specific ports?" I told him that while that would certainly be easy, English solicitors have a robust agency system. This means local work can easily be delegated to local professionals by any solicitor.

[P012c] Therefore, a client could simply place all his affairs in the hands of one trusted man, who would then ensure all the client's wishes were carried out seamlessly without any extra hassle.

[P012d] "But," he countered, "I would be completely within my rights to manage those local agents myself. Isn't that correct?"

[P013_014] "Of course," I replied. "Businessmen who don't want any single person knowing the full extent of their affairs do that all the time."

[P015a] "Good!" he said. He then launched into a detailed interrogation about the logistics of shipping consignments, the necessary paperwork, and all the potential legal or practical pitfalls that could be avoided with proper planning. I explained everything as best as I could. When we finished, I was left with the distinct impression that he would have made a brilliant solicitor himself; there wasn't a single contingency he hadn't anticipated. For a man who had never set foot in England and didn't seem particularly involved in heavy commerce, his legal and business acumen was staggering.

[P015b] Once he was satisfied with his questions and I had double-checked the answers against the available reference books, he suddenly stood up and asked:

[P016] "Have you written to our friend Mr. Peter Hawkins or anyone else since your first letter?" It was with a heavy, bitter heart that I admitted I hadn't, explaining that I simply hadn't found an opportunity to send mail to anyone.

[P017] "Then write now, my young friend," he said, placing a heavy hand on my shoulder. "Write to our friend and anyone else you choose. And please tell them you will be staying with me for another month."

[P018] "Do you really need me to stay that long?" I asked, my blood running cold at the mere suggestion.

[P019] "I desire it greatly; in fact, I won't accept a refusal. When your employer agreed to send someone on his behalf, the clear understanding was that my needs would dictate the schedule. I have certainly paid well enough for the privilege. Isn't that right?"

[P020] What could I possibly do but bow in surrender? This was Mr. Hawkins's business, not mine, and I had to prioritize his interests over my own. Besides, as Count Dracula spoke, there was a dark, commanding intensity in his eyes and posture that brutally reminded me I was his prisoner. Even if I wanted to object, I had no real choice. The Count immediately sensed his victory in my submission and saw the trapped panic on my face. He capitalized on it instantly, using his smooth, undeniably forceful tone:

[P021a] "I must ask, my good young friend, that you write of nothing but business in these letters. I'm sure your friends will be thrilled to hear that you are well and looking forward to coming home. Isn't that right?" As he spoke, he handed me three sheets of paper and three envelopes. They were made of the thinnest, most translucent foreign stationary. Looking at the paper, and then up at his quiet smile—noting those sharp canine teeth resting over his red lower lip—I understood his unspoken threat perfectly.

[P021b] I had to be extremely careful about what I wrote, because he was undoubtedly going to read it. I decided to write only generic, formal notes for now. I plan to write fully detailed, secret letters to Mr. Hawkins and Mina later. I can write to Mina in shorthand, which will baffle the Count if he manages to intercept it. After I finished writing my two brief letters, I sat quietly reading a book while the Count drafted several notes of his own, frequently consulting books on his desk.

[P021c] When he finished, he scooped up my letters, placed them with his, and packed away his writing supplies. The absolute second the door clicked shut behind him, I lunged across the desk to inspect the letters he had placed face-down. I felt no guilt about snooping; given my desperate circumstances, I feel completely justified in doing whatever it takes to protect myself.

[P022a] One letter was addressed to Samuel F. Billington at No. 7, The Crescent, Whitby; another to Herr Leutner in Varna; the third to the bankers Coutts & Co. in London; and the fourth to Herren Klopstock & Billreuth, bankers in Budapest. The letters to Varna and Budapest were unsealed. I was just reaching out to peek at their contents when the door handle clicked. I threw myself back into my chair, barely having enough time to replace the letters and bury my face in my book before the Count walked back in, holding one more letter.

[P022b] He gathered the stack from the table, carefully stamped them, and turned to me:

[P023] "I hope you will forgive me, but I have a great deal of private work to attend to this evening. I trust you will find everything you need in your rooms." At the door, he turned back, paused for a moment, and said:

[P024a] "Let me give you a piece of advice, my dear young friend—no, let me give you a deadly serious warning. If you leave these rooms tonight, do not, under any circumstances, fall asleep in any other part of the castle. It is an ancient place with many dark memories, and terrible nightmares await those who sleep foolishly. Consider yourself warned! If you feel sleep overtaking you now or at any point, hurry back to your own bedroom or these specific rooms, where you will be safe.

[P024b] Because if you aren't careful about this..." He finished his sentence with a deeply unsettling gesture, rubbing his hands together as if washing them. I understood his meaning perfectly. My only question was whether any nightmare could possibly be worse than the horrific, unnatural web of dread and mystery that was slowly suffocating me while I was awake.

[P024c] * * * * *

[P025_026] Later.—I stand by what I wrote previously, only this time there is no doubt in my mind. I am not afraid to sleep anywhere, as long as the Count isn't there. I have hung the crucifix directly over the head of my bed. I imagine my rest will be freer from nightmares this way, and that is exactly where it is going to stay.

[P027a] When he left me, I returned to my room. After a little while, hearing no sound at all, I slipped out and climbed the stone stairs to a spot where I could look out toward the south. Staring out at the vast, open expanse—even though it was entirely inaccessible to me—gave me a small sense of freedom compared to the cramped darkness of the courtyard. Looking out at it, I truly felt the reality of my imprisonment, and I desperately wanted a breath of fresh air, even if it was just the night breeze.

[P027b] This nocturnal existence is starting to take its toll on me. It is destroying my nerves. I jump at my own shadow and am plagued by all sorts of horrific imaginations. God knows I have plenty of reasons to be terrified in this cursed place! I gazed out over the beautiful landscape, bathed in a soft yellow moonlight that made it almost as bright as day. In that gentle light, the distant hills seemed to melt away, and the shadows in the valleys and gorges were a deep, velvety black.

[P027c] The sheer beauty of the scene seemed to cheer me up; every breath I took brought a sense of peace and comfort. As I leaned out the window, my eye was caught by a movement one story below me and slightly to my left. Judging by the layout of the castle, I figured that must be where the windows of the Count's personal room were located. The window I was standing at was tall, deep, and divided by stone mullions.

[P027d] Although it was heavily weathered, the stonework was still intact, but the glass and wooden casing had clearly been missing for years. I shrank back behind the stone frame and carefully peeked out.

[P028a] What I saw was the Count's head emerging from his window. I couldn't see his face, but I instantly recognized him by the shape of his neck and the movement of his back and arms. I certainly couldn't mistake those hands, which I had had so many opportunities to study closely. At first, I was simply interested, and even a little amused—it's amazing how the smallest things can entertain a man when he's a prisoner. But my mild curiosity instantly morphed into pure repulsion and absolute terror when I saw his entire body slowly slide out of the window.

[P028b] He began to crawl down the sheer castle wall over that terrifying abyss, face down, with his black cloak spreading out around him like massive wings. For a second, I couldn't believe my own eyes. I tried to convince myself it was just a trick of the moonlight or some weird optical illusion caused by the shadows. But I kept watching, and it was no hallucination. I saw his fingers and toes grip the corners of the ancient stones, which had been stripped of their mortar by centuries of weather.

[P028c] Using every tiny projection and uneven surface, he moved rapidly downward, exactly like a lizard scurrying down a wall.

[P029a] What kind of man is this? Or what kind of creature is this that only wears the shape of a man? I feel the absolute dread of this horrific place crushing me. I am terrified—sick with fear—and there is no way out. I am surrounded by horrors that I don't even dare to think about....

[P029b] * * * * *

[P030_031] 15 May.—I have seen the Count leave in his lizard-like manner once again. He scrambled downwards diagonally, dropping about a hundred feet and moving quite a bit to the left, before vanishing into a hole or window. Once his head disappeared, I leaned far out to try and see where he went, but it was useless—the distance was too great and the angle was wrong. However, knowing he had left the castle gave me a perfect opportunity to explore further than I had previously dared. I hurried back to my room, grabbed a lamp, and began testing all the doors.

[P031b] As I expected, they were all locked, and the locks themselves felt relatively new. But I continued down the stone stairs all the way to the main entrance hall where I had first arrived. I found that I could easily pull back the heavy iron bolts and unhook the massive chains, but the main door was locked solid, and the key was nowhere to be found! That key has to be in the Count's room. I must keep a close watch, and if his door is ever unlocked, I have to steal that key and escape.

[P031c] I went back to thoroughly searching the various staircases and corridors, trying every door I could find. One or two small rooms near the hall were open, but they contained nothing except ancient, moth-eaten furniture buried in thick dust.

[P031d] Finally, at the very top of one staircase, I found a door that seemed locked at first, but gave slightly when I pushed it. I shoved harder and realized it wasn't actually locked; the resistance came from the heavy door having dropped on its sagging hinges, dragging against the floor. This was an opportunity I might not get again, so I put my back into it and, with a massive effort, forced it open enough to squeeze through.

[P031e] I was now in a wing of the castle situated further right and one story lower than the rooms I was familiar with. Looking through the windows, I could see that this suite of rooms stretched along the south face of the castle, with the windows in the final room facing both west and south. Outside both the south and west windows lay a sheer, massive precipice. The castle was built right on the edge of a towering rock, making it completely impregnable from three sides.

[P031f] Because arrows, slings, or cannons could never reach this high, the builders had installed massive windows here, allowing for light and comfort that would be impossible in the heavily fortified sections of the castle. To the west lay a massive valley, backed in the distance by jagged mountain peaks rising one after another. The steep rock faces were dotted with mountain ash and thorn bushes, their roots desperately clinging to the cracks and crevices.

[P031g] This section of the castle had clearly been the ladies' quarters in centuries past, because the furniture looked much more comfortable than anything else I had seen here. The windows were missing their curtains, and the bright yellow moonlight flooded through the diamond-shaped panes. The light was so bright I could actually make out colors, though it softened the thick layer of dust covering everything and somewhat hid the damage caused by time and moths. My oil lamp seemed almost useless against the brilliant moonlight, but I was still glad I brought it.

[P031h] There was a dreadful, oppressive loneliness in this place that chilled my heart and made me shake. Still, it was much better than hiding in the rooms I had grown to despise because of the Count's presence. After taking a few deep breaths to steady my nerves, a gentle sense of calm washed over me. So here I sit, at a small oak table where, centuries ago, some beautiful lady might have sat blushing while struggling to write a misspelled love letter.

[P031i] And I am using it to write my diary in modern shorthand, recording everything that has happened since my last entry. It is a harsh collision of the nineteenth century with the deep past. Yet, unless my senses are completely deceiving me, those ancient centuries possessed—and still possess—dark powers of their own that mere "modernity" can never truly kill.

[P031j] * * * * *

[P032_033] Later: the Morning of 16 May.—God help me keep my sanity, because that is all I have left. Safety, and even the illusion of safety, are completely gone. As long as I am trapped here, my only hope is that I don't lose my mind—assuming I haven't gone mad already. If I am still sane, then it is truly maddening to realize that out of all the foul, horrific things lurking in this nightmare, the Count is actually the least terrifying to me.

[P033b] He is the only one I can look to for any sort of safety, even if that safety only lasts as long as I am useful to him. Great God! Merciful God! I must stay calm, because panic will only lead to true madness. I am starting to see things that used to puzzle me in a terrifying new light. Until now, I never really understood what Shakespeare meant when he had Hamlet scream:

[P034] "My tablets! quick, my tablets! 'Tis meet that I put it down," etc.

[P035] Now, feeling as though my own mind has shattered, or as if I have suffered a shock that must inevitably lead to madness, I turn to my diary for comfort. The mechanical habit of recording exactly what happens should help soothe me.

[P036] The Count's mysterious warning terrified me at the time, but it terrifies me even more now that I think about it, because he now holds a dreadful power over me. I will be too afraid to even doubt anything he says!

[P037a] After writing in my diary and fortunately slipping my notebook and pen back into my pocket, I began to feel drowsy. The Count's warning echoed in my mind, but I found a perverse pleasure in disobeying it. The heavy weight of sleep was upon me, and with it came the stubbornness that always precedes it. The soft moonlight was soothing, and the massive, open landscape outside gave me a refreshing sense of freedom. I decided that I would not return to those gloomy, haunted rooms tonight.

[P037b] Instead, I would sleep right here—where, centuries ago, ladies had sat, sung, and lived sweet lives, their gentle hearts aching for the men fighting in relentless wars far away. I dragged a large couch out of its corner so that I could lie down and look out at the beautiful eastern and southern views. Without a single thought or care about the dust, I settled in to sleep. I suppose I must have fallen asleep; I truly hope I did. But I am afraid, because everything that happened next was horrifyingly real.

[P037c] It was so real that, sitting here now in the bright, full morning sunlight, I cannot bring myself to believe it was just a dream.

[P038a] I was not alone. The room was exactly the same, unchanged since I first entered. In the bright moonlight, I could clearly see my own footprints tracking through the thick, untouched dust on the floor. Standing in the moonlight across from me were three young women, dressed and carrying themselves like nobility. At first, I was certain I was dreaming, because even though the moonlight was shining directly behind them, they cast absolutely no shadows on the floor. They approached me, stared down at me for a long time, and then began whispering to one another.

[P038b] Two of them had dark hair, high arched noses just like the Count, and large, piercing dark eyes that seemed to glow almost red against the pale yellow moonlight. The third was incredibly fair-skinned, with thick, wavy masses of golden hair and eyes like pale sapphires. I somehow felt like I recognized her face, associating it with some hazy, dream-like terror, but in that moment, I couldn't remember how or where I had seen her. All three had dazzlingly white teeth that shone like pearls against their deep red, voluptuous lips.

[P038c] There was something about them that made me deeply uneasy—a confusing mix of intense longing and deadly, paralyzing fear. I felt a wicked, burning desire in my heart to have those red lips kiss me. I know I shouldn't write this down, in case Mina ever reads it and it breaks her heart, but it is the absolute truth. They whispered together, and then all three burst into a silvery, musical laugh. But it was a harsh sound, as if such a noise could never have genuinely come from soft human lips.

[P038d] It sounded exactly like the agonizing, tingling sweetness of crystal water glasses being played by a skilled hand. The fair-haired girl shook her head flirtatiously, while the other two urged her on. One said:

[P039] "Go on! You are first, and we will follow; it is your right to begin." The second added:

[P040] "He is young and strong; there are enough kisses for all of us." I lay perfectly still, peeking through my eyelashes in an agonizing mix of terror and delightful anticipation. The fair girl stepped forward and leaned over me until I could physically feel her breath on my face. On one level, her breath was sweet—as sweet as honey—and it sent the same thrilling tingle through my nerves as her voice. But beneath that sweetness lurked a bitter, foul stench, exactly like the metallic smell of fresh blood.

[P041a] I was too terrified to fully open my eyes, but I could see everything perfectly through my lashes. The girl dropped to her knees and hovered over me, simply gloating. Her movements possessed a deliberate, sensual quality that was both thrilling and deeply repulsive. As she arched her neck, she actually licked her lips like a wild animal. In the moonlight, I could clearly see the moisture glistening on her scarlet lips and red tongue as it slid over her sharp white teeth.

[P041b] She lowered her head further and further, until her lips moved past my mouth and chin, hovering right above my throat, preparing to latch on. Then she paused. I could hear the wet, churning sound of her tongue licking her teeth and lips, and I could feel her hot breath burning against my neck. Then the skin on my throat began to prickle, the way your flesh tingles when a hand reaching out to tickle you gets closer and closer.

[P041c] I felt the incredibly soft, shivering touch of her lips on the hyper-sensitive skin of my neck, followed by the hard, sharp pressure of two teeth, pressing lightly and pausing right there. I closed my eyes in a state of languorous ecstasy and waited—my heart pounding as I waited.

[P042a] But in that exact second, another sensation ripped through me like lightning. I suddenly became aware of the Count's presence, and he felt as though he was engulfed in a storm of absolute fury. My eyes snapped open involuntarily, and I saw his massive, powerful hand grab the fair-haired woman by her slender neck and violently yank her backward with the strength of a giant. Her blue eyes were wild with rage, her white teeth gnashed together furiously, and her pale cheeks burned red with sudden passion. But the Count!

[P042b] I never could have imagined such wrath and unholy fury, not even from the demons of hell. His eyes were literally blazing. The red light inside them was sickeningly bright, as if the actual flames of hellfire were roaring behind them. His face was deathly pale, the muscles drawn tight and hard as steel wire. His thick eyebrows, which met above his nose, now looked like a throbbing bar of white-hot iron. With a vicious sweep of his arm, he hurled the woman away from him, then gestured at the others as if physically beating them back.

[P042c] It was the exact same commanding, imperious motion I had seen him use to control the wolves outside. In a voice that was low—almost a whisper—yet seemed to slice through the air and echo around the room, he snarled:

[P043] "How dare you touch him? How dare any of you even look at him when I explicitly forbade it? Get back, all of you! This man belongs to me! Be warned: if you try to meddle with him, you will have to deal with me." The fair girl tossed her head and, with a crude, flirtatious laugh, turned back to answer him:

[P044] "You have never loved anyone! You don't even know how to love!" The other women joined in, and a harsh, soulless laughter—completely devoid of any real joy—rang through the room. The sound was so awful it nearly made me pass out; it sounded exactly like the terrible mirth of fiends. The Count stared intently at my face for a moment, then turned back to them and said in a soft whisper:

[P045] "Yes, I can love; you yourselves know that from the past. Isn't that true? Well, I promise you this: when I am completely finished with him, you may kiss him as much as you like. Now go! Get out! I must wake him up, because there is work to be done."

[P046a] "Are we getting nothing tonight?" one of the women asked with a low laugh, pointing to a bag the Count had thrown onto the floor. The bag was writhing, as if something alive was trapped inside it. In response, he simply nodded. One of the women lunged forward and tore it open. Unless my ears completely deceived me, I heard a gasp and a weak, muffled wail that sounded exactly like a half-smothered child. The women immediately swarmed over the bag while I watched, paralyzed by absolute horror.

[P046b] But as I stared, they vanished into thin air, taking the dreadful bag with them. There was no door anywhere near them, and it was impossible for them to have slipped past me without me noticing. They simply seemed to dissolve into the moonbeams and drift out the open window. For a brief second, I could see their dim, shadowy forms floating outside before they faded away entirely.

[P046_047] Then the sheer horror of everything I had seen overwhelmed my mind, and I collapsed into unconsciousness.
