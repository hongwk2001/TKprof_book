"What should we do now? Where on earth should we go to ask for help? We must give her another blood transfusion quickly. If we don't act right away, that poor child won't even last an hour. You are already exhausted, and I too am completely drained. Even if those maids muster up the courage to agree, we can't trust them with it. Where on earth can we find someone willing to give their blood for her?"

"What then about me?"

A familiar voice was heard from the sofa across the room. That voice brought me great relief and overwhelming joy. For it was the voice of Quincey Morris. Van Helsing looked startled and displeased by the sudden sound, but when I gladly called his name, his expression soon softened and sparkled with joy. "Quincey Morris!" I ran to him with my arms wide open.

"What brings you all the way here?" I cried out, feeling the warmth of his clasped hands.

"Because of Arthur."

He handed me a telegram.

"I haven't heard from Seward for three days, and my heart is burning with anxiety. I can't possibly leave my post. My father is still critically ill. Let me know how Lucy is right away. Do not delay. — Holmwood."

"It seems I arrived just in time. Just tell me what needs to be done."

Van Helsing strode over, looked straight into Quincey's eyes, held his hands, and said.

"When a woman is in trouble, there is nothing more precious than the blood of a brave man. You are a true man indeed. Even though the devil is trying his best to ensnare us, God sends the exact person needed in such a desperate moment."

We had to perform that gruesome surgery once again. I don't even want to recall that terrible process. Lucy had suffered a tremendous shock, and its aftermath was far more fatal than before. Even after a large blood transfusion, her body didn't respond as well as it used to. Her desperate struggle to not let go of the thread of life was so gruesome that it was hard to keep one's eyes open to watch. Fortunately, the functions of her heart and lungs slightly improved, and she gradually regained stability when Van Helsing injected morphine under her skin as before. Having collapsed in a swoon, she soon fell into a deep sleep.

While the professor stayed by her side, I went downstairs with Quincey Morris, sent a maid out, and had her pay the fare to the driver waiting outside. I offered Quincey a glass of wine, told him to get some shut-eye, and instructed the cook to prepare a hearty breakfast. Then suddenly remembering something, I turned my steps back to Lucy's room.

As I carefully opened the door and entered, Van Helsing was deep in thought, holding a page or two of letter paper in his hand. As if he had just finished reading the contents, he sat rubbing his forehead with his hand, looking like he was pondering over something. As if his doubts were resolved, his face clearly showed a look of satisfaction. He handed out the paper and said briefly. "It fell from her chest when we carried her to the bathtub."

Taking the paper, I stopped reading halfway and stared blankly at the professor. It was a long time before I could barely manage to speak. "What on earth is all this about? Has she gone mad, or what terrible threat is lurking?" My mind went blank, and I couldn't say another word. Van Helsing silently reached out, took back the paper, and said.

"Don't worry about it now. Just completely forget it for the time being. When the time comes, you'll naturally come to know everything. That's a matter for later. More importantly, what was it you were trying to tell me earlier?"

Only then coming back to my senses, I regained my composure and brought up the main issue.

"I came to discuss the matter of the death certificate. If we don't handle this situation wisely, we might end up being investigated by the coroner, and then we might even have to submit that paper as evidence. We must avoid an investigation at all costs. Should the police by any chance get involved, even if not for any other terrible reason, poor Lucy would surely die from the shock. Both you and I know that Mrs. Westenra usually suffered from a heart condition. Her former physician knows this fact too. So it would be better to process her cause of death as a heart attack. I will write the death certificate right away, submit it to the registrar, and bring the undertaker."

"Good, oh, my friend John! That's an excellent idea! Truly poor Miss Lucy is suffering surrounded by cruel enemies, but it is a relief that she has friends by her side who genuinely care for her. One, two, three, and even an old man like me, making four. Four men willingly gave their blood for her, didn't they? Ah, I know everything, friend John. Because my eyes are not blind! That's why I hold you even more dear! Now, go quickly."

When I came out to the hallway, Quincey Morris was pacing around. He was about to send a telegram to Arthur with the news of Mrs. Westenra's passing, adding that Lucy had also fallen ill but was now improving, and that Van Helsing and I were by her side. I told him my destination. Pushing my back to tell me to hurry, he added in a low voice.

"When you get back, Jack, you and I have something very important to discuss quietly just between us."
