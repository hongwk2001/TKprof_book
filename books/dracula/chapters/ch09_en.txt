[P002] Mina Harker to Lucy Westenra.

[P003] "Budapest, August 24.

[P004] "My dearest Lucy,—

[P005a] "You must be very curious about what happened since we parted at the Whitby train station. Well, my dear friend, I safely arrived in Hull and took a ship to Hamburg. From there, I took a train to Budapest here in Hungary. I hardly remember the journey here. My only thought was that I was going to Jonathan. I just kept thinking I should sleep whenever I had the chance so I could nurse him.... Finally, I met my beloved Jonathan. Oh, he was so thin and pale. He looked truly frail.

[P005b] I couldn't find his old firm resolve in those sweet eyes. The calm and dignified expression I told you about has completely disappeared. He was like a broken shell of a man. Furthermore, he remembers absolutely nothing of what happened to him recently. At least he seems to want me to believe that. I have no intention of interrogating him either. He must have suffered some terrible shock. I am afraid that trying to force him to remember might hurt his exhausted mind even more.

[P005c] Sister Agatha is a very good and naturally wonderful nurse. She said that when Jonathan lost consciousness, he rambled about terrifying things. I begged her to tell me what they were, but she crossed herself and firmly refused. The ravings of a sick person are a secret only God should know. Even if a nurse happens to overhear, she must strictly keep the patient's secret. She is a very sweet and kind lady. The next day, seeing me constantly worrying, the nun brought it up first.

[P005d] She said she couldn't repeat exactly what my poor fiancé had said, but she would tell me this much. 'Dear Mina, I can tell you this. He did not do anything wrong himself. So you, as his future wife, have nothing to worry about. He has not forgotten you, nor has he forgotten his promise to you. The things he feared were too massive and terrible. They were things an ordinary person could never understand.' This sweet nun must have thought I was jealous, worrying that Jonathan might have fallen in love with another woman. Me, jealous of Jonathan!

[P005e] But Lucy, honestly, want to hear a secret? When I learned that the cause of his distress was not another woman, a thrill of relief and joy washed over me inside. Now I am sitting by his bed so I can watch him sleep. Oh, he is waking up!...

[P006a] "He asked for his coat as soon as he woke up, saying he needed to take something out of his pocket. I asked Sister Agatha to bring all his luggage. Seeing a notebook in his luggage, I was about to ask if I could look at it in case it held a clue to what was bothering him. But Jonathan seemed to read my eyes; he sent me to the window, saying he wanted to be alone for a moment. Then he called me back right away.

[P006b] When I approached, he had his hand resting on the notebook and spoke very solemnly.—

[P007a] "'Wilhelmina'—it was the first time he called me by my full name since the day he proposed, so I knew right away it was very serious.—'You know how I feel about trust in a marriage, right? There should be no secrets, and nothing should be hidden from each other. I suffered a massive shock. My head spins whenever I try to remember what it was. I don't even know if the things I experienced were real or the nightmare of a madman. The meningitis I suffered from practically means I was out of my mind.

[P007b] The secret is written right here in this notebook. But I don't want to know what it is. I just want to marry you here and start my life anew.' Actually, dear Lucy, we decided to get married right here as soon as the paperwork is done. 'Wilhelmina, can you share this ignorance with me? Take this notebook and keep it safe. You may read it if you want. But never tell me what is in it. We'll make an exception only if an absolute, unavoidable duty forces me to face these bitter memories again.

[P007c] Whether I was asleep or awake, sane or mad, the things written in this notebook, I mean.' After saying that, Jonathan completely exhausted, collapsed onto his pillow. I put the notebook under his pillow and kissed him. I asked Sister Agatha to ask the Mother Superior for permission to marry this afternoon. We are currently waiting for the answer....

[P008] *       *       *       *       *

[P009] "The nun has returned. They sent for a minister from the English Mission Church. We will be married within an hour, or as soon as Jonathan wakes up....

[P010] *       *       *       *       *

[P011a] "Lucy, the time has finally come and passed. My heart feels very reverent, yet indescribably happy. Jonathan woke up a little over an hour later, and everything was ready. He propped himself up on his pillow and sat up in bed, and when the time came, he answered firmly and strongly, 'Yes, I do.' My heart was so full and my throat choked up that I almost couldn't even answer. The nuns were really so incredibly kind. By the grace of God, I will never forget them for the rest of my life.

[P011b] Nor will I forget this solemn and beautiful responsibility I have just accepted. I will tell you about the wedding present I gave him, too. When the minister and the nuns left the room, and finally my husband and I were left alone—oh, Lucy, this is the first time I have written the words 'my husband'!—I took the notebook out from under the pillow. I wrapped it in white paper and tied it with the pale blue ribbon I was wearing around my neck. Then I dropped some wax on the knot and sealed it.

[P011c] I used my real wedding ring as a seal. I kissed the notebook, showed it to my husband, and told him I would keep it exactly like this for the rest of my life. I said this would be a physical, visible token showing our complete trust in each other. I promised never to open this notebook unless it was for his safety or some unavoidable duty arose. He took my hand. Oh, Lucy, it was the first time he held his wife's hand. He said this trust was the most precious thing in the whole world.

[P011d] He said he would even go through all that terrible past again to earn this trust if necessary. My poor, dear husband probably meant to say 'part' of the past. But right now, his sense of time is completely jumbled. I wouldn't be surprised if he confuses not only the month but even the year for a while.

[P012] "My dear friend, what else could I have said? I just told him that I was the happiest woman in the world. I said I had nothing to give him but myself, my life, and my absolute trust. And along with all that, I promised to dedicate my lifelong love and devotion. When he pulled me close and kissed me with his poor, thin hand, it was like a deep, sacred vow between us....

[P013a] "Dear Lucy, do you know why I am telling you all this? It's not just because these moments are precious to me. It's because you are, and have always been, so precious in my heart. When you graduated from school and were preparing for the real world, it was a great privilege for me to be your friend and guide. Now I wanted to show you, through the eyes of a very happy wife, where duty and love have led me. It's because I hope you will find the exact same happiness when you marry.

[P013b] My dear friend, I pray that by God's blessing, your life will unfold perfectly as promised. I wish you only long, dazzlingly clear days without fierce storms, neglected duties, or a lack of trust. I won't wish for your life to be completely free of pain. That's impossible in this world. But I sincerely hope you will always be as happy as I am right at this moment. Goodbye, my dearest friend. I'll mail this letter right now. I will probably write again very soon. I must close now.

[P013c] Jonathan is waking up, and I have to go to my husband's side!

[P013d] "With eternal love,

[P015] "Mina Harker."

[P022] Dr. Seward's Diary.

[P023a] August 20.—My patient Renfield's case is getting more interesting. He has become noticeably calmer, and now has distinct rest periods between his manic episodes. Throughout the first week after his first seizure, he was relentlessly aggressive and violent. Then a few nights ago, right around the time the moon rose, he suddenly became very quiet and began muttering to himself. "Now I can wait. I can wait now." The head warder rushed over to inform me, and I went straight down to his cell to observe him.

[P023b] He was still locked in the padded isolation room wearing a straitjacket, but the flushed, angry expression had completely vanished from his face. Instead, his eyes held a soft, pleading—perhaps even submissive—light that I hadn't seen in a while. I was convinced he would no longer harm himself or others, so I ordered the staff to remove his straitjacket. The staff visibly hesitated but ultimately followed my orders without complaint. Interestingly, this patient was sane enough to notice the staff's distrust. He glanced nervously at them, leaned in close to me, and whispered.—

[P024] "Those fools must think I might hurt you! Me, hurt you! What idiots!"

[P025a] It gave me a strange comfort to know that even in the deeply diseased mind of this poor madman, I am considered a separate ally from the others. However, I still cannot fully grasp his underlying logic. Does he believe we have some common ground or alliance? Or does he expect to get something extremely important from me, making my safety and well-being an essential part of his plan? This is something I must eventually find out. But tonight, he stubbornly refused to speak any further.

[P025b] I even offered to give him a kitten or a full-grown cat, but he showed not the slightest interest. His only answer was this. "I have no interest in cats right now. I have much more important things to focus on. I can wait. I can wait, I say."

[P026] I left him alone after a while. The night warder later reported that Renfield remained perfectly quiet until just before dawn. Then he began to grow anxious, and finally turned violent once more, throwing a tantrum until he had a severe physical fit. The seizure left him completely exhausted, and he lost consciousness in a state virtually indistinguishable from a coma.

[P027] *       *       *       *       *

[P028a] ... This exact pattern has repeated for three consecutive nights now. He is extremely aggressive during the day, but from the time the moon rises until the sun comes up, he becomes perfectly calm. I wish I could figure out what triggers this pattern. It feels as if some external force or influence approaches and withdraws at regular times. Wait, a brilliant idea just struck me! Tonight we will pit the wisdom of sane minds against his madness. He has previously escaped his cell without our help. Tonight we will secretly help him escape again.

[P028b] We will deliberately give him the chance to escape, but keep staff nearby to tail him in case intervention is needed....

[P029] *       *       *       *       *

[P030a] August 23.—When the English statesman Benjamin Disraeli said, "The unexpected always happens," he certainly understood life well. Tonight, our elusive 'bird' absolutely refused to fly away even after noticing the cage door was open. So our elaborate plan became completely useless. However, we did confirm one important fact. His calm state lasts for a fairly predictable amount of time. From now on, we can safely untie his restraints for several hours every day. I instructed the night warder to simply lock him in the padded room when he calms down, and maintain that state only until an hour before sunrise.

[P030b] Even if his shattered mind cannot fully comprehend what is happening, his body will surely benefit from the relief of being freed from restraints. Wait! The unexpected has happened again! The warder is calling for me. He says the patient has escaped again.

[P031] *       *       *       *       *

[P032a] Later.—We had quite a commotion tonight. Renfield cleverly hid and waited for the exact moment the warder opened the door to patrol. Then he shoved the warder and darted down the hallway. I immediately ordered the staff to chase him. Once again, he fled to the courtyard of Carfax, the abandoned mansion next door, and we cornered him in the exact same spot as last time. He was pressing himself hard against the heavy wooden door of the old chapel. When he saw me approach, he was consumed by extreme rage.

[P032b] Had the staff not quickly restrained him, he surely would have tried to kill me on the spot. But a very strange thing happened while we struggled to subdue him. He suddenly struggled twice as fiercely to break free, and then in the blink of an eye, let all his strength go and became completely docile. I instinctively looked around to see what had caused this change, but I saw nothing. Then I noticed where the patient's gaze was fixed, and I too looked up at the moonlit night sky.

[P032c] All I could see was a very large bat flapping silently westward like a ghost. Usually, bats fly in erratic circles hunting for bugs, but this peculiar creature was flying straight ahead in a line, as if it knew exactly where it was going or was on some special mission. Renfield calmed down by the second and soon said quietly.—

[P033] "There is no need to tie me up. I will follow you quietly." We brought him back to the asylum without any further incident. Nevertheless, I cannot shake the feeling that there is something deeply ominous behind his sudden change of heart. Tonight's events will not be easily forgotten....

Honestly, I don't know when that will be. How I wish Mina were here with me right now. I feel so miserable. Last night, I think I started having those strange dreams from Whitby again. It might have been triggered by the change of environment or returning home. This whole experience feels dark, confusing, and frightening. [P035b] I am just left with a vague and overwhelming sense of fear, and my body feels incredibly exhausted and weak. Arthur dropped by around lunchtime today, and he seemed genuinely upset to see how I looked. I didn't even have the energy to force a smile for him. Would I feel a bit better if I slept in Mother's bedroom tonight? I should make up an excuse and try that.

[P036] *       *       *       *       *

[P037a] August 25.—I had another terrible night. Mother didn't seem too thrilled about the idea of me sleeping in her room. She's not feeling well herself, and she was clearly trying not to show her worry for me. I tried my best to stay awake, and I succeeded for a while. But I woke up startled from a light sleep by the chiming of the grandfather clock striking midnight, so I must have unwittingly fallen asleep. I vaguely recall hearing a scratching or flapping sound against the windowpane, but I just ignored it.

When Arthur visits later, I'll have to pretend to be as bright as possible somehow. Otherwise, seeing me like this will make him thoroughly miserable.

[P064b] Then the Professor started chatting very cheerfully about every topic except ourselves or illness. That cheerfulness was so infectious that I could see poor Lucy's forced liveliness soon turn into genuine liveliness. Then, without any sudden shift in mood, the Professor smoothly brought the topic back to the purpose of our visit and spoke gently.—

[P065a] "'My dear young lady, it makes me feel good to know you are loved by so many people. Do you know what I said when those people told me you lacked energy and were pale as a ghost? [P065b] What does this fellow know about young ladies anyway?' [P065c] They tell an old man like me, who has experienced all kinds of sorrow and can see right through to the cause. So, dear lady, let's send this fellow out into the garden to smoke, and you and I can have a quiet chat alone.'

[P065d] I took the hint and went outside for a walk. A moment later, the Professor came to the window and called for me. With a serious expression, he said. 'I examined her very carefully, but I couldn't find any physical dysfunction. I agree with you that she lacks blood, as you mentioned earlier. But her condition is definitely not simple anemia. I asked her maid to be sent in so I wouldn't miss even a single trivial detail. I have a question or two to ask. I already know well what that maid will say.

[P065e] But there must be a cause. Every effect always has a cause. I need to go home and think about it carefully. Send me telegrams every day with updates on her condition. And if you need my help, I will return immediately. Feeling unwell without a clear reason is also a kind of illness, so this illness certainly piques my interest. Besides, there's that lovely young lady. She is a truly charming person. Even if it weren't for your request or the illness, I would return solely for her sake.'

[P066a] "As I told you, the Professor didn't add a single word more even when we were alone. So Arthur, now you know everything I know. I intend to keep a very strict eye on her. I sincerely hope your father overcomes his illness. My old friend, it must be a terrible agony to be put in such an awkward position between two people who are so precious to you. I know how heavy your sense of duty to your father is, and I think it is absolutely right for you to uphold that obligation.

[P066b] But if anything happens, I will immediately send word for you to rush to Lucy. So please try not to worry too much until you hear from me."

[P067] Dr. Seward's Diary.

[P068a] September 4.—The patient with the Zoophagous tendency is still drawing our keen interest. He had only one fit, and it was yesterday at a completely different time than usual. Just before noon, he began to grow restless and anxious. The warder recognized the warning signs and immediately called for backup. Fortunately, the staff managed to run up in time. Right at noon, he became violently aggressive, and the staff had to hold him down with all their might. But after about five minutes, he gradually began to quiet down, and finally sank into a kind of deep melancholy.

[P068b] He has remained in that state ever since. According to the warder, the screams he let out during the fit were truly terrifying. When I entered the ward, I also had a very hard time calming the other patients who were startled by his screams. Considering even I was unsettled from quite a distance away, I fully understand the effect the sound had on the patients. Now it's past dinner time in the ward, but my patient is still huddled in the corner, lost in deep thought.

[P068c] His face wears a dull, gloomy, and sorrowful expression, which seems more like it's hinting at something rather than directly revealing an emotion. I simply cannot understand it.

[P069] *       *       *       *       *

[P070a] Later.—There is another change in my patient. I went to check on him around 5 o'clock, and he looked quite happy and content, just like before. He was catching and eating flies, making nail marks on the edge of the door between the soft pads to keep count of how many he caught. When he saw me, he approached and apologized for his atrocious behavior. Then, with a very humble and obsequious attitude, he begged me to let him return to his room so he could write in his notebook again.

[P070b] I decided it was best to humor him, and he is now back in his room with the window open. He has spread some sugar he secretly swiped from his tea on the windowsill, luring and catching quite a few flies. He isn't eating the flies now, but collecting them neatly in a box like before, and is already scanning the corners of the room for spiders. Thinking any clue to reading his mind would be incredibly helpful, I tried to strike up a conversation with him about what happened over the last few days.

[P070c] But he cleverly sidestepped my questions. He looked very sad for a moment, then spoke in a blank, hollow voice, as if talking to himself rather than to me.—

[P071] "It's all over! It's all over! He abandoned me. Now I have no hope unless I manage on my own!" Then suddenly, he turned to me with a resolute expression and said. "Doctor, would you do me a kindness and get me a little more sugar? I think it would be very good for me."

[P072] "Good for the flies too?" I asked.

[P073a] "Of course! The flies like sugar, and I like flies, so in the end, I like sugar too." There are foolish people who think madmen cannot think logically. I got him double the amount of sugar and left the room, leaving behind a man who looked happier than anyone in the world. How I wish I could fathom his true feelings.

[P074] *       *       *       *       *

[P075a] Midnight.—There is another change in him. I had just returned from examining Miss Westenra, whose condition had improved somewhat. I was standing at the door of the ward looking at the sunset when I heard him shouting again. His room is on this side of the building, so I could hear him much more clearly than in the morning. I had to tear my eyes away from the spectacular yet smoky sunset spread over London—tinged with an ominous red, ink-dark shadows, and all sorts of bizarre colors one might find only on dirty water.

[P075b] It was truly a huge shock to then face this cold stone building filled with the breath of endless agony, this ruthless and cruel reality, and my own desolate heart that must silently endure it all. I reached his room just as the sun was setting, and I watched the red sun sink through the window. As the sun set, his madness gradually subsided, and when the sun finally disappeared completely below the horizon, he slipped out of the hands of the people restraining him and slumped to the floor. But the intellectual resilience of madmen is truly marvelous.

[P075c] In just a few minutes, he got up very calmly and looked around. Wanting to see how he would act, I signaled the warders not to hold him. He went straight to the window, brushed away the sugar crumbs, picked up his box of flies, emptied the contents outside, and threw the box away too. Then he closed the window and went to his bed, sitting down quietly. This series of actions was so surprising that I asked him. "Are you not going to keep flies anymore?"

[P076] "Yes," he replied nonchalantly. "I am sick of such trash now!" He is a truly remarkable and fascinating subject for study. I wouldn't wish for anything more if I could catch even a small glimpse into his mind or the cause of his sudden fits. Wait, if I could figure out the reason why his fits happened exactly at noon and sunset today, it could be an important clue. Just as the moon affects certain people, could some malicious power or influence of the sun act on people with particular tendencies at specific times? We shall see.
