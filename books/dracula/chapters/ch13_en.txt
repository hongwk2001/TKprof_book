[P086c] The poor young man Arthur spoke of the terrible surgery where he transfused his own blood into Lucy's veins. I watched Professor Van Helsing's face turn white as a sheet and then suddenly flush red and blue. Arthur confessed that after finishing the transfusion, he felt as if the two of them had truly become husband and wife, and he believed that in the eyes of God, she was undeniably his wife. Of course, none of us mentioned a word about the other transfusions she had received, and we will remain silent forever.

[P086d] After Arthur and Quincey left for the station, Van Helsing and I returned here. As soon as we were left alone in the carriage, surprisingly, the professor had a severe hysterical fit. Although he later insisted that it was not hysteria at all, and made excuses that a bizarrely absurd sense of humor had just been triggered amidst the terrible situation. He laughed uproariously until he was in tears, and I had to hurriedly lower the carriage blinds for fear that passersby might see and misunderstand.

[P086e] After bursting into laughter, he soon burst into tears, and like a weak woman, he repeated crying and laughing endlessly. I sternly scolded him as if dealing with a woman in such a state, but to no avail. At the moment when the nerves become weak to the point of breaking, the reactions of men and women are so different! Finally, when a solemn and serious look returned to his face, I asked him why he had laughed as if he had lost his mind, and why of all times such laughter had erupted in such a terrible moment.

[P086f] His answer was somewhat typical of him. It was logical, intense, and extremely bizarre. He said this.

[P087a] "Ah, you do not understand at all, my friend John. Just because I laughed pointlessly does not mean I am not sad. Look, didn't I cry my eyes out even when the bizarre laughter was choking me. But conversely, when I am wailing, I am not entirely just sad either. Because even in that terrible moment, laughter inevitably comes. Keep in mind that a laugh which knocks politely and asks 'May I come in?' is never a real laugh. No! King Laugh is absolute and omnipotent, deciding entirely on his own when and how to come.

[P087b] He asks no one for permission and chooses no particular time. He simply declares 'I have arrived!'. Now look, for example, right now my heart is tearing apart with sorrow in the face of the death of a girl who was so lovely. Although I am old and tired, I devoted my blood, my time, my medical skills, and even my sweet sleep without regret to save her. I poured everything into her, even abandoning my other patients. Yet I ended up bursting into laughter right beside her grave.

[P087c] At the very devastating moment when the clods of dirt falling from the worker's shovel poured thud, thud onto her coffin and drained the color from my face.

[P087d] When I think of the poor boy Arthur, my heart weeps tears of blood. If I had been blessed by heaven and my son had lived, he would surely be exactly that age. Because of that lovely child who even had the same hair and eye color. Now, you can probably guess why I feel so affectionately toward that young man.

[P087e] But the moment he confessed an unbearable, terrible secret as a husband that dug into my heart, and so the very moment my paternal love leaned toward him so fiercely that it would not turn to any other man—not even to you, John, since we are closer to equal comrades in arms than father and son—'King Laugh' suddenly came, roaring into my ear 'I have come! I have arrived!'. Until my veins throbbed again and he brought the sunlight he held to my pale cheeks. Oh, friend John. The world is a truly bizarre place.

[P087f] It is a grey world stained with misery, sorrow, and hardship. However, when 'King Laugh' arrives, he makes all that sorrow and pain dance to the tune he plays. A bleeding heart, dry bones tumbling in a graveyard, and even the hot tears flowing down cheeks—all begin to dance in unison to the bizarre music he blows from lips entirely devoid of a smile. And trust me, friend John. His sudden arrival is in a way fortunate and something to be thankful for. We humans are like a rope pulled taut from both sides.

[P087g] And then when tears burst forth, like rain soaking the rope, those tears tighten us until we cannot move. If we aren't careful, the rope might snap under the tension. But at that time, 'King Laugh' arrives like warm sunlight and loosens the taut tension. Thanks to that, we do not collapse and can move forward carrying our respective heavy burdens."

[P087h] I did not want to hurt him by pretending not to understand his thoughts and picking a fight. But because I simply could not comprehend the reason he had laughed like a madman, I ended up asking anyway. The face he returned had a much more solemn look, and his voice had also changed completely.

[P088_089] "Oh, that was because of the terrible and cruel irony created by the whole situation. Such a lovely girl was laid out decorated with fresh flowers, looking as beautiful as if she were alive and breathing. Because of that, didn't we all anxiously doubt whether she was really dead? She lay coldly in the magnificent marble mausoleum of her relatives standing tall in the lonely cemetery, beside the mother who cherished her and whom she loved so much. The holy funeral bell rang 'Ding! Ding!

[P089b] Ding!' so sorrowfully and majestically, and the priests dressed in angelic white robes read the Bible holy. But their eyes did not look at the Bible even once. And what about us. We all bowed our heads reverently. What is the use of all that grandiose ceremony? She is dead. Is she not?"

[P090] "Well, I tell you on my life, Professor," I retorted. "I cannot find a single part to laugh at in that devastating moment. The explanation you just gave only makes this situation even more puzzling. Even if we concede a hundred times that the funeral ceremony was ridiculous, what about poor Arthur, who is mourning? That pitiful young man's heart was truly being torn to shreds, wasn't it."

[P091] "Exactly. Didn't he say that thanks to sharing his blood into her veins, he finally welcomed her as his true bride?"

[P092] "He did. That single thought brought infinitely sweet comfort to his heartbroken soul."

[P093] "Indeed it did. But there is one terrible problem, my friend John. If what he says is true, what becomes of the rest of the blood donors? Ho, ho! In that case, this virtuous lady suddenly ends up following polyandry, and even I—who have a poor wife who is already dead to me under canon law, and though she has gone mad and only her shell remains, I have remained utterly devoted to this wife who is not a wife—even I suddenly end up becoming a bigamist."

[P094] "Even so, I simply cannot understand how a joke could come out at that point!" I shot back bluntly, feeling very displeased. Then the professor laid his hand gently on my arm and said.

[P095a] "Friend John, if my lighthearted babbling has upset you, please forgive me. Fearing I might hurt others, I could not bear to reveal my inner thoughts, and I only confessed them to you, my old friend whom I trust and rely on.

[P095b] If you could have looked into my dark heart at the moment I was laughing like crazy, if you could have read my devastated inner thoughts when that bizarre laughter suddenly struck, and if you could completely see through my wretched feelings right now when 'King Laugh' has packed his bags and left me far behind—perhaps you would pity me the most in the whole world."

[P096] Moved by his infinitely gentle and bitter tone, I asked him what on earth was the matter.

[P097] "Because I know that terrible truth!"

[P098] And now we have scattered in all directions. For a long time to come, a terrible loneliness will spread its gloomy wings and hover over the roof of our house. Lucy lies in a lonely cemetery far from bustling London. A place with clear air, brilliant sunlight rising over Hampstead Hill, and unknown wildflowers blooming in profusion. Inside the grand mausoleum where her relatives sleep, she has entered eternal rest.

[P099] With this, I intend to close this long diary. Only God knows when the day will come for me to open this diary again. If I ever hold a pen and open this old diary again, it will undoubtedly be to record completely different people and unfamiliar stories. The beautiful romance that embroidered my life has now ended, and at this final moment, just before I must take up the boring thread of daily life to which I must devote my entire life, I leave this one phrase filled with deep resignation and sorrow.

[P102] Hampstead Mystery.

[P103a] Lately, the Hampstead area has been plagued by a bizarre series of disappearances. Gossips whisper that these incidents bear an uncanny resemblance to the so-called 'Kensington Horror,' the 'Woman with the Knife,' and the 'Woman in Black' cases that caused an uproar in the past. In just two or three days, there have been successive incidents of young children wandering lost from home or vanishing without a trace while playing on the Heath. The missing children were all too young to coherently explain the terrible situations that befell them.

[P103b] However, if you carefully pick apart the children's gibberish, one chilling commonality is found: they were with a 'bloofer (beautiful) lady'. The children always vanished in the late evening when twilight settled, and two of them were found the next early morning in a cold state.

[P103c] Because the first child to go missing stated, "A beautiful lady coaxed me to go for a walk and I followed her," the local people assume that the other children simply overheard that plausible excuse and are mimicking it exactly. Considering that a game of deceiving each other with brazen lies is currently in full swing among the little ones, such adult speculation is not unreasonable. According to a message sent by our correspondent, the sight of muddy little brats scrambling over one another to play-act as the 'beautiful lady' is quite ridiculous.

[P103d] He added that if our newspaper's satirical cartoonists saw the scene firsthand, they could capture a bizarre irony within the dizzying gap between reality and imagination. The fact that the 'beautiful lady' has undeniably emerged as a popular role in the kids' impromptu outdoor plays might just be due to the shallow human nature of following trends. The correspondent joked aimlessly that seeing the dirt-covered kids acting alluringly with their wild imaginations, not even the greatest actress of our time, Ellen Terry, could pull off such a cute and charming performance.

[P104a] However, behind this farce, there might be a chilling truth hidden that can never be brushed off lightly. This is because the children who returned after going missing in the middle of the night all had sharp scratches or torn gashes on their necks. At a glance, they look like bite marks from a rat or a small wild dog, so one might casually overlook them. But whatever beast left these wounds, it strongly suggests that it possesses some meticulous and creepy hunting method or bizarre habit.

[P104b] The local police station has issued strict orders to thoroughly monitor whether young children in particular are wandering around Hampstead Heath and getting lost, and whether there are any starving wild dogs roaming the area.
