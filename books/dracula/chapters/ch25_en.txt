[P002] Dr. Seward's Diary

[P003] October 11, Evening.—Jonathan Harker asked me to record this. He lacks the energy to do it himself, but he wants to ensure an accurate record is kept.

[P004] No one was surprised when Mina Harker asked to see us at sunset. We had recently realized that sunrise and sunset offered her special periods of freedom. During these times, the dark forces could not control or suppress her, and she returned to her true self. This usually began about thirty minutes before sunrise or sunset and lasted until the sun was high in the sky or the clouds above the horizon still held the light. At first, she would appear dazed, as if a binding cord had been untied, but she soon regained complete freedom. However, when this window of freedom closed, a short, ominous silence would come first, and she would quickly slip back into that terrible state of suppression.

[P005] When we gathered tonight, she was somewhat rigid, as if fiercely fighting something inside her. I thought she was desperately trying to control herself as soon as she regained her freedom. Fortunately, she found her peace within a few minutes. Calling her husband to the side of the sofa where she was half-lying, she motioned for the rest of us to pull our chairs closer. Then, holding his hand tightly, she began to speak.

[P006] "This might be the last time we gather like this during my hour of freedom! I know, my love, that you will stay by my side until the very end," she said to her husband, who squeezed her hand even tighter in response. "Tomorrow morning we set out on our mission. Only God knows what fate awaits each of us. You have graciously decided to take me with you. I know that all of you, brave and devoted, will give everything for this poor, weak woman who is in terrible danger, who might lose her soul, though she hasn't yet. But you must not forget one thing. I am different from you. There is a poison flowing in my blood and soul that will destroy me. That poison will surely destroy me unless we find a way to break this curse. Oh, my friends. You know how perilous my soul is right now. I know there is only one way to end this suffering, but neither you nor I must ever choose that path!" She looked at each of us pleadingly, starting with her husband.

[P007] "What path is that?" Professor Van Helsing asked in a hoarse voice. "What path are you saying we must never take?"

[P008] "To die right now, by my own hand or another's, before a greater evil is completed. I know, and you know, that once I die, you can free my immortal soul just as you did for poor Lucy. If death, or the fear of death, were the only things holding me back, I would have gladly chosen to die right now, surrounded by my loving friends. But death is not the end. There is still hope ahead of us, and a difficult mission to finish, so it cannot be God's will for me to die here. So I am willingly giving up the certain chance of eternal rest, and walking into the darkness where the most terrible things in this world and the next might be lurking!" We were all silent, instinctively sensing that this was merely an introduction. In the midst of this intense tension, Jonathan Harker's face had turned ashen. He seemed to guess better than any of us what she was going to say next. She continued.

[P009] "This is all I can offer as my share." In such a serious and devastating situation, it was strangely memorable that she used the unique old English legal term "hotch-pot." "What will each of you give?" she added urgently. "It might be easy for brave men to give their lives. Your lives belong to God, so you can return them to Him. But what will you give me?" She looked at us again, as if asking, but this time she carefully avoided her husband's face. Quincey Morris nodded as if he understood her intention, and her face brightened slightly. "Then I will clearly state what I want. We no longer have the luxury of leaving even the slightest hesitation or uncertainty between us. All of you must promise me. Every single one of you, even you, my loving husband. If that moment comes, you must promise to kill me."

[P010] "When exactly is that moment?" Quincey asked in a suppressed, low voice.

[P011] "It is when I have changed so terribly that you become convinced it would be better for me to die. If my body meets its end in that way, please do not delay for a single second—drive a stake through my heart and cut off my head. No, you must do whatever it takes as long as I can find true rest!"

[P012] After a short silence, Quincey was the first to rise from his seat. He knelt before her, took her hands, and swore a deep and solemn oath.

[P013] "I am a rough man, so I might not be worthy of such a glorious duty. But I swear by everything I hold sacred, if that moment comes, I will carry out the duty you asked without a single moment of hesitation. And I promise to make it certain. The moment I have even the slightest doubt, I will consider that the time has come!"

[P014] "My true friend!" she managed to utter amidst pouring tears. She leaned forward and kissed his hand.

[P015] "I too swear, dear Madam Mina!" said Van Helsing.

[P016] "I swear as well!" said Lord Godalming. They took turns kneeling and pledging to her. I followed suit. Then her husband looked at her with empty eyes. His face was a sickly green, making his pure white hair stand out even more. He asked.

[P017] "Must I, oh my wife, must I too make that promise?"

[P018] "You too, my love," she replied with deep, desperate compassion. "You must not hesitate either. You are the closest and most precious person to me, my entire world. Our souls are joined as one for this life and eternity. Remember, my love. There were times in history when brave men had to kill their own wives and lovers to prevent them from falling into enemy hands. Their hands did not hesitate just because their loved ones begged to be killed. In such harsh trials, it is a true man's duty to do so for the one he loves! And oh, my love, if I must die by someone's hand, let it be by the hand of the one who loves me most. Dr. Van Helsing, I have not forgotten that you showed mercy to poor Lucy and gave the opportunity to the one who loved her—" She blushed and quickly corrected herself. "—to the one who had the most rightful claim to give her rest. If such a time comes for me too, I hope you will help my husband so that it will remain a peaceful memory for him later that it was his loving touch that freed me from this terrible bondage that crushes me."

[P019] "I swear once again!" the Professor's booming voice rang out. Mina Harker smiled. She smiled genuinely, breathed a sigh of relief, and leaned back on the sofa.

[P020] "And now, I have one last warning that you must not forget. If that moment comes, it will happen very quickly and suddenly. You must not waste a single second in such a situation. You must seize the chance immediately. Because when that time comes, no, if that moment comes, I will undoubtedly be in league with your enemy."

[P021] "I have one more request," she said in a very resolute voice. "This is not a matter of life and death like the promise we just made, but I would like you to do one thing for me, if you don't mind." We all silently agreed without needing to speak.

[P022] "I want you to read the 'Burial Service' prayer for me." She paused as her husband let out a deep, heartbreaking groan. She took his hand, placed it on her chest, and continued. "You must read that prayer for me someday. No matter how this terrible situation ends, it will be a comfort to all of us, or at least to some of us. My love, I want you to read it. That way, whatever happens, your voice reading that prayer will remain in my memory forever!"

[P023] "But oh, my love," he pleaded. "Death is still so far away from you."

[P024] "No," she stopped him, raising her hand. "I am closer to death right now than if I were buried deep under a heavy mound of earth!"

[P025] "Oh, my wife, must I really read it?" he asked hesitantly.

[P026] "It will give me peace, my dear!" was her reply. When she handed him the book, he began to read the prayer.

[P027a] How could I, or anyone, put that strange scene into words? The solemnity, the deep melancholy, the profound sorrow, the overwhelming terror... and the sweetness strangely mixed in between it all. Even a deeply cynical skeptic, who only finds cruel jokes in religious or moving events, would not have been able to hold back tears seeing these loyal and loving friends kneeling around this suffering and grieving woman. Or if they had heard her husband's mournful yet sincere voice reading the simple and beautiful prayer of the 'Burial Service for the Dead,' stopping often as he choked up with constant surges of emotion. I—I can write no more about this. The words... my voice... I just feel overwhelmed!

[P027b] *       *       *       *       *

[P028_029] Her intuition was entirely correct. No matter how bizarre this whole situation was, and no matter how absurd it might feel looking back later, it gave us immense comfort. Even when silence fell, signaling that Mina Harker's time of freedom was over and she had returned to her original state of suppression, the atmosphere did not feel as desperate as we had feared.

[P061] October 28.—When the telegram announcing the arrival at Galatz flew in, surprisingly, none of us seemed greatly shocked. Of course, nobody knew exactly when, where, or how that decisive blow would fall, but we were all inwardly expecting something terribly ominous to happen. As the arrival at Varna kept being delayed, we each instinctively sensed that things would not go exactly our way, and we were simply waiting with bated breath to see where that damn variable would pop up from. Even so, when it actually became reality, it was incredibly bewildering. Human nature tends to lean toward hope no matter what, so it seems that even when we clearly see how things are going, we ultimately want to believe they will work out the way we desire. Transcendent faith might be a lighthouse lighting the way for angels, but to mere mortals, it might be nothing more than a fickle will-o'-the-wisp. It was a truly bizarre experience, and we each absorbed the shock in our own way. Van Helsing threw both hands up over his head for a moment, as if protesting to almighty God, but he said nothing, and a few seconds later he stood up with a resolute expression. Quincey Morris tightened his belt with a familiar and quick motion I knew very well. During our old wandering days, that motion always meant "Time for action." Mrs. Harker's face turned terrifyingly pale, making the scar on her forehead look remarkably fiery red, but she quietly folded her hands and looked up at the empty air as if praying. Jonathan Harker smiled. He truly smiled. It was the dark, bitter smile of a man who had lost all hope. But at the same time, his hand instinctively found the handle of his large kukri knife and gripped it tightly, an action entirely at odds with his eerie smile. Van Helsing spoke up to all of us. "When does the next train to Galatz leave?"

[P062a] "Tomorrow morning at 6:30!" We were all startled. Because the person who answered was none other than Mrs. Harker.
