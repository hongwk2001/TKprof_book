[P001] Chapter 12

[P002] Dr. Seward's Diary

[P003] September 18.—I drove rapidly and arrived early at Hillingham. Leaving the carriage outside the gate, I walked alone up the driveway. Worried that I might wake Lucy or her mother from a deep sleep, I knocked and rang the bell very cautiously, hoping just one maid would quietly come out. After waiting a long time with no sign of movement, I knocked and rang again, but still there was no answer. Since it was already past 10 a.m., I inwardly blamed the laziness of the servants lingering in bed, and knocked and rang the bell roughly with a more anxious hand. However, the inside of the house was as quiet as a dead mouse. Until then, I had considered it merely the servants' negligence, but suddenly a terrible fear swept over my entire body. Could this eerie silence be yet another link in the chain of doom tightening around us? What if I had arrived too late and it had already become a house of death? The thought that if Lucy had by any chance suffered that terrible fit again, even a single second's delay could put her in fatal danger, made me desperately circle the house, trying to find any opening to get inside.

[P004] But there was no way to get in. The windows and doors were all firmly locked, and I returned to the front door, engulfed in bewilderment. Then the sound of galloping horse hooves struck my ears from afar. The carriage stopped in front of the gate, and just a few seconds later Professor Van Helsing ran panting up the driveway. Spotting me, he gasped for breath and cried out.

[P005] "It was you, you just arrived. How is Lucy? We're not too late, are we? Didn't you get my telegram?"

[P006] I answered as quickly and clearly as possible that I had rushed over without delay as soon as I received the telegram early this morning, but no one inside was opening the door. He paused for a moment, took off his hat, and said grimly.

[P007] "Then it seems we are a step too late. May God's will be with us!" Then, regaining his usual vigor in an instant, he continued. "Let's go. If there's no open door, we must make one and get in. Right now, every minute and every second is a matter of life and death for us."

[P008] We went around to the back of the house where the kitchen window was. The professor took a small surgical saw from his bag, handed it to me, and pointed at the iron bars blocking the window. I immediately started sawing and cut off three bars in an instant. Then I slid a long, thin knife into the gap, undid the latch, and threw the window open. I went in first and helped the professor get in. The kitchen and the nearby servants' rooms were completely empty. Passing through the hallway and checking the rooms one by one, we found four maids collapsed on the dining room floor, faintly illuminated by a ray of light seeping through the crack in the shutters. They were not dead. The strong smell of opium filling the room and the sound of their rough breathing were enough to make it clear that they had fallen asleep intoxicated by drugs. Van Helsing and I exchanged glances, and as we left the room, he said briefly. "We can take care of them later." We ran straight up to Lucy's room. We held our breath and listened for a moment in front of the door, but we couldn't feel even the slightest presence. With pale faces and trembling hands, we carefully opened the door and stepped into the room.

[P009] With what words could I possibly explain the sight that unfolded before our eyes? Lucy and her mother were lying side by side on the bed. The mother on the inside was covered with a white sheet, and as the edge of the sheet was blown back by the wind seeping through the broken window, her pale face, frozen in terror, was completely revealed. Next to her lay Lucy, looking even more haggard and deathly pale. The wreath of garlic flowers that had wrapped around Lucy's neck was somehow resting on her mother's chest, and on the defenseless nape of Lucy's neck, those two small wounds from before were hideously festering, leaving a pale mark. The professor, without a word, leaned over the bed and put his ear close to poor Lucy's chest. Then, as if catching even a microscopic sound, he quickly raised his head, sprang up like a spring, and shouted to me.

[P010] "It's not too late yet! Hurry! Bring the brandy right away!"

[P011] I ran downstairs in a breath and brought the brandy. Fearing that this liquor, like the sherry left on the table, might have been drugged, I didn't forget to carefully smell and check the taste. The maids were still breathing, but seeing them tossing and turning more frequently, the effect of the sleeping pills seemed to be wearing off. Without further delay, I returned to Van Helsing. As usual, he rubbed brandy on Lucy's lips, gums, and both wrists and palms. He instructed me.

[P012] "I will take charge here. For now, this is the best method. You go and wake the maids. Slap their cheeks hard with a towel soaked in cold water. Wake them up, have them light a fire, and prepare hot bathwater right away. This poor soul has grown as cold as the corpse lying next to her. We must first warm her body before we can do anything."

[P013] I immediately went down and woke three of the maids, easily bringing them back to their senses. However, the fourth was a very young girl, and perhaps because the drug had spread deeper, she did not wake up, so I left her lying on the sofa. The awakened maids were bewildered at first, but as soon as their memories returned, they burst into fits of crying and began to sob. I firmly scolded them and stopped them from babbling nonsense. I threatened them that since one person had already lost her life, if they too delayed, Miss Lucy would also die. Only then did the maids, with their clothes half-undone, scatter in tears to light the fire and start heating water. Fortunately, the embers in the kitchen and boiler were still alive, so there was no great difficulty in preparing hot water. We quickly brought the bathtub in and carefully laid Lucy in it. While we were busily rubbing her limbs for a long time, there was a knock at the front door. One of the maids hurriedly threw on some clothes and went out to open the door. She came back, approached us, and whispered that a gentleman had come with a message from Mr. Holmwood. I told her to pass on the message to ask him to wait a moment as we couldn't see anyone right now. After the maid left, I became fully engrossed in caring for Lucy again and completely forgot about the visitor.

[P014] In all my life, I had never seen the professor cling so desperately. Since I too felt to my bones that this was an unyielding struggle to the death with the grim reaper, I took advantage of a brief pause to catch my breath and confided my inner thoughts to him. He threw back words I could hardly understand, but his face held a determination I had never seen before.

[P015] "If that is all, I will wash my hands of this right now and leave her to close her eyes in peace. Because I see no more ray of hope beyond the horizon of her life." He turned his body once again and continued the treatment with all his might, driven by an even more frantic passion.

[P016] Before long, both of us noticed that the heat was slowly taking effect. Lucy's heartbeat, heard through the stethoscope, became much clearer, and the movement of her lungs also noticeably revived. Van Helsing's face lit up with joy. When we carefully lifted her from the bathtub, wrapped her in a warm sheet, and wiped off the moisture, he said in an overwhelmed voice.

[P017] "This is our first victory! In terms of chess, it's like calling check on the enemy king!"

[P018] We carried Lucy to another room we had prepared in advance, laid her on the bed, forced her mouth open, and let a few drops of brandy flow down her throat. Van Helsing carefully wrapped her neck with a soft silk handkerchief. She still hadn't regained consciousness, and it was the most horrific state we had seen her in so far, or at least comparable to it.

[P019] Van Helsing called in a maid, strictly instructed her never to take her eyes off her until we returned, and gestured for me to come out of the room.

[P020] "Let's discuss what we should do from now on." He opened his mouth as we went down the stairs. Reaching the hall, he opened the dining room door, went in, and carefully closed the door. In accordance with the strict etiquette kept by English women of the lower classes when mourning a death, the shutters were open, but the blinds were tightly drawn. The room was dim and dark, but it was sufficient for us to talk. A look of bewilderment flashed across Van Helsing's stiff face, and there were clear signs of softening. Seeing him deep in thought about something, I waited for a moment. Eventually, he spoke.

[P021] "What should we do now? Where on earth should we go to ask for help? We must give her another blood transfusion quickly. If we don't act right away, that poor child won't even last an hour. You are already exhausted, and I too am completely drained. Even if those maids muster up the courage to agree, we can't trust them with it. Where on earth can we find someone willing to give their blood for her?"

[P022] "What then about me?"

[P023] A familiar voice was heard from the sofa across the room. That voice brought me great relief and overwhelming joy. For it was the voice of Quincey Morris. Van Helsing looked startled and displeased by the sudden sound, but when I gladly called his name, his expression soon softened and sparkled with joy. "Quincey Morris!" I ran to him with my arms wide open.

[P024] "What brings you all the way here?" I cried out, feeling the warmth of his clasped hands.

[P025] "Because of Arthur."

[P026] He handed me a telegram.

[P027] "I haven't heard from Seward for three days, and my heart is burning with anxiety. I can't possibly leave my post. My father is still critically ill. Let me know how Lucy is right away. Do not delay. — Holmwood."

[P028] "It seems I arrived just in time. Just tell me what needs to be done."

[P029] Van Helsing strode over, looked straight into Quincey's eyes, held his hands, and said.

[P030] "When a woman is in trouble, there is nothing more precious than the blood of a brave man. You are a true man indeed. Even though the devil is trying his best to ensnare us, God sends the exact person needed in such a desperate moment."

[P031] We had to perform that gruesome surgery once again. I don't even want to recall that terrible process. Lucy had suffered a tremendous shock, and its aftermath was far more fatal than before. Even after a large blood transfusion, her body didn't respond as well as it used to. Her desperate struggle to not let go of the thread of life was so gruesome that it was hard to keep one's eyes open to watch. Fortunately, the functions of her heart and lungs slightly improved, and she gradually regained stability when Van Helsing injected morphine under her skin as before. Having collapsed in a swoon, she soon fell into a deep sleep.

[P032] While the professor stayed by her side, I went downstairs with Quincey Morris, sent a maid out, and had her pay the fare to the driver waiting outside. I offered Quincey a glass of wine, told him to get some shut-eye, and instructed the cook to prepare a hearty breakfast. Then suddenly remembering something, I turned my steps back to Lucy's room.

[P033] As I carefully opened the door and entered, Van Helsing was deep in thought, holding a page or two of letter paper in his hand. As if he had just finished reading the contents, he sat rubbing his forehead with his hand, looking like he was pondering over something. As if his doubts were resolved, his face clearly showed a look of satisfaction. He handed out the paper and said briefly. "It fell from her chest when we carried her to the bathtub."

[P034] Taking the paper, I stopped reading halfway and stared blankly at the professor. It was a long time before I could barely manage to speak. "What on earth is all this about? Has she gone mad, or what terrible threat is lurking?" My mind went blank, and I couldn't say another word. Van Helsing silently reached out, took back the paper, and said.

[P035] "Don't worry about it now. Just completely forget it for the time being. When the time comes, you'll naturally come to know everything. That's a matter for later. More importantly, what was it you were trying to tell me earlier?"

[P036] Only then coming back to my senses, I regained my composure and brought up the main issue.

[P037] "I came to discuss the matter of the death certificate. If we don't handle this situation wisely, we might end up being investigated by the coroner, and then we might even have to submit that paper as evidence. We must avoid an investigation at all costs. Should the police by any chance get involved, even if not for any other terrible reason, poor Lucy would surely die from the shock. Both you and I know that Mrs. Westenra usually suffered from a heart condition. Her former physician knows this fact too. So it would be better to process her cause of death as a heart attack. I will write the death certificate right away, submit it to the registrar, and bring the undertaker."

[P038] "Good, oh, my friend John! That's an excellent idea! Truly poor Miss Lucy is suffering surrounded by cruel enemies, but it is a relief that she has friends by her side who genuinely care for her. One, two, three, and even an old man like me, making four. Four men willingly gave their blood for her, didn't they? Ah, I know everything, friend John. Because my eyes are not blind! That's why I hold you even more dear! Now, go quickly."

[P039] When I came out to the hallway, Quincey Morris was pacing around. He was about to send a telegram to Arthur with the news of Mrs. Westenra's passing, adding that Lucy had also fallen ill but was now improving, and that Van Helsing and I were by her side. I told him my destination. Pushing my back to tell me to hurry, he added in a low voice.

[P040] "When you get back, Jack, you and I have something very important to discuss quietly just between us."

[P041] I nodded and went outside. The death registration was handled without any hitches, and I told the local undertaker to come to the mansion around evening to take measurements and order the coffin.

[P042] Returning to the mansion, Quincey was waiting for me. I told him I would first check on Lucy's condition and then come back, and went upstairs. She was still sound asleep, and the professor was keeping watch by her side without moving an inch from his spot earlier. Judging from him bringing his index finger to his lips, it seemed Lucy was about to wake up soon and he didn't want to disturb her natural recovery. I quietly went downstairs and led Quincey into the morning room. Since the blinds were drawn up, the room was much brighter than the other rooms in the mansion, and the gloomy atmosphere was less. When we were left alone, he spoke heavily.

[P043] "Jack Seward, I know well that it's not my place to interfere, but this is by no means an ordinary situation. As you know, I truly loved that girl and even thought of marriage. Although all of that came to nothing, I still cannot turn a blind eye to the suffering she is going through. What on earth is happening to Lucy? When you guys came in just now, I heard that Dutch professor—I can recognize he's a truly great old gentleman—say that another blood transfusion is needed and that you are both exhausted. It's only natural for doctors to keep their patient's condition a secret, and I know well that one shouldn't pry into private medical opinions. But this is not an ordinary matter. Whatever it is, I too have done my duty by sharing my blood. Haven't I?"

[P044] "You are right." When I answered, he continued.

[P045] "I presume both you and Van Helsing have already shared the blood I gave today. Haven't you?"

[P046] "We have."

[P047] "Art must have been among them too. When I saw him at the country estate four days ago, his complexion was terrible. In the past, when I was staying on the pampas of South America, a mare I cherished dearly lost her life overnight. A vampire bat bit her neck and sucked her blood all night, and the mare was so drained of blood that she couldn't even stand on her own feet. In the end, I had to shoot her myself to relieve her pain. Since then, I've never seen anyone's blood dry up so quickly in an instant like Art's. Jack, tell me as much as you can without breaking your medical confidentiality. Arthur was the first person to give his blood. Am I right?"

[P048] All the while he was speaking, this poor friend's face was filled with anxiety. In the midst of the terrible suffering the woman he loved was going through, his complete ignorance of the truth behind the incident was driving him into a greater hell. His heart was torn to shreds and bleeding, and it was evident that he was barely holding his crumbling mind together with his noble and strong masculine nature. I hesitated for a moment, unable to speak hastily. It was due to the sense of responsibility that I should not reveal with my own mouth the secret the professor was trying to keep. However, he had already noticed too many things, and his guess touched upon the truth. Since there was no reason to hide the truth, I answered briefly, borrowing his words. "You are right."

[P049] "How long has this terrible thing been going on?"

[P050] "For about ten days."

[P051] "Ten days! Jack Seward, then that means the blood of four strapping men has been pouring into the body of that slender young lady we cherish so much for ten days. Look here, with that small frame, she wouldn't even be able to handle all that immense blood." He came close to me and whispered in an agitated, raspy voice. "What on earth sucked all that blood?"

[P052] I shook my head heavily. "That is exactly the problem. Even Van Helsing is on the verge of going mad over this, and I too am completely helpless. I can't even guess. The elaborate plans we set up to protect Lucy went awry every time due to trivial variables. But there won't be a mistake like that ever again. Whether the whole situation improves, or irreversibly heads toward a catastrophe, we plan to stay here."

[P053] Quincey held out his hand with a stiff expression. "I will join you too. If you and that Dutch professor just tell me what to do, I will do anything."

[P054] When Lucy woke up in the late afternoon, she first fumbled around her chest and, surprisingly, pulled out the exact same paper Van Helsing had shown me. The prudent professor, fearing she might be shocked to find the paper missing when she woke up, seemed to have quietly put it back in its place. Looking back and forth between Van Helsing and me keeping watch by her side, Lucy seemed relieved. But then, as she looked around and realized where she was lying, she suddenly trembled all over. Bursting into bitter tears, she covered her pale face with her slender hands. We both instantly understood what her action meant. She had fully realized her mother's death. We soothed and comforted her with all the words we could muster. Perhaps our sincere sympathy was of some comfort as she nodded, but she still remained in deep grief and sobbed silently for a long time. We promised that from now on at least one of us would stay by her side day and night, and at those words she seemed somewhat relieved.

[P055] Around dusk, Lucy fell into a light sleep. Then a truly bizarre sight occurred. While sound asleep, she took that paper out from her bosom and tore it right in half. Van Helsing quickly approached and snatched the torn pieces of paper. Nevertheless, as if she were holding paper in her hands, she continued to mime tearing something to shreds in the air. Finally, perhaps thinking she had torn the paper completely, she raised her hands high and spread them out as if scattering the pieces in the air. Van Helsing, looking startled, furrowed his thick eyebrows and fell into deep thought, but in the end said nothing.

[P056] *       *       *       *       *

[P057] September 19.—All last night she slept fitfully, extremely afraid of falling asleep, and every time she woke up, her energy was noticeably depleted. The professor and I took turns keeping watch by her side all night, and we did not leave her alone for even a single second. Quincey Morris didn't express it with his own mouth, but I knew he was pacing around the inside and outside of the mansion, patrolling with wide open eyes all night long.

[P058] As the morning sunlight brightly illuminated the room, it was clearly revealed how ruthlessly poor Lucy's body had weakened. She couldn't even hold her head up properly, and even the few sips of liquid food she swallowed with difficulty seemed to bring no improvement. She intermittently lost consciousness and fell asleep, and Van Helsing and I noticed the eerie difference between when she was asleep and when she was awake. When asleep, Lucy looked even more haggard, but strangely seemed full of energy, and her breathing was much more even. Between her slightly parted lips, her pale gums were pushed back, making her teeth gleam unusually long and sharp compared to usual. On the other hand, when she woke up and regained consciousness, even though she was standing on the threshold of death, her usual sweet and gentle eyes returned, and she reverted to her completely original self. Around the afternoon, she anxiously called for Arthur, and we hurriedly sent a telegram. Quincey went out to the station to meet him.

[P059] When Arthur arrived, it was already close to 6 p.m. The warm, red sunset light came in through the window, breathing a faint flush into Lucy's pale cheeks. Facing Lucy lying on the sickbed, Arthur could barely breathe due to overwhelming sorrow, and we too were choked up and couldn't say a word. As time went by, the frequency of her falling asleep or slipping into a coma increased, and even the time we could talk to her was drastically shrinking. However, Arthur's presence seemed to act as a powerful stimulant for her. Surprisingly, she regained her energy and spoke to him with a bright smile we hadn't seen once since we arrived. Arthur, too, desperately suppressing his sorrow, answered in the brightest and most cheerful voice he could, trying his best to erase the despairing atmosphere by her side.

[P060] It is now heading towards 1 a.m. Arthur and Van Helsing are keeping watch by Lucy. I intend to change shifts with them in 15 minutes, and am using the remaining spare time to leave this record on Lucy's phonograph. They plan to get some sleep until 6 a.m. Unfortunately, the poor child is failing to recover her strength in the end. Because the terrible shock was too cruel to endure, I fear our desperate watch will also come to an end tomorrow. Oh Lord, please look after us.

[P065] Report from Patrick Hennessey, M.D., M.R.C.S., to John Seward, M.D.

[P066] "September 20.

[P067] "Dear Doctor,—

[P068] "As per your request, I enclose a report on all the circumstances you entrusted to me. Regarding the patient Renfield, I have something to add. He had another fit. It almost turned into a terrible situation, but thank heavens there were no casualties. This afternoon, two laborers drove a transport wagon to the empty house adjacent to our hospital—the very place where, as you may remember, the patient fled and hid twice before. Perhaps it was their first time here, the men stopped the wagon in front of the hospital gate to ask the porter for directions. Having finished dinner, I was smoking and looking out the study window, and I saw one of them walking towards the hospital building. As he passed under the window of Renfield's room, the patient inside suddenly started screaming at the top of his lungs at him and pouring out all sorts of unspeakable curses. The man, who looked like a fairly decent person, tried to end it by retorting, 'Shut your mouth, you foul-mouthed beggar.' However, our patient jumped up and down wildly, claiming that the man was trying to rob and kill him, and that even if he had to be hanged for it, he would stop him himself. I opened the window and gestured to the man to ignore him. Only then did the man look around and realize he was in a mental hospital, click his tongue, and say. 'My God, sir. I shouldn't care what kind of nonsense a bloody mental hospital lunatic spouts. I just feel sorry for you and the director, sir, who have to live under the same roof with such beastly scoundrels.' Then he asked for directions very politely, and I told him the location of the empty house's gate. He left, leaving behind the threats and curses pouring from our patient. Renfield is usually a very polite person, and apart from violent fits, he had never caused a disturbance like this, so I went down to check what on earth had provoked him so much. Surprisingly, however, his face inside the room was very calm and affable. I discreetly brought up the commotion from earlier, but he rather tilted his head asking what I was talking about, and acted as if he had completely forgotten the incident, almost fooling even me. But unfortunately, that was just another proof showing his cunning. Less than 30 minutes later, he caused another incident. This time, he smashed the window of his room and ran madly towards the driveway. Thinking he had made up his mind with some scheme, I called the attendants and chased after him. The moment I saw the wagon from earlier, full of heavy wooden boxes, coming down the road from afar, my ominous premonition hit the mark. The two laborers were wiping off sweat with their faces flushed red, as if they had finished a hard day's work. Before I could even approach, the patient pounced on them like a beast, dragged one of the men on the wagon down to the ground, and blindly started bashing his head against the floor. If I hadn't torn him off at that very moment, he surely would have beaten the man to death on the spot. The other laborer on the wagon jumped down and mercilessly struck Renfield's head with a thick whip handle. Despite the terrible blow that felt like it would break bones, he didn't care and grabbed that man too, dragging us back and forth as if we were kittens, and fought madly against the three of us. As you know, I don't easily lose in terms of strength, and the two laborers were also burly men. At first, we too kept our mouths shut and held on tenaciously. But as we pinned him to the ground and the attendants rushed in to put a straitjacket on him, he started screaming and wailing. 'I will ruin everything! You can't rob me! I won't let you bleed me dry! I will fight for my master and Lord!' He endlessly poured out this incoherent nonsense. The process of dragging him back to the ward and locking him in an isolation room padded with soft walls was truly a continuation of hardships. Hardy, one of the attendants, broke his finger. I gave him first aid right away, so you don't need to worry too much.

[P069] "At first, the two laborers were furious, demanding immediate compensation for the damages, and jumped up and down saying they would impose every punishment the law allows. However, their fierce threats contained a pathetic excuse to cover up the shame that two strapping men were helplessly overpowered by a single skinny lunatic. They raised their voices saying that if they hadn't exhausted all their energy carrying heavy boxes, they would have knocked him down in a single blow. Furthermore, they made the excuse that their throats were burning dry due to the hard, dusty work, but they couldn't even use their strength because even the nearest tavern from the worksite was too far away. I saw right through their true intentions, offered them each a glass of strong, dark rum, and pressed a one-pound gold coin into each of their hands. Then, as if they were never angry, they let go of their anger and laughed heartily, saying that if it's a drink offered after meeting an 'awesomely good person' like the boss, they would be more than willing to fight an even worse lunatic. I wrote down the names and affiliations of the two men just in case. Their identities are as follows: Jack Smollet, living at George Road, The Dings, Rents, Great Walworth, and Thomas Snelling, living at Guide Court, Peter Pallis Row, Bethnal Green. Both belong to the Harris & Sons transport company located at Orange Masters Yard, Soho.

[P070] "I will report any unusual occurrences happening here without leaving anything out, and if an urgent matter arises, I will send a telegram immediately.

[P071] "Dear Doctor, I wish you peace.

[P072] "Faithful friend,

[P073] "Patrick Hennessey."

[P077] "To my dearest Lucy in the world, —

[P078] "A very tragic thing has happened to us. Mr. Hawkins suddenly passed away. Some people might think we wouldn't be so sad since we didn't share a single drop of blood. But because we genuinely followed him like a real father, it is a great shock to me, as if I lost my own father. Since I was born without even knowing my parents' faces, the death of that warm and sweet elder breaks my heart even more. Jonathan is extremely heartbroken. Having lost a wonderful person who was a strong pillar of support for him all his life, and who left him a massive inheritance on his final journey that ordinary people like us dare not even imagine, his sorrow must be great. But it's not just for that reason that Jonathan is having such a hard time. Such a massive sense of responsibility suddenly weighing down on his shoulders seems to make him very afraid and at a loss. He has suddenly lost his confidence and is shaken. I am trying my best to comfort my husband somehow, and my husband is also steeling his heart while looking at me. But every time the scars of those terrible memories from before seem to torment Jonathan like this, it breaks my heart so much. Oh, how the pure and strong nature of my husband, who thanks to Mr. Hawkins's grace was able to rise from a lowly clerk to a respectable master, somehow got wounded and broken by that terrifying incident! The heavens are truly indifferent. I'm so sorry to bring you such gloomy news at a time when you must be overflowing with happiness, my love. But Lucy, even though I'm trying hard to smile and pretend to be brave in front of Jonathan on the outside, on the inside I am burning up too. Without a single person by my side to openly lean on, I couldn't bear it without pouring this out to you like this. I have to go to London the day after tomorrow for the funeral, and my steps are already heavy. Poor Mr. Hawkins left a will saying he wanted to be buried next to his father. Since he had no family, Jonathan must naturally act as the chief mourner. Even if just for a few minutes, I will make time and rush to see you, my love. Sorry for only rambling on with gloomy stories. May all blessings be with you.

[P079] "Loving you,

[P080] "Mina Harker."

[P081] Dr. Seward's Diary.

[P082] September 20.—Only firm will and habit make me sit at my desk tonight and write this diary. I am so miserable, devastated, and completely exhausted with this world and everything in my life. Even if the angel of death flapped its wings behind my back right now, I feel like I could accept it calmly. In fact, recently that angel has been hovering around us with some ominous purpose, flapping its creepy wings. Lucy's mother, Arthur's father, and now... sigh, I'd better just record what I need to do.

[P083] I handed over the care of Lucy to Van Helsing and safely changed shifts. We advised Arthur to get some shut-eye as well, but he stubbornly refused. It was only when I repeatedly persuaded him that he must help us so Lucy wouldn't suffer during the day, and that we shouldn't all collapse at once from lack of sleep, that he barely nodded. Van Helsing treated him very affectionately. "Come here, my child," he said. "Come with me. You are very sick and weak right now. Haven't you endured not only physical fatigue but also terrible sorrow and mental agony that we cannot fathom? You shouldn't be left alone. Because the moment you are left alone, fear and anxiety will swallow you up. Let's go to my sitting room where a fire is burning warmly and there are two soft sofas. You lie on one and I will lie on the other, and even if we don't exchange words, or even if you fall asleep first, just being in the same space will be a great comfort to each other." Arthur looked back sorrowfully at Lucy's face, which had turned paler than a pillow, and followed Van Helsing out. Lucy was lying without even a slight movement, and I slowly looked around the room to check if everything was treated properly. I could tell at a glance that the professor had firmly placed garlic in this room just like the other rooms. A strong smell of garlic vibrated around all the window frames, and a roughly woven wreath of garlic flowers was placed over the silk handkerchief Van Helsing had wrapped around her. Lucy was breathing heavily, and with her mouth open revealing her pale gums, the sight was terribly wretched. In the faintly flickering dim light, her teeth looked strangely longer and sharper than when I saw them in the morning. Perhaps it was a trick of the lighting, but especially her canine teeth looked pointed enough to give me goosebumps. When I sat by the bed, she soon tossed and turned, showing signs of unease. At that very moment, a dull flapping sound of something striking the glass was heard from the window. I muffled my footsteps, approached, and peeked outside through the gap in the blinds. Under the brightly shining full moon, I saw a giant bat circling and flapping its wings. The creature, seemingly drawn to the faint light leaking from the room, occasionally tapped the window with its fluttering wings. When I returned to the bedside, Lucy had torn the garlic flower wreath from her neck while tossing and turning. I hurriedly put the wreath back on and sat in the chair, watching her all night.

[P084] Before long, Lucy opened her eyes, and I fed her the light liquid food Van Helsing prescribed. She moistened her lips just a little, with great difficulty. That tenacious vitality that had desperately struggled for life even in unconsciousness until now seemed completely extinguished. The surprising thing is that the moment she regained consciousness, she urgently pulled the garlic flowers and embraced them to her chest. While breathing heavily and slipping into a coma, she would unconsciously tear the flowers off roughly, but when she regained consciousness, she repeatedly did this bizarre action of desperately holding onto the flowers all night long.

[P085] At 6 a.m., Van Helsing came in to take over. Arthur was still sound asleep, oblivious to the world, and the professor mercifully let him rest without waking him. I heard a suppressed gasp from the professor as he examined Lucy's face. He whispered sharply. "Pull up the blinds! I need light!" Then he bent down closely until his face almost touched Lucy's nose and carefully examined her condition. The moment he removed the garlic flower wreath and lifted the silk handkerchief from Lucy's neck, he flinched back and let out a suppressed moan. "My God (Mein Gott)!" I also bent down and examined the nape of her neck, and the moment I saw it with my own eyes, a chilling shudder that reached my bones wrapped around my entire body.

[P086] The two bite marks that had clearly remained on her neck had vanished completely.

[P087] Van Helsing stood frozen like a stone in that spot for a full five minutes, staring down at her intently. Eventually, he turned to me and said in a grave voice.

[P088] "She is dying. There is not much time left now. It makes a tremendous difference whether she closes her eyes in her right mind or dies in this terrible coma. Keep that in mind. Hurry and wake the poor boy up so he can be with her at the end. We promised him, and he trusts us."

[P089] I went across to the dining room and woke Arthur. He was bewildered in his sleep, and when he saw the morning sunlight leaking through the shutters, he was startled, wondering if he was too late. While comforting him that Lucy was still sound asleep, I conveyed as carefully as possible that both Van Helsing and I had an intuition that the end was near. Arthur knelt down on the floor by the sofa with his face buried in his hands, and laying there for about a minute, prayed with bitter tears, his shoulders heaving. I held his hand tightly and helped him up. "Now, my old friend," I said. "Gather all your indomitable courage. Because that is the only way to make Lucy's journey peaceful."

[P090] When we entered Lucy's room, Van Helsing, meticulously and carefully tidying up the surroundings as usual, had made an effort to make her look as comfortable and beautiful as possible. He even combed Lucy's disheveled hair, smoothing it so that her signature dazzling golden waves naturally flowed down over the pillow. When we approached, she slowly opened her eyes and whispered affectionately, looking at Arthur.

[P091] "Arthur! Oh, my love, I'm so glad you came!" As Arthur bent down to kiss her forehead, Van Helsing hurriedly stopped him. "No!" he whispered firmly. "Not yet! Just hold her hand tightly. For now, that will be comfort enough."

[P092] Arthur knelt down and tightly held her small, pale hand. Lying on the sickbed, Lucy looked infinitely sublime and beautiful, as if an angel had descended. Before long, her two eyes gently closed and she fell into a deep sleep. Like an exhausted child, her thin chest rose and fell slightly along with her wheezing breath.

[P093] But that bizarre change I had witnessed last night raised its head again in her unconsciousness. Her breathing grew rough like a beast, her lips parted, and her pale gums rolled back, revealing teeth that were sharper and more gleaming than ever before. Opening her dim, unfocused eyes like a sleepwalker, she whispered softly in a chillingly sensual and sticky voice that I had never heard before.

[P094] "Arthur! Oh, my love, I'm so glad you came! Kiss me!" Arthur, as if possessed, leaned in to kiss her. But Van Helsing, appalled by her voice as much as I was, threw himself like a beast and grabbed Arthur's nape with both hands. It's hard to tell where such strength sprang from his old body, but Van Helsing ruthlessly yanked him back and flung him into a corner of the room.

[P095] "Your life depends on it!" The professor shouted, the veins in his neck bulging. "Not only for your soul, but for her soul too, absolutely not!" Van Helsing blocked their path, glaring murderously like a beast that had lost its young.

[P096] Arthur stood blankly, completely out of his mind with shock. In the fleeting moment he was swept by a violent urge to resist, facing the terrible reality, he just froze in place with bated breath.

[P097] Van Helsing and I fixed our eyes on Lucy. We clearly saw the shadow of uncontrollable anger sweep across her haggard face like a spasm. The sound of sharp teeth clicking together echoed chillingly. Then, her eyes closed as if rolling back, and she breathed heavily as if tearing at her chest.

[P098] Before long, Lucy opened her eyes again with her original gentle and kind eyes. Reaching out her slender, pale hand, she pulled Van Helsing's large, rough hand and kissed it. "My true friend," she pleaded in a faint voice filled with indescribable sorrow. "My true friend, and Arthur's friend! Please protect him, and grant me rest!"

[P099] "I swear!" The professor knelt by her sickbed and solemnly replied, raising one hand high as if swearing to God. Then he turned around and said to Arthur. "Come here, my child. Hold her hand tightly with your hand, and kiss her forehead. Just once."

[P100] Instead of their lips, their deep and sad eyes met, and thus the two bid their eternal farewell.

[P101] As Lucy's eyes completely closed, Van Helsing, who had been watching with bated breath, firmly grabbed Arthur's arm and made him step back.

[P102] Lucy's breath, which had grown rough and shallow again, suddenly stopped as if it snapped.

[P103] "It's all over," Van Helsing said in a low voice. "She's gone!"

[P104] I supported the devastated Arthur and took him to the living room. Collapsing onto the sofa, he covered his face with both hands and sobbed uncontrollably as if the world was falling apart, his back shaking.

[P105] When I returned to the room, Van Helsing was looking down at the corpse with a graver and stiffer face than ever before. But a bizarre change had occurred to her body. Death was rather returning the beauty it had taken from her. Her sunken cheeks and forehead regained their soft curves, and even her lips, which had been pale and bloodless, took on a lively color. It was as if the remaining blood heading to the heart was trying to cover the cold and cruel traces of death with a little warmth.

[P106] "We only thought she was asleep, but in fact she was dying, and only after she breathed her last does she finally look like she is peacefully asleep."

[P107] I approached Van Helsing's side and said in a low voice.

[P108] "Ah, poor girl. At last all her suffering is over and she has found rest. Now it is all over!"

[P109] Then the professor turned his head, looked straight at me, and answered in a grim and chilling voice.

[P110] "That is not so. Ah! It is certainly not so. This is merely the beginning!"

[P111] When I asked what he meant, he just shook his head heavily and added briefly.

[P112] "There is still nothing we can do. We just have to wait and see."
