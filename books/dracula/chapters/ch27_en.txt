[P002] Mina Harker's Diary

[P003a] November 1.—We drove at a fairly fast pace all day long. The horses willingly ran at top speed, as if they knew they were being well treated. Because a long carriage journey required changing exhausted horses for fresh ones, we changed horses several times, and each time we experienced unchanging kindness, which gave us hope that the rest of our journey would be smooth. Dr. Van Helsing spoke very little. He only told the farmers briefly that we had to hurry to Bistritz, and handed them generous amounts of money for providing fresh horses. We got warm soup, coffee, or tea, and immediately set out on the road again. This is a lovely countryside full of every beauty imaginable. The people were brave, strong, and simple, and seemed to have good hearts. But they were extremely superstitious. At the first house we stopped, the woman serving us saw the scar on my forehead and crossed herself. Then she held out two fingers toward me, saying it would ward off the evil eye that curses others. They seemed to intentionally put a lot of garlic in our food to avoid the evil eye, but I absolutely despise garlic. After that, I was careful not to take off my hat or veil so as not to arouse unnecessary suspicion. We are moving very quickly, and since there is no separate coachman to spread rumors, we are staying one step ahead of the village gossip. But their blind fear of the evil eye seems likely to follow us closely throughout our journey.

[P003b] The Professor was tireless. Even though he didn't rest for a moment all day, he made sure that I at least slept well. At sunset, the Professor hypnotized me, and he said I answered that I heard darkness, the lapping of water, and the creaking of wood, just as usual. So our enemy is definitely still on the river. My heart sinks when I think of my husband Jonathan, but somehow the fear for him and myself has now vanished. I am writing this diary while waiting for the horses to be readied at a farmhouse. Dr. Van Helsing is sleeping. Poor, precious man. He looks very tired, old, and haggard, but his lips are firmly shut like a conqueror. Even in his sleep, he is full of resolute determination. When the carriage starts again, I will take the reins and let the Professor rest. We still have a few rough days of travel ahead of us, and I intend to firmly tell him that he must not collapse when we need his strength the most.... Finally, all preparations are complete. We are departing soon.

[P004_005] November 2, Morning.—My plan was successful, and we took turns driving the carriage through the night. Now, a dazzlingly bright but freezing morning has broken. A strange heaviness hangs in the air. I call it heaviness for lack of a better word, but it is a terrible pressure that feels like it is crushing the breath out of both of us. It is biting cold, and only the multiple layers of thick winter fur barely keep our body heat in. At dawn, Professor Van Helsing hypnotized me. He said I answered that I heard darkness, the creaking of wood, and loud rushing water, so it seems the current is getting rougher the further up the river we go. I earnestly pray that my beloved husband is not facing more terrible danger than necessary, but now everything is entirely in God's hands.

[P006_007] November 2, Night.—Drove the carriage all day. The further we advanced, the rougher and more desolate the scenery became. The massive ridges of the Carpathian Mountains, which only looked low and distant beyond the horizon from Veresti, now seem to soar dizzyingly high, surrounding us on all sides. We both seem in good spirits on the outside. We are trying hard to cheer each other up, and it seems we are finding comfort in doing so ourselves. Dr. Van Helsing said we will reach the Borgo Pass tomorrow morning. There are hardly any civilian houses to be found now, so the Professor said we will likely have to make do with the horses we have until the end. Because the prospect of finding fresh horses ahead is bleak. Fortunately, thanks to getting two additional horses, we are now driving a rough four-horse carriage. These grateful beasts are patient and gentle, giving us no trouble at all. There is no chance of running into other travelers, so even a woman like me can easily hold the reins. We plan to arrive at the pass while the sun is shining brightly. Because we don't want to arrive even a little early and wander in the dark. So we are taking our time, taking turns resting fully, and conserving our strength. Ah, what fate awaits us tomorrow? We are walking into the very hell where my poor, beloved husband suffered such terrible agony. I pray with folded hands that God will guide us safely, and watch over my husband on the threshold of death and all those we love. As I am now, I don't even dare deserve to stand before God's eyes. Alas! Having been bitten by a vampire, I am nothing but an unclean being in God's eyes. I will live forever defiled until God accepts me again as a clean soul that has not incurred His wrath.

[P007b] Abraham Van Helsing's Memorandum.

[P008_009] November 4.—I leave this writing to my old and true friend, Dr. John Seward in Purfleet, London, just in case I don't return alive. I only hope the records I leave behind can explain the situation. Morning has broken. I am writing this sitting by the campfire I kept burning all night with Madam Mina's help. It's cold, so very cold. The heavy, ashen sky, crushed by the terrible cold, looks as if it will pour snow at any moment. Once it starts snowing, it will only pile up over the frozen ground throughout the winter. It seems this terrible cold has also badly affected Madam Mina. Yesterday she complained of a heavy head all day and was not her usual self at all. She sleeps, sleeps, and keeps sleeping! She, who is usually so alert, literally didn't lift a single finger all day today. She even seems to have lost her appetite. She hasn't left a single line of record in that little diary she used to meticulously fill whenever she had a spare moment. I have an ominous premonition that something unusual is happening. But tonight, she seems much more lively. Having slept soundly all day, she seems to have regained her strength and recovered. She has returned to her lovely, bright self like before. I tried to hypnotize her at sunset, but alas! It was useless. The power of hypnosis had noticeably weakened day by day, and tonight it failed completely. Well, everything is left to God's will. Whatever that will may be, and wherever it may lead us!

[P009b] Now I must continue the record. Since Madam Mina is not writing her diary in shorthand, I must leave behind the day's events without missing a single detail, even if it is in this cumbersome and old-fashioned way.

[P010_011] We arrived at the Borgo Pass just after sunrise yesterday morning. As the dawn light seeped in, I prepared for the hypnosis. I stopped the carriage and stepped outside to avoid being interrupted by anyone. I laid out some furs to make a spot for her, and Madam Mina lay down on it, falling into a trance as usual. But it took longer than usual to fall asleep, and the hypnosis didn't last long. She answered that she saw darkness and swirling water, just like before. Then she woke up looking bright and radiant, and we hurried on our way, soon reaching the pass. Right at that time, in that place, she was suddenly swept up in intense passion. As if a new power was guiding her, she pointed to a side road and spoke firmly.

[P011b] "This is the way."

[P012_013] "How do you know?" I asked.

[P014] "Of course I know," she replied, and after hesitating for a moment, added. "Didn't my husband Jonathan travel this road and record it vividly in his diary?"

[P015] At first I thought it was somewhat strange, but soon realized there was only one such side road. It was a deserted place, quite different from the wide, solid carriage road leading from Bukovina to Bistritz.

[P016] So we went down this road. Whenever the side road split into several branches, the paths were so neglected and covered in snow that we couldn't even tell where the road was, but the horses uniquely seemed to know the way. I left the reins to the horses, and they silently moved forward. Before long, we began to discover one by one the landmarks Jonathan had written about in his amazing diary. And we kept going for what seemed like an endless time. At first, I told Madam Mina to get some shut-eye, and she managed to fall asleep shortly after. She slept the whole time. Eventually, growing suspicious, I tried to wake her up. But she just kept sleeping, and no matter how hard I tried, she wouldn't wake up. Considering how much she had suffered, I didn't want to force her awake, thinking sleep might be her only comfort right now. Then it seems I dozed off too. I woke up with a start, feeling a guilty conscience as if I had committed some terrible wrong, only to find myself sitting upright holding the reins while the grateful horses continued trudging along. I looked down, and Madam Mina was still fast asleep. It was already sunset, and the bright yellow sunlight pouring over the snow was casting our long, massive shadows over the rugged mountain range. We climbed up and up the steep road. The entire landscape unfolding before us was so rough and desolate, as if it were the end of the world.

[P017] Then I woke Madam Mina. She woke up without much difficulty this time, and I attempted hypnosis. But she didn't fall asleep. She acted as if I had become invisible. I kept trying, but at some point I realized that darkness had completely enveloped us. I looked around, and the sun was already gone. Madam Mina suddenly burst into laughter, and I looked at her in surprise. She was fully awake and looked more lively than I had ever seen her since that terrible night we first set foot in the Count's Carfax estate. I was surprised and anxious, but because she took care of me so affectionately and attentively, I forgot all my fears. When I built a fire with the firewood we brought, she prepared the food while I untied the horses, hitched them in a place sheltered from the wind, and fed them. When I returned to the campfire, she had my dinner ready. I asked her to eat with me, but she smiled and said she had already eaten because she was too hungry to wait. I was not at all pleased and an ominous suspicion bloomed, but I said nothing for fear of frightening her. By her side, I finished my meal alone.

[P018_021] Then we lay down next to the campfire wrapped in furs, and I told her to sleep, saying I would stand guard. But soon I completely forgot even the fact that I was supposed to stand guard, and when I suddenly came to my senses, she was lying quietly but wide awake, staring at me with eyes shining more bizarrely than ever. That happened a couple more times, and eventually, I fell into quite a deep sleep before morning broke. Upon waking, I tried to hypnotize her again. Alas! She obediently closed her eyes but in the end failed to fall asleep. It was only when the sun rose high towards the middle of the sky that sleep finally came to her, and she slept so deeply that she showed no signs of waking up. I had to harness the horses, pack all the luggage, and carry the fast-asleep lady in my arms to lay her down in the carriage. She was still sleeping, and her sleeping face looked much healthier and flush with red color than before. I terribly hated the sight of that. I am afraid, so very afraid! I am afraid of everything, even my own thoughts, but I must walk this path to the very end. Because the gamble we placed our bets on was a matter of life and death, no, even the salvation of our souls, and we could never back down here.

[P021b] November 5, Morning.—I must record everything without a single inch of error. Because when you first read this, even though you and I have weathered all sorts of bizarre events together, you might think that I, Van Helsing, have finally gone mad. That my reason has finally snapped, crushed by endless terror and prolonged tension.

[P021c] All day yesterday, we delved deep into the rugged mountain range and entered an endlessly rough and desolate land. Massive, menacing cliffs and countless roaring waterfalls were scattered about like the aftermath of a wild party thrown by Mother Nature. Madam Mina slept like the dead. Even as I satisfied my hunger alone, I couldn't bear to wake her up to feed her. I was suddenly terrified that the fatal curse harbored by this demonic place was seeping into the lady, who had shared the Count's terrible blood. 'Alright,' I muttered to myself. 'If she sleeps all day, I will definitely stay awake all night tonight.' Running along the rough, ancient dirt road, I unwittingly dropped my head and fell into a deep sleep. When I opened my eyes with a start, overcome by terrible guilt once again, quite some time had already passed. Madam Mina was still asleep, and the sun was beginning to set. But the surrounding scenery had completely changed. The suffocating mountain range had receded, and we had reached the top of a steeply rising hill, and right on that very peak towered the eerie castle Jonathan had described in his diary. I felt both ecstasy and a terror that made my hair stand on end. Because whatever way fate flowed, the end was finally approaching.

[P022_025] I woke Madam Mina and tried hypnosis one more time, but alas! It was already too late and useless. Even after the sun set, the light reflecting off the snow kept the surroundings quite bright for a while, but before even that twilight vanished and complete darkness fell, I untied the horses, tied them in a place sheltered from the wind, and fed them. I built a campfire, laid out soft blankets next to it to make a spot, and Madam Mina, who had fully awakened and grown even more beautiful, came and sat down comfortably. I prepared food and offered it to her, but she refused, saying she wasn't hungry. Knowing it would be a waste of effort to force her to eat, I didn't press her any further. But since I needed to keep my strength up, I ate my meal. To prepare for whatever emergencies might lie ahead, I had to be strong myself. Then, suppressing my fear of what was to come, I drew a wide circle around the spot where she was sitting. Then I crushed the holy bread, the Host, and sprinkled it along that circle, casting a perfect barrier. She sat motionlessly like a dead person the whole time, and didn't utter a single word with a face paler than snow. But when I approached, she clung to me desperately, and I could feel clearly that her poor soul was trembling agonizingly from head to toe like an aspen tree. When she had calmed down a bit, I asked.

[P025b] "Would you like to come a little closer to the fire?" It was a trick to test how far she could move right now. She obediently stood up from her seat, but taking only a single step forward, she froze in place as if her whole body had been paralyzed.

[P025c] "Why won't you come closer?" I asked, and she shook her head and slumped back down in her original spot. Then, her eyes wide open like someone who had just woken up from sleep, she looked at me and blurted out indifferently.

[P026] "I can't go!" She left it at that and clamped her mouth shut again. I cheered inwardly. Because if even she couldn't cross the barrier, it meant the evil monsters we feared so much couldn't cross this line either. Her body might be in danger, but her soul was perfectly safe inside here!

[P027_028] Before long, the horses began to scream as if being torn apart, struggling as if they would break their tethers and run away. I quickly approached and calmed them down. Relieved at my touch, they let out soft cries, licked my hands, and remained quiet for a while. Until the bone-chilling hour before dawn, when all of Mother Nature falls into its deepest slumber, I comforted them all night long, and each time they barely regained their calm. When the pitch-black, freezing time arrived, the campfire slowly began to die down, and I was about to step out to add more firewood. Right at that moment, a fierce blizzard rolled in, bringing a cold fog. Due to the unique aura of snow-covered places, a bizarre light lingered somehow even in the thick darkness. The swirling blizzard and fog tangled together, seemingly transforming into the shapes of women approaching, dragging long robes. Amidst the terrifying silence as if everything were dead, only the horses were cowering in ultimate terror, howling desperately.

[P029] Uncontrollable fear washed over me, but the thought that it was safe inside this circle where I stood soon steadied my mind. I forced myself to blame my imagination, telling myself that these grotesque visions were merely hallucinations born of the thick darkness and the extreme anxiety I had been enduring. It felt as if the chilling experiences Jonathan had recorded in his diary were rummaging through my mind and mocking me. Because the fluttering snowflakes and fog were swirling around, perfectly recreating the blurry silhouettes of those women who had tried to kiss Jonathan in the past. Then, the horses let out agonizing groans as if their flesh were being ripped off, and collapsed flat on the ground. They seemed to have frozen, entirely overcome with terror, unable even to muster the thought of running away. As these bizarre figures circled us and closed in, I couldn't bear my worry for my beloved Madam Mina. Looking back, she was watching me with a surprisingly calm face, smiling faintly. As I tried to step outside the barrier to build the fire, she grabbed the hem of my clothes tightly, pulling me back, and whispered in a low, faint voice like in a dream.

[P030_039] "No! You must never go outside. Because it is safe here!" I turned to her, looked straight into her eyes, and said.

[P039b] "But what about you? The one I worry about more than anything else is you!" At that, she burst into a low, bizarre laugh that had not a shred of reality to it, and replied.

[P040_041] "You worry about me! Why on earth do you worry about me? Out of everyone in this world, I am the one they will keep completely safe without touching a single hair." Just as I was wondering what her pointed words meant, a sudden fierce wind blew, making the campfire flare up, and in that light, the vivid red scar stamped on her forehead flashed. Alas! Only then did I realize everything. Even if I hadn't realized it then, I would have soon known. Because the shapes of swirling snow and fog hovered right outside the holy barrier and came right up to our noses. Then those shapes slowly began to take on a distinct physical form. If God had not taken away my reason, what I saw clearly with my own two eyes must be true. The very three vampire women who had greedily tried to kiss his neck at the Count's castle, as Jonathan said, revealed themselves right before my eyes in their true forms of flesh and blood. Their swaying, seductive figures, flashing cruel eyes, pure white teeth and vibrant red complexions, and voluptuous lips that seemed ready to burst. I recognized who they were at once. They constantly flashed blatant smiles at poor, precious Madam Mina. Along with a terrible laughter that tore through the silence of the night, they locked arms, pointed at her, and whispered in the very voice Jonathan had described. It was a terrifyingly sweet voice, like the ringing sound when you tap a crystal water glass.

[P041b] "Come here, sister. Come into our arms. Come here! Come here now!" I looked back at poor Madam Mina, paralyzed by a fear that felt like it would stop my heart. But alas! My heart burned like a flame with overwhelming joy. Because the terrible terror, disgust, and shock filling her lovely eyes were like a ray of hope to me. Thank God, it was clear she had not yet become a monster like them. I grabbed a piece of firewood nearby, held the Host high, and strode toward the campfire facing them. They stumbled backward, pushed back by my momentum, yet still let out low, chilling laughter. As I stoked the fire, I did not fear those wicked creatures in the slightest. Because I knew deep in my bones that inside this barrier where I stood, it was perfectly safe. Not only could they not lay a finger on me, armed as I was, but as long as she stayed inside the barrier, they could never touch her either. Just as they could not step inside the barrier, she too could not take a single step outside it. The horses had already ceased their agonizing groans and lay collapsed on the ground, stiff as stones. The silently falling snowflakes gradually covered them in white. I intuitively sensed that there would be no more terrible fear for the poor beasts.

[P041c] We stayed awake like that until the red hue of dawn seeped through the snow-covered, ashen twilight. We were completely isolated, gripped by bone-chilling fear, sorrow, and terrible terror, but when the dazzling sun finally began to peek over the horizon, we felt the relief of being alive again. As the first light of dawn illuminated the snow, those terrible figures melted away vainly into the swirling snow and fog, and the transparent, ghost-like shapes scattered away toward the castle in the distance and soon vanished without a trace.

[P041d] Once day had completely broken, I instinctively turned to attempt to hypnotize Madam Mina. But she had already fallen into a sleep so deep and sudden that even I could not possibly wake her. I tried to force a hypnosis on the fast-asleep lady, but she didn't show a single ounce of reaction. Finally, full morning had broken. But I am still afraid to make any hasty moves. I rekindled the fire and checked on the horses, but they had all already breathed their last. Because I have many terrible things to do here today, I intend to wait without moving until the sun rises high in the sky. Because in the cursed place I must go, a handful of sunlight will be the only shield to protect me, even if it is blurry, obscured by snow and fog.

[P041e] Only after I eat a hearty breakfast and fully regain my strength will I begin my terrible mission. Madam Mina is still sound asleep, and thank God, her sleeping face looks incredibly peaceful....

[P041g] Abraham Van Helsing's Memorandum (Afternoon)

[P042] November 5, Afternoon.—At least my mind is still intact. The process of proving that to myself was truly like hell, but anyway, I am simply grateful for God's mercy in holding onto my reason to the very end. Leaving Madam Mina safely asleep inside the holy barrier, I headed to the castle alone. The blacksmith's heavy hammer I had brought in the carriage all the way from Veresti proved its worth completely. Although the castle doors were all open, I smashed all the rusty hinges to pieces for fear of being trapped like a rat due to someone's malicious trick or an accidental closing of the doors. Here, the terrible experiences Jonathan had suffered served as a great guide for me. Slowly tracing Jonathan's diary entries, I finally found the old chapel, and I intuitively knew my terrible mission was waiting for me right here. The air was crushingly heavy, and the disgusting smell of sulfur that occasionally wafted up made my head dizzy. I couldn't even tell if the ringing in my ears was tinnitus or the sound of starving wolves howling from afar. At that moment, thinking of my beloved Madam Mina left all alone, I fell into a terrible dilemma.

[P043_052] Since I couldn't bear to bring her into this devil's lair, I had left her inside the holy barrier where vampires couldn't approach, but in reality, that place was also never safe from the threat of a starving wolf pack! But my life-risking mission was right here in this chapel, and I steeled my heart, deciding to accept it as unavoidable God's will if she were to meet a tragic end by the wolves. Because at worst, she would be torn apart by wolves and achieve true freedom. So I made a cruel decision purely for her sake. If it had been a matter of my own life alone, without a second thought, I would have chosen to enter the belly of a wolf and be torn to pieces rather than enjoy eternal life in a vampire's filthy grave! I braced myself and decided to continue my terrible task.

[P052b] I knew I had to find at least three graves where the vampires were lurking. After frantically searching and searching among the graves, I finally found one of them. Inside lay a female vampire, overflowing with vitality and voluptuous beauty, in a deep sleep, and I shuddered, trembling all over as if I were a murderer who came to butcher an innocent person. Alas, without a doubt, in the very distant past there must have been countless men who visited this place with a terrible mission like mine. And nine out of ten of them probably fell to their knees at this very last moment, unable to endure the heart-stopping terror and extreme nervous breakdown. Postponing and postponing the terrible ritual, they were eventually seduced and hypnotized by the suffocating beauty and fatal temptation exuded by these fallen undead monsters. Thus, they must have guarded her side, mesmerized, until the sun set and the vampire awoke from her long sleep. Soon, when the seductive eyes of that beautiful monster flew open and cast a bewitching gaze, and the luscious lips approached craving a sweet kiss—a weak human man would have had no choice but to helplessly crumble before that temptation. Thus, another poor victim would have been added to the vampire's lair, and the army of these terrible and eerie undead would have swelled its ranks just as much!...

[P053_054] Despite reeking of the exact same disgusting blood scent I smelled at the Count's terrible hideout, and despite lying in a dusty grave piled up over centuries, the mere fact that the wicked creature lay breathing right before my eyes emitted a powerful and fatal magic that shook my reason. Yes. Even I, Van Helsing, who was thoroughly armed with a clear purpose and boiling hatred, was severely shaken at that moment. A bizarre craving to somehow postpone this terrible ritual, which seemed to completely paralyze my reason and even suffocate my soul, was swallowing me up. Perhaps my mind was being eaten away due to severe sleep deprivation from staying up several nights, or perhaps due to the bizarre air of this chapel crushing my lungs. One thing is certain, I was slowly yielding to that terribly sweet fascination, falling into a hypnotic state with my eyes wide open. Right at that desperate moment, a long, low, incredibly sorrowful and mournful wail echoed through the quiet, snow-covered ridges. That sound snapped my blurred mind wide awake, like a sharp trumpet call waking a sleeping soldier. It was the voice of my beloved Madam Mina.

[P054b] I forced myself to pull it together and began the terrible task. When I pried open another tomb cover, another sister, a vampire with pitch-black hair, appeared. Remembering how I had almost been bewitched by the first vampire, I didn't even dare look her straight in the eye and hurriedly searched the next graves. Finally, inside a remarkably high and massive tomb, seemingly built for someone who was deeply loved by someone, I found the third sister, who was dazzlingly beautiful. It was the very blonde woman Jonathan described as having appeared when the fog condensed in the thick darkness. She was so dazzlingly radiant and bursting with sensuality that she wildly stimulated the possessiveness and protective instincts a man would naturally harbor, pushing me into an uncontrollable whirlwind of emotions. But thank God, that sorrowful wail squeezed out from the bottom of Madam Mina's soul still lingered in my ears. Before that fatal magic could swallow me whole, I snapped to my senses and hurriedly continued my harsh mission. By that time, I had searched all the graves in the chapel that I could look into, and recalling that there were only three undead visions that attacked us last night, I was convinced there were no more awakened undead. Except, in one corner, there was another magnificent and aristocratic tomb that overwhelmed all the others. On top of that tomb, which was enormous in size and perfect in proportion, only one word was clearly engraved.

[P054c] DRACULA.

[P054d] If so, this was definitely the resting place of the vampire king who commanded all those countless vampires. The empty tomb was vividly proving what I had guessed. Before the terrible task of sending these women back to true death, I placed the Host right in the middle of Dracula's empty tomb. With this, I permanently banished the devil so that he could never set foot in this tomb again as an undead.

[P055_059] Soon, my terrible mission began, but my whole body was almost paralyzed with fear. If there had been only one monster, I would have finished it somewhat easily. But three! To have to repeat the same thing twice more after going through a heart-stoppingly terrible act once... Even with the fragile and lovely Miss Lucy, I was suffocating with fear, so how much worse would it be to face these unfamiliar monsters who had survived for hundreds of years and become as ferocious as those terrible years. I shuddered to think how desperately they would have struggled to hold onto their filthy lives if these wicked creatures could only move their bodies.

[P059b] Ah, my old friend John. It was more akin to slaughter. If I hadn't been held up by the sorrow for the dead who had already been sacrificed, and the firm resolve that I must save the living who are drowning in the swamp of terrible fear that might strike at any moment, I could never have made it to the end. Even at this very moment, my whole body is trembling. It's just a relief that by God's grace, my thread of reason didn't snap until everything was over. If I hadn't witnessed with my own two eyes the ecstasy blooming on their faces as they realized their souls were finally saved, right at the first moment peace arrived and just before the final annihilation of scattering into dirt, I could never have endured this terrible slaughter to the end. Because I could never have endured in my right mind the screams that tore through the air when the sharp stake pierced their hearts, the desperate struggles of their thrashing bodies, and those lips puking up red bloody foam. Now I can gladly shed tears and feel compassion for those poor souls who slowly scattered in the peace given by eternal death, even if it is only for a fleeting moment. It was as if the death that should have come hundreds of years ago finally woke from its heavy sleep and loudly proclaimed to the whole world, "I have arrived!"

[P059c] Before leaving the terrible castle behind, I took thorough measures at every entrance so that Count Dracula could never set foot in there again in an undead state.

[P059d] When I carefully stepped into the barrier where Madam Mina was sleeping, she woke up shortly after. Seeing me, she let out a pitiful scream, saying I must have gone through terrible suffering.

Her face was pale and haggard, as if she might collapse at any moment, but her eyes were shining with a clearer and more passionate fire than ever before. Seeing her bloodless and sickly appearance, I actually felt a wave of relief. Because the memory of that vampire-specific, reddish, and horrifyingly vibrant sleep that had been stuck in my eyes just a moment ago was still filling my mind.

[P060_062] So we hurried our steps eastward, carrying a sliver of hope, faith, and a still-lingering great fear. It was to meet our old friends who, just as Madam Mina said, were rushing madly toward us, and to face that terrible villain.

[P062b] Mina Harker's Diary.

[P063a] November 6.—Knowing that Jonathan and the others were chasing us, it was around late afternoon when the Professor and I set out on the road heading east. The path was a steep downhill, but we couldn't hurry our steps because we had to carry a bunch of heavy blankets and coats. We couldn't risk being isolated without winter clothes in this biting blizzard. It was a complete wasteland with no trace of human presence, and not even a sign of people living beyond the pouring snow, so we also had to pack plenty of food. When we had walked down about a mile, exhausted from carrying the heavy load, I sat down to rest. When I looked back, the distinct silhouette of Dracula's castle touching the pitch-black sky came into view. Because we had come so deep down below the hill where the castle sat, even the Carpathian Mountains looked much lower than the castle. Towering a full thousand feet high at the top of a sheer cliff, completely cut off from the surrounding mountains by massive, deeply carved valleys, the magnificent figure of that bizarre castle caught my eye at a glance. An indescribably rough and eerie aura lingered there. From far away, the sound of starving wolves howling could be heard. Although it sounded somewhat muffled because it came from very far away and the pouring snow swallowed the sound, it was full of chilling terror. Seeing Dr. Van Helsing scanning the surroundings with sharp eyes, it was clear he was looking for a good strategic point to defend against a possible attack. The roughly carved road continued endlessly downhill, and we carefully felt our way along that blurry trail, pushing through the piled-up snow.

[P063b] After a while, the Professor beckoned, and I got up and approached him. He had found an excellent hiding place where two massive rocks interlocked to form an entrance like a natural cave door. Taking my hand to lead me inside, he said, "Now, look! [P095] Epilogue (Jonathan Harker's Record, 7 Years Later)

[P096_097] Seven years ago today, our party all survived by plunging bare-bodied through those hellish flames. And I firmly believe that the overwhelming happiness some of us have enjoyed by our side since that day more than fully compensates for the terrible agonies we had to endure so desperately. The fact that the very day the brave Quincey Morris passed away became the birthday of our son born later was another unspeakable blessing for my wife Mina and me. I know well that my wife Mina believes deep in her heart that a piece of the soul of that noble friend who left our side has seeped intact into our son and breathes within him. Our son's long full name carries the names of all the members of our small expedition who fought together risking our lives, but we always call him by his nickname, 'Quincey'.

[P098] This summer, our family revisited that unfamiliar land of Transylvania, full of vivid and terrible nightmare memories stamped deep in our bones from so long ago. It was hard to believe ourselves whether all the things we witnessed with our own two eyes and heard clearly with our own two ears back then were indeed living truth. The traces of all those terrible events that stained the past with blood had been wiped completely clean, as if it were a lie. Only that cursed castle towered bizarrely over the rough and desolate ruins, just as it did before.

[P099] After returning safely to England, we gathered in small groups and calmly talked about those terrible old days. Because Lord Godalming and Dr. Seward had both found their respective partners and started happy families, we could look back on those days calmly, without the despair of being held back by past nightmares. I pulled out the bundles of old papers I had kept deep inside the safe ever since we barely made it back alive from that terrible hell so long ago. Except for Mina's, Dr. Seward's, and my own later diary notes, and the occasional scribbled memos from Professor Van Helsing, they were just bundles of dryly typed papers.
