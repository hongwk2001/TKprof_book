Chapter 8

Mina Murray's Journal

Same day, 11 PM. - Oh, I am so tired! If I hadn't made it a duty to keep a journal, I wouldn't have even opened it tonight. We had a very pleasant walk. Lucy soon regained her spirits. It was thanks to the cute cows that surprised us by sniffing around near the lighthouse fields. Gripped by fear, all other thoughts completely vanished. Thanks to that, my complicated mind cleared up, and it seems we could make a fresh start. We had a wonderful dinner and tea at a lovely old inn in Robin Hood's Bay. It overlooked a beach covered in seaweed from a window bulging out over the rocks. If they had seen our enormous appetites, even the snobbish "New Women" would have been shocked. It is such a relief that men are more generous in that regard! After that, we walked home. We walked slowly, terrified that a wild bull might appear. Lucy was extremely exhausted, so we intended to hurry to bed. However, the young curate visited, and Mrs. Westenra held him back to stay for dinner. Lucy and I had to fight a tough battle against the oncoming sleepiness. At least for me, it required a tremendous amount of patience. Someday, the bishops should gather and discuss a plan to train curates who know how to politely decline a dinner invitation no matter how much it is offered. Ones who can notice when girls are tired. Lucy is fast asleep, breathing softly. She looks truly lovely with a rosier flush on her cheeks than usual. Mr. Arthur Holmwood fell in love just seeing her in the drawing-room, but what would he say if he saw her like this now? Perhaps future "New Woman" writers might argue that men and women should see each other sleeping before proposing or accepting. But a future New Woman wouldn't just receive proposals. They will propose themselves and do it brilliantly! That thought gives me a little comfort. I am very happy tonight because my beloved Lucy looks much better. I want to believe that we are truly past the crisis and the sleepwalking issue is now over. I would be happier than anything if I only knew how Jonathan is doing.... May God bless and protect him.

*       *       *       *       *

August 11, 3 AM. - I am writing in my journal again. I can't sleep now, so it's better to write. My mind is so anxious that I can't fall asleep. I went through something truly terrible and agonizing. I fell asleep as soon as I closed my journal.... Then I jolted awake with a terrible sense of dread and a chilling feeling that the space around me was empty. The room was so dark that I couldn't see Lucy's bed, so I crept over and felt around with my hands. The bed was empty. I struck a match and saw that Lucy was not in the room. The door was closed, but not locked as I had left it. Terrified of waking my recently ailing mother, I hastily threw on some clothes and got ready to go find Lucy. As I was leaving the room, I thought I could guess where she went based on the clothes she wore. If it was indoor clothing, she'd be in the house; if it was outdoor clothing, it meant she went outside. However, both her indoor and outdoor clothes were hanging in their places. "Thank God," I sighed with relief inside. "She's in her nightgown, so she couldn't have gone far." I ran downstairs and checked the living room, but she wasn't there! Then I searched all the other open rooms in the house, but my fear only grew. Finally, I reached the front door, and it was open. It wasn't wide open, but it was unlocked. Since the people in this house strictly lock the doors every night, I was gripped by terror, certain that Lucy must have gone outside in her nightgown. I didn't even have time to weigh the consequences. A vague, overwhelming fear made my mind go blank. I grabbed a large, thick shawl and rushed outside. When I reached the crescent street, the clock struck 1 AM, and there wasn't even a shadow of a person on the street. I ran along the North Terrace, but I couldn't see the white figure I was looking for. From the edge of the West Cliff above the pier, I looked over the harbor toward the East Cliff, hoping Lucy would be at our favorite spot, or hoping she wouldn't be. The full moon was bright, but dark clouds quickly rolled in, making the whole world sway with light and shadow. When the thick clouds covered St. Mary's Church, I couldn't see anything for a moment. Then the clouds passed, and the abbey ruins appeared again. As the narrow, sharp moonlight shifted like a blade, the church and the graveyard were illuminated in turn. My sad premonition was not wrong. Right there, on our favorite bench, I saw a snow-white figure half-reclining under the moonlight. The clouds moved quickly, and my vision soon darkened again. But for a brief moment, I thought I saw something dark standing bent over behind the white figure. I had no way of knowing if it was a person or a beast. Without a moment to spare, I darted down the steep stairs toward the pier. I passed the fish market and crossed the bridge, the only path to the East Cliff. The town was dead silent without a soul in sight, which rather gave me a sense of relief that no one would see poor Lucy like this. The distance felt infinitely far, and as I climbed the endless stairs leading to the abbey, my knees trembled and I was out of breath. I ran very fast, but my feet felt as heavy as lead, and my joints creaked as if they were rusted. By the time I reached the top, I was close enough to distinguish the bench and the white figure even in the shadows. Undoubtedly, something long and dark was bending over the half-reclining white figure. Horrified, I shouted, "Lucy! Lucy!" and it raised its head. I could clearly see a white face and two eyes glowing red. Lucy didn't answer, and I kept running toward the tomb entrance. When I entered the gateway, the church blocked the bench, momentarily obscuring my view. When my vision cleared again, the clouds had lifted, and the moonlight was pouring down dazzlingly. Lucy was half-lying with her head resting on the back of the bench. There was no one beside her, and not a single living thing in sight.

Going closer and looking down, she was still fast asleep. Her lips were parted, and unlike her usual soft breathing, she was panting heavily and roughly with every breath, as if trying to fill her lungs with air. As I got closer, she raised her hand in her sleep and pulled the collar of her nightgown around her neck. Then she shivered as if she felt a chill. Afraid she might catch a cold in the deadly night air in just a thin nightgown, I quickly covered her with the warm shawl and wrapped it tightly around her neck. Careful not to wake her, I fastened the shawl at her neck with a large safety pin. But I must have been so nervous that I pinned it clumsily. For as her breathing calmed down, she brought her hand to her neck again and let out a groan. After wrapping her well, I took off my shoes, put them on her feet, and carefully shook her awake. At first, there was no reaction, but gradually she began to groan, sigh, and toss around. Time was running out, and for various reasons, we had to return home quickly, so I shook her harder. Finally, she opened her eyes. Not fully realizing where she was, she wasn't surprised to see me. Lucy always wakes up gracefully. Even in the shocking moment of waking up shivering in the cold in a graveyard at midnight in thin clothes, she didn't lose her elegance. She trembled slightly and hugged me, and then obediently got up like a child when I told her we had to go home immediately. As we walked, she flinched when her bare feet were pricked by the gravel path, and she stopped, stubbornly insisting on giving my shoes back. But I flatly refused. However, as we left the graveyard and passed a puddle left by the storm, I deliberately coated both her feet in mud so that they wouldn't be noticeable if we happened to run into someone.

Luckily, we arrived home safely without encountering a single person. We did see a drunk man staggering on the way, but we hid in a doorway until he disappeared down a narrow, steep Scottish-style alley. My heart pounded so violently the whole time that I thought I would faint. I was consumed with worry that the cold midnight wind might ruin Lucy's health, or that if this incident leaked out, her reputation might be tarnished. Once inside, I washed her feet, we said a prayer of thanks together, and I put her to bed. Just before falling asleep, Lucy earnestly begged me not to tell anyone, not even her mother, about her sleepwalking outside. At first, I hesitated to promise. But considering how weak Mrs. Westenra's heart is, how shocked she would be if she found out, and how the story might get twisted if a rumor spread, I decided it was wiser to keep it buried. I pray this is the right decision. I locked the door securely and tied the key to my wrist. Nothing more will happen tonight. Lucy is fast asleep, and dawn is already breaking far out over the sea....

*       *       *       *       *

Same day, noon. - Fortunately, nothing happened. Lucy slept deeply until I woke her, and it seems she didn't toss or turn once all night. The midnight adventure, rather than doing harm, seems to have been medicine, as her complexion is much better than it has been in recent weeks. I felt terribly sorry when I realized I had hurt her by pinning the safety pin wrong. It actually pierced the skin on her neck, so it could have been dangerous. I must have accidentally pinched the loose skin and pierced it. There were two distinct red spots like needle pricks, and drops of blood on the collar of her nightgown. When I didn't know what to do because I was so sorry, Lucy smiled brightly, patted my hand, and said she didn't feel a thing. The wound is so small that it probably won't leave a scar, which is a relief amidst the misfortune.

*       *       *       *       *

Same day, night. - We had a truly perfect day. Clear air, dazzling sunshine, and a cool breeze. We packed a lunch and headed to Mulgrave Woods. Mrs. Westenra went up the main road in a carriage, and Lucy and I walked along the cliff path and met her at the entrance. My nose tingled as I thought of how perfect the day would have been if Jonathan had been by my side. But I suppose I'll have to endure a little longer! In the evening, we strolled along the Casino terrace, listened to wonderful music by Spohr and Mackenzie, and went to bed early. Lucy looked more relaxed than she had in a long time and fell into a deep sleep as soon as her head hit the pillow. It seems nothing will happen tonight, but just in case, I plan to lock the door and tie the key tightly to my wrist like yesterday.

*       *       *       *       *

August 12. - I was completely wrong. I woke up during the night because Lucy tried to go outside twice. Even in her sleep, she seemed quite angry that the door was locked and grumbled as she returned to bed. When I woke up around the break of dawn, I heard the chirping of birds outside the window. I was relieved to see Lucy looking much healthier than the previous morning when she opened her eyes. It seemed her former lively self had fully returned. She burrowed into my bed, hugged me tight, and rattled on about Arthur. When I confessed how anxious I was worrying about Jonathan, she did her best to comfort me. It was a huge consolation. Even if sympathy doesn't change reality, it gives you the strength to endure.

*       *       *       *       *

August 13. - We had a peaceful day, and I lay in bed with the key tied to my wrist as before. When I woke up again in the middle of the night, Lucy was sitting straight up in bed, still asleep, pointing at the window. I quietly got up, pulled back the blind, and looked out. Under the dazzling moonlight, the sea and the sky blended softly; it was breathtakingly beautiful, as if plunged into a massive, silent mystery. Between me and the moonlight, a large bat was wildly flying in circles. Once or twice, it swooped close to the window, but perhaps startled by seeing me, it flew across the harbor toward the abbey. When I turned back from the window, Lucy was lying peacefully, fast asleep again. She didn't move an inch until morning after that.

August 14. - I sat on the East Cliff all day reading and writing. Lucy seems as captivated by that spot as I am, so it's quite a struggle to get her home for meals. This afternoon, she said something truly incomprehensible. On our way back for dinner, we stopped to admire the view at the top of the steps above the West Pier, as we always do. The sun was just about to set behind Kettleness, and the red sunset was dazzlingly tinting the East Cliff and the old abbey ruins in rosy hues. We stood in silence when Lucy suddenly muttered to herself.

"His red eyes are back! Just like before." I was startled by her abrupt words. Trying not to make it obvious that I was staring, I turned my body slightly to observe her, and Lucy had a strange expression, half-dazed. Unable to figure out what was going on, I silently followed her gaze. She was staring straight at our designated seat. There was a dark figure sitting there alone. For a moment, I was a little taken aback too. The stranger's eyes seemed to flash like fiercely burning flames. But upon closer inspection, it was just an optical illusion. The red sunset was merely reflecting off the windows of St. Mary's Church behind our seat. As the sun set, the angle of the light changed slightly, making the glare look like it was moving. When I pointed out that bizarre trick of the light, Lucy was startled and snapped out of her daze, but she still looked sad. Perhaps she remembered that terrible night. Since we agreed not to breathe a word about that incident, I didn't press her either, and we went straight home and ate dinner. Lucy got a headache and went to bed early. Seeing her fast asleep, I went out for a short walk by myself. While walking on the West Cliff, I fell into a sweet melancholy thinking about Jonathan. By the time I returned home, the moonlight was so bright that even though the front of our building was shaded, everything was clearly visible. I looked up at our room window, and Lucy was leaning out. Thinking she was waiting for me, I waved my handkerchief. But she didn't budge. Just then, the moonlight rounded the corner of the building and illuminated our window. It clearly revealed Lucy leaning her head against the window frame with her eyes tightly shut. She was fast asleep. Beside her on the windowsill sat something like a rather large bird. Worried she might catch a cold, I ran upstairs in a single breath. But by the time I entered the room, she was already stumbling back to bed, panting heavily in her sleep. She was clutching her neck tightly with both hands, as if trying to protect it from the cold.

I didn't bother to wake her and warmly covered her with blankets. I double-checked carefully that the door was locked and the window was tightly closed.

She looks incredibly lovely when sleeping, but my heart is heavy because she is much paler than usual and has dark circles under her eyes. She must be secretly agonizing over something on her own. I wish I could figure out what it is.

*       *       *       *       *

August 15. - I woke up later than usual. Lucy looked so exhausted she couldn't steady herself, and she fell back asleep even after the maid woke us and left. During breakfast, I heard some welcome news. Arthur's father's condition has improved, and he wants them to have the wedding soon. Lucy calmly rejoiced, and her mother seemed to have mixed feelings. Late in the afternoon, Mrs. Westenra told me the reason. She is sad to let Lucy go to another man, but she is more than happy that her daughter now has a strong protector to watch over her. Poor, sweet lady! She confided only to me that she essentially received a death sentence. She repeatedly begged me to keep it a secret from Lucy. She was told by the doctor that her heart is failing and she won't last more than a few months. Even a very minor shock could be immediately fatal to her right now. Oh, it was a truly excellent choice to keep Lucy's sleepwalking incident a secret from her that night.
