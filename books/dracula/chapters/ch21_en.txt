[P001] CHAPTER 21

[P002] DR. SEWARD'S DIARY

[P003] October 3.—I will write down exactly what happened, to the best of my memory, since my last journal entry. I must not leave out a single detail I can recall; I need to stay completely calm as I do this.

[P004] When I arrived at Renfield's room, I found him lying on his left side on the floor in a gleaming pool of blood. As I tried to move him, it became immediately clear that he had suffered terrible injuries. His body parts lacked the natural coordination that even a sluggishly sane person exhibits. When his face became visible, I saw it was horribly bruised, as if it had been repeatedly smashed against the floor—in fact, the pool of blood came from those facial wounds. The attendant kneeling beside him said to me as we rolled him over:

[P005] "I think his back is broken, sir. Look, his right arm, leg, and the entire right side of his face are paralyzed." The attendant was incredibly confused about how this could have happened. He looked completely baffled, frowning deeply as he continued:

[P006] "I can't make sense of two things. He could have injured his face like that by smashing his own head against the floor. I once saw a young woman do exactly that at the Eversfield Asylum before anyone could stop her. And I guess he could have broken his neck by falling out of bed at an awkward angle. But for the life of me, I can't imagine how both happened. If his back was already broken, he couldn't have beaten his head; and if his face was already injured before he fell out of bed, there would be blood stains up there." I told him:

[P007] "Go to Dr. Van Helsing and ask him to come here immediately. I need him without a second's delay." The attendant ran off, and the Professor appeared a few minutes later, wearing his dressing gown and slippers. When he saw Renfield on the ground, he examined him sharply for a moment, then turned to me. I think he read my thoughts in my eyes, because he said very quietly, making sure the attendant could hear:

[P008] "Ah, a tragic accident! He will need very careful monitoring and a lot of attention. I will stay here with you myself, but I must go get dressed first. If you stay here, I will be back with you in a few minutes."

[P009] The patient was now breathing with a harsh, rattling sound, and it was obvious he had suffered a severe injury. Van Helsing returned incredibly fast, carrying a surgical case. He had clearly been thinking and had already made up his mind, because before he even looked at the patient again, he whispered to me:

[P010_011] "Send the attendant away. We must be alone with him when he wakes up after the surgery." So I said:
"I think that will be all for now, Simmons. We have done everything we can at the moment. You should go on your rounds, and Dr. Van Helsing will perform the surgery. Let me know instantly if you notice anything unusual anywhere."

[P012] The attendant left, and we began a thorough examination of the patient. The facial wounds were only superficial; the real injury was a depressed skull fracture that extended right up into the motor area of the brain. The Professor thought for a moment and said:

[P013] "We must relieve the pressure and try to restore normal conditions as much as possible. The speed of the bleeding shows the terrible severity of his injury. The entire motor area seems affected. The blood pooling in the brain will increase quickly, so we must perform surgery to drill into the skull immediately, or it may be too late." As he was speaking, there was a soft tapping at the door. I went over and opened it, finding Arthur and Quincey in the corridor, wearing their pajamas and slippers. Arthur spoke first:

[P014] "I heard your attendant call for Dr. Van Helsing and mention an accident. So I woke Quincey up—or rather, I called for him, since he wasn't asleep either. Things are moving too quickly and strangely for any of us to get a sound sleep lately. I've been thinking that by tomorrow night, things will be very different. We'll have to look back—and forward—a little more carefully than we have before. May we come in?" I nodded and held the door open until they entered; then I closed it behind them. When Quincey saw the patient's twisted posture and condition, and noticed the horrifying pool of blood on the floor, he said softly:

[P015] "My God! What has happened to him? The poor, poor man!" I gave him a brief explanation and added that we expected him to regain consciousness after the surgery—at least for a little while. Quincey immediately went and sat on the edge of the bed, with Godalming beside him; we all waited patiently and watched.

[P016] "We will wait," said Van Helsing, "just long enough to determine the best spot to drill into the skull, so we can remove the blood clot as quickly and cleanly as possible; it is obvious that the bleeding is getting worse."

[P017] The few minutes we waited passed with terrifying slowness. I felt a dreadful sinking feeling in my stomach, and I could tell from Van Helsing's face that he was also feeling fear and anxiety about what was to come. I dreaded what Renfield might say. I was genuinely afraid to even think; but I was weighed down by a certainty of what was coming, much like what I've read about condemned men waiting for their execution. The poor man gasped for air irregularly. Every second it seemed like he would open his eyes and speak, but then he would take a long, rattling breath and fall back into an even deeper state of unconsciousness. Even though I was used to hospital beds and death, this suspense just kept growing inside me. I could practically hear my own heartbeat, and the blood rushing through my temples sounded like hammer blows. The silence eventually became pure agony. I looked at my companions one by one, and I could see from their flushed faces and sweaty foreheads that they were going through the exact same torture. A nervous tension hung over all of us, as if a terrifying bell were about to ring out above us right when we least expected it.

[P018] Finally, it became obvious that the patient was fading rapidly; he could die at any second. I looked up at the Professor and met his gaze. His face was intensely serious as he spoke:

[P019] "There is no time to lose. His words might save many lives; I have been thinking exactly that as I stood here. A soul may be at stake! We will operate just above the ear."

[P020_021] Without another word, he performed the surgery. The rattling breathing continued for a few moments. Then, he took a breath so deep and long that it seemed like it would tear his chest open. Suddenly, his eyes snapped open and locked into a wild, helpless stare. This lasted for a short time; then, his expression softened into glad surprise, and a sigh of relief escaped his lips. His body convulsed, and as it did, he said:
"I'll be quiet, Doctor. Tell them to take off this straitjacket. I had a terrible dream, and it left me so weak that I can't move. What's wrong with my face? It feels swollen, and it hurts terribly." He tried to turn his head, but even that small effort made his eyes look glassy and unfocused again, so I gently moved his head back. Then Van Helsing said in a quiet, serious tone:

[P022] "Tell us about your dream, Mr. Renfield." Hearing the voice, his terribly mutilated face brightened, and he said:

[P023] "That's Dr. Van Helsing. It's so good of you to be here. Please give me some water, my lips are dry; then I will try to tell you. I dreamed—" he stopped and looked like he was going to faint. I called quietly to Quincey, "The brandy—it's in my study—quick!" He bolted out and returned with a glass, a decanter of brandy, and a pitcher of water. We wet his dry lips, and the patient quickly revived. It seemed, however, that his damaged brain had been working the whole time, because when he was fully conscious again, he stared at me with an intense, agonized confusion that I will never forget, and said:

[P024] "I shouldn't lie to myself; it wasn't a dream, it was a grim reality." Then his eyes scanned the room. When he saw the two figures sitting patiently on the edge of the bed, he continued:

[P025] "Even if I wasn't already sure, I would know just by looking at them." For a moment his eyes closed—not from pain or sleep, but intentionally, as if he were trying to pull all his thoughts together. When he opened them, he spoke urgently and with more energy than he had shown so far:

[P026] "Quick, Doctor, quick. I'm dying! I feel like I only have a few minutes left; and then I have to go back to death—or something worse! Wet my lips with the brandy again. I have something I must say before I die, or at least before my damaged brain completely shuts down. Thank you! It was the night you left me, when I begged you to let me go. I couldn't speak then because my tongue felt tied, but I was just as sane then as I am now, except for that. I was in complete despair for a long time after you left; it felt like hours. Then, I suddenly felt peaceful. My brain seemed to clear up, and I realized where I was. I heard dogs barking behind our building, but not from where He was!" As he spoke, Van Helsing didn't blink, but he reached out and gripped my hand tightly. He didn't let his emotions show, though; he just nodded slightly and said quietly, "Go on." Renfield continued:

[P027a] "He came up to the window in the mist, just like I'd seen him do so many times before. But he was solid this time—not a ghost—and his eyes were fierce, like an angry man's. He was smiling with his red mouth; his sharp white teeth flashed in the moonlight when he turned to look back over the trees where the dogs were barking. I wouldn't invite him in at first, even though I knew he wanted to come inside—just as he had wanted to all along. Then he started promising me things. Not with words, but by showing me." The Professor interrupted him:

[P027b] "How?"

[P028_029] "By making them happen. Just like he used to send in the flies when the sun was out. Huge, fat ones with steel and sapphire colors on their wings. And at night, large moths with skull-and-crossbones patterns on their backs." Van Helsing nodded at him while instinctively whispering to me:

[P030] "The Acherontia Atropos—what people call the 'Death's-head Hawkmoth'?" The patient continued without pausing.

[P031] "Then he started whispering: 'Rats, rats, rats! Hundreds, thousands, millions of them, and every single one is a life. And dogs and cats to eat them, too. All of them lives! All full of red blood and years of life, not just annoying flies!' I laughed at him, because I wanted to see what he was really capable of. Then the dogs started howling from far away, past the dark trees near His house. He waved me over to the window. I got up and looked out. He raised his hands and seemed to summon something without making a sound. A dark mass spread over the grass, moving like a wave of fire. Then He parted the mist to the sides, and I saw thousands of rats with glowing red eyes—just like His, only smaller. He raised his hand, and they all froze. It felt like he was saying to me: 'I will give you all these lives, and many more, far greater ones, for eternity, if you just bow down and worship me!' Then a red cloud, the color of blood, seemed to cover my eyes. Before I even realized what I was doing, I was opening the window and telling Him, 'Come in, Lord and Master!' The rats vanished, but He slipped right through the window, even though I had only opened it an inch. He came inside just like the moonlight used to slip through the smallest cracks to shine in my room in all its glory."

[P032] His voice was getting weaker, so I moistened his lips with the brandy again, and he kept talking. It seemed, however, that his mind had skipped ahead while he was drinking, because his story jumped forward. I was about to stop him and bring him back to where he left off, but Van Helsing whispered to me: "Let him continue. Don't interrupt him. He can't go back, and if he loses his train of thought, he might not be able to finish." Renfield continued:

[P033] "I waited all day to hear from him, but he didn't send me anything, not even a single fly. By the time the moon came out, I was quite angry with him. When he slid through the closed window without even knocking, I lost my temper. He just sneered at me. His pale face appeared out of the mist, his red eyes gleaming, and he walked around like he owned the place and I was nobody. He didn't even smell the same when he walked past me. I couldn't stop him. For some reason, I was convinced that Mrs. Harker had come into the room."

[P034] The two men sitting on the bed stood up and moved behind him, so he couldn't see them but they could hear him better. They both stayed quiet, but the Professor flinched and trembled. His face, however, just grew even grimmer and more severe. Renfield continued without noticing:

[P035] "When Mrs. Harker came to visit me this afternoon, she was different. She seemed drained, like tea that has been watered down." We all reacted to this, but no one said a word; he kept going:

[P036] "I didn't even know she was there until she spoke, and she didn't look like herself. I don't care for pale people; I prefer them full of blood, and it seemed like all of hers was gone. I didn't realize it right then, but after she left, I started thinking about it. It made me furious to realize that He had been draining her life away." I could feel the others trembling with shock, just as I was, but we didn't move or speak. "So when He arrived tonight, I was ready for Him. I saw the mist creeping in, and I grabbed it hard. I had heard that madmen possess unnatural strength, and since I knew I was a madman—at least sometimes—I decided to use all my power. And yes, He felt it too, because He had to materialize out of the mist just to fight me off. I held on tight. I thought I was going to win, because I was determined not to let Him take any more of her life—until I saw His eyes. They burned right into me, and my strength melted away like water. He broke free, and when I tried to cling to Him again, He lifted me up and hurled me to the ground. I saw a red cloud, heard a sound like thunder, and then the mist seemed to slip away under the door." His voice was growing fainter, and his breathing was getting worse. Van Helsing stood up instinctively.

[P037] "Now we know the worst," he said. "He is here, and we know his true purpose. It might not be too late. Let's arm ourselves—just like we did the other night—but don't waste any time; we don't have a second to spare." There was no need to say our fears out loud, or rather, our certainties—we all felt exactly the same way. We all hurried to our rooms and grabbed the same items we had taken when we broke into the Count's house. The Professor already had his ready, and when we met back in the corridor, he pointed to them meaningfully and said:

[P038] "I never let these out of my sight, and I won't until this miserable business is finished. Be smart, my friends. We are not dealing with an ordinary enemy. Oh, the tragedy! To think that our dear Madam Mina is suffering!" He stopped, his voice breaking with emotion, and I honestly didn't know whether pure rage or terror was stronger in my own heart.

[P039a] We paused outside the Harkers' door. Art and Quincey hung back a little, and Quincey asked:

[P039b] "Should we wake her?"

[P040_041] "We have to," Van Helsing said grimly. "If the door is locked, I will break it down."

[P042] "Won't that terrify her? It's completely inappropriate to break into a lady's room!"

[P043] Van Helsing replied very seriously, "You are absolutely right, but this is a matter of life and death. To a doctor, all bedrooms are the same; and even if they weren't, they are all the same to me tonight. My friend John, when I turn the handle, if the door doesn't open, put your shoulder against it and shove hard. And you two help as well. Ready? Now!"

[P044] He turned the handle as he spoke, but the door wouldn't budge. We threw our weight against it, and it burst open with a loud crash, sending us stumbling into the room. The Professor actually fell down, and as he was scrambling to his hands and knees, I looked past him. What I saw horrified me completely. I felt the hair on the back of my neck stand straight up, and my heart seemed to stop beating.

[P045a] The moonlight was so bright that even through the thick yellow blinds, the room was lit up enough to see clearly. On the bed near the window lay Jonathan Harker, his face flushed and his breathing heavy, as if he were drugged into a deep stupor. Kneeling on the edge of the bed closer to us, facing outwards, was his wife, dressed in a white nightgown. Beside her stood a tall, thin man dressed entirely in black. His face was turned away, but the second we saw him, we all recognized the Count instantly—from his overall appearance right down to the scar on his forehead.

[P045b] With his left hand, he was gripping both of Mrs. Harker's hands, holding her arms fully stretched out away from her body. His right hand clutched the back of her neck, aggressively forcing her face down against his chest. Her white nightgown was smeared with blood, and a thin stream of blood trickled down the man's bare chest, which was exposed by his ripped-open shirt. The way he was holding her looked horrifyingly like a child forcing a kitten's face into a bowl of milk to make it drink. As we burst in, the Count turned his head, and the demonic look I had heard others describe seemed to flash across his face. His eyes burned red with an evil intensity; his large nostrils flared and quivered, and his sharp white teeth snapped together like a wild animal's behind his thick, blood-dripping lips. With a violent shove that threw his victim back onto the bed as if she had been tossed from a great height, he turned and lunged at us.

[P045c] But by that time, the Professor had scrambled to his feet and was holding out the envelope containing the Communion Wafer toward him. The Count stopped dead in his tracks, just like poor Lucy had done outside her tomb, and shrank back. He kept cowering backwards as we moved forward, holding up our crucifixes. Suddenly, the moonlight vanished as a huge black cloud covered the sky. When Quincey struck a match and lit the gas lamp, the Count was gone, and we saw nothing but a faint mist. As we watched, the mist drifted away under the door, which had swung shut again after we broke it open. Van Helsing, Arthur, and I rushed over to Mrs. Harker. By then, she had caught her breath and let out a scream so wild, so ear-piercing, and so full of absolute despair that I feel like I'll hear it echoing in my head until the day I die. For a few seconds, she just lay there, completely helpless and disheveled. Her face was a ghostly white, making the blood smeared across her lips, cheeks, and chin look even more horrific. A thin line of blood was dripping from her throat, and her eyes were wild with sheer terror. Then, she covered her face with her poor, bruised hands, which still showed the red marks from the Count's brutal grip on her pale skin. From behind her hands came a low, heartbreaking wail. That slow sobbing made her terrible scream seem like just a brief outburst of an endless grief. Van Helsing stepped forward and gently pulled the blanket over her, while Arthur stared at her face in despair for a second before running out of the room. Van Helsing whispered to me:

[P046] "Jonathan is in a trance, the kind we know vampires can cause. We can't do anything for poor Madam Mina until she recovers a bit from the shock; I have to wake him up!" He dipped the corner of a towel into cold water and started snapping it lightly against Harker's face. The whole time, his wife kept her face buried in her hands, sobbing in a way that broke my heart to hear. I raised the blinds and looked out the window. The moonlight was bright again, and I saw Quincey Morris run across the lawn and hide in the shadow of a large yew tree. I was confused about what he was doing, but right then I heard Harker let out a sharp cry as he started to wake up, so I turned back to the bed. Unsurprisingly, he looked absolutely horrified and confused. He seemed dazed for a few seconds, but then it was like full consciousness hit him all at once, and he bolted upright. His sudden movement startled his wife, and she turned to him, reaching her arms out as if to hug him. However, she instantly pulled back, brought her elbows together, hid her face in her hands, and began trembling so violently that the entire bed shook.

[P047] "In God's name, what is going on?" Harker yelled. "Dr. Seward, Dr. Van Helsing, what is it? What happened? What's wrong? Mina, darling, what is it? Where did that blood come from? My God, my God! Has it really come to this!" He pushed himself up onto his knees and clapped his hands together frantically. "Good God, help us! Help her! Oh, please help her!" Moving quickly, he jumped out of bed and started throwing on his clothes—fully awake and ready to fight now that immediate action was needed. "What happened? Tell me everything!" he shouted without stopping. "Dr. Van Helsing, I know you care deeply for Mina. Please, do something to save her. It can't be too late yet. Keep her safe while I go look for him!" Despite her own terror, horror, and pain, his wife realized he was putting himself in deadly danger. Instantly forgetting her own trauma, she grabbed onto him and cried out:

[P048] "No! No! Jonathan, you can't leave me. I've been through enough suffering tonight, God knows, without the added terror of him hurting you too. You have to stay here with me. Stay here with these friends; they will protect you!" She looked absolutely frantic as she pleaded with him. He gave in to her, and she pulled down to sit on the edge of the bed, clinging to him desperately.

[P049] Van Helsing and I tried to calm them both down. The Professor held up his small golden crucifix and spoke with remarkable calmness:

[P050] "Don't be afraid, my dear. We are here, and as long as this is near you, no evil thing can come close. You are safe for tonight. We need to stay calm and figure out what to do together." She shivered and fell silent, resting her head on her husband's chest. When she finally looked up, his white nightshirt was stained with blood where her lips had pressed against it, and where the thin cut on her neck had dripped. The second she saw the blood, she shrank back with a low wail and whispered through choking sobs:

[P051_052] "Unclean, unclean! I can never touch him or kiss him again. Oh, it breaks my heart that I am now his worst enemy, and the thing he has the most reason to fear." To this, he replied firmly:
"Nonsense, Mina. I am ashamed to even hear you say such a thing. I wouldn't let anyone else say it about you, and I certainly won't listen to you say it yourself. May God judge me and punish me with something even worse than this nightmare if I ever do anything, intentionally or not, to let something come between us!" He opened his arms and pulled her tightly against his chest, and she just lay there sobbing for a while. He looked at us over her bowed head; his eyes were wet and blinking above his quivering nostrils, and his jaw was set like steel. After a while, her sobs began to quiet down, and he spoke to me. He used a forced calmness that I could tell was taking every ounce of his self-control:

[P053] "And now, Dr. Seward, tell me everything. I already know the horrible result too well; tell me exactly how this happened." I told him precisely what had occurred, and he listened, appearing almost emotionless. But his nostrils twitched and his eyes blazed with fury when I described how the Count's ruthless hands had forced his wife into that horrifying position, pressing her mouth to the open wound on his chest. It was fascinating to see, even in that awful moment, that while his pale face twitched with barely suppressed rage, his hands gently and lovingly stroked his wife's tangled hair. Just as I finished my story, Quincey and Godalming knocked on the door. They came in when we called for them. Van Helsing looked at me with a questioning expression. I understood he was asking if we should use their arrival to distract the unhappy couple from each other and their current misery, if possible. I nodded in agreement, so he asked the two men what they had seen and done. Lord Godalming answered:

[P054] "I couldn't find him anywhere in the hallway or in any of our rooms. I checked the study; he had clearly been there, but he was gone. He had, however—" He stopped abruptly, looking at the poor, slumped figure on the bed. Van Helsing said seriously:

[P055] "Keep going, my friend Arthur. We don't want any more secrets here. Our only hope now lies in knowing everything. Speak freely!" So Arthur continued:

[P056] "He was definitely in there, and even though it could only have been for a few seconds, he completely trashed the place. All the manuscript pages were burned, and blue flames were still flickering in the white ashes. The cylinders from your phonograph were also thrown into the fire, and the melting wax was fueling the flames." I interrupted him here. "Thank God there is another copy safe in the vault!" His face lit up for a second, but then fell again as he continued: "I ran downstairs after that, but there was no sign of him. I looked in Renfield's room, but there was no trace of him there either, except—!" He paused again. "Keep going," Harker said hoarsely. Arthur bowed his head, licked his lips, and added: "Except that the poor man is dead." Mrs. Harker raised her head, looked at each of us, and said solemnly:

[P057] "May God's will be done!" I couldn't shake the feeling that Arthur was holding something back; but since I assumed he had a good reason for it, I stayed quiet. Van Helsing turned to Morris and asked:

[P058] "And you, friend Quincey, do you have anything to report?"

[P059] "A little," he answered. "It might turn out to be important later, but I can't say for sure right now. I figured it would be smart to track where the Count went after leaving the house, if possible. I didn't see him personally, but I did see a large bat fly out of Renfield's window and head west. I expected to see him go back to Carfax, in whatever form he took, but he clearly went looking for a different hiding spot. He won't be coming back tonight; the sky is turning red in the east, and dawn is almost here. We have to get to work tomorrow!"

[P060] He spoke those last words through clenched teeth. There was silence for maybe two minutes, and I could swear I heard the sound of all our hearts beating. Then Van Helsing placed his hand very gently on Mrs. Harker's head and said:

[P061] "And now, Madam Mina—my poor, dear, sweet Madam Mina—tell us exactly what happened. God knows I don't want to cause you any more pain, but we absolutely must know everything. More than ever, we need to act quickly, sharply, and with deadly seriousness. The day when we must end this whole nightmare is approaching fast, and this is our chance to survive and gather the facts."

[P062] The poor, sweet lady shivered, and I could see how incredibly tense she was as she hugged her husband tighter and buried her face deeper into his chest. Then, she raised her head proudly and reached one hand out to Van Helsing. He took it, bowed to kiss it respectfully, and then held it securely. Her other hand was tightly locked in her husband's, and he had his other arm wrapped around her to protect her. After a brief pause, clearly taking a moment to organize her thoughts, she began:

[P063] "I took the sleeping pill you so kindly gave me, but it didn't work for a long time. In fact, I felt even more awake, and millions of horrifying thoughts started flooding my mind—all about death, vampires, blood, pain, and misery." Her husband groaned involuntarily. She turned to him and said lovingly: "Don't worry, darling. You need to be brave and strong, and help me get through this awful ordeal. If you only knew how hard it is for me to even talk about this terrifying thing, you'd understand how much I need your help. Anyway, I realized I needed to use my willpower to help the medicine work if it was going to do me any good, so I forced myself to try to sleep. Sure enough, sleep must have come quickly after that, because I don't remember anything else. Jonathan coming in didn't wake me; the next thing I remember, he was already lying next to me. There was the same thin, white mist in the room that I had noticed before. I can't remember if I told you about it, but you'll see it in my diary when I show it to you later. I felt the same vague terror and the same feeling that someone else was there. I turned to wake Jonathan, but he was sleeping so deeply it seemed like he was the one who had taken the sleeping pill, not me. I tried, but I just couldn't wake him. That terrified me, and I looked around in a panic. Then my heart truly sank: standing beside the bed—as if he had stepped right out of the mist, or rather, as if the mist had actually transformed into him, because the mist was completely gone—was a tall, thin man dressed all in black. I recognized him instantly from everyone's descriptions. The waxy face; the high, hooked nose catching a thin line of light; the parted red lips showing sharp white teeth; and the red eyes that reminded me of the sunset reflecting on the windows of St. Mary's Church in Whitby. I also recognized the red scar on his forehead where Jonathan had struck him. For a second, my heart stopped, and I would have screamed, but I was completely paralyzed. In that moment of silence, he pointed at Jonathan and spoke in a sharp, cutting whisper:"

[P064] "'Quiet! If you make a sound, I will take him and smash his brains out right in front of you.' I was completely horrified and too shocked to move or speak. With a mocking smile, he grabbed my shoulder tightly with one hand and exposed my throat with the other, saying: 'First, a little refreshment as a reward for my hard work. You might as well stay quiet; this isn't the first or even the second time your veins have quenched my thirst!' I was totally disoriented, and, bizarrely enough, I didn't even want to stop him. I guess that's just part of the horrible curse that takes over when he touches his victims. And oh, my God, my God, have mercy on me! He pressed his foul, reeking lips against my throat!" Her husband groaned again. She squeezed his hand tighter, looking at him with pity as if he were the one who had been attacked, and continued:

[P065] "I felt my strength draining away, and I half-fainted. I have no idea how long that nightmare lasted, but it felt like a very long time before he finally pulled his foul, awful, sneering mouth away. I saw it dripping with fresh blood!" The memory seemed to overwhelm her for a moment; she slumped and probably would have collapsed if her husband hadn't been holding her up. With a massive effort, she pulled herself together and went on:

[P066a] "Then he spoke to me, mocking me: 'So you, like the rest of them, thought you could match wits with me. You thought you could help these men hunt me down and ruin my plans! Now you know exactly what happens when you cross me, and they already know a little bit—and will soon know it entirely. They should have focused their energy on protecting their own home. While they were trying to outsmart me—me, who commanded nations, plotted conspiracies, and fought wars hundreds of years before they were even born—I was already trapping them. And you, their most beloved, are now bone of my bone, blood of my blood, and kin of my kin. For a while, you will be my bountiful wine-press, but eventually, you will be my companion and my helper. You will get your revenge, too, because every single one of them will end up serving you. But for now, you must be punished for what you've done. You helped them try to thwart me; now you will come whenever I call. When my mind commands you to "Come!", you will cross land and sea to do my bidding. And this is how we ensure that!'

[P066b] With that, he ripped his shirt open and used his long, sharp nails to tear open a vein in his chest.
