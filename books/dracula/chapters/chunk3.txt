I nodded and went outside. The death registration was handled without any hitches, and I told the local undertaker to come to the mansion around evening to take measurements and order the coffin.

Returning to the mansion, Quincey was waiting for me. I told him I would first check on Lucy's condition and then come back, and went upstairs. She was still sound asleep, and the professor was keeping watch by her side without moving an inch from his spot earlier. Judging from him bringing his index finger to his lips, it seemed Lucy was about to wake up soon and he didn't want to disturb her natural recovery. I quietly went downstairs and led Quincey into the morning room. Since the blinds were drawn up, the room was much brighter than the other rooms in the mansion, and the gloomy atmosphere was less. When we were left alone, he spoke heavily.

"Jack Seward, I know well that it's not my place to interfere, but this is by no means an ordinary situation. As you know, I truly loved that girl and even thought of marriage. Although all of that came to nothing, I still cannot turn a blind eye to the suffering she is going through. What on earth is happening to Lucy? When you guys came in just now, I heard that Dutch professor—I can recognize he's a truly great old gentleman—say that another blood transfusion is needed and that you are both exhausted. It's only natural for doctors to keep their patient's condition a secret, and I know well that one shouldn't pry into private medical opinions. But this is not an ordinary matter. Whatever it is, I too have done my duty by sharing my blood. Haven't I?"

"You are right." When I answered, he continued.

"I presume both you and Van Helsing have already shared the blood I gave today. Haven't you?"

"We have."

"Art must have been among them too. When I saw him at the country estate four days ago, his complexion was terrible. In the past, when I was staying on the pampas of South America, a mare I cherished dearly lost her life overnight. A vampire bat bit her neck and sucked her blood all night, and the mare was so drained of blood that she couldn't even stand on her own feet. In the end, I had to shoot her myself to relieve her pain. Since then, I've never seen anyone's blood dry up so quickly in an instant like Art's. Jack, tell me as much as you can without breaking your medical confidentiality. Arthur was the first person to give his blood. Am I right?"

All the while he was speaking, this poor friend's face was filled with anxiety. In the midst of the terrible suffering the woman he loved was going through, his complete ignorance of the truth behind the incident was driving him into a greater hell. His heart was torn to shreds and bleeding, and it was evident that he was barely holding his crumbling mind together with his noble and strong masculine nature. I hesitated for a moment, unable to speak hastily. It was due to the sense of responsibility that I should not reveal with my own mouth the secret the professor was trying to keep. However, he had already noticed too many things, and his guess touched upon the truth. Since there was no reason to hide the truth, I answered briefly, borrowing his words. "You are right."

"How long has this terrible thing been going on?"

"For about ten days."

"Ten days! Jack Seward, then that means the blood of four strapping men has been pouring into the body of that slender young lady we cherish so much for ten days. Look here, with that small frame, she wouldn't even be able to handle all that immense blood." He came close to me and whispered in an agitated, raspy voice. "What on earth sucked all that blood?"

I shook my head heavily. "That is exactly the problem. Even Van Helsing is on the verge of going mad over this, and I too am completely helpless. I can't even guess. The elaborate plans we set up to protect Lucy went awry every time due to trivial variables. But there won't be a mistake like that ever again. Whether the whole situation improves, or irreversibly heads toward a catastrophe, we plan to stay here."

Quincey held out his hand with a stiff expression. "I will join you too. If you and that Dutch professor just tell me what to do, I will do anything."

When Lucy woke up in the late afternoon, she first fumbled around her chest and, surprisingly, pulled out the exact same paper Van Helsing had shown me. The prudent professor, fearing she might be shocked to find the paper missing when she woke up, seemed to have quietly put it back in its place. Looking back and forth between Van Helsing and me keeping watch by her side, Lucy seemed relieved. But then, as she looked around and realized where she was lying, she suddenly trembled all over. Bursting into bitter tears, she covered her pale face with her slender hands. We both instantly understood what her action meant. She had fully realized her mother's death. We soothed and comforted her with all the words we could muster. Perhaps our sincere sympathy was of some comfort as she nodded, but she still remained in deep grief and sobbed silently for a long time. We promised that from now on at least one of us would stay by her side day and night, and at those words she seemed somewhat relieved.

Around dusk, Lucy fell into a light sleep. Then a truly bizarre sight occurred. While sound asleep, she took that paper out from her bosom and tore it right in half. Van Helsing quickly approached and snatched the torn pieces of paper. Nevertheless, as if she were holding paper in her hands, she continued to mime tearing something to shreds in the air. Finally, perhaps thinking she had torn the paper completely, she raised her hands high and spread them out as if scattering the pieces in the air. Van Helsing, looking startled, furrowed his thick eyebrows and fell into deep thought, but in the end said nothing.

*       *       *       *       *

September 19.—All last night she slept fitfully, extremely afraid of falling asleep, and every time she woke up, her energy was noticeably depleted. The professor and I took turns keeping watch by her side all night, and we did not leave her alone for even a single second. Quincey Morris didn't express it with his own mouth, but I knew he was pacing around the inside and outside of the mansion, patrolling with wide open eyes all night long.

As the morning sunlight brightly illuminated the room, it was clearly revealed how ruthlessly poor Lucy's body had weakened. She couldn't even hold her head up properly, and even the few sips of liquid food she swallowed with difficulty seemed to bring no improvement. She intermittently lost consciousness and fell asleep, and Van Helsing and I noticed the eerie difference between when she was asleep and when she was awake. When asleep, Lucy looked even more haggard, but strangely seemed full of energy, and her breathing was much more even. Between her slightly parted lips, her pale gums were pushed back, making her teeth gleam unusually long and sharp compared to usual. On the other hand, when she woke up and regained consciousness, even though she was standing on the threshold of death, her usual sweet and gentle eyes returned, and she reverted to her completely original self. Around the afternoon, she anxiously called for Arthur, and we hurriedly sent a telegram. Quincey went out to the station to meet him.

When Arthur arrived, it was already close to 6 p.m. The warm, red sunset light came in through the window, breathing a faint flush into Lucy's pale cheeks. Facing Lucy lying on the sickbed, Arthur could barely breathe due to overwhelming sorrow, and we too were choked up and couldn't say a word. As time went by, the frequency of her falling asleep or slipping into a coma increased, and even the time we could talk to her was drastically shrinking. However, Arthur's presence seemed to act as a powerful stimulant for her. Surprisingly, she regained her energy and spoke to him with a bright smile we hadn't seen once since we arrived. Arthur, too, desperately suppressing his sorrow, answered in the brightest and most cheerful voice he could, trying his best to erase the despairing atmosphere by her side.

It is now heading towards 1 a.m. Arthur and Van Helsing are keeping watch by Lucy. I intend to change shifts with them in 15 minutes, and am using the remaining spare time to leave this record on Lucy's phonograph. They plan to get some sleep until 6 a.m. Unfortunately, the poor child is failing to recover her strength in the end. Because the terrible shock was too cruel to endure, I fear our desperate watch will also come to an end tomorrow. Oh Lord, please look after us.
