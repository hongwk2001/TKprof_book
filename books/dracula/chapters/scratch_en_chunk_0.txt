Chapter 21

Dr. Seward's Diary

October 3. — I will record the events since my last entry as accurately as I can remember them. I must not leave out a single trivial detail. To do so, I must maintain a very calm and composed state of mind.

When I arrived at Renfield's room, he was lying on his left side on a floor slick with red blood. The moment I approached to move him, I immediately realized he was terribly injured. Even for a patient in a lethargic state, there was not a single natural movement of the body parts that should have been visible. When his face was revealed, it was frightfully bruised as if it had been smashed repeatedly against the floor, and the pool of blood on the floor came directly from those wounds. The attendant, who had been kneeling beside him, laid him straight and spoke to me.

"Doctor, I think his spine is broken. Look, his right limbs and the entire half of his face are completely paralyzed." The attendant was greatly perplexed, saying he had no idea how such a thing could have happened. With a heavily furrowed brow and a bewildered expression, he continued.

"Two things just don't add up. It's possible to injure a face like that by smashing it against the floor. When I was at the Eversfield Asylum, I saw a young woman self-harm like that before anyone could stop her. Or, he could have fallen wrong from the bed and broken his neck. But I'll bet my life that I can't even imagine how these two things happened at the same time. If his spine was broken, he wouldn't have had the strength to smash his head, and if his face was already like that before he fell from the bed, there should be bloodstains around the bed." I gave him instructions.

"Go to Dr. Van Helsing and ask if he can come here immediately. Do not delay for a single second." The attendant rushed off, and a few minutes later the Professor appeared in his dressing gown and slippers. As soon as he saw Renfield lying on the floor, he examined him sharply and then turned his head to me. It seemed he knew exactly what I was thinking just from the look in my eyes. The Professor spoke in a quiet but clear voice, as if meant for the attendant beside us to hear.

"Ah, what a truly tragic accident! We will need very careful observation and care. I shall stay here with you myself. But first, I must go dress properly. If you stay by his side, I will return in a few minutes."

The patient was now taking rough breaths mixed with a metallic wheeze, and it was obvious to anyone that he was horribly injured. Professor Van Helsing returned with his bag of surgical instruments with surprising speed. He had clearly assessed the situation and made a decision on the way. He whispered to me immediately, even before examining the patient again.

"Send the attendant away. When the patient wakes up after the surgery, only the two of us must be by his side." I spoke up.

"Simmons, that will be enough for now. I think we have done all we can here. You go and patrol the other wards. Dr. Van Helsing will perform the surgery himself. If you see anything even slightly unusual anywhere, let me know immediately."

Once the attendant left, we began to examine the patient meticulously. The wounds on his face were only gruesome in appearance; the truly fatal injury was a depressed skull fracture extending to the motor area of the brain. The Professor fell into thought for a moment and then spoke.

"We must relieve the pressure on the brain and restore it as closely to its normal state as possible. The speed at which blood is pooling in the brain shows just how severe the injury is. It seems the entire motor area is ruined. The congestion of the brain will rapidly worsen, so we must drill a hole in the skull immediately to drain the blood. Otherwise, it will be too late." As he spoke, someone knocked softly on the door. I stepped over and opened it to find Arthur and Quincey standing in the corridor in their pajamas and slippers. Arthur spoke first.

"The attendant said there was an accident while calling for you, Doctor. So I woke Quincey up. Well, Quincey was already awake, so I should say I just called him. Things are moving so fast and strangely these days that none of us can sleep deeply. It felt like things would be completely different by tomorrow night. We thought we should look around a bit more carefully than before. May we come in?" I nodded, leaving the door open until the two entered, and then closed it. Seeing the patient's oddly twisted body and the blood splattered on the floor, Quincey spoke quietly.

"Good God! What on earth happened here? What a truly pitiful man!" I briefly explained the situation and added that I hoped he would regain consciousness, even if only for a moment, after the surgery. Quincey walked straight over and sat on the edge of the bed, and Lord Godalming took a seat beside him. We all watched the situation in silence.

"We must wait a little," Dr. Van Helsing said. "I just need time to find the optimal position to drill the skull so we can drain the blood clots as quickly and cleanly as possible. The brain hemorrhage is clearly still worsening even now."

The few minutes of waiting passed with terrifying slowness. My heart felt as if it were sinking, and fear and anxiety about what was to come could be seen even on Professor Van Helsing's hardened face. I was dreadfully afraid of the words that might pour from Renfield's mouth. Frankly, I was even terrified to think about it. But like a condemned man awaiting his execution, the certainty that something was about to happen weighed heavily upon me. Poor Renfield breathed in irregular gasps. It seemed as if he would open his eyes and speak at any moment, but then he would let out a long, metallic breath and sink repeatedly into a deeper swamp of unconsciousness. Though I was accustomed to sickbeds and death, my inner tension snowballed. The sound of my heartbeat rang in my ears, and the sound of blood rushing to my temples was as loud as hammering. Finally, the silence itself became an agony. Looking at my colleagues one by one, I could feel from their flushed faces and sweaty foreheads that they too were enduring the exact same torture. A nervous tension hung taut in the air above all of us, as if a terrifying bell might loudly toll at any unexpected moment.

At last, the critical moment arrived when it would not have been strange for the patient to stop breathing at any second. When I looked up at the Professor, he too fixed his gaze on me. His voice flowed over his firm and resolute face.

"There is no time to delay any longer. A few words from this man might save countless lives. That was all I could think about while standing here. Perhaps a soul is at stake! I will make the incision right above the ear."

Without another word, he immediately began the surgery. For several minutes, rough breathing continued. Then came the sound of a long, deep breath being drawn in, as if tearing the chest apart. Suddenly, the patient's eyes snapped open, and he stared blankly at one spot with a wild, unfocused gaze. This state lasted for a moment, but soon his eyes softened, changing into an expression mixed with gladness and surprise. A sigh of relief escaped through his lips. Shivering as if in a seizure, he spoke.

"I will be quiet, Doctor. Please tell them to take off this straitjacket. I had a terrible nightmare, and my strength is so drained I cannot move my body. What is wrong with my face? It's swollen and hurts so much." He tried to turn his head, but even at that small movement, his pupils dilated again and lost focus. I gently turned his head back to its original position. Then Dr. Van Helsing spoke in a calm and solemn tone.
