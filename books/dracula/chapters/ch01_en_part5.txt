Just then, a dog started barking mournfully from a farmhouse far below. It sounded like a terrifying, agonizing scream that made my hair stand on end. As if answering the call, a neighbor's dog barked, and then another. Soon, carried by the eerie wind sweeping through the mountain pass, the horrific howling of wild dogs echoed from every direction. Startled by the first howl, the horses snorted fiercely and reared up on their hind legs, but calmed down once the driver soothed them. However, they still trembled like leaves and sweat poured off them. At that moment, a louder, sharper roar erupted from deep within the mountains on both sides. It was the howling of wolves.

The horses and I panicked at the same time. I wanted to jump out of the carriage and run away immediately. The horses reared up again and jumped wildly, forcing the driver to use incredible strength to hold them down. But by the time my ears grew accustomed to the horrific howling, the horses had also gradually calmed down enough for the driver to step down in front of them. He stroked and petted the horses, whispering in their ears in a low voice. He seemed like a master horse trainer. The effect was truly amazing. The horses were still trembling, but they quickly became as obedient as gentle sheep. When the driver got back in his seat and shook the reins, we sped off like an arrow.

This time, we drove all the way to the end of the pass before sharply turning onto a narrow mountain road leading to the right.

Soon, dense trees surrounded us, their branches forming an arch overhead that made it feel like we were passing through a deep tunnel. Massive, imposing rocks glared down at us from both sides. Even inside the carriage, the eerie howling of the wind squeezing through the rock crevices assaulted my ears. Every time the carriage rushed forward, the branches thrashing about in the wind let out loud screams. The air grew bitingly cold, and soon fine, powdery snow began to fall, burying us and everything around us in white dust. Over the fierce wind, the howling of wild dogs could be heard sparsely, but it seemed to be fading away. In contrast, the ferocious howling of the wolves grew clearer and clearer, as if they were closing in on us from all sides. I wasn't the only one consumed by extreme terror. The horses were also going mad with anxiety. Yet, the driver remained perfectly calm. He merely kept looking left and right, though to my eyes, there was nothing but pitch-black darkness.

Suddenly, a faint blue flame flickered in the distance on my side. The driver saw it too. He abruptly stopped the carriage, jumped to the ground, and vanished completely into the darkness. With the wolves' howling practically at our throats, I didn't know what to do. While I sat trembling in pure terror, the driver silently reappeared, took his seat, and the carriage started moving again. I wished it were just a nightmare. No, it had to be a nightmare, because the exact same thing happened over and over again in a maddening loop. Looking back, those moments were a hellish nightmare itself. Once, the flame appeared so close to the road that I could clearly see the driver, even in the thick darkness.

He walked over to where the blue flame was flickering. Strangely, the flame did not light up its surroundings at all. The driver gathered some stones to make some sort of marker. When he stood between the flame and me, a bizarre phenomenon occurred that made me doubt my own eyes. Even though his massive body stood in front of the flame, blocking it, I could see right through him and clearly observe the ghostly blue glow flickering behind him. I was shocked, but since it happened in a split second, I assumed it was just a trick of my eyes from staring too hard into the deep darkness.

After that, the blue flame did not appear for a while. We raced through the darkness, surrounded by the howling of a wolf pack that seemed to be moving in a giant circle, tracking our every move.

Finally, the time came when the driver had to walk much further away than before. While he was gone, the horses trembled even more violently, snorting and going insane with fear. I couldn't figure out why. The wolves' howling had completely stopped. Right at that moment, the moonlight broke through the dark clouds and peeked out from behind a sheer, pine-covered rocky peak. In the pale moonlight around us, wolves with massive, hairy legs were forming a giant circle, completely surrounding us with their white teeth bared and their bright red tongues hanging out. It was a hundred times more terrifying to see them crouching in dead silence than when they were howling. My entire body froze in fear. It is only when a person faces such overwhelming terror head-on that they truly understand what it looks like.

Suddenly, as if on cue, the wolves all pointed their noses to the moonlight and began to howl. The horses rolled their eyes back until only the whites showed and kicked their front legs in a frenzy. But trapped inside a living wall of terror enclosing us from every side, we couldn't take a single step. I screamed for the driver to return at once. My only hope was for him to break through the pack and get back to the carriage. To somehow create a gap, I pounded on the side of the carriage and yelled at the top of my lungs. But out of nowhere, the driver's thunderous roar tore through the air.

I looked toward the sound and saw him standing solidly right in the middle of the road. As he swung his long arms as if pulling back an invisible curtain, the wolf pack unbelievably tucked their tails and slunk backward. Just then, a heavy cloud swallowed the moon, and the world was plunged back into perfect darkness.

When my vision returned, the driver was climbing onto the covered carriage, and the wolf pack had vanished without a trace. The whole situation was so bizarre and horrifying that I couldn't move an inch or even make a sound. While we raced through the pitch-black darkness hidden from the moonlight by clouds, one minute felt like a year. Sometimes we shot down steep descents, but for the most part, the carriage was climbing a steep uphill path. When I finally came to my senses, the driver was stopping the carriage in the courtyard of an incredibly massive, ruined castle. There wasn't a single light in the tall, pitch-black windows, and only the crumbling battlements showed off their grotesque silhouettes against the moonlight.
