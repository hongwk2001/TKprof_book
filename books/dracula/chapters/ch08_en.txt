[P001] Chapter 8

[P002] Mina Murray's Journal

[P003a] Same day, 11 PM. - Oh, I am so tired! If I hadn't made it a duty to keep a journal, I wouldn't have even opened it tonight. We had a very pleasant walk. Lucy soon regained her spirits. It was thanks to the cute cows that surprised us by sniffing around near the lighthouse fields. Gripped by fear, all other thoughts completely vanished. Thanks to that, my complicated mind cleared up, and it seems we could make a fresh start. We had a wonderful dinner and tea at a lovely old inn in Robin Hood's Bay. It overlooked a beach covered in seaweed from a window bulging out over the rocks. If they had seen our enormous appetites, even the snobbish "New Women" would have been shocked. It is such a relief that men are more generous in that regard! After that, we walked home. We walked slowly, terrified that a wild bull might appear. Lucy was extremely exhausted, so we intended to hurry to bed. However, the young curate visited, and Mrs. Westenra held him back to stay for dinner. Lucy and I had to fight a tough battle against the oncoming sleepiness. At least for me, it required a tremendous amount of patience. Someday, the bishops should gather and discuss a plan to train curates who know how to politely decline a dinner invitation no matter how much it is offered. Ones who can notice when girls are tired.
[P003b] Lucy is fast asleep, breathing softly. She looks truly lovely with a rosier flush on her cheeks than usual. Mr. Arthur Holmwood fell in love just seeing her in the drawing-room, but what would he say if he saw her like this now? Perhaps future "New Woman" writers might argue that men and women should see each other sleeping before proposing or accepting. But a future New Woman wouldn't just receive proposals. They will propose themselves and do it brilliantly! That thought gives me a little comfort. I am very happy tonight because my beloved Lucy looks much better. I want to believe that we are truly past the crisis and the sleepwalking issue is now over. I would be happier than anything if I only knew how Jonathan is doing.... May God bless and protect him.

[P003b] *       *       *       *       *

[P004_005] August 11, 3 AM. - I am writing in my journal again. I can't sleep now, so it's better to write. My mind is so anxious that I can't fall asleep. I went through something truly terrible and agonizing. I fell asleep as soon as I closed my journal.... Then I jolted awake with a terrible sense of dread and a chilling feeling that the space around me was empty. The room was so dark that I couldn't see Lucy's bed, so I crept over and felt around with my hands. The bed was empty. I struck a match and saw that Lucy was not in the room. The door was closed, but not locked as I had left it. Terrified of waking my recently ailing mother, I hastily threw on some clothes and got ready to go find Lucy. As I was leaving the room, I thought I could guess where she went based on the clothes she wore. If it was indoor clothing, she'd be in the house; if it was outdoor clothing, it meant she went outside. However, both her indoor and outdoor clothes were hanging in their places. "Thank God," I sighed with relief inside. "She's in her nightgown, so she couldn't have gone far." I ran downstairs and checked the living room, but she wasn't there! Then I searched all the other open rooms in the house, but my fear only grew. Finally, I reached the front door, and it was open. It wasn't wide open, but it was unlocked. Since the people in this house strictly lock the doors every night, I was gripped by terror, certain that Lucy must have gone outside in her nightgown. I didn't even have time to weigh the consequences. A vague, overwhelming fear made my mind go blank. I grabbed a large, thick shawl and rushed outside.
[P004_005b] When I reached the crescent street, the clock struck 1 AM, and there wasn't even a shadow of a person on the street. I ran along the North Terrace, but I couldn't see the white figure I was looking for. From the edge of the West Cliff above the pier, I looked over the harbor toward the East Cliff, hoping Lucy would be at our favorite spot, or hoping she wouldn't be. The full moon was bright, but dark clouds quickly rolled in, making the whole world sway with light and shadow. When the thick clouds covered St. Mary's Church, I couldn't see anything for a moment. Then the clouds passed, and the abbey ruins appeared again. As the narrow, sharp moonlight shifted like a blade, the church and the graveyard were illuminated in turn. My sad premonition was not wrong. Right there, on our favorite bench, I saw a snow-white figure half-reclining under the moonlight. The clouds moved quickly, and my vision soon darkened again. But for a brief moment, I thought I saw something dark standing bent over behind the white figure. I had no way of knowing if it was a person or a beast.
[P004_005c] Without a moment to spare, I darted down the steep stairs toward the pier. I passed the fish market and crossed the bridge, the only path to the East Cliff. The town was dead silent without a soul in sight, which rather gave me a sense of relief that no one would see poor Lucy like this. The distance felt infinitely far, and as I climbed the endless stairs leading to the abbey, my knees trembled and I was out of breath. I ran very fast, but my feet felt as heavy as lead, and my joints creaked as if they were rusted. By the time I reached the top, I was close enough to distinguish the bench and the white figure even in the shadows. Undoubtedly, something long and dark was bending over the half-reclining white figure. Horrified, I shouted, "Lucy! Lucy!" and it raised its head. I could clearly see a white face and two eyes glowing red. Lucy didn't answer, and I kept running toward the tomb entrance. When I entered the gateway, the church blocked the bench, momentarily obscuring my view. When my vision cleared again, the clouds had lifted, and the moonlight was pouring down dazzlingly. Lucy was half-lying with her head resting on the back of the bench. There was no one beside her, and not a single living thing in sight.

[P006] Going closer and looking down, she was still fast asleep. Her lips were parted, and unlike her usual soft breathing, she was panting heavily and roughly with every breath, as if trying to fill her lungs with air. As I got closer, she raised her hand in her sleep and pulled the collar of her nightgown around her neck. Then she shivered as if she felt a chill. Afraid she might catch a cold in the deadly night air in just a thin nightgown, I quickly covered her with the warm shawl and wrapped it tightly around her neck. Careful not to wake her, I fastened the shawl at her neck with a large safety pin. But I must have been so nervous that I pinned it clumsily. For as her breathing calmed down, she brought her hand to her neck again and let out a groan. After wrapping her well, I took off my shoes, put them on her feet, and carefully shook her awake. At first, there was no reaction, but gradually she began to groan, sigh, and toss around. Time was running out, and for various reasons, we had to return home quickly, so I shook her harder. Finally, she opened her eyes. Not fully realizing where she was, she wasn't surprised to see me. Lucy always wakes up gracefully. Even in the shocking moment of waking up shivering in the cold in a graveyard at midnight in thin clothes, she didn't lose her elegance. She trembled slightly and hugged me, and then obediently got up like a child when I told her we had to go home immediately. As we walked, she flinched when her bare feet were pricked by the gravel path, and she stopped, stubbornly insisting on giving my shoes back. But I flatly refused. However, as we left the graveyard and passed a puddle left by the storm, I deliberately coated both her feet in mud so that they wouldn't be noticeable if we happened to run into someone.

[P007a] Luckily, we arrived home safely without encountering a single person. We did see a drunk man staggering on the way, but we hid in a doorway until he disappeared down a narrow, steep Scottish-style alley. My heart pounded so violently the whole time that I thought I would faint. I was consumed with worry that the cold midnight wind might ruin Lucy's health, or that if this incident leaked out, her reputation might be tarnished. Once inside, I washed her feet, we said a prayer of thanks together, and I put her to bed. Just before falling asleep, Lucy earnestly begged me not to tell anyone, not even her mother, about her sleepwalking outside. At first, I hesitated to promise. But considering how weak Mrs. Westenra's heart is, how shocked she would be if she found out, and how the story might get twisted if a rumor spread, I decided it was wiser to keep it buried. I pray this is the right decision. I locked the door securely and tied the key to my wrist. Nothing more will happen tonight. Lucy is fast asleep, and dawn is already breaking far out over the sea....

[P007b] *       *       *       *       *

[P008_009] Same day, noon. - Fortunately, nothing happened. Lucy slept deeply until I woke her, and it seems she didn't toss or turn once all night. The midnight adventure, rather than doing harm, seems to have been medicine, as her complexion is much better than it has been in recent weeks. I felt terribly sorry when I realized I had hurt her by pinning the safety pin wrong. It actually pierced the skin on her neck, so it could have been dangerous. I must have accidentally pinched the loose skin and pierced it. There were two distinct red spots like needle pricks, and drops of blood on the collar of her nightgown. When I didn't know what to do because I was so sorry, Lucy smiled brightly, patted my hand, and said she didn't feel a thing. The wound is so small that it probably won't leave a scar, which is a relief amidst the misfortune.

[P009b] *       *       *       *       *

[P010_011] Same day, night. - We had a truly perfect day. Clear air, dazzling sunshine, and a cool breeze. We packed a lunch and headed to Mulgrave Woods. Mrs. Westenra went up the main road in a carriage, and Lucy and I walked along the cliff path and met her at the entrance. My nose tingled as I thought of how perfect the day would have been if Jonathan had been by my side. But I suppose I'll have to endure a little longer! In the evening, we strolled along the Casino terrace, listened to wonderful music by Spohr and Mackenzie, and went to bed early. Lucy looked more relaxed than she had in a long time and fell into a deep sleep as soon as her head hit the pillow. It seems nothing will happen tonight, but just in case, I plan to lock the door and tie the key tightly to my wrist like yesterday.

[P011b] *       *       *       *       *

[P012_013] August 12. - I was completely wrong. I woke up during the night because Lucy tried to go outside twice. Even in her sleep, she seemed quite angry that the door was locked and grumbled as she returned to bed. When I woke up around the break of dawn, I heard the chirping of birds outside the window. I was relieved to see Lucy looking much healthier than the previous morning when she opened her eyes. It seemed her former lively self had fully returned. She burrowed into my bed, hugged me tight, and rattled on about Arthur. When I confessed how anxious I was worrying about Jonathan, she did her best to comfort me. It was a huge consolation. Even if sympathy doesn't change reality, it gives you the strength to endure.

[P013b] *       *       *       *       *

[P014_015] August 13. - We had a peaceful day, and I lay in bed with the key tied to my wrist as before. When I woke up again in the middle of the night, Lucy was sitting straight up in bed, still asleep, pointing at the window. I quietly got up, pulled back the blind, and looked out. Under the dazzling moonlight, the sea and the sky blended softly; it was breathtakingly beautiful, as if plunged into a massive, silent mystery. Between me and the moonlight, a large bat was wildly flying in circles. Once or twice, it swooped close to the window, but perhaps startled by seeing me, it flew across the harbor toward the abbey. When I turned back from the window, Lucy was lying peacefully, fast asleep again. She didn't move an inch until morning after that.

[P016_017] August 14. - I sat on the East Cliff all day reading and writing. Lucy seems as captivated by that spot as I am, so it's quite a struggle to get her home for meals. This afternoon, she said something truly incomprehensible. On our way back for dinner, we stopped to admire the view at the top of the steps above the West Pier, as we always do. The sun was just about to set behind Kettleness, and the red sunset was dazzlingly tinting the East Cliff and the old abbey ruins in rosy hues. We stood in silence when Lucy suddenly muttered to herself.

[P018] "His red eyes are back! Just like before." I was startled by her abrupt words. Trying not to make it obvious that I was staring, I turned my body slightly to observe her, and Lucy had a strange expression, half-dazed. Unable to figure out what was going on, I silently followed her gaze. She was staring straight at our designated seat. There was a dark figure sitting there alone. For a moment, I was a little taken aback too. The stranger's eyes seemed to flash like fiercely burning flames. But upon closer inspection, it was just an optical illusion. The red sunset was merely reflecting off the windows of St. Mary's Church behind our seat. As the sun set, the angle of the light changed slightly, making the glare look like it was moving. When I pointed out that bizarre trick of the light, Lucy was startled and snapped out of her daze, but she still looked sad. Perhaps she remembered that terrible night. Since we agreed not to breathe a word about that incident, I didn't press her either, and we went straight home and ate dinner. Lucy got a headache and went to bed early. Seeing her fast asleep, I went out for a short walk by myself.
[P018b] While walking on the West Cliff, I fell into a sweet melancholy thinking about Jonathan. By the time I returned home, the moonlight was so bright that even though the front of our building was shaded, everything was clearly visible. I looked up at our room window, and Lucy was leaning out. Thinking she was waiting for me, I waved my handkerchief. But she didn't budge. Just then, the moonlight rounded the corner of the building and illuminated our window. It clearly revealed Lucy leaning her head against the window frame with her eyes tightly shut. She was fast asleep. Beside her on the windowsill sat something like a rather large bird. Worried she might catch a cold, I ran upstairs in a single breath. But by the time I entered the room, she was already stumbling back to bed, panting heavily in her sleep. She was clutching her neck tightly with both hands, as if trying to protect it from the cold.

[P019] I didn't bother to wake her and warmly covered her with blankets. I double-checked carefully that the door was locked and the window was tightly closed.

[P020a] She looks incredibly lovely when sleeping, but my heart is heavy because she is much paler than usual and has dark circles under her eyes. She must be secretly agonizing over something on her own. I wish I could figure out what it is.

[P020b] *       *       *       *       *

[P021_022] August 15. - I woke up later than usual. Lucy looked so exhausted she couldn't steady herself, and she fell back asleep even after the maid woke us and left. During breakfast, I heard some welcome news. Arthur's father's condition has improved, and he wants them to have the wedding soon. Lucy calmly rejoiced, and her mother seemed to have mixed feelings. Late in the afternoon, Mrs. Westenra told me the reason. She is sad to let Lucy go to another man, but she is more than happy that her daughter now has a strong protector to watch over her. Poor, sweet lady! She confided only to me that she essentially received a death sentence. She repeatedly begged me to keep it a secret from Lucy. She was told by the doctor that her heart is failing and she won't last more than a few months. Even a very minor shock could be immediately fatal to her right now. Oh, it was a truly excellent choice to keep Lucy's sleepwalking incident a secret from her that night.

[P022b] *       *       *       *       *

[P023_024] August 17. - I haven't been able to write in my journal for two days. I just haven't had the energy for it. It feels as if dark clouds are hanging heavily over our happiness. There is still no news from Jonathan, Lucy is growing weaker by the day, and her mother's life has entered a countdown. I have no idea why Lucy is withering away like that. She has a hearty appetite, sleeps well, and gets fresh air, but the color in her cheeks is fading, and she becomes more lethargic and listless every day. At night, I hear her panting heavily, as if she can hardly breathe. Every night I tie the room key tightly to my wrist, but she still gets up, paces the room, and slumps by the open window. Last night, I woke up terrified to find her leaning halfway out the window. I tried shaking her awake, but she didn't budge. She was completely unconscious. By the time I managed to revive her, she was limp as wet cotton, silently pouring out tears while gasping for breath painfully. When I asked why she went to the window, she shook her head and avoided my eyes. I only pray that the wound from that safety pin hasn't become infected. When I checked her neck while she was sleeping a little while ago, the wound hadn't healed at all; in fact, it was still open and seemed larger than before. The skin around it was pale white, making it look exactly as if there were two red spots side by side in the middle of a white patch. I intend to watch her for another day or two, and if it doesn't get better, I'll have a doctor examine her immediately.

[P040_041] Mina Murray's Journal

[P042] August 18. - I am extremely happy today. I am sitting on a bench in the churchyard writing this. Lucy has become remarkably healthier. Last night, she slept soundly without waking me up once. She still looks pitifully pale and gaunt, but a rosy vitality has already begun to return to her cheeks. She is now cheerfully regaining her energy and sparkling with vitality. She seems to have completely washed away her recent morbid reticence. She even playfully brought up what happened that night—when I found her sleeping on this very bench—a memory I don't even need to dwell on. Lucy tapped the stone slab with the heel of her shoe and added.

[P043] "My poor little feet didn't make much noise back then! If old Mr. Swales were alive, he probably would have said I did it because I didn't want to wake Geordie (the owner of this grave)." Since Lucy was in such a talkative mood, I asked her what she dreamt about that night. She frowned before answering, an adorable expression that Arthur reportedly loves. I can completely understand why he fell for her. Soon after, she spoke in a half-dreamy tone, as if trying to drag the memory out of her mind.

[P044] "It was all too vivid to be a dream. I just wanted to be right here. I don't know why. I was afraid of something. Even without knowing what it was. I remember passing through the streets and crossing the bridge while asleep. A fish jumped up and I leaned over to look, and as I climbed the stairs, I heard all the dogs in the neighborhood barking. Then, just like I saw at sunset, a long, dark figure with flashing red eyes vaguely appeared, and a bittersweet feeling suddenly embraced me. After that, I felt like I was sinking deep into dark blue water, and that ringing sound people supposedly hear when they are drowning echoed in my ears. Then everything seemed to fade away. It felt like my soul left my body and was floating in the air. I think I also saw the West Lighthouse beneath my feet. Then, a pain hit me as if there were an earthquake, and when I regained my senses, you were shaking my body. I saw you shaking me before I even felt it on my body."

[P045a] Then Lucy burst into laughter. I felt somewhat terrified and listened to her words with bated breath. Since I was rather reluctant and felt it wasn't good to keep clinging to that topic, I changed the subject, and Lucy returned to her bright old self. By the time we arrived home, the fresh wind had revived her spirits, and her pale cheeks were tinged with a rosy hue. Her mother was also very delighted to see Lucy like that, and we all spent a very happy evening together.

[P045b] *       *       *       *       *

Now that I know the reason, I'm not afraid to think or talk about it. Mr. Peter Hawkins passed the letter to me, and oh, how sweetly he wrote to me directly. I plan to leave first thing tomorrow morning to nurse Jonathan by his side and bring him safely home. Mr. Hawkins suggested it might not be a bad idea to have the wedding right there while we're at it. I held the nun's warm letter to my chest and cried my eyes out. Since it's a letter with news of Jonathan, I must keep it closest to my heart. For my heart is completely filled with Jonathan. All the travel plans are made, and my bags are packed. I only packed one outfit, and Lucy will take my trunk to London and keep it for me until I contact her. Perhaps... I should stop here. I need to save the things I want to say for Jonathan, my future husband. This letter, which he must have touched and looked at, will comfort me until we meet again.

[P057] Dr. Seward's Diary

[P058] August 19. - A sudden and bizarre change appeared in Renfield last night. Around 8 o'clock, he became highly excited and sniffed around everywhere like a hound looking for prey. The attendant was surprised by this sight and, knowing I was very interested in him, kept trying to talk to him. He, who was usually obedient and sometimes even groveling to the attendant, acted very arrogantly last night. He refused to even speak to the attendant, spitting out only this.

[P059] "I don't want to talk to you. You are nothing now. The 'Master' is near."

[P060] The attendant judged that he was gripped by sudden religious mania. If true, we must prepare for any contingencies. A man of monstrous strength with a combination of homicidal impulses and religious mania is extremely dangerous. When these two combine, it is a truly horrific disaster. At 9 o'clock, I went to see him myself. His attitude towards me was no different from how he treated the attendant. Wrapped up in sublime narcissism, the difference between me and the attendant seemed to mean nothing to him. It definitely looks like religious mania, and soon he will delude himself into thinking he is God. To such an omnipotent being, minor differences among humans would seem trivial. See how easily these madmen reveal their true colors! The true God watches even a single sparrow fall to the ground, but a god born of human vanity cannot even distinguish between an eagle and a sparrow. Oh, if only people realized this truth!

[P061] For over thirty minutes, Renfield became increasingly and violently excited. I didn't make it obvious I was watching him, but I didn't let my guard down for a single moment. Suddenly, the cunning gleam that madmen often have when they are fixated on something flashed in his eyes, followed by the characteristic nervous twitching of his head and back that mental hospital attendants are sickeningly familiar with. Then Renfield became unbelievably docile, sitting on the edge of the bed as if resigned, staring blankly into the air with dull eyes. To test whether his lethargy was real or an act, I casually brought up the topic of his pets (bugs), a cheat code that had never failed. At first he kept his mouth shut, but finally snapped in irritation.

[P062] "It's all annoying! I don't care the slightest bit about those things."

[P063] "What?" I asked back. "You don't mean you've lost interest in spiders, do you?" (His recent hobby was raising spiders, and his notebook was filled with tables crammed with tiny numbers.) To this, he threw an enigmatic answer.

[P064] "The bridesmaids delight the eyes while waiting for the bride. But when the bride approaches, those bridesmaids lose their light in eyes that are already filled."

[P065] He refused to explain further, and stubbornly just sat on the bed the whole time I was there.

[P066a] Tonight I am terribly exhausted and depressed. I can't shake off thoughts of Lucy, and my mind is full of thoughts about what would have happened if things had turned out differently. If I can't fall asleep right away, chloral (sleeping pill)—C₂HCl₃O·H₂O, the modern-day Morpheus (God of Dreams)! I must be careful not to get addicted to it. No, I will not take anything tonight! Since I am thinking of Lucy, I will not desecrate her by mixing sleeping pills. If worse comes to worst, I will stay up all night with my eyes wide open....

[P066b] *       *       *       *       *

[P067_068] Later. - I am so glad I did that. And even more glad that I kept that resolution. While tossing and turning unable to sleep, I only heard the clock strike two (around 2 AM), when the ward night watchman rushed up and reported that Renfield had escaped. Knowing he was too dangerous a patient to let roam free without restraint, I hastily threw on some clothes and ran straight downstairs. There was no telling what harm his dangerous delusions might inflict on strangers. The attendant was waiting for me. He said just ten minutes ago when he looked through the observation window, the patient seemed to be sleeping in his bed. Then, startled by the sound of the window being ripped out, he ran over and saw the patient's feet slipping out the window, so he urgently sent someone to fetch me. Being in his nightclothes, he couldn't have run far. The attendant judged that chasing him while he was exiting the building might actually result in losing him, so it was better to watch his movements to see where he was heading. Renfield was large, so it was hard for him to slip out the window all at once. Being thin, I climbed out the window feet first with the attendant's help, and landed safely without injury as it was only a few feet above the ground. Hearing from the attendant that the patient turned left and ran straight ahead, I also chased after him at top speed. As I passed through a dense patch of trees, I saw a white figure easily leaping over the high wall separating the asylum and the abandoned Carfax estate.

[P069] Fearing that our patient might do something unpredictable, I immediately turned back and instructed the watchman to bring three or four men and follow me into the Carfax estate. I found a ladder myself, climbed over the wall, and jumped down the other side. Seeing Renfield's figure dart behind a corner of the house, I ran after him. Running around to the back of the house, I found Renfield clinging tightly to the old, iron-studded oak door of the chapel. He seemed to be conversing with someone, but I didn't dare get close enough to eavesdrop on what he was saying, lest he get startled and run away. Chasing a swarm of lost bees is preferable to chasing a naked madman gripped by an instinct to escape! However, after a few minutes, realizing he was completely oblivious to his surroundings, I mustered the courage to take a step closer. I felt much more at ease because my men had climbed over the wall and surrounded him. I clearly heard what he was saying.

[P070] "Master, I am here to do your bidding. I am your slave, and I will be loyal, so please grant me my reward. I have worshipped you from afar for a very long time.
