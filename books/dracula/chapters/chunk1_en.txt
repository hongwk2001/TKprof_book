"Aha!" the Professor answered with sparkling eyes. "Your comparison to a tiger is absolutely brilliant. I will borrow your excellent analogy, Madam. The beast that the people of India call a man-eating tiger, once it tastes the hot blood of a human, pays no attention to the meat of other beasts and restlessly prowls around the village until it snatches a human. The monster we drove out is also a tiger, a terrible man-eater at that. He will never stop prowling. No, his very nature is not that of someone who would shrink back and cower. Back when he still lived a life with human blood flowing in his veins, he crossed the rugged Turkish border and charged right into the middle of the enemy camp. Even though he was defeated and retreated, did he give up there? Absolutely not! He came, and came again, stubbornly invading once more. Look at his terrible obsession and patience. Even with a childlike brain, he schemed to cross over to the capital of a great empire for a long time. So what did he do? He relentlessly found the most perfect stage in the world for him to run wild. Then, with terrifying patience, he put his plan into action step by step. He carefully calculated how much power he had, and fiercely learned unfamiliar languages. He absorbed all the rules of the new world that had completely changed since he was born—politics, law, finance, science, and even customs. His brief experience peering into the land of England only whetted his appetite and sharply honed the blade of his desire. No, it rather expanded his brain even more cunningly. Because he confirmed with his own two eyes that all his careful hypotheses had fallen perfectly into place. He achieved all these miracles entirely on his own in a forgotten land, inside a rotting tomb! If that vast world of knowledge is thrown wide open, what on earth wouldn't he be able to commit? He is a creature who will laugh at death and survive unharmed and thrive even amidst a terrible plague sweeping the whole country. Oh, if such a being were not a bastard of the devil but a great apostle sent by God, what a shining blessing he would have been to this sick world. But we have sworn to liberate this world from his clutches. Our fight must be fought in suffocating silence, kept secretly to our very bones. Because in this arrogant age of enlightenment that distrusts even the miracles seen with its own two eyes, the foolish doubts of intellectuals will be the Count's most powerful weapon. Those doubts will immediately become a steel armor wrapping the Count, and a weapon of slaughter that ruthlessly tramples us who are ready to willingly throw away even our souls to save the lives of our loved ones—and furthermore, for the salvation of humanity and the glory of God."

After a long discussion, we decided not to make any hasty decisions tonight. For now, the idea was to get some good rest holding all these heavy truths, clear our heads, and try to make rational judgments. We agreed to gather around the breakfast table tomorrow morning, share our thoughts openly, and then make a concrete plan on how to act going forward.



Tonight is unbelievably peaceful and drowsy. It feels as if the terrible evil spirit that was strangling me has been completely washed away. Perhaps like this...

My fragile hope did not reach a conclusion. It couldn't. Because the moment I saw the red brand on my forehead reflected in the mirror, I keenly realized the fact that I myself was still terribly unclean.

Dr. Seward's Journal.

October 5.—I woke up early in the morning. Perhaps thanks to a good night's sleep, everyone's faces looked lively. When we sat around the early breakfast table, a cheerful laughter that none of us thought we would ever taste again filled the room.

There is truly a marvelous aspect to human resilience. If we just clear away the obstacles blocking our path—even if it is death—we stand up bravely finding hope and joy as if nothing ever happened. While sitting at the table laughing and chatting, I had to rub my eyes several times, wondering if all the hellish events we went through over the past few days were just a terrible nightmare. I was thrown back into cold reality only when I saw the clear red scar left on Mrs. Harker's forehead. Even right at this moment while I am lost in deep thought with a serious expression, the fact that the root of all these disasters is still alive and breathing seems utterly unreal. Even Mrs. Harker herself seems to completely forget her terrible fate for a while. Only when something suddenly awakens that nightmare does she startle and touch her scar. We decided to gather in my study in thirty minutes to discuss our plans going forward. But instinctively, I feel a huge wall. It's closer to a bone-chilling primal fear than a rational judgment. We will all have to pour out the stories in our hearts without hiding anything, but for some reason, I have an ominous premonition that poor Mrs. Harker's lips are tightly sewn shut by an unseen evil power. I know that she is drawing some conclusion on her own. And judging from the wisdom she has shown so far, I can also guess how sharp and accurate that conclusion will be. But she will never say it out loud, or she won't be able to. I confessed this uneasy premonition to Van Helsing, and we decided to dig deep into this matter when we were left alone. It's clear that the Count's terrible poison is running through her veins, slowly gnawing at her body and mind. When the Count forced her to drink what Van Helsing calls the "vampire's bloody baptism," there must have been his own wicked intention hidden in it. Even in excellent food, rotten toxins are formed. In this blindly rushing age where even the existence of invisible toxins hasn't been fully revealed, what more is there for us to be surprised about! The only thing I am certain of is that if my terrible premonition about poor Mrs. Harker's bizarre silence is correct, a terrifying trap that we cannot even imagine is yawning open on the path we must take. That evil power that tightly sealed her lips might, conversely, force her mouth open to strangle us. I don't want to imagine any further. For harboring such a terrible suspicion itself would be an insult to that noble and pitiful woman.

Van Helsing will come to my study a step ahead of the others. I intend to gather my courage and bring up this heavy topic to him.



Later.—As soon as the Professor entered the room, we discussed the situation with serious faces. I immediately noticed that he was hesitating to speak, even though he was carrying a heavy burden in his heart. After beating around the bush for a long time, he finally opened his mouth heavily.

"Friend John, there is an issue that just you and I need to address right now. Of course, we will have to confess it to the other friends later." He cleared his throat and carefully chose his words for a moment, and I swallowed dryly and waited. He continued in a somber voice.

"Madam Mina... Our poor, lovely Madam Mina is slowly changing." The moment my worst imagination hardened into fact, a cold chill ran down my spine. Van Helsing continued with a devastated expression.

"We made a painful mistake of losing Miss Lucy. This time, we must strike first before the situation irreversibly worsens. Our mission has become unimaginably difficult, and because of this damn variable, every minute and second has become a blood-drying fight. In my eyes, I can clearly see the shadow of the vampire casting over Madam's face. It's still very faint, but if you take off the glasses of prejudice and look straight at it, it's a change that can never be hidden. Madam's teeth have noticeably sharpened, and sometimes she flashes an eerily cold look. But that's not all. Just like Miss Lucy did, right now Madam is keeping her mouth shut too often and escaping into silence. Miss Lucy also didn't utter a single word out loud even when writing in her diary what she wanted to leave for us later. This is exactly what I fear most right now. If we can hypnotize Madam to eavesdrop on what the Count sees and hears, then conversely, couldn't the Count, who controls Madam by feeding her his blood, also dig through her mind at any time he wants and thoroughly uncover our plans?" I nodded silently in despair, and he sighed and continued.

"Then there is only one thing we can do. We have no choice but to completely hide our plans from Madam so that they don't reach the Count's ears. Because she can't spill what she doesn't know. Oh, it is truly cruel! Just thinking about it breaks my heart, but there's nothing we can do. When the meeting starts today, I will have to give Madam an excuse that I cannot even bear to say, and notify her to stop attending the meetings and just receive our protection." Was it because of the guilt of having to drive another dagger into a poor woman suffering from terrible pain? Thick beads of sweat had already formed on the Professor's wrinkled forehead. I thought that if I confessed that I, too, had reached the same conclusion as the Professor, I could at least slightly lighten the burden of him agonizing over whether his judgment was right. I honestly told him my thoughts, and as expected, he let out a deep sigh of relief.

It is already time for everyone to gather. Van Helsing hardened his heart and left with heavy steps to prepare for that cruel notification. He is probably back in his room, kneeling alone and praying earnestly.



Later.—Even before the meeting started, Van Helsing and I felt a tremendous sense of relief as if a ten-year-old blockage had gone down. Mrs. Harker sent a note through her husband, politely notifying her absence by saying that it would be better for her to stay out as her presence would only hinder the discussion of our moves. The Professor and I exchanged glances for a fleeting moment, and an unmistakable smile of relief spread across both our lips. Since she herself had sensed the impending danger and removed herself from the seat, we had managed to evade a terrible danger without causing painful wounds. We exchanged knowing looks and quietly placed our fingers on our lips, making a silent promise to take this terrible suspicion to our graves until we were left alone later. And then we immediately dove into discussing the pursuit operation without delay. Van Helsing outlined the rough framework and briefed us on the situation.

"The 'Czarina Catherine' slipped out of the Thames yesterday morning. Even if the ship runs day and night at its top speed, it will take at least three weeks to reach Varna. But if we take a train and travel over land, we can arrive in just three days. Now, let's assume the Count uses magic to control the weather and subtract two days from the ship's voyage time. Even if we allow a day or two considering all sorts of unexpected situations we might face while traveling, we are still given nearly two weeks of golden time. To thoroughly prepare for any contingencies, we must leave London by the 17th at the latest. Then we can arrive in Varna a day before the ship reaches the port and complete our full preparations. Of course, we intend to arm ourselves from head to toe. Not to mention dealing with the physical enemy that tears apart the flesh, but also firmly packing weapons to fight against the evil spirit that devours the soul."
