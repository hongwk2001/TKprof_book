Chapter 23

Dr. Seward's Diary

October 3.— The time spent waiting for Godalming and Quincey Morris felt terribly long. Professor Van Helsing constantly talked to us, trying to keep our attention so we wouldn't be distracted by other thoughts. From the occasional side glances he cast toward Jonathan Harker, I could read his kind intentions. Poor Jonathan was crushed under a despair so deep that it was hard to bear looking at him. Just last night, he was a young, cheerful man full of vitality with dark brown hair. But today, he had become a completely different person. His hollow, burning eyes, deeply carved wrinkles, and hair that had turned completely white made him look like an emaciated, aged old man. Fortunately, his spirit had not yet been entirely broken. No, rather, he was like a blazing fire. If everything went well, this fierce energy might be his salvation. This flame would pull him out of the swamp of despair and allow him to face reality once again. Poor friend. I thought my own pain was hard to endure, but in the face of his suffering, I shouldn't even dare to complain! The professor also knew this very well, so he was doing his best to somehow keep Jonathan's mind grounded. Meanwhile, the story the professor told us was fascinating enough to put all our nerves on edge even in such a desperate situation. If I recall correctly, it went roughly like this.

"Ever since the papers concerning this monster came into my hands, I have studied them over and over again. And the more I studied, the more I became convinced that we must completely uproot him. Evidence that the monster is evolving is scattered everywhere. It means that he is not just realizing his own strength, but he is also learning how to control it. According to the research of my friend Arminius in Budapest, Hungary, he was truly a phenomenal figure even when he was a human. He was a soldier and a politician, as well as an alchemist, which was the peak of scientific knowledge at the time. He possessed an extraordinary brain and unmatched scholarly knowledge, and he had a heart that knew neither fear nor remorse. It is said that there was no field of knowledge he didn't touch back then, going so far as to enter the legendary school of Scholomance to learn magic from the devil. Even after his physical body died, his remarkable intellect survived. Although his memory doesn't seem to be fully intact, as I said. Right now, some of his mental abilities are merely those of a child. But he is constantly growing, and the parts that were once like a child's have now taken on a rather adult form. He is brilliantly continuing his experiments. If we hadn't stood in his way, or rather, if we fail this time, he will become the founder of a new race born through death, not life."

Harker groaned and said, "So you mean all of this is aimed at my wife! But what on earth is that monster experimenting on? If we knew that, it would help us defeat him!"

"From the moment he first arrived here, he has been slowly but surely testing his powers. That giant child's brain has been working hard. The one fortunate thing for us is that his brain is still at a child's level. If he had acted boldly from the beginning, he would have already become so powerful that we would have been helpless. But he fully intends to succeed, and since he is someone who will live for hundreds of years, he has the luxury of waiting patiently and advancing his plans slowly. Make haste slowly. This Latin proverb must be his motto."

"I don't quite understand." Harker said in an exhausted voice. "Ah, please explain it a bit more clearly! My head seems to have grown dull from sorrow and agony."

The professor gently placed his hand on Harker's shoulder and said.

"Oh, my poor friend, I will explain it simply. You saw it too, didn't you? How this monster has recently been expanding his knowledge through experiments. Think about how he infiltrated our friend John's house using the madman Renfield, who eats animals. A vampire can freely come and go anytime once it has entered a place, but for the very first time, it must be invited by someone living in the house. But these things are nothing compared to his important experiments. Do you remember how he initially had all those numerous boxes of earth moved by the hands of others? Back then, he thought he had to do it that way. But as that giant child's brain grew, he began to wonder if he could move the boxes himself. So at first, he helped out, and when he realized that was fine, he later tried to move the boxes entirely on his own. As he developed in this way, he scattered his graves here and there, and those hidden locations became known to no one but him. He might have intended to bury those boxes deep underground. Doing so would have made them perfect for use only at night or when he could freely change his shape, and no one would have known that they were his hideouts! But, my boy, do not despair. He realized that knowledge just a hair too late! All but one of his hideouts have already been neutralized by us placing the holy Host, and before sunset, even that remaining one will be taken care of. Then he will have nowhere to move to and nowhere to hide. The reason I delayed our actions this morning was to make absolutely sure of all this. Isn't what we have at stake much greater than his? So it stands to reason that we must be more careful than he is. It is already exactly one o'clock by my watch, so if nothing has happened, our friends Arthur and Quincey should be on their way here right now. Today is our day, and we must proceed slowly but surely, making sure not to miss any opportunities. You see! When those who are returning arrive, there will be five of us."

As the professor was speaking, the urgent knock of a post office telegraph delivery boy rang at the front door, and we were all startled. As if we had promised, we headed to the front hall in unison, and Professor Van Helsing raised his hand to signal us to be quiet, then approached the door and opened it. The boy handed over a telegram. The professor closed the door again, checked the address on the envelope, tore open the telegram, and read it aloud.

"Beware of Count Dracula. He just left Carfax at 12:45 and headed south in a hurry. He seems to be patrolling and might try to meet you. Yours, Mina."

Jonathan Harker's voice broke the silence.

"Now, thank God. We will soon meet him!" Van Helsing quickly turned to him and said.

"God will act in His own way and at His own time. Do not be afraid yet, nor rejoice. What we desire right now might actually lead us to our doom."

"It doesn't matter anymore." Harker replied in an agitated voice. "If only I can wipe that beast off the face of this earth. I would even sell my soul to do so!"

"Oh, hush, be quiet, my boy!" said Van Helsing. "God does not buy souls in such a manner, and the devil may buy a soul but he does not keep his promises. However, God is merciful and just, so He knows all of your pain and devotion to your beloved wife, Mina. Think about how much her pain would multiply if she heard you speaking such harsh words. None of us should be afraid. We are all devoted to this cause, and today we will finally see the end of it. The time to act is approaching. Today, this vampire is limited to the level of human abilities, and he cannot change his shape until the sun goes down. It will take some time for him to get here. You see, it is now one-twenty. No matter how fast he comes, he still has some time left before he arrives. What we must hope for is that Lord Arthur and Quincey arrive first."

About thirty minutes after receiving Mrs. Harker's telegram, there was a quiet but firm knock at the front door. It was an ordinary knock commonly used by many gentlemen, but it made my heart and the professor's beat heavily. We looked at each other and then went out to the front hall together, each of us holding the various weapons we had prepared—a spiritual weapon, the crucifix and the Host, in our left hands, and a physical weapon in our right hands, ready to use them at any moment. Van Helsing pulled back the bolt and stepped back, leaving the door half-open and positioning himself to have both hands free. When we saw Lord Godalming and Quincey Morris standing on the steps right in front of the door, our faces must have clearly shown joy. The two men quickly came inside and closed the door, and Godalming said as he walked down the hallway.

"Everything went well. We found both places. There were six boxes in each, and we destroyed them all!"

"Destroyed them?" the professor asked.

"So that he can't use them!" We stayed silent for about a minute, and then Quincey spoke.
