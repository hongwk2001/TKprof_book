[P001] CHAPTER 4

[P002] JONATHAN HARKER’S JOURNAL—Continued

[P003a] I woke up in my own bed. Unless I was dreaming, the Count must have carried me here. I tried to figure out the truth, but I couldn't reach any definite conclusion. Sure, there were some small clues, like my clothes being folded and put away in a way I don't normally do. My pocket watch was still unwound, and I have a strict habit of winding it right before going to sleep. There were many details like this. However, these things aren't solid proof, as they might just show that my mind wasn't in its normal state. For one reason or another, I was definitely very disturbed. I need to keep looking for real proof. One thing I'm glad about is that if the Count did carry me here and undress me, he must have been in a rush, because my pockets were untouched. I'm sure this diary would have been completely unacceptable to him. He would have either taken it or destroyed it. Looking around this room, even though it used to fill me with fear, it now feels like a safe haven. Nothing could be more terrifying than those awful women who were—and still are—waiting to suck my blood.

[P003b] *       *       *       *       *

[P004_005] May 18.—I went downstairs in the daylight to look at that room again, because I have to know the truth. When I reached the doorway at the top of the stairs, I found it closed. The door had been slammed so hard against the frame that part of the wood was splintered. I could see that the lock wasn't bolted, but the door was fastened from the inside. I'm afraid it wasn't a dream, and I must act on that assumption.

[P005b] *       *       *       *       *

[P006_007] May 19.—I am definitely trapped. Last night, the Count asked me in the smoothest tone to write three letters. The first was to say my work here is almost finished and I'll be heading home in a few days. The second was to say I'm leaving the morning after the letter's date. The third was to say I've left the castle and arrived in Bistritz. I really wanted to resist, but I realized that in my current situation, fighting openly with the Count while I'm completely in his power would be insane. Refusing would only make him suspicious and angry. He knows that I know too much, and he knows I can't be allowed to live because I'm dangerous to him. My only chance is to buy time. Something might happen that gives me a chance to escape. I saw a hint of the same building anger in his eyes that he showed when he violently threw that beautiful woman away from him. He explained that mail service here is unreliable and that writing the letters now would give my friends peace of mind. He was so convincing when he promised to cancel the later letters—which would be held at Bistritz until they were due—if my stay happened to be extended, that arguing with him would have only made him suspicious. So, I pretended to go along with his plan and asked him what dates I should put on the letters. He calculated for a minute and then said:

[P008a] “The first should be June 12, the second June 19, and the third June 29.”

[P008b] Now I know exactly how much time I have left to live. God help me!

[P008c] *       *       *       *       *

[P009_011] May 28.—There's a chance to escape, or at least to send a message home. A group of Szgany have arrived at the castle and set up camp in the courtyard. These Szgany are a type of gypsy; I actually have notes about them in my journal. They are unique to this part of the world, though they are related to ordinary gypsies found everywhere else. There are thousands of them in Hungary and Transylvania who live almost entirely outside the law. They usually attach themselves to a prominent nobleman or boyar and take on his name. They are fearless and have no religion other than their superstitions, and they only speak their own dialects of the Romany language.

[P012a] I'm going to write some letters home and try to get them to mail them for me. I've already spoken to them through my window to try and make friends. They took off their hats, bowed, and made a lot of gestures, but I couldn't understand the gestures any better than I could understand their spoken language...

[P012b] *       *       *       *       *

[P013_014] I've written the letters. The one to Mina is written in shorthand, and I just asked Mr. Hawkins to get in touch with her. I explained my situation to Mina, but I left out the horrors that I can only guess at. If I poured my heart out to her, she would be scared to death. If the letters don't make it, the Count still won't know my secret or exactly how much I've figured out...

[P014b] *       *       *       *       *

[P015_016] I've handed over the letters. I threw them through the bars of my window along with a gold coin, and I used whatever gestures I could think of to ask them to mail them. The man who picked them up pressed them to his chest, bowed, and put them in his hat. There was nothing more I could do. I snuck back into the study and started reading. Since the Count hasn't come in, I've been writing this entry...

[P016b] *       *       *       *       *

[P017_018] The Count came in. He sat down next to me and, opening the two letters, spoke in his smoothest voice:

[P019] “The Szgany handed these to me. I don't know where they came from, but I'll make sure they're taken care of. Look!”—he clearly must have inspected them—“one is from you, to my friend Peter Hawkins; the other”—he spotted the strange shorthand symbols as he opened the envelope, a dark expression fell over his face, and his eyes flashed with malice—“the other is a disgusting thing, an insult to friendship and hospitality! It isn't even signed. Well! In that case, it doesn't matter to us.” He calmly held the letter and envelope over the lamp's flame until they burned to ashes. Then he continued:

[P020] “The letter to Hawkins—I will mail that for you, of course, since it's yours. I treat your letters with the utmost respect. Forgive me, my friend, for accidentally breaking the seal. Would you mind sealing it again?” He held the letter out to me and, with a polite bow, handed me a fresh envelope. All I could do was silently write the address again and hand it back to him. When he left the room, I heard the key turn quietly. A minute later, I walked over to check it, and the door was locked.

[P021] An hour or two later, the Count came back quietly into the room. His arrival woke me up, as I had fallen asleep on the sofa. He was very polite and cheerful, and seeing that I had been resting, he said:

[P022a] “So, my friend, are you tired? Go to bed. That's where you'll get the best rest. I probably won't have the pleasure of chatting with you tonight, since I have a lot of work to do, but I hope you sleep well.” I went to my room and got into bed, and surprisingly, I slept without having any dreams. Despair has a way of making you calm.

[P022b] *       *       *       *       *

[P023_024] May 31.—When I woke up this morning, I figured I should grab some paper and envelopes from my bag and keep them in my pocket. That way, I'd be ready to write if I got a chance. But I was hit with yet another shocking surprise!

[P025] Every single piece of paper was gone. My notes and memos about trains and traveling, my letter of credit—literally everything that could help me once I got outside the castle was missing. I sat there thinking for a moment, and then I had an idea. I quickly searched my suitcase and the wardrobe where I'd hung my clothes.

[P026a] The suit I wore when I traveled here was gone, along with my overcoat and travel blanket. I couldn't find a trace of them anywhere. This seemed like the start of some new, evil scheme...

[P026b] *       *       *       *       *

[P027_028] June 17.—This morning, while I was sitting on the edge of my bed trying to figure out a plan, I heard the cracking of whips and the loud, scraping sound of horses' hooves coming up the rocky path past the courtyard. Thrilled, I rushed to the window and saw two large wagon-carts pulling into the yard. Eight strong horses pulled each cart. Standing by each pair of horses was a Slovak driver wearing a wide-brimmed hat, a thick nail-studded belt, dirty sheepskin clothes, and high boots. They were holding long staffs. I ran to the door, hoping to go down and meet them in the main hall, thinking maybe that path would be open for them to enter. But I was hit with another shock: my door was locked from the outside.

[P029a] So, I ran back to the window and shouted down to them. They just looked up at me with blank expressions and pointed. But right then, the leader of the Szgany—the 'hetman'—came out. When he saw them pointing at my window, he said something to them, and they all laughed. After that, no matter what I did—no matter how pitifully I cried out or desperately begged—they wouldn't even look at me. They completely ignored me. The carts held large, square boxes with thick rope handles. Judging by how easily the Slovaks moved them and the hollow sound they made when handled roughly, the boxes were definitely empty. Once they were all unloaded and stacked in a big pile in a corner of the yard, the Szgany paid the Slovaks. The drivers spat on the money for luck and slowly walked back to their horses. A little while later, I heard the sound of their whips fading away into the distance.

[P029b] *       *       *       *       *

[P030_031] June 24, before morning.—Last night, the Count left me early and locked himself in his room. As soon as I felt safe enough, I ran up the spiral staircase and looked out the south-facing window. I wanted to keep an eye on the Count because I know something is happening. The Szgany are staying somewhere in the castle and working on some kind of project. I know this because I occasionally hear a faint, muffled sound, like shovels and pickaxes digging. Whatever they're doing, it must be the final step in some cruel, evil plan.

[P032] I had been standing at the window for less than half an hour when I saw something coming out of the Count's window. I pulled back and watched carefully as a man climbed completely out. I was shocked again to see that he was wearing the suit I had worn when I traveled here, and he had that terrible bag slung over his shoulder—the same one the women took away. There was no doubt about what he was doing, and he was doing it in my clothes! This is his new wicked plan: he's going to let people see him, thinking it's me, so he can prove I've been seen in towns and villages mailing my own letters. And any horrible crimes he commits will be blamed on me by the locals.

[P033] It makes me absolutely furious to think this can happen while I'm locked up here like a true prisoner, completely stripped of the legal protections that even a criminal has a right to.

[P034] I decided to wait for the Count to return, and I sat stubbornly by the window for a long time. Eventually, I noticed strange little specks floating in the moonlight. They looked like tiny dust particles, swirling around and gathering into cloud-like clusters. Watching them felt strangely soothing, and a sense of calm washed over me. I leaned back into the window frame to get more comfortable and just watch their airy dance.

[P035] A sudden noise startled me—the low, pitiful howling of dogs from somewhere deep in the valley, out of sight. The sound seemed to grow louder in my ears, and the floating dust particles started changing shapes, matching the rhythm of the sound as they danced in the moonlight. I felt an intense struggle inside me to wake up and answer some primal instinct. My very soul felt like it was fighting, and my half-forgotten senses were trying to respond to the call. I was being hypnotized! The dust danced faster and faster; the moonlight seemed to vibrate as it passed me and disappeared into the darkness beyond. The particles gathered closer together until they started taking on faint, ghostly shapes. Suddenly, I snapped fully awake, back in control of my senses, and ran away screaming. The ghostly figures forming in the moonlight were the three terrifying women I am doomed to face. I ran away and felt a bit safer in my own room, where there was no moonlight and my lamp was burning bright.

[P036] A couple of hours later, I heard movement in the Count's room. It sounded like a sharp cry that was instantly cut off. Then there was a deep, terrifying silence that made my blood run cold. With my heart pounding, I tried the door, but I was still locked in my prison and couldn't do a thing. I just sat down and cried.

[P037] While I was sitting there, I heard a noise from the courtyard outside—the agonized scream of a woman. I rushed to the window, threw it open, and looked out through the bars. Down below, there really was a woman with messy hair, clutching her chest like someone exhausted from running. She was leaning against the corner of the gateway. When she saw my face at the window, she lunged forward and screamed in a threatening voice:

[P038] “Monster, give me my child!”

[P039] She dropped to her knees, threw her hands in the air, and cried out the same words in a tone that broke my heart. Then she tore at her hair, beat her chest, and gave herself over completely to wild, overwhelming emotion. Finally, she threw herself forward again. I couldn't see her anymore, but I could hear her bare hands beating against the heavy door.

[P040] Somewhere high up, probably on the tower, I heard the Count's voice calling out in his harsh, metallic whisper. It sounded like his call was answered by the howling of wolves from all directions. Within minutes, a pack of wolves flooded through the wide entrance and into the courtyard, like water bursting through a broken dam.

[P041] There were no more cries from the woman, and the wolves didn't howl for long. Soon, they wandered away one by one, licking their lips.

[P042] I couldn't even feel pity for her, because I knew what had happened to her child, and she was honestly better off dead.

[P043a] What am I going to do? What can I possibly do? How can I escape this living nightmare of darkness, horror, and fear?

[P043b] *       *       *       *       *

[P044_045] June 25, morning.—No one truly knows how sweet and precious the morning can be until they have suffered through the night. When the sun rose high enough this morning to strike the top of the massive gateway opposite my window, that bright spot looked to me like the dove returning to Noah's Ark. My fear melted away like a mist disappearing in the warmth. I need to take some sort of action while the courage of the daylight is still with me. Last night, one of my post-dated letters was mailed—the first in that fatal series designed to erase all evidence of my existence from the world.

[P046] I shouldn't think about that. Action!

[P047] I've only ever been harassed, threatened, or put in fear and danger at night. I still haven't seen the Count during the day. Could it be that he sleeps when others are awake, so he can be awake while they sleep? If only I could get into his room! But there is absolutely no way. The door is always locked; there's no way in for me.

[P048a] Wait, there is a way, if someone is brave enough to take it. If his body could go that way, why couldn't another? I saw him crawl out of his window myself. Why shouldn't I copy him and go in through his window? It's a desperate chance, but my need is even more desperate. I'm going to risk it. The worst that can happen is death, and the death of a man is not like the death of an animal; the terrifying afterlife might still hold some hope for me. God help me with this task! Goodbye, Mina, if I fail; goodbye, my loyal friend and second father; goodbye to everyone, and most of all, Mina!

[P048b] *       *       *       *       *

[P049_050] Same day, later.—I made the attempt, and with God's help, I made it safely back to this room. I need to write down every detail in order. While my courage was still high, I went straight to the south window and climbed out onto the narrow stone ledge that runs around the building. The stones are large and roughly cut, and the mortar between them has washed away over time. I took off my boots and ventured out on this terrifying path. I looked down just once to make sure the sudden sight of the horrifying drop wouldn't paralyze me, but after that, I kept my eyes focused straight ahead. I had a good idea of the direction and distance to the Count's window, and I made my way toward it as best I could, taking advantage of every handhold. I didn't feel dizzy—I guess I was too pumped with adrenaline—and it felt like almost no time had passed before I was standing on the windowsill, trying to lift the window up. However, a wave of panic hit me when I bent down and slid into the room feet first. I looked around for the Count, but to my surprise and relief, I made a discovery: the room was empty! It was barely furnished with strange items that looked like they had never been used. The furniture was in the same style as the south rooms and was covered in dust. I looked for a key, but there wasn't one in the lock, and I couldn't find one anywhere. The only thing I found was a massive pile of gold in one corner—all kinds of gold coins: Roman, British, Austrian, Hungarian, Greek, and Turkish money. It was covered in a layer of dust, as if it had been buried in the ground for a long time. Everything I looked at seemed to be at least three hundred years old. There were also chains and ornaments, some with jewels, but all of them were old and tarnished.

[P051] There was a heavy door in one corner of the room. Since I couldn't find the key to the room or the key to the main outer door—which was what I was really looking for—I had to keep searching, or all this effort would be wasted. I tried the heavy door. It was unlocked and led through a stone passage to a steep, winding circular staircase. I climbed down, watching my step carefully, because the stairs were dark, lit only by small slits in the thick stone walls. At the bottom, there was a dark, tunnel-like passage that smelled like death and sickness—the smell of freshly turned old dirt. As I walked through the passage, the smell got stronger and heavier. Finally, I pulled open a heavy door that was slightly open and found myself in an old, ruined chapel that had clearly been used as a graveyard. The roof was caved in, and in two places there were steps leading down to underground vaults. The ground had recently been dug up, and the dirt was packed into large wooden boxes—clearly the same boxes brought by the Slovaks. No one was around, and I looked for another way out, but there wasn't one. So I searched every inch of the floor, not wanting to miss a single chance. I even went down into the vaults, where only a dim light reached, even though doing so terrified me to my very core. I went into two of them but saw nothing except pieces of old coffins and piles of dust. In the third vault, however, I found something.

[P052a] There, in one of the large boxes—there were fifty of them in total—lying on a pile of newly dug earth, was the Count! I couldn't tell if he was dead or asleep. His eyes were wide open and fixed like stone, but they didn't have the glassy look of death. His cheeks looked pale, but they still had the warmth of life, and his lips were as red as ever. Yet there was no movement at all—no pulse, no breath, no heartbeat. I leaned over him and tried to find any sign of life, but there was nothing. He couldn't have been lying there for very long, because the smell of fresh earth would have faded after a few hours. Next to the box was its lid, which had holes drilled into it here and there. I thought he might have the keys on him, but when I leaned in to search, I saw his dead eyes. Even though they were dead, and even though he seemed completely unaware of me, they held such a look of pure hatred that I ran from the place. I climbed back out of the Count's window and crawled back up the castle wall. Once I was safely in my room, I threw myself onto the bed, panting, and tried to think...

[P052b] *       *       *       *       *

[P053_054] June 29.—Today is the date on my last letter, and the Count took action to make it look authentic. I saw him leave the castle through the same window again, wearing my clothes. As he crawled down the wall like a lizard, I wished I had a gun or some deadly weapon to destroy him. But I'm afraid no weapon made by human hands would even hurt him. I didn't dare wait for him to return because I was terrified of seeing those three weird sisters. I went back to the library and read until I fell asleep.

[P055] I was woken up by the Count. He looked at me as grimly as any man could and said:

[P056a] “Tomorrow, my friend, we must go our separate ways. You will return to your beautiful England, and I am off to some work that may end in such a way that we never meet again. Your letter home has been mailed. Tomorrow I won't be here, but everything will be ready for your trip. In the morning, the Szgany will come to do some work, and some Slovaks will arrive as well. After they leave, my carriage will come for you and take you to the Borgo Pass to catch the stagecoach from Bukovina to Bistritz. But I do hope I'll see more of you at Castle Dracula.” I didn't trust him, and I decided to test his sincerity. Sincerity! It feels like a sin to even use that word when talking about a monster like him, so I asked him directly:

[P056b] “Why can't I leave tonight?”

[P057_058] “Because, my dear sir, my coachman and horses are away on an errand.”

[P059a] “But I would gladly walk. I want to leave right away.” He smiled—a smile so soft, smooth, and downright evil that I knew he was hiding a trick behind his polite words. He said:

[P059b] “And what about your luggage?”

[P060_061] “I don't care about it. I can send someone for it later.”

[P062] The Count stood up and said, with a sweet politeness that seemed so genuine I practically had to rub my eyes:

[P063a] “You English have a saying that I hold close to my heart, because it captures the spirit that guides our nobility: ‘Welcome the coming guest; speed the parting guest.’ Come with me, my dear young friend. I won't make you wait in my house for a single hour against your will, even though I'm sad to see you go, especially so suddenly. Come!” With impressive dignity, he picked up the lamp and led me down the stairs and through the hall. Suddenly, he stopped.

[P063b] “Listen!”

[P064_065] From close by came the loud howling of many wolves. It was almost as if the sound had been summoned simply by him raising his hand, like a grand orchestra starting to play the moment the conductor raises his baton. After a brief pause, he walked over to the door with his usual majestic stride, pulled back the heavy bolts, unhooked the massive chains, and began to pull it open.

[P066] To my absolute shock, I realized the door was unlocked. I looked around suspiciously, but I couldn't see a key anywhere.

[P067] As the door opened, the howling of the wolves outside grew louder and more furious. I could see their red jaws, snapping teeth, and blunt claws as they jumped toward the opening. I knew right then that fighting the Count was pointless. With a whole pack of monsters like these at his command, I was helpless. The door kept opening slowly, and only the Count's body blocked the gap. Then a terrifying thought hit me: this might be the exact moment and method of my death. I was going to be thrown to the wolves, and technically, I had asked for it. It was a wickedly clever plan, perfectly suited for the Count. Taking my only chance, I yelled out:

[P068] “Shut the door; I'll wait until morning!” I covered my face with my hands to hide my tears of bitter disappointment. With one powerful swing of his arm, the Count slammed the door shut, and the heavy bolts made a loud echoing clang across the hall as they slammed back into place.

[P069] We went back to the library in silence, and a minute or two later, I headed to my own room. The last thing I saw was Count Dracula blowing me a kiss. His eyes flashed with a red light of triumph, and he wore a smile so evil that Judas in hell would have been proud of it.

[P070] When I was in my room and getting ready to lie down, I thought I heard whispering outside my door. I walked over quietly and listened. Unless my ears were playing tricks on me, I heard the Count's voice:

[P071] “Get back, back to your own place! Your time hasn't come yet. Wait! Be patient! Tonight is mine. Tomorrow night is yours!” There was a soft, musical sound of laughter. Furious, I threw the door open and saw the three terrifying women outside, licking their lips. When I appeared, they all burst into a horrible, mocking laugh and ran away.

[P072a] I stumbled back into my room and fell to my knees. Is the end really this close? Tomorrow! Tomorrow! Lord, please help me, and help those I love!

[P072b] *       *       *       *       *

[P073_074] June 30, morning.—These might be the last words I ever write in this diary. I slept until just before dawn, and when I woke up, I fell to my knees, determined that if Death came for me, I would be ready for it.

[P075] Finally, I felt that subtle change in the air and knew morning had arrived. Then came the comforting sound of a rooster crowing, and I knew I was safe. With a joyful heart, I opened my door and ran down to the main hall. I had seen that the door was unlocked, and my escape was right in front of me. With hands shaking from sheer excitement, I unhooked the chains and pulled back the massive bolts.

[P076] But the door wouldn't open. Despair washed over me. I pulled and pulled at the door, shaking it until the heavy wood rattled in its frame. Then I saw it: the bolt was locked. Someone had locked it after I left the Count.

[P077] A wild, desperate urge to get that key no matter what the risk took hold of me. I decided right then and there to climb the wall again and get back into the Count's room. He might kill me, but at this point, death seemed like the better option. Without a second thought, I ran upstairs to the east window and scrambled down the wall into the Count's room, just like before. It was empty, but I expected that. I couldn't find a key anywhere, but the pile of gold was still there. I went through the corner door, down the winding staircase, and along the dark passage to the old chapel. I knew exactly where to find the monster I was looking for.

[P078] The giant box was exactly where it had been, pushed up against the wall. The lid was resting on top, but it wasn't nailed down—the nails were just sitting in their holes, ready to be hammered in. I knew I had to search his body for the key, so I lifted the lid and leaned it against the wall. What I saw next filled me with pure horror. The Count was lying there, but he looked like he had regained half his youth. His white hair and mustache had turned a dark iron-gray. His cheeks were fuller, and his pale skin looked like it had a ruby-red glow underneath. His mouth was redder than ever, with drops of fresh blood trickling from the corners of his lips and running down his chin and neck. Even his intense, burning eyes seemed to be sunk into swollen flesh, as his eyelids and the bags under his eyes were bloated. It looked as if the whole terrifying creature was simply stuffed full of blood. He lay there like a disgusting leech, exhausted from gorging himself. I shuddered as I leaned in to touch him, and every fiber of my being recoiled at the contact. But I had to search him, or I was doomed. By tonight, my own body could be a feast for those three horrible women. I searched his entire body, but there was no sign of a key.
[P078b] I stopped and looked at the Count. The mocking smile on his bloated face drove me absolutely crazy. This was the creature I was helping to move to London—where, for centuries to come, he could feed his bloodlust among millions of people and create a massive new army of half-demons to prey on the helpless. The thought made me snap. I was overwhelmed by a terrifying urge to rid the world of this monster. I didn't have a deadly weapon, but I grabbed a shovel the workmen had used to fill the boxes. I raised it high in the air and swung the sharp edge down at his hateful face. But just as I swung, his head turned, and his eyes locked onto me, blazing with a paralyzing, snake-like horror. The sight froze me; the shovel twisted in my hands and glanced off his face, only leaving a deep gash above his forehead. The shovel dropped from my hands onto the box. As I pulled it away, the edge of the blade caught the lid, which flipped back over, hiding the horrible sight from me. My last glimpse was of that bloated, bloodstained face, locked in an evil grin that would have fit perfectly in the deepest pits of hell.

[P079] I racked my brain trying to figure out my next move, but my head was spinning, and a heavy sense of despair washed over me as I waited. While I stood there, I heard the faint sound of a cheerful gypsy song getting closer, mixed with the rumble of heavy wheels and cracking whips. The Szgany and Slovaks the Count mentioned were arriving. I took one last look around the room and at the box holding that vile creature, then ran back to the Count's room, ready to rush out the second the front door opened. I strained my ears and heard the grinding of a key in the great lock downstairs, followed by the heavy door swinging open. There must have been another entrance, or someone had a key to one of the locked doors. Next, I heard the sound of many boots stomping around, eventually fading into a hallway that sent up a metallic echo. I turned to run back down toward the vault, thinking I might find the new entrance they used. But suddenly, a violent gust of wind blew the door to the winding staircase shut with a massive crash that sent dust flying from the doorframe. I ran to push it open, but it was hopelessly jammed. I was a prisoner again, and the net was closing tightly around me.

[P080] As I write this, I can hear the stomping of many boots in the hallway below, along with the loud crash of heavy objects being set down. It's definitely the boxes filled with dirt. Now I hear hammering—they are nailing the boxes shut. The heavy boots are stomping back down the hall, followed by a bunch of lighter, lazy footsteps.

[P081] The door slams shut, and the chains rattle. A key grinds in the lock, and I can hear it being pulled out. Then another door opens and closes, and I hear the creak of a lock and a bolt.

[P082] Listen! From the courtyard and down the rocky path, I can hear the rumble of heavy wheels, the cracking of whips, and the singing of the Szgany fading into the distance.

[P083] I am completely alone in this castle with those awful women. Disgusting! Mina is a woman, but they have absolutely nothing in common. They are demons straight from hell!

[P084] I refuse to stay here alone with them. I'm going to try climbing down the castle wall, further than I've gone before. I'll take some of the gold with me, just in case I need it later. Maybe I can find a way out of this nightmare.

[P085] And then I'll run for home! I'll get to the fastest, closest train and get away from this cursed place, from this cursed land, where the devil and his children still walk the earth!

[P086] At least the mercy of God is better than the mercy of these monsters, and the cliff outside is steep and high. At the bottom of it, a man can at least die—and rest—as a man. Goodbye, everyone! Goodbye, Mina!
