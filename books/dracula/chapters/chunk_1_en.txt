"Now all that's left is to wait here. But if he doesn't show up by five o'clock, we must make a move first. It's too dangerous to leave Mrs. Harker alone after sunset."

"He will arrive soon," Van Helsing said, looking at his notebook. "Listen carefully. According to Madam's telegram, he went south from Carfax. That means he tried to cross the river, and since he could only cross when the water was calm, it must have been shortly before one o'clock. His heading south is significant to us. He is still only suspicious. So he left Carfax and went to the hideout where he was least likely to be disturbed first. You must have arrived in Bermondsey just slightly ahead of him. The fact that he hasn't shown up here yet means he headed to Mile End next. It would have taken some time because he had to cross the river somehow to get there. Trust me, my friends, we won't have to wait long now. We must plan our attack immediately so we don't miss our chance. Quiet, there's no time now. Everyone, draw your weapons! Get ready!" When we all heard the sound of a key being quietly inserted into the front door's keyhole, the professor raised his hand to warn us.

Even in such a desperate moment, I couldn't help but admire the way a born leader takes charge of a situation. Whenever we hunted or went on adventures around the world, it was always Quincey Morris who devised the plans, and Arthur and I would unconsciously follow his directions. Now, that old habit revived instinctively. Quincey quickly scanned the room and, without a single word, set up our ambush by assigning us our positions with mere hand gestures. Van Helsing, Harker, and I stood close behind the door, ready to cut off the intruder's escape route while the professor blocked the entrance. Godalming and Quincey hid out of sight near the front window, taking charge of that side. We waited anxiously as time ticked by slowly like a nightmare. Soon, cautious and deliberate footsteps echoed down the hallway. It was clear that the Count anticipated an ambush, or at least was highly on guard.

Suddenly, the Count leaped into the room in a single breath, tearing through us before we could even react. His swift, panther-like, and almost inhuman movement snapped us all out of our shock. The first to move was Harker. He quickly threw himself forward to block the door leading to the front room. Upon spotting us, the Count twisted his face horribly and growled, bearing his long, pointed fangs. But that wicked smile soon turned into a cold, arrogant glare like a lion's. When we all rushed at him at once, his expression changed once again. Looking back, we should have planned our attack more thoroughly. In that moment, even I didn't know what to do or if our ordinary weapons would even work on him. But Harker was fully prepared to test that. He gripped his large Kukri dagger tightly and swung it fiercely and suddenly at the Count. It was an incredibly powerful strike, and if the Count hadn't dodged backward with demon-like speed, he would have been killed. A second later, and that sharp blade would have pierced his heart. Barely missing, the tip of the blade only tore a wide hole in the Count's coat, from which wads of bills and gold coins poured onto the floor. The expression on the Count's face was so demonic that, even as I watched Harker raise that terrifying blade for another strike, I feared for Harker's life. I instinctively stepped forward, thrusting out the crucifix and Host in my left hand to protect Harker. I felt an immense holy power flow through my arm, and at the same time, I saw the monster flinch and step back as all my friends held out their sacred items as if we had planned it. The hatred, thwarted malice, and hellish fury that flashed across the Count's face were beyond words. In contrast to his burning eyes, his pale skin turned a sickly greenish-yellow, and the red scar on his forehead throbbed like a gruesome wound against his pale flesh. The next moment, the Count bent his body like a snake and slipped under Harker's arm before the next blade could fall. He grabbed a handful of the money scattered on the floor, dashed across the room, and threw himself at the window. With a loud crash, the glass shattered, and he fell onto the stone-paved courtyard below. Over the sound of showering broken glass, we heard the clinking of a few gold coins hitting the stone floor.

We ran to the window and saw him spring up from the ground without a single injury. He bounded up the steps, crossed the stone-paved yard, and roughly pushed open the stable door. There, he turned around and shouted up at us.

"Do you think you can stop me? Lined up with pale faces like a flock of sheep in a slaughterhouse! Every single one of you will regret this! You may think you have destroyed all my resting places, but I still have more hideouts. My revenge has only just begun! I will take my revenge over hundreds of years, and time is on my side. The women you love are already mine. And through them, you and all other humans will eventually belong to me as well. You will become my minions, obeying my commands and bringing me prey whenever I desire! Hah!" The Count let out a mocking sneer and quickly disappeared through the door, and we heard the rusty bolt creaking as he locked it. Then, the sound of another door opening and closing in the distance echoed. Realizing it would be too difficult to chase him through the stable, we turned back toward the front hall, and the professor was the first to speak.

"We have learned many things—so many things! He spoke arrogantly, but he is afraid of us. He fears time, and he fears losing what he has! Why else would he have rushed so much? If my ears did not deceive me, his voice itself betrayed his fear. And why on earth did he grab that money? Hurry and go after him. You are all beast hunters, so you can track his trail. Meanwhile, I will make absolutely sure that he cannot use anything here even if he returns." As soon as the professor finished speaking, he swept the remaining money on the floor into his pocket, threw the bundle of property deeds and remaining documents Harker had dropped into the fireplace, and struck a match to set them on fire.

Godalming and Morris ran out into the yard, and Harker jumped out the window to chase the Count. But the Count had already bolted the stable door from the inside, and by the time they forced the door open, they couldn't even find a trace of him. Van Helsing and I inspected the alley behind the house, but it was empty, and no one had seen the Count leave.

It was already late afternoon, and sunset was not far away. We had to admit the fact that we had lost him for now. With heavy hearts, we agreed with the professor's next words.

"Let us return to the lovely and poor Mrs. Mina. We have done everything we can here. At least we can protect her by her side. But there's no need to despair. There is still one box of earth left, and we must find it. If we just find that box, we can still reverse everything." I could tell that the professor was trying hard to act brave to comfort Harker. That poor friend was completely broken. Occasionally, unable to hold it in, he let out a low groan; his mind was entirely consumed by thoughts of his wife.

We returned to the house with devastated hearts, but surprisingly, Mrs. Harker was waiting for us with a bright face. It was a truly noble demeanor born from her courage and selflessness. However, she turned as pale as a ghost as soon as she saw our faces. She closed her eyes for a second or two as if praying in her heart, and then she spoke cheerfully again.

"I don't know how to thank you all. Oh, my poor love!" She held her husband's graying head with both hands and kissed him. "Rest your weary head here. Everything will be all right, my dear! If it is God's good will, He will surely protect us." Poor Harker groaned. No words could offer comfort in the face of his sublime sorrow.

We hastily had dinner together, and I think it brought us some comfort. Perhaps it was the simple warmth that food gives to starving people who hadn't eaten since morning, or maybe the camaraderie of being together helped. Anyway, we were able to shake off our miserable feelings a little and face tomorrow with a heart that wasn't entirely hopeless. As promised, we told Mrs. Harker everything that had happened today. She turned as white as snow when she heard that her husband was in danger, and flushed bright red when she heard of his devoted actions toward her, but she listened to the story bravely and calmly until the end. When it came to the part where Harker recklessly charged at the Count, she clung tightly to her husband's arm. It was as if she believed she could protect him from all dangers just by holding on tight. But she said nothing until we finished our story and explained our current situation. After the story ended, she stood up among us, still holding her husband's hand. Oh, how can I describe that scene in words. This sweet and good woman, standing in the beauty of radiant youth and vitality, bearing the conscious red scar on her forehead (which we ground our teeth looking at, knowing how it got there). Her lovely kindness countering our cold hatred, her affectionate faith countering all our fears and doubts. Despite all her goodness, purity, and faith, it broke our hearts to know that, symbolically, she was already forsaken by God.

"Jonathan," she called. The name sounded like music, as if it was filled with love and affection. "My dear Jonathan, and my true friends, there is something I want you to keep in mind even in this terrible time. I know you must fight. Just as you had to destroy the monstrous Lucy so that the true Lucy could find peace in the afterlife, you must destroy him too. But this is not something to be done out of hatred. That poor soul who caused all this misery is the saddest being of all. Think of the immense joy he will feel when his wicked part is destroyed and his better part achieves spiritual immortality. Although you cannot stop the hands that will defeat him, you must have pity on him."

As she spoke, I could see Harker's face darkening and hardening. It was as if the anger within him was burning his soul from the inside out. Harker squeezed his wife's hand so tightly that his knuckles turned white. Mina must have been in pain, but she didn't flinch and looked at her husband with more pleading eyes than ever. As soon as she finished speaking, Harker jumped up from his seat, almost shaking off his wife's hand, and shouted.

"If only God would deliver him into my hands for even a moment, so that we can end his cursed earthly life! If only I could send his soul beyond that flesh into the eternally burning hell, I would gladly do so!"

"Oh, please hush! In the name of the good Lord, please be quiet. Do not say such things, my husband Jonathan. Your words crush me with fear and terror. Think about it, dear. I have been thinking about this all day today. Maybe... someday... I might need that pity too. And someone like you, who has every justified reason to be angry, might not show me that pity! Oh, my dear! My husband, if there had been any other way, I would have spared you from having such terrible thoughts. But I pray that God will not hold these harsh words against you, and will merely see them as the heartbreaking wail of a deeply wounded man who loves dearly. Oh, God, let this graying hair be the proof of how much this man has suffered, having endured so many sorrows without doing any wrong in his entire life."

We men were all shedding tears. We couldn't hold it back at all, and we wept aloud. Mina also cried when she saw that her gentle advice had moved our hearts. Harker knelt beside his wife, wrapped his arms around her waist, buried his face in the folds of her dress, and sobbed. Van Helsing gave us a look, and we quietly left the room so that the two loving souls could be alone with their God.

Before the two went to bed, the professor set up wards in the room to prevent the vampire from breaking in and reassured Mrs. Harker that it was safe to sleep. She tried to believe him, and clearly made an effort to appear peaceful to reassure her husband. It was a courageous struggle, and I firmly believe it was rewarded in its own small way. Van Helsing left a bell beside them so they could ring it anytime in an emergency. After they retired, Quincey, Godalming, and I agreed to take turns staying awake through the night to ensure the poor lady's safety. Quincey took the first watch, so the rest of us should go to bed as soon as possible. Godalming is on the second watch, so he has already gone to bed. Now that my work is done, I should go to sleep too.
