[Chapter 3]

You ask me what I think we should do to deal with this boredom. Athenodorus says, "The best thing is to throw yourself into public business, managing state affairs, and fulfilling your duties as a citizen. Just like some people spend their day exercising in the sun and taking care of their bodies, and athletes spend most of their time building the muscles they have devoted their lives to, the same goes for you. If you are training your mind for the struggles of political life, it is much more honorable to be at work than to be lazy. A person who wants to help their fellow citizens and all of humanity is improving themselves and doing good at the same time when they fully commit their abilities to working for both the public and private good."

He continues, "However, because pure intentions are rarely safe among such fierce ambition and so many people trying to push you off the right path, you will certainly face more roadblocks than help. Because of this, we should step back from the public square and political life. Even in a private, quiet life, a great mind can find plenty of room to stretch out and grow. Locking lions and wild animals in narrow cages stops them from leaping, but this does not apply to human beings. Humans often do their most important work after they have stepped back from public life. However, when you step back, do it in a way that allows you to keep your desire to help individuals and all of humanity through your intellect, your voice, and your advice, no matter where you spend your free time."

The person who truly serves the state is not just the one who nominates candidates for office, defends the accused in court, or votes on peace and war. It is also the person who encourages young people to do good. It is the person who steps in when there is a shortage of good teachers and plants the principles of virtue in young minds. It is the person who grabs hold of those running wildly after money and luxury, and even if they cannot stop them completely, at least slows them down. That person serves the public even while living a private life.

A person does far more good when they show others the true meaning of justice, family duty, patience, courage, a fearlessness of death, and an understanding of the gods. They do far more good by showing how a clear conscience helps a person, compared to a judge who simply hands down a verdict based on what his assistant whispers in court.

So, if you take the time you would have spent on public service and dedicate it to philosophy instead, you are not a runaway soldier, and you are not refusing your duty. A soldier is not just the person who stands on the front lines to defend the army's left or right wing. The person guarding the gates is also a soldier. That job might be less dangerous, but it is still hard work. He stands watch and guards the weapons. Even though these jobs do not involve spilling blood, they still count as military service.

The moment you dedicate yourself to philosophy, you will overcome your disgust with life. You will not wish for darkness just because you are tired of the light. You will not be a headache to yourself or useless to others. You will make many friends, and the best people will naturally be drawn to you. No matter how deeply hidden it is, virtue cannot stay hidden. It always sends out signals, and anyone who is worthy will follow the footprints and find it.

But if we abandon all social contact, turn our backs on the whole human race, and live only talking to ourselves, this lonely life without any meaningful work will just lead to empty boredom. We will start building things just to tear them down again, trying to build walls against the sea, digging tunnels through natural obstacles, and basically wasting the time Nature has given us. Some of us are stingy with our time, while others throw it away. Some of us use our time so well that we can show a clear record of profit and loss, while others waste it until there is nothing left to show. Nothing is more shameful than that. Often, an incredibly old person has nothing but their age to prove that they have lived a long time.
