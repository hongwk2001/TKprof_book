Chapter 10

But imagine your life becomes full of trouble. Without even knowing it, you have fallen into a trap set by fate, whether public or private, and you cannot untie or break free from it. In that case, think of people who are bound by chains. At first, they suffer deeply from the heavy weight of the shackles on their legs. But later, when they decide to endure rather than stay angry, necessity teaches them how to be brave, and habit makes the chains easier to bear. If you are willing to make light of bad things instead of hating them, you can find joy, relaxation, and happiness in any situation in life.

If we know that we are born to experience some sorrow, we should be most thankful to nature for creating "habit" to ease the pain of misfortune. This is because habit quickly makes us get used to even the harshest hardships. If misfortune kept hitting us forever with the exact same force as the very first time, no one could survive it. We are all chained to fate. One person's chain might be loose and made of gold, while another person's chain is tight and made of cheap metal. But this makes no difference. We are all stuck in the same captivity, and even those who bound us are bound themselves. Having a chain on your left side doesn't make it lighter to bear. One person might be tied to a public office, and another tied to wealth. Some have to carry the weight of a noble birth, and some the weight of a humble birth. Some obey the orders of others, and some obey only themselves. Some are trapped in one place by being banished, and others must stay in one place because they were chosen as priests. All life is a form of slavery. So, make peace with your fate, complain about it as little as possible, and hold tightly to every good thing within your reach. If you look at things with a fair mind, no situation is so miserable that you cannot find some reward in it.

Even a small piece of land can be used for many different purposes if it is cleverly divided. And if arranged well, even the narrowest room can become a place to live. Call upon common sense to help you face difficulties. For a person who knows how to carry a burden skillfully, it is possible to make rough things smooth, widen what is too narrow, and lighten the heavy pressure pressing down on them.

Moreover, we should not let our desires wander too far. Since desires cannot stand being completely locked up, we must limit them to stay in our immediate neighborhood. We must give up on things that cannot happen or can only be achieved with great difficulty. Instead, chase after things that are close by and within the reach of our hopes. Always remember that everything is equally unimportant, and even though things look different on the outside, they are all just as empty on the inside. We also shouldn't envy people in high positions. The peaks that look so high to us are actually steep and rugged.

Also, people whom harsh fate has placed in dangerous positions will be safer if they show as little pride as possible in their high status, and if they do everything they can to lower their wealth to the level of other people. There are many who must hang on to the high peaks of power because they cannot come down without falling headfirst. Yet, they will tell you that their biggest burden is being a heavy burden to others. They say they are nailed to their high positions rather than raised to them. In that case, let them freely and generously offer justice, mercy, and kindness. This will help them build up support to soften the shock if they ever fall. And by looking ahead, they can maintain their position with more hope.

But nothing frees us from the back-and-forth of hope and fear better than setting a limit on our success. We shouldn't let fate choose when to stop our career. Instead, we should stop on our own well before it seems like we have to. By doing this, certain desires will still wake up our spirits, but because they are kept within limits, they won't lead us to start huge and vague projects.
