Chapter 13

I think Democritus was following this exact rule when he taught that a person who wants to live peacefully should not do a lot of business, whether public or private. Of course, he was talking about unnecessary business. If it is absolutely necessary, we must do not only a lot of business but even endless amounts of it, both public and private. But if no important duty calls us, we are better off staying quiet. A person who does many things often puts themselves in the power of fate. The safest way is not to test fate too often, but always remember it exists, and never promise yourself anything expecting fate to guarantee it.

"I will set sail, if nothing happens to stop me." "I will become a judge, if nothing gets in the way." "My business deals will succeed, if nothing goes wrong." This is why we say that "nothing happens to the wise man that he did not expect." We do not free the wise man from the random accidents of human life, but from the mistakes caused by them. Also, not everything happens exactly the way he wanted, but it does happen exactly the way he thought it would. His very first thought was that his plans might meet some resistance. If a person does not count on success no matter what, the pain of a ruined wish will naturally hurt their mind much less.
