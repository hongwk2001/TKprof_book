Chapter 15

However, we gain nothing even if we get rid of all personal causes of sadness. This is because sometimes we are overwhelmed by a hatred for the whole human race. When you think about how rare innocence is, how unknown purity has become, and how trust is rarely kept unless it brings profit, your mind feels like it's thrown into darkness. When you remember the huge numbers of successful crimes, the equally hateful gains and losses of lust, and an ambition that cannot stand its natural limits and tries to buy honor even through shamelessness, shadows fall before you. It feels as if all virtues have been overthrown, leaving us with absolutely no hope of possessing them or getting any benefit from them.

Therefore, we must reach a state of mind where the public's bad habits do not seem hateful to us, but only ridiculous. We should imitate the philosopher Democritus rather than Heraclitus. Heraclitus cried whenever he went out in public, but Democritus used to laugh. The crying philosopher thought all human actions were miserable, while the laughing philosopher thought they were simply foolish. We must take a higher view of everything and put up with the world more easily. For a human, it is much more fitting to laugh at life than to cry over it.

Furthermore, a person who laughs at humanity deserves better treatment from it than someone who mourns for it. The one who laughs leaves at least some hope for improvement, but the foolish crier is someone who has given up all hope that things can be fixed. A person who looks at the universe and cannot hold back their laughter shows a greater mind than someone who cannot hold back their tears. Their mind is only affected a tiny bit, and they understand that nothing in the structure of this world is truly important, serious, or tragic.

As for the specific things that make us happy or sad, let each person explain it for themselves. They will soon realize the truth in what Bion said: "All human actions are very much like how they began, and there is nothing in their lives more holy or decent than their conception."

Still, it is better to accept public morals and human vices calmly, without bursting into laughter or tears. Constantly getting hurt by other people's suffering will only make you miserable forever. On the other hand, enjoying the suffering of others is an inhuman pleasure. It is just like crying and putting on a sad face simply because someone is burying his son—it is a useless show of humanity.

When it comes to your own misfortunes, you should give them only as much sadness as reason demands, not as much as custom requires. Many people cry purely for show. When no one is watching, their eyes are completely dry, but they think it is shameful not to cry when everyone else is crying. This bad habit of being guided by what others think is rooted so deeply in us that even sadness—the simplest and most natural of all emotions—has become something we fake.
