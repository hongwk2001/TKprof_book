[Chapter 2]
[Seneca]

For a long time, I have been quietly asking myself: My friend Serenus, what should I compare your state of mind to? I find that nothing is more similar than the behavior of people who have just recovered from a long and serious illness. They still feel small aches and pains from time to time. Even though they are past the worst of the disease, they still worry that it has not fully left them. Even when they are perfectly healthy, they still ask the doctor to check their pulse. Whenever they feel a little warm, they suspect their fever is returning. Serenus, people like this are not sick; they are just not used to being healthy. It is like how a calm sea or lake still has a few small ripples right after a storm has passed.

Therefore, you do not need any of those harsh treatments you mentioned. You do not need to constantly restrict yourself, get angry at yourself, or scold yourself strictly. You just need to choose the last option on your list: believe in yourself and trust that you are on the right path. Do not get swept away by the countless crisscrossing paths of wanderers. Some of them are just walking in circles around the right path.

What you want to be undisturbed is a great thing. In fact, it is the greatest thing of all, and it elevates a person almost to the level of a god. The Greeks call this calm and steady mind euthymia, and Democritus wrote an excellent essay about it. I call it peace of mind. There is no need to copy the Greek word exactly. The important thing is to give the concept a name that means the same thing, even if it looks different. What we are looking for is how the mind can always walk a steady, unshakeable path. It should be satisfied with itself and look around with joy. The mind should stay in a peaceful state without this joy ever breaking, and without getting overly excited or deeply depressed. This is true peace of mind.

Now let us look generally at how to achieve it. Then, you can apply this universal cure to your own situation however you like. First, we have to drag the whole disease out into the light so everyone can recognize their own symptoms. At the same time, you will see that your own harsh self-criticism actually causes you less pain than the people who are trapped by the public images they have created. Those people are crushed under grand, honorable titles. They keep up their fake appearances out of shame rather than their own free will.

The same problem affects people who constantly change their minds and suffer from endless fickleness. They always end up loving exactly what they just abandoned. It also affects people who just yawn and waste their time. Add to this the people who toss and turn like they have insomnia. They try one position, then another, until they finally fall asleep purely out of exhaustion. When they build their life habits, they often settle for something not because they hate changing, but because old age has caught up with them and made them too slow to change. Also, add the people who are not fickle at all, but only because they are lazy, not consistent. They just keep living the way they started, not the way they actually want to live.

There are countless other specific forms of this disease, but they all have one result: they make people dissatisfied with themselves. This comes from an unbalanced mind and from desires that people are too scared to express or unable to satisfy. It happens when people do not dare to try as much as they want, or when they fail and have to rely entirely on hope. Such people are always unstable and easily changing, which is bound to happen when you live in a state of suspense. They will use any method to reach their goals, even forcing themselves to do difficult and dishonorable things. When their hard work fails, they become miserable over the shame of losing. They do not regret wanting the wrong thing; they only regret wanting it in vain.

Then they start to regret what they have done, and they are too afraid to start over. Their minds slowly sink into a state of endless wavering because they can neither control their passions nor completely give in to them. They hesitate because their lives cannot move forward properly. As disappointment numbs their minds, they fall into a downward spiral.

All these symptoms get much worse when the disgust for their hard, miserable lives pushes them into laziness and hiding away to study alone. For a mind that naturally wants to be active, participate in public life, and stay busy, this is unbearable. A mind like that finds too few resources inside itself. So, when busy people lose the joy of their work, they cannot stand their own homes, their loneliness, or the walls of their rooms. They start to hate themselves when left completely alone. This creates intense boredom, self-hatred, and a mind that tosses and turns without ever finding rest. They endure their forced free time miserably and unwillingly.

When people hide their pain out of shame, their unexpressed desires pile up inside and choke them. This leads to depression, a loss of energy, and a thousand different doubts in an unstable mind. They hold onto empty hopes and feel crushed when those hopes fail. Because of this, they hate their own laziness, complain about having nothing to do, and look at other people's success with the bitterest jealousy. Unhappy laziness feeds envy, and people who cannot succeed themselves start wishing for everyone else to fail.

This hatred of other people's success and despair over their own creates a mind that is furious at fate. They complain about the times they live in, hide in the corner, and obsess over their own misery until they become sick and tired of themselves. The human mind is naturally quick and meant to move. It loves anything that stimulates it or helps it forget itself. The worse someone's personality is, the more they enjoy this. Just like an infected wound wants to be touched by the very hand that hurts it, or a terrible itch loves being scratched, a bad mind loves to exhaust itself with busy, pointless action.

Similarly, I promise you that these minds, which are covered in passions like nasty ulcers, actually find pleasure in hard work and suffering. There are things that hurt the body but also feel good at the same time, like tossing and turning in bed before you are even tired, or stretching into different positions to cool down. It is like Homer's Achilles, who lies on his face, then on his back, constantly changing positions. Like most sick people, he cannot stand any position for long and uses change itself as a fake cure.

This is why people go on aimless wanderings and travel to faraway shores. Sometimes by sea, sometimes by land, they try to satisfy their restless minds that are never happy with the present. They say, "Let us go to Campania! Actually, I am sick of these rich farmlands. Let us go see the wild, rough mountain passes of Bruttium and Lucania." But even in the wilderness, they eventually want to see something beautiful to soothe their tired eyes after staring at rough wastelands for too long. Then they say, "Let us go to Tarentum! It has a famous harbor, a mild winter, and enough food to feed huge ancient armies. Now let us go back to the city! Our ears have missed the shouting and the noise for too long. It might even be fun to watch some gladiators bleed."

So, one journey ends, and another begins. One sight is replaced by another. As Lucretius said, "In this way, every human runs away from himself." But because they cannot actually escape themselves, they gain nothing. They follow themselves everywhere, weighed down by their own most exhausting companion: themselves.

Therefore, we must realize that our pain is not the fault of the places we visit, but our own fault. We become weak the moment we have to endure anything. We cannot put up with hard work or pleasure, our own business or other people's business, for very long. This endless changing of goals has driven some people to suicide because they always ended up right back where they started, leaving no room for anything new. They became totally sick of life and the world. When all their indulgences lost their flavor, they realized they simply could not keep doing the same things forever.
