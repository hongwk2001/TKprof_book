[Chapter 9]

We will only be satisfied with this humble amount of wealth if we first learn to find joy in simple living. Without thrift, no amount of wealth is ever enough, but with thrift, no amount is ever too little. The solution is always close at hand, because poverty can simply call on thrift for help and turn itself into wealth.

Let us toss aside all the fake appearances and get used to measuring things by their actual usefulness, not their decorative looks. Let food satisfy our hunger, let drinks cure our thirst, and let us keep our lust within necessary limits. Instead of copying new trends, let us learn to manage our bodies, our clothes, and our lifestyles according to the practical examples set by our ancestors. Let us learn to build self-control, crush luxury, set limits on pride, calm our anger, look at poverty without prejudice, and practice thrift even when others are ashamed of it. Let us make it our main job to find cheap solutions to our natural needs, to lock down our wild hopes and desires, and to get our wealth from within ourselves, not from Fortune.

If you spread your sails wide to the wind, you will never be able to completely dodge the huge variety of disasters threatening you. You will inevitably feel the weight of many storms. Instead, we must pull our affairs into a tight, small circle so that Fortune's darts miss us. This is why a light misfortune has sometimes acted as a cure, and a severe disease has been healed by a smaller one. When a mind refuses to listen to good advice and cannot be brought back to its senses with gentle measures, we should realize that poverty, disgrace, or financial ruin might actually be working for its own best interest. One evil balances out another.

Let us teach ourselves to eat meals without the whole city of Rome watching, to live with fewer servants, to buy clothes that actually serve their purpose instead of just showing off, and to live in a smaller house. It is always best to take the inside curve, not just in chariot races at the amphitheater, but in the race of life as well.

Even studying literature, which is the most respectable way a gentleman can spend money, can only be justified if it is kept within limits. There is no point in owning countless books and massive libraries when the owner could not even finish reading all the titles in a lifetime. A student is only crushed by such a huge collection, not educated by it. It is much better to dedicate yourself to a few good authors than to skim through thousands of books.

In Alexandria, forty thousand books were burned. Historians like Titus Livius might have called it an excellent result of the kings' refined taste and careful protection. Someone might have praised that massive library as a noble monument to royal wealth. But in reality, it had nothing to do with taste or protection. It was just a piece of academic luxury, or rather, it was not academic at all. They did not collect those books to learn from them, but just to show them off. They were like the many people who know less about literature than a slave is expected to know, and who use books to decorate their dining rooms rather than to help them study.

A person should only buy as many books as they actually need, and they should never buy them just to show off.

"But spending money on books is much more respectable than buying expensive Corinthian vases or paintings," you might say.

No, it is not. Anything done to an extreme is wrong. There is absolutely no excuse for someone who crazily buys bookshelves made of ivory and rare wood, collects the works of unknown or unreliable authors, and sits yawning among thousands of books, caring more about the covers and the titles than the actual contents. In the homes of the laziest people, you will see bookshelves stacked to the ceiling, packed with the works of every great speaker and historian. Today, a library has become just as essential to a house as a hot and cold bath. I would forgive them immediately if they were genuinely swept away by an extreme passion for literature. But the truth is, the expensive works of these brilliant geniuses, along with all the beautiful pictures decorating them, are bought entirely for show and just act as wall decorations.
