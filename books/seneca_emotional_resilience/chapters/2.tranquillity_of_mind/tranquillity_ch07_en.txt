[Chapter 7]

In all cases, we must be careful when choosing people. We need to see if they are worth dedicating a part of our lives to, or if we will just end up wasting our own time and theirs. Some people even think we owe them simply because we did them a favor. Athenodorus said he would never eat dinner with someone who would not be thankful to him for doing so. I think he meant he would definitely not go to a dinner party where people act like they are doing you a favor just by feeding you. They act as if every course they serve is a charitable donation, and that they are overeating just to honor their guests. If you took away the witnesses and the audience from these people, they would find no joy in their private gluttony.

You have to decide whether your personality is better suited for energetic action or for quiet thinking and studying, and then follow where your nature leads you. Isocrates realized this when he took Ephorus by the hand and led him out of the public square, thinking he would be much more useful writing history books. It does no good to force your mind into a job that does not fit it. Fighting against nature is a waste of time.

But nothing gives the mind as much joy as a loyal and pleasant friendship. What a massive blessing it is to find someone whose heart is ready to safely hold all your secrets! You feel less afraid confessing your actions to him than to your own conscience. His conversation chases away your worries, his advice helps your plans, his cheerfulness cures your sadness, and just seeing him makes you happy!

We should try our best to choose friends who are completely free from strong passions. Vice is contagious. It spreads from one person to their neighbor and hurts anyone who touches them. During a plague, you have to be careful not to sit near infected people where the disease is raging, because just breathing their air puts you in danger and spreads the sickness. In the same way, when choosing the character of our friends, we must be careful to pick people who are as uncorrupted by the world as possible. Mixing healthy people with rotten people is the easiest way to breed disease.

However, I am not telling you to only follow or hang out with a perfectly wise sage. We have been searching for a sage for centuries with no luck, so it is highly unlikely you will find one. Instead of looking for the absolute best person, just choose the one who is the least bad. If you could have found a good person among Socrates' students, like Plato or Xenophon, or if you could have picked someone from the time of Cato the Elder, you would rarely have found a better time to make a choice. That era produced many people worthy of being born in Cato's time. It also produced many of the worst people ever seen, the masterminds of the most horrific crimes. Both groups were necessary to make people understand Cato. He needed the good people to support him, and he needed the bad people to fight against in order to prove his strength. But today, when good people are so rare, you have to be a little less picky when choosing your friends.

But above all else, avoid gloomy people who grumble about everything and constantly look for something to complain about. Even if this person remains loyal and friendly to you, your peace of mind will be destroyed if you spend time with a twisted companion who reacts to everything with a groan.
