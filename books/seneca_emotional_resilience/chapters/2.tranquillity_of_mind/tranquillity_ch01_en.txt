[Chapter 1]
[Serenus]

Seneca, when I look closely at myself, I see some vices that are right on the surface and easy to reach. Some are less obvious and harder to grasp. Others are not always there but come back from time to time. I would call these the most annoying ones. They are like a roaming enemy that attacks whenever there is a chance. They keep me from staying on guard like in a war, but they also keep me from resting without fear like in peace.

I will be as honest with you as I would be with a doctor. I often find that I am neither completely free from the bad habits I hate, nor completely controlled by them. My state of mind is not the absolute worst, but it is very discontented and sulky. I am neither sick nor entirely well.

It is no use telling me that all virtues are weak at first and gain strength and solidness over time. I know very well that even the virtues that only improve our outward appearance, like dignity, a reputation for speaking well, and everything else that attracts people, gain power as time goes on. Both the virtues that give us real strength and those that just make us look better require a long time before they become a natural part of us. But I am afraid that habit, which hardens most things, will plant this vice deeper and deeper into me. Knowing both good and bad people for a long time makes you judge them all the same way.

When my mind pauses between two choices, not leaning strongly toward good or bad, this weakness is hard to explain all at once. It is better to show it to you piece by piece. I will tell you what happens to me, and you can figure out the name of this disease.

I must confess that I absolutely love simple living. I do not care for a bed with beautiful curtains, or clothes kept in a chest and ironed perfectly smooth. I prefer cheap, ordinary clothes that I do not have to worry about storing or wearing. When it comes to food, I do not want meals that require a whole army of servants to prepare and admire. I do not want food ordered days in advance and handled by many people. I want food that is easy to find, available anywhere in the world, and not brought from far away or expensive. It should not be a heavy burden on my wallet or my body, and it should not be difficult to digest.

I like a rough, unpolished servant who was born right in my own house. I like the heavy silver plates my father used in the country, stamped without a famous maker's name. I do not want a beautifully patterned table, or one that is famous all over town just because many trendy people owned it before me. I just want a practical table that does not make my guests stare in awe or feel jealous.

But just when I am feeling completely satisfied with all this, I remember the incredibly fancy clothes of some student, the slaves dripping in gold, and a whole crowd of glittering attendants. I think of houses where people walk on floors covered in precious stones, where expensive things are scattered in every corner, where even the roof is painted brightly, and where an army of servants helps the owner waste his inherited wealth. I think of banquets where clear water flows around the guests and the whole meal looks like a spectacular theater performance.

After practicing my boring, simple life for so long, I suddenly feel surrounded by this blinding luxury echoing from all sides. My eyes are a bit dazzled by it. It is easier for my mind to reject this luxury than my eyes. When I come home from seeing all that, I am not a worse person, but I am a sadder one. I cannot walk among my own cheap things with the same proud step as before. A feeling of annoyance silently creeps into me, and I start to wonder if their way of life is better than mine. None of these things change my principles, but they all disturb me.

Sometimes I follow the rules of our philosophy and jump into public life. I try to get a government job and become a consul. I do not do this because I am attracted to the purple-bordered robes or the axes carried by guards, but because I want to help my friends, my relatives, my fellow citizens, and all of humanity. Feeling ready and determined, I follow the advice of Zeno, Cleanthes, and Chrysippus. They told people to take part in politics, even though none of them ever did it themselves.

But when my mind gets shaken, since it is not used to shocks, I retreat. When something embarrassing happens, which is common in life, or when things do not go smoothly, or when I have to waste too much time on tiny details, I go back to my quiet life of leisure. Just like tired cows walk faster when heading home, I want to retreat and spend my life safely inside the walls of my house.

I tell myself, "No one is going to steal my day unless they offer something worth the loss. My mind should stay within itself and focus on improving itself. I will not get involved in other people's business, and I will not do anything that requires someone else's approval. I will enjoy a peaceful mind, completely undisturbed by public or private headaches."

But then, when my spirit is lifted by reading brave words or inspired by a noble example, I want to rush into the law courts. I want to speak up for one person, offer my help to another, and try my best even if I fail. Or I want to crush the pride of some arrogant lawyer who got lucky.

But honestly, when it comes to philosophy, I think it is better to see things exactly as they are and speak about them plainly. Words should simply follow the truth wherever it leads.

I tell myself that I do not need to build something that lasts forever just so future generations will talk about me. I was born to die, and a quiet death is the least miserable one. So, I should write something in a simple style, just to pass the time and for my own use, not for publishing. Things are much easier when you do not worry about the future.

But then, when my mind is lifted by grand thoughts, I start using fancy, showy words. The higher my ambitions get, the more elegantly I want to speak, and my language rises to match the majesty of the topic. At those times, I forget my calm and moderate plans. I soar higher than usual and use language that does not even sound like my own.

To make a long story short, I suffer from this weakness of a well-meaning mind in everything I do. I am afraid that I will gradually slip back into this low state. What worries me even more is that I might always be hanging on the edge of falling, and that I might have more problems than I even realize. We always look at our own flaws with a friendly eye, and favoritism ruins our judgment.

I think many people would have actually reached wisdom if they had not assumed they were already there. They purposely lied to themselves about some parts of their character and closed their eyes to others. There is no reason to think that other people's flattery destroys us more than our own. No one has the courage to tell themselves the truth. No matter how many kissing-up followers surround someone, everyone is their own biggest flatterer.

So, if you have a cure to stop this wavering of mine, please consider me worthy of owing my peace of mind to you. I know these mental swings are not dangerous and will not completely ruin me. To use a precise comparison, I am not suffering from a deadly storm, but from seasickness. So, please take this vice away from me, whatever it is, and help someone who is suffering right in sight of land.
