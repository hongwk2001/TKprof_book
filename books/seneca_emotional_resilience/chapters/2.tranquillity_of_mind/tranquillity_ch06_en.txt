[Chapter 6]

But first, we have to look closely at ourselves, then at the task we want to do, and finally at who we are doing it for or who we are doing it with.

Above all, it is absolutely necessary to judge yourself honestly. We usually think we can do much more than we actually can. Some people trust their public speaking skills too much and push themselves forward too aggressively. Some people demand more from their wealth than it can actually produce. Some people force their weak bodies to do exhausting jobs. Some people are just too shy for public affairs, which require a thick-skinned kind of shamelessness. Some people have a stubborn pride that makes them terrible at fitting in at royal courts. Some people cannot control their anger and blurt out careless words at the slightest insult. Some people cannot handle their own sense of humor or stop themselves from making dangerous jokes. For all these people, free time is much better than a job. Anyone with a bold, arrogant, and hot-tempered personality needs to avoid any job that gives them the freedom to speak and act in ways that could ruin them.

Next, we need to weigh the importance of the work we want to do and compare our own strength to the task we are trying to take on. The person carrying a load must always be stronger than the load itself. A weight that is too heavy for the person carrying it will inevitably crush them. Also, some jobs are not that big on their own, but they produce many branches that lead to much more work. You should refuse any job that traps you in new, complicated tasks. You should also avoid getting involved in work that you cannot freely walk away from. Commit yourself to tasks you can actually finish, or at least have a solid hope of finishing. It is best not to get involved in jobs that just keep growing and refuse to stop where you wanted them to.
