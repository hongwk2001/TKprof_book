[Chapter 5]

You probably could not find a city more miserable than Athens when it was being torn apart by the Thirty Tyrants. They murdered one thousand three hundred of the best citizens. And they did not stop there; their cruelty only grew hungrier the more they killed. In that city, which once had the highly respected Areopagus court, a senate, and a democratic assembly just like a senate, a gang of despicable butchers gathered every day. The unhappy senate house was packed with tyrants.

A country with enough tyrants to form an entire personal bodyguard could easily have given up the fight and just rested. People could not even imagine hoping to get their freedom back, and they could not find any cure for such a massive evil. There seemed to be no way for that miserable country to find enough heroes to defeat all those tyrants. But right in the middle of the city, Socrates comforted the grieving leaders, encouraged those who had given up on the republic, and scolded the rich people who were terrified of losing their money, finally making them regret their greed. He walked freely among the thirty masters, serving as a great example for anyone who wanted to copy him.

But Athens eventually executed him in prison. The goddess of Liberty herself could not tolerate the freedom of a man who had mocked a whole gang of tyrants who laughed at liberty. This shows that even in an oppressed country, a wise person can find a chance to step forward and lead. It also shows that in a rich and successful country, arrogant pride, jealousy, and thousands of other cowardly vices often take control.

Therefore, we must expand or shrink our lives depending on how our country treats us or what chances Fortune gives us. But no matter what, we must keep moving and never freeze in fear. Truly, the best person is the one who does not damage or hide their virtue, even when danger threatens them from every side and weapons and chains block their path. Keeping yourself safe does not mean burying yourself alive.

I think Curius Dentatus told the truth when he said he would rather be dead than just alive. The absolute worst evil is leaving the ranks of the living before you actually die. But if you happen to live in a time when it is hard to serve the state, it is your duty to spend more time on leisure and literature. Just like on a dangerous sea voyage, you can pull into a safe harbor from time to time. Do not wait for public life to let you go; free yourself on your own.
