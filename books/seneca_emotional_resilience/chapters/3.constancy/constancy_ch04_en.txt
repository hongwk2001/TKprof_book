[Chapter 4]
You might think that no one will try to injure the wise man. No, someone will definitely try. But the injury will never reach him. The wise man is separated by such a great distance from those beneath him that no evil impulse can be strong enough to harm him.

Even when people in power, backed by loyal followers, try to harm him, their attacks fall far short of his wisdom. It is like shooting arrows at the sky. An arrow shot from a bowstring or a catapult might fly so high that it disappears from sight, but it will always fall back to the ground without ever reaching the sky.

When the foolish King Xerxes shot countless arrows and blocked the sunlight, not a single one reached the sun. When he threw chains into the sea to punish it, it did not reach Neptune, the god of the sea. Sacred things are out of human reach. Just as people cannot truly harm the gods by destroying temples or melting down statues, all attempts to treat the wise man with pride, disrespect, or mockery are simply useless.

You might say, "Wouldn't it be better if there was no one who wanted to do such things in the first place?" But that is like wishing for the entire human race to become perfectly harmless, which is impossible. Besides, when no wrongs are committed, the ones who benefit the most are the people trying to cause harm, not the wise man who is immune to injury anyway. Actually, I sometimes wonder if wisdom shines the brightest when it remains calm in the middle of chaos. The strongest proof of a great general and his army's worth is the quiet confidence they show when they are surrounded by the enemy.
