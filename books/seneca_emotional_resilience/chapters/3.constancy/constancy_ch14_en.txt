[Chapter 14]

Some men are so crazy that they think it is possible to be insulted by a woman. It does not matter who she is, how many slaves carry her litter, how heavy her earrings are, or how soft her cushions are. She is always the same thoughtless creature. Unless she gains true knowledge and deep learning, her desires will remain fierce and wild.

Some people get annoyed just from bumping into a hairdresser. They call it an "insult" when a nobleman's doorkeeper is slow to open the door, when an announcer acts arrogant, or when a chamberlain shows contempt. There is so much comedy hidden in these situations. A person's mind can be filled with great amusement when they compare the frantic foolishness of others with their own inner peace.

You might wonder if the wise person will simply refuse to approach a door guarded by a rude doorkeeper. That is not the case at all. If he has necessary business there, he will deal with the doorkeeper, no matter how fierce the guard acts. He will tame the guard the exact same way a person tames a dog by offering it food. He remembers that some bridges charge a toll, so he will not get angry about paying an entrance fee. He knows that people will always buy whatever is put up for sale, so he will simply pay the fee to whoever runs the business of letting visitors inside.

It shows a very weak mind to feel proud of yourself for answering a doorkeeper rudely, breaking his staff, forcing your way inside to see the master, and demanding that the guard be whipped. Anyone who argues with another person makes himself an equal rival. To overcome your opponent, you first have to lower yourself to his level.

If the wise person receives a slap in the face, he will act exactly like Cato did when he was struck. Cato did not get angry and take revenge for the assault. He did not even "forgive" it. He simply ignored it. He showed much greater strength of mind by refusing to acknowledge the blow at all, rather than by forgiving it.

We do not need to spend any more time on this point. It is a known fact that the wise person and the general public have completely different views on what is good and what is evil. He does not care about what everyone else considers low or miserable. He does not follow the crowded, beaten path. Just as the stars move in the opposite direction of the earth's rotation, the wise person walks a path that runs opposite to everyone else's prejudices.
