[Chapter 11]

Most insults come from arrogant and haughty people who cannot handle their own prosperity. Because of this, the wise person has a powerful tool to repel their attitude. That tool is magnanimity, the noblest of all virtues. Magnanimity dismisses all these insults as empty illusions. They are like dreams or night visions that have no real substance or truth.

At the same time, the wise person realizes that these people are simply too low to look down upon someone so far above them. The Latin word for insult is "contumelia," and it actually comes from the word for contempt. No one injures another person this way unless they hold them in contempt. But no matter how contemptuous someone acts, they cannot show true contempt toward a person who is better or more virtuous than they are.

For example, children strike their parents' faces. Infants rumple and pull their mother's hair, spit on her, expose what should be covered, and use dirty words without hesitation. However, we do not call any of this behavior "contemptuous." The child doing these things simply lacks the ability to show true contempt.

For the exact same reason, the crude jokes slaves make about their masters only amuse us. If they make fun of the master first, their boldness even gives them the right to mock the guests. The more despicable and ridiculous the slave is, the more freely he speaks. Some people even buy bold slave boys just for this purpose. They encourage the boys' crude behavior and send them to school to learn how to deliver rehearsed insults. We do not call this an "insult," but rather a "witty remark."

It is complete madness to be amused by the exact same thing at one time and feel insulted by it at another. It is incredibly foolish to call a phrase an "insult" when a friend says it, but an "amusing joke" when a slave boy says it.
