[Chapter 19]

We should not get dragged into quarrels and pointless arguments. We need to step back and ignore all the foolish things that thoughtless people do, because only thoughtless people act that way. We should place equal value on both the praises and the insults of the crowd. We should not let an insult hurt us, and we should not let praise please us.

If we do not do this, we will end up neglecting our most important duties. We will abandon our responsibilities in both public and private life, simply because we are too afraid of insults or too exhausted by them. Sometimes, while tortured by a childish pain at hearing something we do not like, we will even miss out on opportunities that would truly benefit us. At other times, when we get furious with powerful people, we will expose our weakness through reckless anger.

It is a mistake to think that freedom means never experiencing any pain. True freedom comes from raising your mind above injuries and becoming a person whose happiness is entirely internal. It means separating yourself from external circumstances so that you do not have to live a disturbed life, constantly afraid of everyone's laughter and gossip. After all, if anyone on the street can throw an insult at you, you will never truly be safe.

The wise person and the person who is just trying to become wise will approach this problem differently. Only those whose philosophical training is incomplete—those who still guide themselves by public opinion—will feel doomed to spend the rest of their lives surrounded by insults and injuries. However, even for them, everything becomes much easier to endure if they are simply prepared for it mentally. The nobler a person is—whether by birth, reputation, or wealth—the more bravely they should act. They must remember that the tallest soldiers always stand in the front line of battle.

When it comes to insults, offensive words, disrespect, and similar petty attacks, a person should bear them the same way they bear an enemy's shouts. They are like arrows and stones thrown from a distance that just rattle harmlessly against a helmet. You should treat actual injuries like real wounds in combat. Some hit your armor and some hit your body, but you must endure them without falling down or leaving your place in the formation.

Even if you are violently attacked and cornered by the enemy, it is shameful to retreat. Hold firmly to the position that nature has assigned to you. This position is simply the position of being a human being.

The wise person has another advantage that is the exact opposite of your struggle. You are fighting hard, but he has already won the victory. Do not fight against what is best for you. Keep this hope alive in your mind until you finally reach the truth. Be willing to welcome the idea of a better life, and encourage it with your respect and prayers. It is in the greatest interest of all humanity that there should be at least one person who remains unconquered, and against whom fortune has absolutely no power.
