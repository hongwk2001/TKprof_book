[Chapter 13]

We can find this exact same attitude in other people for entirely different reasons, so there is no reason to doubt that the wise person possesses this firmness of mind. No doctor gets angry at a crazy patient. No one takes it as a personal insult when a feverish patient curses at them for refusing to give them cold water.

The wise person keeps this exact same mindset when dealing with everyone. He acts like a doctor treating a patient. A doctor does not refuse to touch the shameful parts of a patient's body if it is necessary for treatment. He does not refuse to examine the patient's waste, and he does not get angry if a frantic patient insults him. The wise person knows that all those people strutting around in purple-edged robes, showing off their healthy and tanned skin, are actually sick in the head. He sees them as patients full of foolishness. Therefore, he does not get angry if they act a little rudely toward their doctor due to their illness.

The wise person places no value on their honorable titles, and he places just as little value on their rude behavior. He will not think highly of himself just because a beggar flatters him. He will not feel insulted if someone at the bottom of society fails to return his greeting. In the same way, he will not admire himself even if a crowd of rich men praises him. He knows that those rich men are no different from beggars. In fact, they are even more miserable. A beggar only wants a little, but a rich person wants a great deal.

He will not be bothered if the King of the Medes or the King of Asia walks past him in silence with a scornful look instead of returning his greeting. He knows that such a king's position is no more enviable than the job of a man managing sick and crazy servants in a huge household. You should not get upset if a slave trader who buys and sells worthless people near the temple of Castor fails to return your greeting. His shop is packed with the worst kind of slaves. There can be no goodness in a person who owns nothing but bad people.

Just as the wise person is indifferent to the politeness or rudeness of a slave trader, he is equally indifferent to the attitude of a king. He will tell the king that the king may rule over the Parthians and Bactrians, but the king only controls them through fear. He will say that owning those people means the king can never lower his bow, because those subjects are fierce enemies who are ready to be sold and are eager for a new master.

The wise person will not be shaken by an insult from anyone. Even though all people are different, the wise person views them all equally because they are all equally foolish. If he ever lowered himself enough to be affected by an injury or an insult, he would never feel safe again. Safety is the greatest advantage the wise person enjoys. He will never bow to the person who hurt him by admitting that he was injured. Logically, anyone who gets upset by someone's scorn must also place value on that person's praise.
