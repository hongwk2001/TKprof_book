[Chapter 15]

Stop saying that the wise person will receive an injury if he is beaten or if his eye is knocked out. Stop saying he will receive an insult if a mob of thugs boos him out of the public square. Do not ask what will happen if he is forced to leave a royal banquet and eat with the lowest slaves, or if he is forced to endure the most humiliating things you can imagine.

No matter how many or how severe these trials may be, they are all exactly the same kind of thing. If small trials cannot affect him, then neither will the large ones. If a few trials do not bother him, neither will a whole crowd of them. You are imagining the wise person's massive mind based entirely on your own weakness. You think about how much suffering you can endure yourself, and then you set the limit of his endurance just a tiny bit further. But his virtue has placed him in an entirely different universe that shares nothing in common with yours.

Gather up all the absolute worst sufferings. Bring together everything that is hard to bear, repulsive to hear, or horrible to see. He will not be overwhelmed even if all of them hit him at once. He will bear their combined crushing weight exactly the same way he bears each individual pain. Anyone who tries to limit the wise person's greatness by claiming he can endure this but cannot endure that is wrong. Fortune defeats us completely unless she is entirely defeated.

Do not think this is just a strict and harsh Stoic idea. Even Epicurus—the philosopher you treat as the patron saint of your laziness, and who supposedly taught only soft, lazy things meant for pleasure—agreed. He said that fortune rarely stands in the wise person's way. Notice how close he came to a truly manly thought! I challenge you to go one step further and speak more boldly. Clear fortune completely out of the way!

This is the house of the wise person. It is narrow, unadorned, and free from all noise and splendor. There are no doorkeepers pushing away crowds of visitors based on the size of their bribes. Yet fortune cannot even cross this open, unguarded threshold. She knows perfectly well that there is absolutely no room for her in a place where nothing belongs to her.
