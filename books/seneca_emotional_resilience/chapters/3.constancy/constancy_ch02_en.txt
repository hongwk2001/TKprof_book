[Chapter 2]
As I told you then, it is completely reasonable for you to worry about the republic. On one side, Publius Clodius was selling out the country, and on the other side, Vatinius and the worst scoundrels were doing the exact same thing. They were so blinded by greed that they did not realize selling out the state meant selling themselves along with it.

But I told you not to worry for Cato himself. A wise man can never be injured or insulted. It is far more certain that the immortal gods gave Cato to us as a true model of a wise man than that they gave Ulysses or Hercules to earlier generations. After all, our Stoic philosophers have always declared those heroes to be wise men. They were wise men who remained unbroken through countless hardships, stayed away from worldly pleasures, and conquered all fears.

Cato did not kill wild beasts like hunters or farmers do. He did not fight mythical monsters with a sword and fire, nor did he live in a time when people believed a single man could hold up the sky. He threw away old superstitions and lived in an era when material knowledge reached its peak. In that world, he had to fight against a many-headed monster called ambition. He fought against a boundless desire for power that could not be satisfied even when the entire world was divided among three men.

Cato was the only one who stood alone against the collapse of a corrupt society that was breaking down under its own massive weight. He propped up the falling republic as best as one man could, and he held on until his support was finally pulled away. In the end, he faced the collapse he had prevented for so long, and met his end alongside the freedom he refused to survive without. Cato did not survive without freedom, and freedom did not survive without Cato.

Even when people stripped Cato of his public office and tore his toga to pieces, they could never truly hurt such a great man. It did not matter at all even if they spat dirty water from their mouths onto his sacred head. A wise man is always safe. No injury or insult can ever reach him.
