[Chapter 6]
Now think about whether a thief, a false accuser, a stubborn neighbor, or a rich man who abuses the power of a childless old age could really injure this wise man. Neither war nor an enemy army whose job is the noble art of tearing down city walls could take anything from him. Surrounded by flashing swords, rioting and looting soldiers, flames, blood, and the ruins of a fallen city, and even while temples crashed down onto the statues of the gods, one man remained completely at peace.

You should not think of this as a reckless brag. If my words are not enough, I will give you an example. It is truly hard to believe that anyone could have such great firmness and greatness of mind. But here is a man who steps forward to prove your doubts wrong. He shows that a mere human can rise above human limits and calmly watch pains, losses, diseases, wounds, and huge natural disasters roaring all around him. He calmly bears hard times and enjoys good times with self-control, without giving in to the bad times or relying on the good ones. Through all the changes of the goddess of Fortune, he always stays exactly the same, and he considers nothing to be truly his own except himself—and even then, only his better part.

He says, "Look. A conqueror might shake walls with a battering ram, bring down high towers with hidden tunnels, and build dirt mounds as high as the highest fortress. But I am here to prove that no weapon has ever been invented that can shake a firm and settled mind. I have just crawled out of the ruins of my house. With fires shooting up everywhere, I escaped the flames covered in blood. I do not know what fate has happened to my daughters—whether they suffered something worse than what happened to our country. I am a lonely old man, and I see everything around me in the hands of the enemy. But I still declare that my property is completely whole and unhurt. I still have and hold everything that was truly mine.

"There is no reason at all for you to think that I am conquered or that you have conquered me. It is only your luck that has beaten my luck. I do not know where those changing possessions are that get new owners. But what truly belongs to me is with me, and it will be with me forever. I see rich people who have lost their lands. I see people full of lust who have lost their lovers, the mistresses they treasured while taking on so much shame. I see ambitious people who have lost the senate, the courts, and the public stages where they showed off their bad habits. I see moneylenders who have lost the account books where they happily counted their fake wealth in greed. But I possess everything I have, completely whole and untouched. Leave me alone. Go ask those people who are crying and sobbing over their lost money, those who offer their bare chests to swords to protect it, or those who run away from the enemy carrying heavy bags of coins."

So, Serenus, remember that the perfect wise man, filled with human and divine virtues, can lose nothing. His property is surrounded by strong, solid walls that cannot be broken. You cannot compare them to the walls of Babylon that Alexander the Great destroyed, or the forts of Carthage and Numantia that were both conquered by the same man's hands. You cannot compare them to the Capitol Hill and fortress of Rome, which have the scars of a winner's insults. The walls that protect the wise man are safe from fire and enemy attacks. Those walls do not allow anyone to enter. They are high, impossible to conquer, and holy.
