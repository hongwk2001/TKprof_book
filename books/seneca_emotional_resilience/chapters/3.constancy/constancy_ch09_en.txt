[Chapter 9]
The wise man endures everything with the exact same mindset he uses to endure winter cold, harsh weather, fevers, diseases, and all kinds of random accidents. He does not value people enough to believe they are acting with a truly rational purpose. That kind of rational purpose belongs only to the wise man. Everyone else has no real plans. They only have plots, tricks, and changing impulses, which the wise man considers to be exactly the same as pure accidents. And we cannot say that things caused by pure accident are attacking us on purpose.

He also knows very well that the biggest sources of injury come from things meant to deliberately attack us, such as a bribed accuser, a false charge, or someone stirring up the anger of powerful people against us. These are just the civilized world's way of doing highway robbery. Another common kind of injury happens when someone loses a profit or a prize they have been chasing for a long time. For example, an inheritance they tried hard to get might go to someone else, or they might lose the favor of a noble family that was bringing them a lot of money. The wise man avoids all these injuries because he does not even know what it means to live for hope or fear.

In addition to this, I must add that no one receives an injury without feeling disturbed or upset by it. But the wise man, who is free from mistakes, has no such disturbance. He is his own master, and he enjoys a deep and calm peace of mind. If an injury actually reached him, he would be disturbed and shaken. But the wise man has no anger, and anger is caused by the appearance of an injury. A person cannot be free from anger unless they are also free from injury, and the wise man knows that no injury can ever be done to him. This is exactly why he is so upright and cheerful, and always filled with constant joy. In fact, the wise man does not avoid clashing with circumstances or people. Instead, he uses injuries to test himself and prove his own virtue.

While we prove that the wise man is free from injury, please listen with an open and fair mind. This does not take away any of your arrogance, greedy desires, blind rashness, or pride. We are only claiming this freedom for the wise man without touching your bad habits. We are not trying to stop you from causing injuries. We are trying to let the wise man sink all injuries beneath himself and protect himself through his own great mind.

This is just like in the sacred games, where many fighters win simply by patiently enduring the blows of their attackers until the attackers wear themselves out. Think of the wise man as belonging to this group of people. Through long and faithful practice, he has built the strength to endure all the violence his enemies can throw at him and actually tire them out.
