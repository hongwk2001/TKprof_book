[Chapter 1]
Serenus, I can confidently say that the difference between the Stoics and other philosophers is as big as the difference between men and women. Both contribute equally to human society, but one is born to command, and the other is born to obey. Other philosophers treat us gently and kindly, just like a family doctor treats our bodies. They do not use the best and fastest treatment. They only use the methods that the patient allows.

On the other hand, the Stoics take a much more direct and manly approach. They do not care at all if their methods look attractive to beginners. Their only goal is to pull us out of the everyday world as quickly as possible. They want to lead us to a high and safe peak, where no weapon can reach and even the goddess of Fortune cannot touch us.

You might argue, "But the path we have to climb is too steep and rugged." Of course it is. You can never reach the top by walking on a flat road. But the climb is not as steep as people think. It is only the very first part where rocks and cliffs block the way and there is no clear path. It is like looking at a mountain from far away. Because the distance tricks our eyes, the mountains look very steep and packed closely together. But as you get closer, those same mountains slowly reveal their true shape. The parts that looked like steep cliffs from far away start to turn into gentle slopes.

A little while ago, we were talking about Marcus Cato, a philosopher and politician who pursued perfect virtue. At that time, your strong sense of justice made you angry. You were furious that the people of his time treated Cato as if he was worse than Vatinius, a corrupt and mocked politician. This happened even though Cato was a much greater man than Caesar or Pompey. You were deeply shocked to hear that when Cato protested an unfair law in the forum, the public square where citizens gathered, his toga, the traditional Roman outer garment, was torn to pieces. He was dragged by a rioting mob all the way from the speaker's platform to the Arch of Fabius. He had to endure the insults, the spitting, and the fierce anger of that crazy crowd.
