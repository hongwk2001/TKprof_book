[Chapter 14]

Theophrastus says it is impossible for a good person not to get angry at bad people. According to this logic, the better a person is, the more easily they should get angry. But the truth is that the better a person is, the calmer they should be, the freer from passions they should be, and they should hate no one.

A good person has no reason to hate a sinner. This is because what leads people to crimes is usually just a simple mistake. It makes no sense for a wise person to hate those who make mistakes. If he did, the wise person would eventually have to hate himself too. Think about how many times he has acted against morals, and how many of his actions need forgiveness. He would soon be angry with himself. Because a fair judge does not hand down a different sentence for himself than he does for others.

I assure you, no one can honestly consider themselves completely innocent. A person who claims to be innocent is just hoping to be praised by others, rather than looking into their own conscience. It is much more humane to treat people who have made mistakes with a gentle, fatherly heart, and to gently guide them back to the right path instead of driving them away. If someone is lost and wandering through your field, it is better to show them the right path than to chase them out.
