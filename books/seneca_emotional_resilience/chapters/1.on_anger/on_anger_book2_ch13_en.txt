[Chapter 13]

There is no reason at all to defend these passions or try to justify their excess by declaring that they are useful or unavoidable. Of course, every vice has its defenders. But that does not mean we should declare anger to be something that cannot be rooted out. The evils we suffer from can be cured. Because we are born with a natural inclination toward goodness, nature (or Providence) itself will help us if we try to improve our lives. Also, the path to virtue is not as steep or rugged as some people think. It can easily be reached even on flat ground. The story I am telling you is not a lie. The path to happiness is easy. You only need to step onto it, with good fortune and the excellent help of the gods.

What you are doing right now is much more difficult. Nothing is more comfortable than a peaceful mind, and nothing is more exhausting than anger. Nothing is more peaceful than mercy, and nothing is busier than cruelty. Humility rests, while vice is crushed by work. In short, it is easy to cultivate any virtue, but vice demands a heavy price. Anger must be removed from our minds. Even those who say anger should be controlled admit this to some extent. Get rid of it completely. There is nothing to gain from it. Without anger, we can end crimes, punish bad people, and improve their lives much more easily and justly. The wise person will do their duty in everything without the help of any evil passion. They will not use any aids that they must carefully watch to keep from escaping their control.
