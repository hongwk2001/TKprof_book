[Chapter 4]

Now we have clearly explained what anger is. The difference between anger and a hot-tempered personality is very clear. It is like the difference between a drunk person and a heavy drinker, or between a frightened person and a coward. An angry person might not be naturally hot-tempered, and a hot-tempered person is not always angry at any given moment.

I will skip over the other kinds of anger that the Greeks call by special names. This is because our language does not have specific words that can point them out exactly. Of course, we do call people bitter, harsh, irritated, frantic, noisy, blunt, and fierce. But these are all just different forms of a hot-tempered personality. You can also see sulkiness or pouting as a slightly milder form of this personality.

Some anger stops at just yelling, while other anger is as stubborn as it is common. Some anger acts rough but saves its words. Some anger wears itself out with bitter insults and curses. Other anger just complains and turns its back. Some anger is massive, deeply rooted, and crouches quietly inside a person's heart. There are a thousand different shapes to this complicated evil.
