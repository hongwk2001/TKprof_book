We must also ask whether men are truly "angry" when they have a boundless love for cruelty, delight in shedding human blood, and kill people who have done them no wrong. Tyrants like Apollodorus or Phalaris killed people who, even in their own minds, had done them no harm. This is not anger, but sheer savagery. They do not inflict pain because they were injured; they are even willing to be injured just for the chance to inflict pain. They do not long to flog and mutilate others to avenge a wrong, but simply for their own pleasure. The conclusion is clear: this evil springs from anger. When anger is fueled by long habit and indulgence, it makes a person forget mercy and drives all human connection from the mind, eventually turning into pure cruelty. Therefore, such men laugh, rejoice, and thoroughly enjoy themselves. Their facial expressions are the exact opposite of someone who is angry, because for them, cruelty is entertainment.

It is said that the Carthaginian general Hannibal, looking at a trench filled with human blood, exclaimed, "Oh, what a beautiful sight!" He would have thought it even more beautiful if that blood had filled a river or a lake. It is no surprise that you, born in a world of bloodshed and raised in slaughter since childhood, are fascinated by this sight above all else. Lady Fortune will follow you and support your cruelty for twenty years, showing you that beloved sight everywhere. You will see that blood at Lake Trasimene, at the Battle of Cannae, and finally in your own city of Carthage. Not long ago, under Emperor Augustus, the governor of Asia Minor named Volesus beheaded three hundred people in a single day. As he strutted among the corpses with an arrogant attitude, as if he had achieved something great and remarkable, he cried out in Greek, "What a kingly act!" It is terrifying to imagine what this man would have done if he had been an actual king. This was not anger, but a mental disease much greater and more incurable than anger.
