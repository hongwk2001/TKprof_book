[Chapter 18]
If only these brutal acts were limited to foreigners, and if only the savage anger and punishments hadn't been imported into our Roman customs along with other strange vices! Marcus Marius, the man whose statues stood on every street and to whom people offered incense and wine, had his legs broken, his eyes gouged out, and his hands cut off by the order of Lucius Sulla. His body was slowly torn to pieces. It was as if Sulla killed him over and over again with every single wound he inflicted.

The man who carried out Sulla's order was Catiline, someone whose hands were already trained to commit all sorts of evil deeds. Catiline tore Marius to pieces right in front of the tomb of Quintus Catulus, placing a terrible burden on the ashes of a man who was known for his kindness. Marius was the idol of the people, and even if the public loved him blindly, he still deserved their love, yet he had to shed his blood drop by drop over a tomb. Even if Marius was a criminal who deserved to be tortured, it was just like Sulla to give such an order, and it was just like Catiline to carry it out. But it was entirely unfitting for the Roman state to be stabbed by both its enemies and the people claiming to seek revenge for it.

We do not even need to dig into ancient history. Very recently, Gaius Caesar whipped and tortured Sextus Papinius, the son of a former consul, Betilienus Bassus, his own finance officer, and several other senators and knights, all on the same day. He didn't do this to conduct a legal investigation; he did it purely for his own amusement. Truly, his horrifying cruelty could not wait a moment longer for pleasure and could not stand any delay in getting it. So, while walking along the path between the colonnade and the river in his mother's garden with senators and noblewomen, he had them beheaded by lamplight.

He had nothing to fear. There was no public or private danger threatening him that night. It would have been a very small favor for him to wait until morning so he wouldn't kill Roman senators while still wearing his slippers.
