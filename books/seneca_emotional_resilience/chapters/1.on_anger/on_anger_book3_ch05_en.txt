[Chapter 5]
You might ask where all this is leading. I would answer that it is to prove that no one should feel safe from anger, because it can provoke even a naturally gentle and quiet person to act brutally and violently. Physical strength and careful attention to health are useless against a plague that attacks the strong and the weak alike. In the same way, calm and cheerful people are just as vulnerable to fits of anger as those with unstable personalities. In fact, for calm people, anger is even more shameful and terrifying, because it drastically changes their normal behavior.

The first step is to avoid becoming angry in the first place. The second step is to let go of anger, and the third is to be able to heal not only our own anger but also the anger of others. I will first explain how we can avoid falling into anger. Next, I will show you how we can escape from it. Finally, I will show you how to restrain an angry person, calm them down, and bring them back to their senses.

Sometimes, if we clearly picture all the evils associated with anger and evaluate its true cost, we will succeed in avoiding it. Anger must be brought before the court of our mind and convicted. Its harms must be thoroughly investigated and exposed. Let's compare anger to the worst vices so we can truly understand what it is. Greed gathers and hoards wealth only for someone better to use it later, but anger simply wastes money. Very few people can indulge their anger without spending anything. Think of how many slaves an angry master has driven to run away or commit suicide! He loses far more from his anger than whatever small incident made him angry in the first place! Anger brings grief to a father, divorce to a husband, hatred to a public official, and defeat to an election candidate. Anger is worse than luxury. Luxury enjoys its own pleasure, but anger enjoys the pain of others. Anger is even worse than malice or jealousy. Malice and jealousy merely wish for someone to be unhappy, but anger actively tries to make them unhappy. Malice might rejoice when bad luck strikes someone by chance, but anger cannot wait for chance. Anger desperately wants to hurt its victim personally, and it is never satisfied with the person merely getting hurt in some other way.

Nothing is more dangerous than jealousy, and this jealousy is born right out of anger. Nothing is more destructive than war, and war is the result of powerful people getting angry. Even the anger of an ordinary person with low status is just like a war, only without weapons or an army. Even if you ignore the immediate consequences like great losses, sneaky plots, and the constant worry that comes from fighting, anger hurts you the exact moment you use it to hurt someone else. It does this by taking away your human kindness. Human nature urges us to love, but anger urges us to hate. Human nature commands us to do good deeds, but anger commands us to cause harm.

On top of this, even if anger seems to come from excessive pride and looks like a display of high spirit, it is actually despicable and cowardly. If you feel insulted by someone, it means you consider that person to be better than you. However, a truly great mind that knows its own worth is never hurt by insults, so it does not bother seeking revenge. Just as weapons bounce off a hard surface, and hitting a solid object only hurts the person hitting it, a strong mind is never hurt by any insult because the insult itself is much weaker than its target. It is far more glorious to brush off all unfairness and insults, like a person wearing perfect armor that blocks every weapon. Revenge is just a way of admitting that we were hurt. A mind that is shaken by an injury can never be a truly great mind. The person who hurt you is either stronger or weaker than you. If they are weak, forgive them. If they are strong, endure it for your own sake.
