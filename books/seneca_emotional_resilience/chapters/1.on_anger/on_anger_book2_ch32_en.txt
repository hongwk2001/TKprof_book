[Chapter 32]

You might say, "But anger has a certain pleasure to it, and it feels sweet to pay back the pain you have suffered."

Not at all. It is not honorable to repay an injury with another injury, even though it is honorable to repay a favor with another favor. When it comes to kindness, it is a shame to be outdone. But when it comes to causing pain, it is a shame to be the one who outdoes the other.

Revenge and retaliation are words that people use every day, and many even think they are righteous ideas. Yet, they do not really differ from doing wrong, except in the order they happen. The person who returns pain for pain simply has a slightly better excuse for their bad behavior, and that is all.

Once, a man who did not recognize the great philosopher and politician Marcus Cato struck him in a public bath out of pure ignorance. No one would have knowingly hurt Cato. Later, when the man came to apologize, Cato simply replied, "I do not remember being struck." He thought it was much better to ignore the insult than to seek revenge.

You might ask if the man suffered any punishment for his rudeness.

No, rather, much good came to him. He actually got to meet and speak with the great Cato.

Despising and ignoring the wrongs done to you is the true mark of a great mind. The most contemptuous form of revenge is simply deciding that your enemy is not even worth taking revenge on. By seeking revenge, many people end up taking small injuries much more seriously than they ever needed to. A truly great and noble person is like a large, powerful wild animal that calmly listens to tiny dogs barking at it without moving an inch.
