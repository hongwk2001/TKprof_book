[Chapter 37]

If someone makes jokes at a dinner party specifically to hurt your feelings, simply stop eating with rude people. People who have no manners when they are sober become much more reckless and insulting after they have been drinking. You have probably seen your friend explode in anger at the doorman of a rich lawyer because he wasn't allowed inside. You yourself have probably gotten angry on your friend's behalf at the lowest of slaves. You should not get angry at a guard dog tied to a chain. Even a fierce dog becomes calm if you just give it some food after it barks.

So step back and smile. Right now, that doorman thinks he is important because he guards a door surrounded by a crowd of desperate people. Right now, the rich man sitting inside feels successful. He actually thinks that having a door that is hard to open is the true sign of power. He doesn't realize that the hardest door of all to open is the door to a prison cell. Be ready to put up with a lot. No one is surprised when they get cold in the winter, when they get sick on a boat, or when they get bumped on a crowded street. The mind is strong enough to handle any trouble that it is already prepared for.

If you are not given the best seat at a dinner table, you might get angry at the other guests, your host, and the person who took your spot. Do not be a fool. It makes absolutely no difference where you sit. A cushion cannot give you honor, and it cannot take your honor away. You might glare at someone because they insulted your talents. Apply this rule to yourself instead. If you insulted the poetry of Ennius, he would have hated you. If you criticized the speeches of Hortensius, he would have fought with you. If you laughed at Cicero's poems, he would have become your sworn enemy. If you are running for office, you should not be angry at how people choose to vote.
