[Chapter 26]

We get angry either at people who can hurt us, or at things that cannot hurt us at all. This second group includes non-living objects like books. If the print is too small, we throw the book away, or if it is full of errors, we rip it apart. We might even destroy an item of clothing just because we do not like how it was designed.

How foolish it is to get angry at objects that do not deserve our anger and cannot even feel it!

You might argue, "But it is actually the people who made these things who are insulting us." 

My answer to this is simple. First, we usually get angry long before we even think about the person who made the object. Second, even the maker might have a perfectly good excuse. Maybe they were just not skilled enough to make a better product. Their lack of talent is not a deliberate insult aimed at you. Someone else might have just been doing their job without the slightest intention of offending you. Finally, it is pure insanity to take the bad feelings you have toward people and unleash them on lifeless objects.

Just as it is crazy to get angry at objects, it is equally crazy to be angry at animals. Animals cannot wrong us because they do not have the ability to make deliberate plans. You cannot call something a "wrong" unless someone did it on purpose. Sure, an animal can hurt us, just like a sword or a falling rock can hurt us, but it cannot actively choose to wrong us.

Even so, some people feel insulted when a horse is calm for one rider but throws a fit for them. They act as if the horse deliberately chose to be difficult. They completely fail to realize that some horses are just easier to control depending on the rider's habits and skill. Just as it is foolish to be angry at animals, it is equally foolish to get angry at children, or at adults who have no more sense than children. When you stand before a fair judge, true ignorance is just as good an excuse as innocence.
