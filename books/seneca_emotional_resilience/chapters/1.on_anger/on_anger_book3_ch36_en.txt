[Chapter 36]

All our senses must be trained to be strong. They are naturally capable of enduring a great deal, as long as our mind does not spoil them. The mind must be called in for a daily inspection. The philosopher Sextius had a great habit. Every night, before going to sleep, he would ask his own mind, "What bad habit did you cure today? What flaw did you fight back against? In what way are you a better person today?" Anger will naturally calm down and become gentler when it knows it has to face a judge every single night.

Nothing is more excellent than this habit of reviewing the entire day. The sleep that follows this self-examination is incredibly sweet. It is deep, calm, and totally relaxed, knowing that your mind has either been praised or corrected. Your own secret judge has reviewed your morals for the day.

I use this exact method and argue my own case before myself every single day. When the lamp is taken away and my wife, who knows my habit, falls silent, I review my entire day. I go over everything I have said and done. I hide nothing from myself, and I skip nothing. Why should I be afraid to look at my own mistakes, when I have the power to say to myself, "I forgive you this time, but make sure you never do it again. In that argument, you were way too aggressive. Do not argue with ignorant people anymore. People who have never been taught usually do not want to learn. You criticized that person much too harshly. Because of that, you only offended him instead of actually helping him improve. In the future, do not just consider whether you are telling the truth. You must also consider whether the person you are talking to can actually handle hearing the truth." A good person loves receiving advice, but the worst people absolutely hate being told what to do.
