[Chapter 12]

Our opponent says, "If you want to remove anger, you must remove vice from the system of nature. But both of these are impossible." First, according to the system of nature, it is possible not to feel cold even in winter, and not to suffer from heat even when the calendar says it is summer. You might be protected from the harsh seasons by living in a favored region, or you might have toughened your body by building up your endurance so that you do not feel heat or cold. Next, turn this statement around. You must remove anger from your mind before you can accept virtue into it, because vice and virtue cannot be combined. You cannot be an angry person and a good person at the same time, just as you cannot be sick and healthy at the same time.

He says, "It is impossible to completely remove anger from the mind, and human nature does not allow it." But there is nothing so stubborn and difficult that the human mind cannot overcome it. There is nothing that cannot become familiar through constant study and effort. There are no passions so wild and uncontrollable that they cannot be tamed by discipline. The mind can carry out whatever command it gives itself. Some people have succeeded in never laughing. Some have given up wine, sex, or all kinds of drinks. Some who are satisfied with short periods of rest have learned to stay awake for long periods without fatigue. People have learned to run on the thinnest of ropes stretched at an angle, to carry huge loads that seem too heavy for human strength, and to dive to immense depths and stay at the bottom of the sea without a chance to breathe. There are thousands of other examples proving that endurance overcomes every obstacle, and that nothing is difficult when the mind is determined to endure it.

The people I just mentioned earn no reward, or a reward completely unsuited to their relentless efforts. A person does not gain anything great by pouring their mental energy into walking a tightrope, carrying a heavy load on their shoulders, fighting off sleep, or reaching the bottom of the ocean. Yet their persistent labor accomplishes all these things for a trivial reward. If that is so, then we—who are waiting for the enormous reward of the unbroken tranquility of mind that makes a happy life—must surely call upon the help of endurance. It is a true blessing to be free from anger, the chief of all evils, and to escape the madness, savagery, cruelty, and frenzy that always follow it.
