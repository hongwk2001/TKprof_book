[Chapter 5]

We have explored what anger is, whether it exists in creatures other than humans, how it is different from a hot-tempered personality, and how many different forms it takes. Now let us look at whether anger is natural, and whether a small amount of anger is useful or worth keeping.

Whether anger is natural will become clear if we look closely at human nature itself. A person is extremely gentle when in their normal state. However, nothing is more cruel than anger. Humans are naturally creatures who feel affection for others. However, nothing is more savage toward others than anger.

Mankind was born to help one another, but anger was born to destroy one another. Humanity loves community, but anger wants isolation. One wants to do good deeds, but the other wants to cause harm. One wants to help even a stranger, but the other tries to attack even its closest friends. One is willing to sacrifice itself for the good of others, but the other is ready to dive right into danger as long as it can drag someone down with it.

Anyone who thinks this cruel and harmful vice suits humans, who are nature's finest and most refined creations, is someone completely ignorant of nature. As I said before, anger is an emotion that is anxious to punish someone. The idea that such a desire should live in a peaceful human heart goes completely against our nature. Human life is built on kindness and harmony. Humanity is tied together in an alliance for the common good, and this is thanks to our love for each other, not terror.
