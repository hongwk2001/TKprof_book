[Chapter 3]

None of these things that casually affect the mind deserve to be called passions. The mind doesn't so much create these feelings as allow them to act upon it. Therefore, a passion does not consist of simply reacting to what we see. Instead, it involves giving in to our feelings and acting on these chance impulses.

If you think that turning pale, bursting into tears, feeling sexual desire, sighing deeply, or getting a sudden flash in your eyes are true signs of passion that reveal the mind's state, you are mistaken. You must understand that these are merely physical impulses of the body. The bravest person often turns pale while putting on armor. When the signal for battle is given, the knees of the boldest soldier might shake for a moment. A great general's heart might leap into his throat just before the armies clash. Even the most eloquent speaker's hands and feet might grow cold and stiff while preparing to begin a speech.

Anger doesn't just stir us; it breaks through boundaries. This is because anger is a strong impulse, and no impulse can happen without the mind's consent. It is impossible to pursue revenge and punishment without the mind being fully aware and approving of it.

A person might feel injured and want to avenge the wrong. But someone might persuade them to give up the idea and calm down. I do not call that anger. That is merely a mental emotion that is still under the control of reason. True anger goes beyond reason and sweeps it away completely.

Therefore, the first moment of confusion when your mind is struck by the thought of an injury is no more anger than the perceived injury itself. True anger is the mad rush that follows. This rush not only accepts the impression of being harmed but acts on it as if it were completely true. That is anger: a state of excitement in the mind directed toward revenge, coming from deliberate choice and resolve.

There is no doubt that fear makes us flee and anger makes us charge forward. Consider, then, whether you believe we can actively seek or avoid anything without the direct participation of the mind.
