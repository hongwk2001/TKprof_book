[Chapter 25]

As another result of this training, we will stop getting furious over the smallest and most useless things. It is pure madness to lose your temper just because a servant is slow, your drinking water is a little warm, your bed is messy, or your table is poorly set. A person must be in terrible health to shrink away from a gentle breeze. Their eyes must be sick if looking at white clothing hurts them. They must be completely ruined by a soft life if just watching someone else work causes them pain.

There is a story about a man named Mindyrides from the ancient city of Sybaris. One day, he saw a man digging with a heavy tool. Mindyrides complained that just watching the man work made him tired, and he ordered the man to stop working where he could see him. This is the exact same man who once complained that his bed of rose petals hurt him because one of the petals was folded in half. When a life of luxury corrupts both the body and the mind, everything feels impossible to endure. This is not because the situation is actually hard, but because the person suffering through it has grown incredibly weak. There is absolutely no good reason to fly into a rage just because someone coughs or sneezes, a fly is not swatted away fast enough, a dog hangs around you, or a clumsy servant drops a key.

If the sound of a chair dragging across the floor hurts your ears, you will never be able to handle the harsh insults of a political debate or the vicious attacks in the Senate. If you get angry at a servant for not putting enough ice in your drink, you will never survive the hunger and thirst of a long summer march. Therefore, nothing feeds anger more than endless, spoiled luxury. You must harden your mind with rough treatment, so that you do not even feel a blow unless it is a heavy one.
