[Chapter 23]

This man's grandson was Alexander the Great. He used to throw his spear at his own guests. He also took two of his friends and threw one to a wild beast, and left the other to his own anger. Interestingly, the friend thrown to the lion actually survived.

Alexander didn't inherit this terrible habit from his grandfather or his father. In fact, it was a special virtue of King Philip of Macedonia to endure insults patiently. This patience served as a great shield for his kingdom.

Demochares was nicknamed "The Truth Teller" because he spoke with a reckless and shameless tongue. He once came to Philip as an ambassador along with others from Athens. After listening graciously to everything they had to say, Philip asked, "Tell me, what can I do to please the Athenians?"

Demochares rudely interrupted, "Hang yourself."

Everyone standing nearby exploded in anger at such a brutal insult. However, Philip ordered them to be quiet and allowed this arrogant man to leave unharmed.

Philip then told the other ambassadors, "Go back to Athens and tell them this. Those who say such things are far more arrogant than those who simply listen without seeking revenge."

The late Emperor Augustus also did and said many memorable things, proving that he never let anger control him. A historian named Timagenes made several offensive remarks about Augustus, his wife, and his entire family. Naturally, his jokes spread quickly, since nothing catches on faster or stays in people's minds longer than reckless gossip. Augustus frequently warned him to watch his mouth. When Timagenes continued to offend people, the emperor finally banned him from his house.

After this, Timagenes spent his later years living with Asinius Pollio and became incredibly popular throughout Rome. Just because Caesar closed his doors didn't mean everyone else did. Timagenes continued to read his new history books out loud to the public, but he deliberately burned all the books detailing the actual achievements of Augustus Caesar. He remained an enemy of the emperor, yet no one was afraid to be his friend. No one avoided him as if he had a contagious disease. Even though he lost the emperor's favor, he easily found people willing to support him.

Augustus handled all of this with absolute patience. He didn't even get angry when the historian aggressively attacked his own glorious achievements. He didn't complain about the man who gave his enemy a place to stay. Instead, he simply said to Asinius Pollio, "You are keeping a wild beast."

When Pollio tried to make excuses for his behavior, Augustus stopped him and said, "Enjoy his friendship, my dear Pollio. Enjoy it to the fullest."

Pollio then offered, "If you command me to, Caesar, I will immediately kick him out of my house." Augustus replied, "Why would I do that, when I was the one who made you two friends again in the first place?"

He said this because Pollio had previously been angry with Timagenes, and he only let go of his anger when Augustus himself became angry.
