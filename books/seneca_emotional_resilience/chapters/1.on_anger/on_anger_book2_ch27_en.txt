[Chapter 27]

There are certain things that are completely unable to hurt us, and their power is meant only to be helpful and good. For example, the immortal gods do not want to harm us, nor are they even capable of doing so. Their nature is gentle and peaceful, and it is just as impossible for them to hurt others as it is for them to hurt themselves.

Foolish people who do not understand the truth often blame the gods for violent storms at sea, heavy rains, or long, bitter winters. But the truth is, whether these natural events hurt us or help us, they happen without any regard for us at all. The universe does not cycle through summer and winter just for our sake. These seasons follow their own natural laws to keep the universe functioning as it should. We think far too highly of ourselves if we imagine we are so important that the universe makes these massive changes just for us. None of these things happen to injure us; in fact, they all work together for our ultimate benefit.

I mentioned that there are things that cannot hurt us, and things that simply do not want to. This second group includes good leaders, good parents, teachers, and fair judges. When they punish us, we should accept it with the exact same attitude we use when we face a surgeon's knife, endure a temporary fast, or go through any painful experience that is meant only to heal us.

Suppose you are being punished. Do not just focus on the pain you are feeling; think about the actions that brought you there. Put your own past behavior on trial. If you are truly willing to be honest with yourself, you will quickly realize that your wrongdoings deserve a much harsher punishment than you are actually getting.
