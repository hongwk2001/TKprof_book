[Chapter 20]
Thus, a Persian king cut off the noses of an entire nation in Syria, and because of that, the place is called Rhinocolura, meaning the city of the people with cut-off noses. You shouldn't think he was being merciful just because he didn't chop off their heads entirely. He was simply thrilled that he had invented a new kind of punishment.

Something very similar must have happened to the people in Ethiopia, who are called the long-lived because of their incredibly long lifespans. Cambyses flew into a violent rage because they did not thankfully accept slavery by raising their hands to the sky, and instead sent messengers who used independent language, which kings call insulting. Without any knowledge of the roads or taking any food supplies, he led all his soldiers into a dry, pathless wasteland, and they ran out of basic supplies on the very first day of the march. That barren, uncultivated land where no one had ever walked offered absolutely no food.

At first, the tenderest leaves and shoots eased their hunger, followed by leather softened by fire, or anything else that extreme desperation forced them to eat. When they marched further and found a desert with no roots, no grass in the sand, and not even any animals, they drew lots. Every tenth man was chosen, and they ate a meal that was far crueler than starving.

His anger still drove the king madly forward, and it was only after losing part of his army and eating another part that he started to fear he might have to risk his own life and draw a lot himself. Only then did he finally order a retreat. But even during all this, his well-trained falcons were never sacrificed, and while his soldiers were drawing lots to see who would die a miserable death and who would live an even more miserable life, feast food was still being carried on camels just for him.
