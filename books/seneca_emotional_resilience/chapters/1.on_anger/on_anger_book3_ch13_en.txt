[Chapter 13]
Fight fiercely with yourself. If you cannot conquer anger, at least do not let anger conquer you. If anger cannot reveal itself and finds no outlet to escape, you have already started to subdue it. Let's hide the symptoms of anger and keep them as secret as possible. This will be very difficult, because anger desperately tries to burst out, light up our eyes, and twist our faces. But if we let anger show on our faces, it becomes our master.

Instead, let's lock anger away in the deepest part of our hearts. Let's endure anger rather than letting it drive us. In fact, we should change all its symptoms into their exact opposites. Let's make our expression calmer than usual, soften our voice, and slow down our walking speed. Our inner thoughts will gradually be influenced by our outward appearance.

In Socrates' case, lowering his voice and speaking less were signs of anger. When he did that, it was obvious he was holding himself back. His friends would notice him acting this way and realize he was angry. He was not offended when people pointed out that he was hiding his anger. He could not help but be glad that many people noticed his anger, yet no one was hurt by it. But just as he had the right to criticize his friends' behavior, if he had not given them the same right to criticize his, they would have suffered from his anger.

It is absolutely necessary for us to do this. We should ask our closest friends to speak their minds most freely exactly when we find criticism the hardest to bear. We should also beg them never to cater to us when we are angry. When we are sane and in control of ourselves, let's ask for help against this powerful evil that we unfairly favor.

People who cannot control their drinking and fear they might act recklessly or rudely will order their slaves to drag them out of the banquet hall when they get drunk. People who know from experience how unreasonably they act when they are sick will order that no one should obey them when they are ill. It is best to set up obstacles in advance against bad habits we already know we have. Above all, we must steady our minds so we can endure even the most sudden and violent shocks without getting angry. Or, if a massive, unexpected wrong does trigger our anger, we must bury it deep inside and hide the hurt.

You will see that this is possible if I quote just a few examples out of many. Through these stories, we can learn how great the evils are when anger completely takes over people with absolute power, and how perfectly they can control themselves when their anger is crushed by fear.
