[Chapter 35]

No one wants to strike an enemy so hard that they get their own hand stuck in the wound, or that they lose their balance and fall over after the punch. Yet, anger is exactly that kind of weapon. Once you swing it, it is almost impossible to pull back. We are very careful to choose light, easy-to-handle swords for ourselves. In the same way, we should avoid heavy, clumsy impulses of the mind that cannot be stopped once they are set in motion. The only kind of speed that is truly useful is the kind that stops when it is told to, goes no further than needed, can be guided, and can slow down from a run to a walk. We know our muscles are sick if they twitch and move against our will. A person who breaks into a run when they are only trying to walk must be very old or very weak. Let us remember that the strongest and healthiest actions of our minds are the ones we completely control, not the ones that run wild on their own.

However, nothing helps more than taking a long look at how ugly and dangerous anger really is. No passion looks more disturbed. It twists the most beautiful face and makes a peaceful expression look fierce. Every bit of grace disappears from an angry person. Even if they are wearing fashionable clothes, they will drag them on the ground and completely ignore how they look. Even if their hair is naturally neat or carefully styled, it will stand straight up to match their mood. Their veins swell, their chest heaves with quick breaths, and their neck bulges as they scream their frantic words. Their arms and legs shake, their hands twitch, and their whole body sways back and forth. If they look this shocking on the outside, you can imagine what the inside of their mind must look like. Inside their chest, they carry a much more terrifying expression, a much sharper arrogance, and a much more violent rage that will destroy them if it does not find a way out.

Let us picture anger looking like someone covered in the blood of their enemies or savage animals, or like someone just about to butcher them. Picture anger like the monsters of the underworld that poets describe, wearing belts of snakes and breathing fire. Imagine them bursting out of hell, terrifying to look at, just to start wars, stir up fights between countries, and destroy peace. Let us picture anger with eyes glowing like fire. Listen to its voice hissing, roaring, grinding, and making the most horrible noises imaginable. It swings weapons in both hands without any thought of protecting itself. It is dark, bloody, covered in scars, and bruised from its own wild swings. It staggers around like a maniac, wrapped in a thick, dark cloud, running everywhere to spread panic and destruction. Anger is hated by everyone, but it hates itself most of all. If it cannot find any other way to hurt its enemy, it will gladly tear apart the earth, the sea, and the sky. It is completely harmful and completely hateful. If we must picture it, it should look exactly the way Roman poets have described it:

"Bellona, the goddess of war, fights with her blood-stained whip, and Discord, the goddess of chaos, rejoices in her torn clothes."

Or, if possible, we should invent an even more terrifying face for this terrifying passion.
