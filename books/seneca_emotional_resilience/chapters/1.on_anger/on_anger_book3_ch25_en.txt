[Chapter 25]

It comforts an ordinary person in trouble to know that even the greatest people suffer from bad luck. A poor person weeping over their dead child in a small shack feels calmer when they see a tragic funeral procession leaving a royal palace. In the same way, we can handle insults and injuries much better if we remember that no one is so powerful that they are immune to being hurt. If even the wisest people make mistakes, anyone can find a decent excuse for their faults.

Look back at your own youth. Think about how often you ignored your duties, spoke shamelessly, or partied too hard. If someone is angry, give them enough time to think about what they have done. They will eventually correct themselves. Even if they truly deserve to be punished, that is no reason for you to sink to their level.

When a person completely ignores their tormentor, they rise above the common crowd and look down from a higher place. It is a sign of true greatness not to feel the blows you receive. A massive wild beast slowly turns and stares calmly at a pack of yelping little dogs. A giant ocean wave crashes helplessly against a solid cliff.

A person who refuses to get angry remains unshaken by injuries. A person who gets angry is immediately thrown off balance. But someone who stands completely out of reach of harm holds the ultimate good in their heart. They can confidently say to any person, or even to bad luck itself, "Do whatever you want. You are too weak to disturb my peace of mind. My reason, which guides my life, completely forbids it. Getting angry would actually do me much more harm than your violence ever could."

You might wonder if getting angry really causes that much harm.

The answer is yes. I might know exactly how much you have hurt me, but I have no idea how far my own anger might push me out of control.
