[Chapter 8]

There is no need to dwell on individual cases. When you look at the Roman public forum crowded with throngs of people, the voting booths, or the great chariot-racing arena where masses gather all at once, you can be sure there are just as many vices among them as there are people. Even among men wearing the toga, the symbol of peace, there is no peace. For a little profit, any of them would gladly ruin another. No one can gain anything without someone else losing. They hate the lucky and despise the unlucky. They grudgingly tolerate those in power while oppressing the weak. They burn with various lusts. They would destroy everything for a little pleasure or plunder. They live as if they are in a gladiator school, fighting against the very people they live with. It is like a society of wild beasts. The only difference is that wild beasts are gentle to each other and do not tear their own kind apart, while humans tear each other to pieces and greedily devour one another. This is the only difference between humans and dumb animals: beasts are tame to those who feed them, but human anger preys on the very people who raised them.
