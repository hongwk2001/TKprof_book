[Chapter 38]

If someone insults you, it is probably not worse than what happened to the Stoic philosopher Diogenes. An arrogant young man spat directly in his face while he was giving a lecture on the topic of anger. Diogenes handled it with incredible calmness and wisdom. He said, "I am not angry, but I am honestly not sure whether I should be or not."

Our own Roman leader Cato handled an insult even better. While Cato was arguing a case in court, a man named Lentulus—who was famous for being an aggressive troublemaker—spat a huge glob of phlegm right onto Cato's forehead. Cato calmly wiped his face and said, "Lentulus, I will prove to the whole world that people are absolutely right when they call you shameless."
