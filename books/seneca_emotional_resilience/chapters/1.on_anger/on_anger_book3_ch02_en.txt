[Chapter 2]
Anger does not skip any stage of life, and no nation is immune to it. Some nations have been saved from luxury thanks to the blessing of poverty. Some have avoided laziness through an active, nomadic lifestyle. People living a simple country life might be unaware of courtroom tricks, lies, and various other evils. But there is no nation that does not get stirred up by anger. Anger is just as powerful among Greeks as it is among foreigners. It is just as destructive among people who follow the law as it is among those who only respect brute force.

Finally, while other emotions capture individuals, anger is sometimes the only emotion that takes over an entire nation. An entire country has never fallen madly in love with a single person, nor has a whole nation ever been completely obsessed with profit and money. Ambition tempts individuals, but uncontrollable, raging anger is the only emotion that sweeps across a whole society. People often erupt in anger as a mob. Men and women, young and old, leaders and commoners—everyone acts the same way. Once an entire crowd is provoked by just a few short words, they unleash an anger even fiercer than the person who stirred them up. People immediately grab torches and swords. They declare war on neighboring countries or start civil wars against their own people. Entire houses are burned down along with the families inside. A person who was just recently respected for their brilliant speaking skills suddenly realizes that their words have driven the public into madness.

Roman soldiers point their weapons at their own commanders, and the whole public clashes with the upper class. The senate recklessly chooses leaders without even waiting to draft an army or appoint generals. Their anger drives them to search Roman homes, hunt down people from noble families, and kill them with their own hands. Diplomats are insulted, international laws are broken, and an unnatural madness grips the nation. Without even giving the public time to calm down, ships are quickly launched and filled with hastily gathered soldiers. Without asking the gods for guidance or making careful plans, the crowd rushes into battle driven only by anger. They grab whatever weapons they can find, and the reckless boldness born of their anger ultimately costs them a terrible defeat.

This is the usual fate of primitive tribes when they plunge into war. Their easily excited minds are triggered the moment they feel they have been wronged, and they charge forward immediately. Driven only by their wounded feelings, they gladly chase danger without any discipline, fear, or safety precautions, crashing into the Roman legions like an avalanche. They do not care if they get hurt. They blindly hurl themselves at the enemy's weapons and die from the wounds they brought upon themselves.
