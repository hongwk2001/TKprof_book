[Chapter 22]
These examples should be seen as things to avoid. On the other hand, the examples I am about to share are actions of gentle and generous people that should be followed. These were people who had every reason to be angry and the power to take their revenge. What could have been easier than for King Antigonus to order the execution of two ordinary soldiers who were leaning against his tent, doing what people love doing the most while taking the biggest risks—badmouthing their king? There was only a piece of cloth between the speakers and the listener, so Antigonus naturally heard everything they said. He gently lifted the tent flap and said, "You better step a little further away, or the king might hear you."

Also, one night, he heard some of his soldiers cursing him out because he had led them down a terrible, muddy road. He went over to the men who were in the worst trouble. He pulled them out of the mud without letting them know who he was, and then he said, "Go ahead and curse Antigonus for getting you into this mess, but make sure you bless the man who pulled you out of this mud."

This same Antigonus was incredibly tolerant of insults, not only from his own people but also from his enemies. So, when he was laying siege to a few Greeks in a small fortress, the Greeks felt safe in their strong position and mocked him. They threw countless jokes at his ugly appearance. Once they mocked his short height, and another time they mocked his broken nose. He replied, "I am glad to have Silenus, the forest spirit, in my camp, and I expect good luck to come from it."

After he conquered these witty troops by starving them out, he dealt with them by organizing the ones fit for service into regiments and selling the rest at a public auction. He said he would not have gone that far if it weren't for the fact that people with such foul mouths really needed to be controlled by a master.
