[Chapter 10]

For this reason, reason never asks for help from blind and savage impulses. Reason has no power to control those impulses. It can only hold them back by clashing other powerful emotions against them. For example, it might use fear to fight anger, anger to fight laziness, or greed to fight cowardice. May virtue never fall so low that reason has to run to vices for help!

The mind can never find true peace in that way. If the mind's safety relies entirely on its flaws, it will always be shaken and tossed around like a ship in a storm. If you cannot be brave without anger, if you cannot be hard-working without greed, and if you cannot be peaceful without fear, then you are living under the tyranny of being a slave to your emotions. You should be ashamed to put your virtues under the protection of your vices.

Furthermore, if reason cannot do anything without emotion and falls to the same level, it loses its power. If reason without emotion is just as helpless as emotion without reason is reckless, then there is no difference between the two. If one cannot exist without the other, then they are on exactly the same level. No one should accept the idea that emotions should be treated as equal to reason.

Our opponents might say that emotions are useful as long as we only have a moderate amount of them. No, that is not true. Emotions are only useful when they are good by nature. If emotions rebel against authority and reason, the only benefit you get from having a moderate amount of them is that the less you have, the less damage they cause. Therefore, a moderate emotion is just a moderate evil.
