[Chapter 29]

The greatest cure for anger is delay. When you first feel anger rising, beg it to give you just a little bit of time. Do this not to forgive the offense, but so you can form a clear, correct judgment about it. If your anger is delayed, it will eventually fade away. Do not try to crush it all at once, because its first strikes are fierce. By pulling it apart piece by piece, we can get rid of the whole thing.

We get angry either because of things we hear from others, or things we hear and see ourselves. First, we must be very slow to believe the stories people tell us. Many people tell lies simply to trick us, while others tell lies because they have been tricked themselves. Some people try to win our favor through false accusations, making up fake wrongs so it looks like they are fiercely defending us. Another person might lie purely out of spite, trying to tear close friends apart. Some do it just because they are suspicious and bored, seeking entertainment by turning people against each other so they can watch the fight from a safe distance.

If you were a judge in a court case dealing with even a tiny amount of money, you would never accept anything as proof without a witness, and a witness would mean nothing without taking an oath. You would allow both sides to present their case. You would give them plenty of time. You would not rush to settle the matter in a single sitting, because the more a case is examined, the clearer the truth becomes. So why would you condemn your friend in an instant? You must not get angry with a friend before you hear their side of the story, before you have questioned them, and before they even know who is accusing them or what the charges are. You know perfectly well that you must hear both sides, just like a judge in a courtroom. The very person who came to you with the gossip will suddenly refuse to speak if they are forced to prove what they said. They will say, "There is no need to involve me as a witness. If you drag me into this, I will deny everything I said. If you don't promise to keep me out of it, I won't tell you anything else." This person fuels your anger while making sure to escape the actual fight. A person who only tells you things in secret is practically telling you nothing at all. There is nothing more unfair than quietly believing a rumor in secret, and then openly exploding with anger.
