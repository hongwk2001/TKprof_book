[Chapter 7]

Some people might argue that even if anger is not natural, it is often useful, so it is right to use it. Anger wakes up the mind and excites it. Unless courage is fueled by the flames of anger, it will not achieve anything great on the battlefield. Anger is the driving force that pushes bold people to face danger. Therefore, some people argue that instead of driving anger away completely, it is best to just control it, cut off the extreme parts, and force it to stay within useful limits. They want to keep the part of anger that stops us from becoming lazy and prevents our mental strength from fading.

First of all, it is easier to completely drive out dangerous emotions than it is to control them. It is much easier to stop emotions from entering the mind in the first place than to suppress them after they have settled in. Once emotions take root in the mind, they become stronger than the lawful ruler. They will never allow themselves to be weakened or limited.

Next, reason, which holds the reins of the mind, is strong only when it is separated from emotions and not mixed with them. If reason gets mixed with emotions and becomes dirty, it can no longer control the very forces it could have pushed out of the way earlier. Once the mind is excited and shaken, it will be dragged wherever the emotions lead.

There are some things that we can control at first, but as they grow, their own momentum drags us along and leaves no way back. People who jump off a cliff cannot control their fall. Once they start, they cannot stop or slow down. Because of their irreversible recklessness, there is no room for reflection or regret, and they are forced to fall all the way down into a disaster they could have avoided in the first place.

In the same way, if the mind gives itself over to anger, love, or any other emotion, it cannot stop itself. The sheer weight of the emotion and the downward pull of the vice will inevitably sweep the person away and throw them into the deepest pit.
