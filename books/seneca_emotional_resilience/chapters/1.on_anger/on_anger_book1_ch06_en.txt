[Chapter 6]

Some people might argue that it is sometimes necessary to correct mistakes. Of course it is necessary. But it must be done with careful thought, not with anger. Proper correction does not cause injury; rather, it helps to heal under the appearance of an injury. We use fire and forcefully pound wedges to straighten a bent spear shaft. This is not to break the shaft, but to straighten the bent part. In exactly the same way, we apply mild pain to the body or mind to correct a character trait twisted by vice.

A doctor dealing with a mild illness tries not to change the patient's daily habits too much at first. The doctor simply tries to improve the patient's health by changing the order in which the patient eats, drinks, and exercises. The next step is to see if adjusting the dosage helps. If changing the order or amount does not work, the doctor makes the patient stop eating certain foods and reduce others. If even this fails, the patient must stop eating entirely and fast to empty the body. If mild treatments prove useless, the doctor opens a vein. If a body part is ruining the whole body and spreading the disease, the doctor may perform surgery on a limb. But as long as these treatments are meant to restore the patient's health, none of these methods are considered harsh.

Likewise, it is the duty of a judge or a nation's ruler to correct people with twisted characters by using words, especially gentle words, for as long as possible. The goal is to persuade people to do what they should, to inspire a love of honor and justice, and to make them hate vice and value virtue. Next, the ruler should use stricter words while maintaining the line between advice and scolding. Only at the very end should the ruler rely on punishment, but even then, it should be kept light and temporary. Extreme punishment should be handed down only for the most terrible crimes. A person's life should be taken only if dying is actually better for the criminal themselves.

There is only one difference between a ruler and a doctor. A doctor tries to provide a peaceful death to a patient who cannot be saved, but a ruler executes death-row criminals with public disgrace. This is not because the ruler enjoys seeing someone get punished. A wise person is far removed from such inhuman cruelty. Rather, this is done so the criminal's end serves as a warning to everyone. Since they were completely useless while alive, the nation can at least benefit from their deaths.

Therefore, human nature has no true desire to cause pain. And because anger is the desire to cause pain, anger goes against human nature.

I will also borrow an argument from Plato. There is no harm in using an argument from someone on our side. Plato said that a good person harms no one. Only punishment harms. Therefore, punishment does not suit a good person. Because punishment and anger go together, anger also does not suit a good person. If a good person gets no joy from punishing, they will get no joy from the mental state that enjoys punishing. In conclusion, anger is not natural to humans.
