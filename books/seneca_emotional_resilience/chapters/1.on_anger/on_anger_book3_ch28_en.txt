[Chapter 28]

If you let yourself be angry, you will end up fighting with this person, and then with that one. You will fight first with your slaves, then with your freedmen. First with your parents, then with your children. First with your acquaintances, then with complete strangers. Unless your reason steps in to stop you, you will always find a new excuse to be angry. Your rage will drag you from one place to another. Your madness will constantly find fresh things to trigger it, and it will never leave you alone.

You will have no time for love, you miserable person. You are wasting the good times of your life on something evil. It would be far better to win over friends and disarm your enemies. You should focus on helping your community or managing your own business, instead of constantly plotting how to hurt someone else. You are always looking for ways to attack their reputation, their money, or their physical body. But you can never actually do these things without risking a struggle and hurting yourself in the process, even if your opponent is weaker than you.

Suppose your enemy is handed over to you in chains, and you are free to torture them as much as you want. Even then, striking someone too hard often dislocates your own joint, or damages a nerve when you break their teeth. Anger often makes people crippled or sick, even when their victim doesn't fight back. On top of that, no creature is so weak that it can be destroyed without posing some danger to its attacker. Desperation and sheer luck often allow the weakest creatures to match the strength of the strongest.

We must remember that most of the things that enrage us are just minor insults, not actual injuries. There is a huge difference between someone actively blocking my goals and someone simply failing to help me achieve them. There is a huge difference between someone stealing from me and someone refusing to give me a gift. Yet we react exactly the same way to both. We get just as angry whether someone takes something away, or simply refuses to give us what we want. We get just as angry whether they completely destroy our hopes, or just delay them. We do not stop to consider whether they are actively trying to hurt us, or simply trying to help themselves. We ignore whether they are acting out of love for someone else, or out of hatred for us.

Some people are forced to oppose us not only because it is fair, but because it is the honorable thing to do. One person is defending their father, another their brother, another their country, and another their friend. Yet we do not forgive people for doing exactly what we would criticize them for not doing. It might be hard to believe, but we often praise a brave action, while still hating the person who did it. A truly great and fair-minded person respects their bravest enemies. They admire the stubborn defender who fights for their freedom and their country, and they secretly wish they had such a loyal person fighting on their own side.
