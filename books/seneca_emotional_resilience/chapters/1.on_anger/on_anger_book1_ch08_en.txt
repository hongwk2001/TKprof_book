[Chapter 8]

The best approach is to put out the first spark of anger as soon as you see it. You must fight against the very beginnings of anger and be careful not to get swept away by it. If anger starts to swallow you up, it is very difficult to return to a healthy state of mind. Once you willingly hand over authority to an emotion that has entered your mind, reason loses all its power. From then on, the emotion will do whatever it wants, not just what you allow it to do.

Let me say it again: the enemy must be fought and pushed back at the very edge of the border. Once the enemy enters the city and passes through the gates, it will not let its prisoners set limits on its victory. The mind cannot stand safely outside and watch like a spectator to see if the emotion crosses the line. This is because the mind itself turns into the emotion. Therefore, once a force that used to be useful and healthy gets twisted and misused, the mind can no longer control it. As I said before, emotion and reason do not live in separate, divided areas of the mind. Emotion and reason are simply changes in the mind itself as it gets better or worse.

Reason cannot recover when it is conquered and held down by vice, or when it gives in to anger. In a chaotic situation taken over by low impulses, reason cannot free itself.

Our opponents might argue that some people control themselves when they are angry. If so, they either control themselves perfectly enough to do absolutely nothing that anger tells them to do, or they only do a part of what anger wants. If they do nothing that anger commands, this proves that anger is not necessary to get things done, even though you claimed anger was stronger than reason.

In the end, anger must be either stronger or weaker than reason. If anger is stronger than reason, reason cannot set any limits on anger, because only the weak obey. If anger is weaker, reason can get things done without anger and does not need the help of a weaker emotion.

Someone might say that some angry people stay consistent and hold themselves back. But they only do this at certain moments. They do this not when their anger is burning hottest, but when their anger is dying down and fading away on its own. When anger is hot, anger is stronger than they are.

Sometimes even people at the peak of anger let their enemies go without hurting them. But this only happens under specific conditions. It happens when one emotion overpowers another, and fear or greed takes control for a moment. In those cases, anger is not calmed down by reason, but by an unstable and temporary truce between different emotions.
