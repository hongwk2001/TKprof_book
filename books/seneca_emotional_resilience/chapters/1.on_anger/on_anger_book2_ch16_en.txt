[Chapter 16]

Our opponent argues, "The animals considered the most noble are those with the greatest capacity for anger."

He makes a mistake when he holds up creatures that act on impulse rather than reason as models for humans to follow. For humans, reason takes the place of impulse.

Moreover, even among animals, different traits benefit different creatures. Timidity benefits the deer, boldness benefits the hawk, running away benefits the dove, and anger benefits the lion. It is not even true that the best animals are the angriest. Wild beasts that get their food through violence might be better off the angrier they are. But I would praise the oxen and horses that obey the reins thanks to their constancy and endurance.

However, there is absolutely no reason to lead humanity toward such miserable examples. You have the entire universe and Providence, or God. Only humans, among all living things, imitate Him, because only we can understand Him.

Then the opponent speaks again: "The most quick-tempered people are often considered the most honest of all."

They are considered honest only because they are being compared to swindlers and cheaters. They seem simple because they do not hide things and speak plainly. I would not call such people simple; I would call them careless. We give the title "simple" to all fools, gluttons, spendthrifts, and anyone whose vices are completely exposed on the surface.
