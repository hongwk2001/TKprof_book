[Chapter 10]
A sickness can never stand being handled without complaining. Therefore, the best thing to do is to apply a remedy to yourself the moment you feel a problem starting, to hold back your words as much as possible, and to control your impulses. It is easy to notice the first signs of our emotions because the symptoms always show up before the actual illness. Just as the signs of a storm and rain come before the storm itself, there are certain warnings that come before anger, love, and all the other storms that trouble our minds.

People who suffer from epilepsy know a seizure is coming when their hands and feet get cold, their vision blurs, their muscles twitch, their memory fades, and they feel dizzy. So, they apply their usual treatments to hold back the growing illness. They try to keep their senses by smelling or tasting certain medicines. They fight off the cold and stiffness in their limbs by using hot compresses. If all treatments fail, they step away privately and fall down where no one can see them.

It is very helpful for a person to understand their own illness and break its power before it fully develops. Let us look at what specifically provokes us. Some people are offended by insulting words, while others are offended by actions. One person wants their family background respected, while another wants their physical appearance respected. This person wants to be seen as exceptionally trendy, while that person wants to be thought of as incredibly smart. Someone cannot stand arrogance, while someone else cannot stand stubbornness. One person thinks it is undignified to get angry at their slaves, while another is cruel at home but gentle in public. Someone imagines they were nominated for an office because they are unpopular, while another thinks they were insulted because they were not nominated. Not everyone gets offended in the same way, so you must figure out what your own weakness is and pay special attention to defending it.
