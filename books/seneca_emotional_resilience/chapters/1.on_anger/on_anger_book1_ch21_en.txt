[Chapter 21]

Therefore, no matter how powerful anger may seem and how much it seems to ignore both gods and men, there is never anything great or noble inside anger.

A person who thinks anger creates greatness of mind probably also thinks luxury creates greatness. A luxurious person wants to sleep in an ivory bed, wear purple clothes, and have ceilings made of solid gold. He wants to move the land, build walls into the sea, change the flow of rivers, and hang forests in the air.

That person would also think greed shows greatness of mind. A greedy person piles up gold and silver like mountains. He treats an entire province as if it were a field on his personal estate, and he leaves more land to a single manager than ancient leaders used to rule over by drawing lots.

He would think lust also shows greatness of mind. A person consumed by lust swims across dangerous seas, ruins the lives of many young people, and faces the swords of angry husbands, showing complete contempt for death.

He would even think ambition shows greatness of mind. An ambitious person is not satisfied with holding public office just once a year. If he could, he would try to fill the entire calendar of public honors with only his own name, and plaster his titles all over the world.

It does not matter how far these emotions go, or how high they reach. They are all just narrow, miserable, and pathetic. Only virtue is high and noble, and nothing can ever be truly great unless it is truly peaceful.
