[Chapter 20]

Just as human nature makes some people prone to anger, there are many other causes that have the same power. Some people reach this state because of an illness or a physical injury. Others become easily angered due to hard work, staying awake for too long, sleepless nights filled with anxiety, intense desires, and even love. Anything that hurts the body or the mind pushes an unhealthy mind to constantly look for faults. All these things, however, are just the beginnings and triggers of anger. Mental habits have immense power, and if those habits are harsh, the mind's sickness only grows deeper. As for human nature, it is very difficult to change. We cannot simply alter the mixture of elements that was set for us at birth. Even so, learning and knowledge can be a huge help. Hot-tempered people should stay away from wine. The great philosopher Plato also believed that boys should not drink wine, so that the fire in their minds does not burn even fiercer. Hot-tempered people should not overeat, either. If their bodies grow bloated, their minds will expand along with them and become arrogant.

These people should exercise regularly, but they must stop before they get exhausted. This cools down their natural heat without completely draining it, allowing them to burn off their excess fiery energy. Games and hobbies are also helpful, because moderate pleasure relaxes the mind and restores a healthy balance. On the other hand, people with a moist, dry, or rigid nature are not at risk for anger. Instead, they are in danger of falling into worse vices, like cowardice, grumpiness, despair, and suspicion. People with these personalities need to be softened, comforted, and cheered up. Since anger and depression require completely opposite remedies, we must always target the vice that is currently trying to control our mind.
