[Chapter 6]
There is no better proof of a great mind than the fact that nothing that happens can make it angry. The higher regions of the universe are perfectly ordered and close to the stars, so they never gather dark clouds, get tossed around by storms, or get swept up in cyclones. They are free from all chaos, and lightning only flashes in the areas below them. In the same way, a noble mind always stays in a calm, clear atmosphere and uses reason to suppress any impulse that might cause anger. It is humble, inspires respect, and always remains composed and calm.

You will never find any of these qualities in an angry person. When a person is overcome with grief and anger, the first thing they throw away is their sense of shame. When they are excited and confused, getting ready to attack someone, they toss aside whatever normal humility they might have had. An angry person pays no attention to what their duties are or what order they should be done in. They do not use restrained language. They cannot keep any part of their body still. It is exactly like someone running at full speed who can no longer stop themselves.

We can learn a great lesson from the sound advice of Democritus. He said that peace of mind comes from not taking on too many tasks, whether public or private, that are too demanding or beyond our strength. If a person juggles too many different things, their day will never go smoothly enough to avoid some unpleasant event that might make their mind ripe for anger. When you hurry through crowded city streets, you inevitably bump into many people, slip in one place, get blocked in another, and get splashed with mud somewhere else. In the same way, if your life is filled with scattered, inconsistent tasks and wandering, you are bound to face many headaches and complaints.

Someone will deceive our hopes, another will delay our success, and yet another will completely destroy it. Our plans will not go smoothly exactly as we intended. No one is so blessed that Fortune always stays on their side when they constantly test her. Therefore, a person who sees many of their projects twisting out of their control becomes frustrated with both people and circumstances. At the slightest provocation, they explode with intense anger toward people, their jobs, places, luck, or even themselves.

Therefore, to achieve peace of mind, we must not rush around trying to do everything, nor should we exhaust ourselves with huge tasks that are too overwhelming or beyond our abilities. We can easily carry a light load by shifting it from shoulder to shoulder without dropping it. But we struggle to bear the heavy burdens that other people's hands place on us, and when the weight crushes us, we dump it onto our neighbors. Even if we manage to stand up straight while carrying the load, we will eventually stagger under a weight that is beyond our strength.
