[Chapter 24]

Whenever you feel provoked, you should say to yourself, "I do not have more power than King Philip of Macedonia. Yet he let a man curse him without punishing him. I do not have more authority in my own house than the Emperor Augustus had over the entire world. Yet he was perfectly content to just walk away from his critic. I have no reason to beat or chain my slave just because he answered me too loudly, gave me a stubborn look, or mumbled something I couldn't hear. I am not so important that offending my ears should be considered a crime. Many great people have forgiven their sworn enemies. I should certainly forgive people for being lazy, careless, or gossiping."

We should make allowances for people based on their situation. Excuse a child because of their age, a woman because of her gender, a stranger because they don't know our rules, and a servant because they are too comfortable around us. If this is their first mistake, remember how long they have pleased you. If they have messed up many times before, just keep tolerating what you have already tolerated for so long. If the person is a friend, assume they didn't mean to do it. If they are an enemy, assume they were simply doing what enemies do.

If the person is sensible, believe their excuse. If they are foolish, simply forgive them. No matter who they are, remind yourself that even the wisest people often make mistakes. No one is so careful that they never slip up. No one is so mature that a sudden situation cannot push them into a hot-headed reaction. Ultimately, even those who try their hardest not to hurt others will inevitably make mistakes.
