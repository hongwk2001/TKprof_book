[Chapter 18]

Now that we have discussed the main questions about anger, let us move on to its remedies. In my opinion, these are divided into two categories. The first category prevents us from becoming angry, and the second category stops us from making mistakes when we are already angry. Just as we adopt a certain routine to keep our bodies healthy and use different rules to regain our health when we fall sick, we must repel anger in one way and extinguish it in another.

To avoid anger completely, we must engrave into our minds a few general rules of behavior that apply to everyone's life. We can divide these into rules that are useful when educating the young, and rules that are useful later in life.

Education must be carried out with the greatest and most useful consistency. It is easy to shape the mind when it is still tender, but it is extremely difficult to uproot the vices that have grown up with us into adulthood.
