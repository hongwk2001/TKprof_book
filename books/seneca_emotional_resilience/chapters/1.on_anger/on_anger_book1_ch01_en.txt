[Book 1]

[Chapter 1]

Novatus, you asked me to write about how to calm anger. I think you are right to be especially afraid of this feeling. Anger is the ugliest and most savage of all emotions. The other emotions still have some peace and quiet in them. But anger is entirely full of action and the urge to cause pain. It rages with an inhuman desire for weapons, blood, and torture. It does not care what happens to itself as long as it hurts someone else. It rushes blindly onto the tip of a sword, blinded by a desire for revenge that drags even the avenger down to ruin.

For this reason, wise people in the past called anger a brief madness. Anger is completely out of control. It ignores all decency and even forgets family ties. It closes its ears to reason and advice, stubbornly obsessing over whatever it decides to do. It easily gets worked up over small things, and it cannot see what is true and just. It acts a lot like a falling rock that shatters to pieces on the very thing it crushes.

If you want proof that people caught by anger are not in their right mind, just look at their appearance. Mad people have clear symptoms. They have a rough and threatening look, a dark forehead, a stiff expression, a hurried walk, restless hands, a changed skin color, and fast, heavy breathing. The signs of an angry person are exactly the same. Their eyes blaze and flash. Their whole face turns red from the blood boiling up from their heart. Their lips quiver, their teeth are tightly clenched, and their hair stands on end. They gasp and pant for breath. They twist their bodies around and make their joints crack. They groan, shout, and spit out words that you can barely understand. They often clap their hands together and stomp the ground with their feet. Their whole body becomes stiff, clearly showing signs of an unstable mind. They show the ugly and shocking picture of a person who has completely twisted and agitated themselves.

It is hard to express in words just how hateful or disgusting this vice is. Other vices can be hidden and grown in secret. But anger shows itself completely on the face. The stronger anger gets, the more clearly it boils over.

Notice how all animals show certain clear signs before they attack. Watch how their whole body loses its usual peace and reveals its wildness. A boar foams at the mouth and rubs its tusks against trees to sharpen them. A bull tosses its horns in the air and kicks the sand with its hooves. A lion growls. An angry snake puffs up its neck. A mad dog puts on a fierce look. Even an animal that is naturally hateful and full of poison shows an even wilder energy when it is caught by anger.

Of course, I know very well that it is hard to hide other emotions. Lust, fear, and boldness all give off signs that show they are there, and we can notice them early. Every strong passion affects the face. But the difference between anger and other emotions is very clear. The other emotions are noticeable, but anger stands out so strongly that it is impossible to miss it.
