[Chapter 41]

If someone has enough power to look down on anger and crush it completely, then they should do so. However, they should only do this if the anger is fierce, inhuman, and bloodthirsty, and can only be cured by the fear of something more powerful. Let us bring peace to our own minds by constantly meditating on healthy thoughts, by doing good deeds, and by focusing only on true honor.

Let us keep our own conscience completely clear, and stop worrying about getting credit from others. As long as we know we are doing the right thing, we should be perfectly satisfied, even if other people speak badly of us.

Someone might argue, "But the common crowd admires aggressive actions! Bold people are respected, while quiet people are just seen as lazy."

It might look that way at first glance. But once people see the steady rhythm of a quiet person's life, they realize that this calmness comes from true peace of mind, not from laziness. Then, that same crowd will respect and admire them.

There is absolutely nothing useful in the ugly and destructive passion of anger. On the contrary, it brings every kind of evil, fire, and violence. Anger completely crushes self-control. It bathes its hands in blood and scatters the limbs of its own children. It leaves nothing untouched by crime. It has no thought of glory and no fear of disgrace. Once anger hardens into pure hatred, it is impossible to fix.
