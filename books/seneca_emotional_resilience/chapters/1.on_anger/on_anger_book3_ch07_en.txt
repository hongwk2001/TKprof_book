Keep in mind that this same rule applies to both public and private life. Simple, manageable tasks usually go smoothly according to the person's plan, but massive tasks that go beyond a person's ability to manage cannot easily be started in the first place. If a person forces themselves to manage those tasks, the work itself gets in the way and presses heavily upon them. Just when they think success is in reach, the tasks suddenly collapse and drag the person down with them. A person's hopes are often frustrated when they refuse to stick to easy tasks, but still expect whatever tasks they do take on to be easy.

Whenever you attempt something, first take a calm, honest look at your own abilities, the size of the task you want to take on, and the resources you have to achieve it. If you have to give up halfway through, the disappointment will twist your personality. In these situations, it makes a difference whether a person has a passionate, fiery personality or a cold, cautious one. Failure will wake up a generous spirit to anger, but it will plunge a dull, slow spirit into deep sadness. Therefore, our tasks should not be too trivial, nor should they be overly reckless. Do not let your hopes stretch too far beyond what you can actually achieve. Do not even attempt an impossible task where, even if you did succeed, you would be surprised by your own success.
