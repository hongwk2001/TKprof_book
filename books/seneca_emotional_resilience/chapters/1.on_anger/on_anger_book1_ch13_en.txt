[Chapter 13]

Furthermore, the traits we should have become better and more desirable the more we have of them. If justice is a good thing, no one would say it is better to have a little less justice. If courage is a good thing, no one would want to chip away at their courage. Therefore, as anger grows, it should become a better thing. Because no one would object to an increase in something good. However, letting anger grow is never a good idea. So, the very existence of anger in the first place is not a good idea. Something that gets worse as it grows can never be good.

Our opponent says that anger is useful because it makes people better prepared to fight. By that logic, getting drunk is also a good thing. Because alcohol makes a person bold and reckless, and many people swing their weapons more freely when they have drunk too much. You might as well call frenzy and madness essential parts of strength, since madmen are often physically stronger. Fear also frequently has the opposite effect, making a person bolder. An extreme fear of death can push even a total coward to fight.

But anger, drunkenness, fear, and things like these are just cheap, temporary boosts. They cannot provide a true weapon for virtue, which does not need the help of a vice. They might occasionally give a lazy and cowardly mind a little whipping, but no one actually becomes braver through anger unless they were not brave at all without it. Therefore, anger does not come to help courage, but only tries to take its place.

If anger were a good thing, it would belong only to the best people. However, in reality, the people who get angry most easily are babies, old people, and sick people. All weak creatures naturally love to complain.
