[Chapter 7]

Nothing is less suited for a wise person than letting their emotions be controlled by the bad behavior of others. If that were the case, the great philosopher Socrates could never have returned home with the same calm expression he had when he left.

Moreover, if it were the wise person's duty to be angry at disgraceful actions and furious at crimes, no one would be more miserable than the wise person. Their entire life would be filled with anger and sadness, because there would never be a moment when they did not see things worthy of blame. Every time they stepped out of their house, they would have to walk among criminals, misers, spendthrifts, and scoundrels who are perfectly happy with how they live. Wherever they turned their eyes, they would find shocking things.

If a person forced themselves to be angry every time reason demanded it, they would simply collapse from exhaustion. Look at the thousands of people rushing to the law courts at dawn. Their reasons for suing are disgraceful, and their lawyers are even worse. One person challenges their father's will, even though they could have inherited everything if they had just behaved a little better. Another person shows up to sue their own mother. A third person comes to testify about someone else's crime, even though they are famous for committing the same crime themselves. Even the judge is often chosen to condemn people who have done exactly what he has done, and the audience is easily swayed by a lawyer's smooth voice to side with the wrong party.
