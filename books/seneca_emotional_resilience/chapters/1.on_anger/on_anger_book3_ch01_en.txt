[Chapter 1]
My friend Novatus, I will now attempt to do what you so eagerly requested. We will try to drive anger out of our minds, or at least control it and hold back its impulses. When this harmful emotion shows up as a mild symptom, we can sometimes deal with it openly and directly. But when our anger becomes blazing hot, and every obstacle in its path only fuels the fire, we must handle it secretly.

It is important to understand just how powerful and intense this anger is. We need to figure out if we can suppress it by force, or if we should step aside and let the first storm pass, otherwise it might destroy the very remedies we have prepared. We must deal with each person differently based on their character. Some people calm down when you plead with them, while others become arrogant and demanding if you act too submissively. Some can be frightened into stopping their anger. Others might be stopped through criticism, by admitting our own mistakes, by making them feel ashamed, or simply by waiting. Delay is a slow cure for such a hasty disease, so we should only use it when all other methods have failed. Other emotions allow us to delay their treatment so we can heal them later. But the fierce and destructive violence of anger does not grow slowly. It reaches its peak the moment it begins.

Unlike other vices, anger does not just disturb a person's mind; it completely takes it away. Anger torments people until they can no longer control themselves. It makes them eager to see everyone ruined together. Anger does not just rage against its target, but lashes out at every obstacle in its path. Other vices affect our minds, but anger throws the mind off a cliff. Even if we cannot defeat our emotions, our emotions should at least stay grounded. But anger grows stronger and stronger. It is like a lightning strike or a hurricane, or anything else that falls from above and cannot stop itself once it starts.

Other vices affect our judgment, but anger steals our sanity. Other vices arrive in mild attacks and grow unnoticed, but a person's mind plunges into anger all at once. There is no emotion so frantic and destructive to itself. Anger becomes arrogant if it succeeds, and goes crazy if it fails. Even when it is defeated, it never gets tired. If a target happens to be out of reach, anger will turn its teeth against itself. Its intensity has nothing to do with how it started. It can spring from the smallest incident and skyrocket to the most terrifying heights.
