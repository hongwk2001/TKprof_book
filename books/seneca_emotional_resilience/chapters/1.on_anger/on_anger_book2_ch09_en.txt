[Chapter 9]

Because everywhere is filled with vice and crime, if the wise person starts getting angry, they will never be able to stop. More evil is committed than can ever be cured by punishment. It is as if people are participating in a massive race of wrongdoing. Every day, the desire to sin grows, and shame shrinks. Throwing away all respect for what is better and more just, lust rushes wherever it sees fit. Crimes are no longer committed in secret; they happen right before our eyes. Wickedness has become so common and rooted in everyone's minds that innocence is not just rare—it practically does not exist. People do not break the laws alone or in small groups. Instead, as if obeying a universal signal, they rise up simultaneously everywhere to destroy the boundaries of right and wrong.

"A host is not safe from his guest, nor a father-in-law from his son-in-law. Love between brothers is rare. A wife tries to ruin her husband, and a husband tries to kill his wife. A stepmother prepares deadly poison, and a child wonders when his father will die so he can inherit the estate."

And even this describes only a small portion of human crimes! The poet did not describe Rome divided into two hostile camps, parents and children enlisting in opposing armies, or Rome burning at the hands of Romans. He does not mention the cruel cavalry scouring the country for the hiding places of the purged, wells poisoned with toxins, plagues created by human hands, or trenches dug by children around their besieged parents. He leaves out the crowded prisons, the great fires that consume entire cities, grim tyrannies, and secret plots to establish dictatorships and destroy the people. He also does not describe those who proudly boast about things like rape, debauchery, and lust—things that were once considered crimes when they could still be controlled. Add to this the public acts of national betrayal, broken treaties, all the defenseless people plundered by the strong, the cheating, theft, fraud, and the debt defaults that even three courthouses cannot handle today. If the wise person wanted to be as angry as the cruelty of human crimes deserves, they would not just be angry; they would have to go mad with rage.
