[Chapter 24]

Being too quick to believe what we hear causes terrible damage. Sometimes it is better not even to listen, because it is often better to be tricked than to constantly suspect that people are lying to you. We must clear our minds of suspicion and doubt, which are the most unreliable causes of anger. We think things like, "That person did not greet me politely," or "He avoided hugging me," or "She interrupted my story," or "He did not invite me to dinner," or "They looked at me with disgust." Suspicion can always find a reason to exist. What we truly need is to be honest with ourselves and to look for the good in every situation. We should not believe anything unless it happens right in front of our eyes and is completely impossible to deny. Whenever our suspicions turn out to be wrong, we should scold ourselves for being so quick to jump to conclusions. Practicing this habit will eventually make us much slower to believe harmful gossip.
