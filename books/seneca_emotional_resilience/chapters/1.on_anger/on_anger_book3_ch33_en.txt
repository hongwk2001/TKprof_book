[Chapter 33]

The biggest fights are almost always about money. Money is what clogs up the law courts, destroys the relationship between father and son, creates deadly poisons, and hands swords to murderers and soldiers alike. It is stained with our blood. Because of money, husbands and wives argue all night long. Crowds crush against the judge's bench. Kings go completely mad and destroy entire nations—nations that took centuries of hard work to build—just so they can dig for gold and silver in the ashes of ruined cities.

You probably enjoy looking at your bags of money sitting in the corner. Because of those bags, people scream until their eyes pop out of their heads. The courtrooms echo with the noise of lawsuits, and judges travel from far away just to decide which person's extreme greed is more legally justified. It is totally absurd when a bitter old man, who is about to die without any heirs, bursts into a fit of rage—not over a bag of gold, but over a handful of cheap copper coins or a tiny debt written down by his slave. It is ridiculous when a sick loan shark, whose feet are crippled by gout and who can no longer even use his hands to count, aggressively demands his tiny monthly interest. He forces his agents to collect his pennies even while he is suffering in the middle of a painful disease.

Suppose you brought me all the money from every mine we are currently digging into. Suppose you brought me all the buried treasure where greedy people have hidden their wealth back in the dirt, making us wish it had never been dug up in the first place. Even if you piled all that money together, I still would not think it was worth causing a single wrinkle on the forehead of a good person. The things that make us cry are truly ridiculous!
