[Chapter 31]

As I mentioned before, there are two situations that cause anger. The first is when we think we have been injured, which we have already talked about enough. The second is when we think we have been treated unjustly. We need to talk about this now. People feel something is unjust either because they think they do not deserve to suffer it, or because they did not expect it at all. When something completely unexpected happens, we feel it is beneath our dignity. As a result, we get especially worked up when things go against our hopes and expectations. This is why we get irritated over the tiniest problems in our own homes, and why we call our friend's simple carelessness a deliberate attack.

Someone might ask, "Then why do we get angry at the harm our enemies do to us?" It is because we did not expect those specific injuries, or at least not on such a large scale. This comes from excessive self-love. We think we are so special that not even our enemies should be able to hurt us. Everyone hides the mind of a tyrant in their chest—we are eager to commit terrible acts, but we refuse to endure them ourselves. Therefore, what makes us angry is either our ignorance or our arrogance. It is ignorance of basic, everyday facts. There is absolutely nothing surprising about bad people doing bad things. There is nothing new about an enemy hurting you, a friend arguing with you, a son going off the rails, or a servant making a mistake. The great Roman general Fabius used to say that the most shameful excuse a commander could make was, "I did not think that would happen." I believe this is the most shameful excuse any human being can make. Think about everything, and expect everything. Even in people with excellent character, something strange will occasionally pop up. Human nature creates minds that are disloyal, ungrateful, greedy, and wicked. When you wonder about one person's morals, think about the morals of the entire human race.

Be especially on guard when you are having a wonderful time. When everything looks perfectly peaceful, remember that disaster has not disappeared; it is only sleeping. Always assume that something will happen to offend you. A wise sailor never spreads all his sails with so much confidence that he forgets to keep the ropes ready to quickly pull them back in. Most importantly, think about how disgusting and hateful it is to have the power to do harm, and how unnatural it is for humans. Thanks to human kindness, even fierce animals can be tamed. Look at how a bull puts his neck into a yoke, how an elephant lets children and women dance safely on its back, how a snake slides harmlessly over our chests or among our drinking cups, how bears and lions quietly let people touch their jaws inside their dens, and how wild beasts wag their tails at their masters. We should be deeply ashamed that we have traded habits with wild animals.

It is a crime to hurt your own country. Therefore, it is also a crime to hurt any person from your country, because they are a part of it. If the whole country is sacred, its parts must be sacred, too. Therefore, it is a crime to hurt any human being, because they are your fellow citizen in a much larger nation—the human race. It would be ridiculous if your hand decided to hurt your foot, or your eye decided to hurt your hand. Just as all the parts of the body work together because keeping each part safe protects the whole body, humans must take care of one another. We were born to live together in society. However, the bond of society cannot survive unless it protects and loves all of its members. We would not even kill poisonous vipers or dangerous predators if we could easily tame them like other animals or stop them from being a threat to us. Therefore, we should not hurt someone just because they have already done wrong. We should only punish them to prevent them from doing wrong again. Our punishment should always focus on the future, never on the past, because it is given to prevent harm, not out of anger. If we tried to punish every single person who had a twisted or bad personality, no one would escape punishment.
