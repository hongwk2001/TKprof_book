[Chapter 9]
People who are easily angered should not get involved in tasks that are too serious, or at least they should stop before they get completely exhausted. Their minds should not be tied down to frustrating problems, but handed over to arts that bring joy. Soften your mind by reading poetry, and find interest in legendary history. Treat your mind to luxurious and elegant things. Pythagoras used to play the lyre to calm his anxious mind. Everyone knows that while the sounds of trumpets and bugles excite people, some music acts like a lullaby and soothes the mind. Just as green is good for tired eyes, and some colors comfort weak vision while the brightness of others causes pain, pleasant activities soothe an unhealthy mind.

We must avoid courtrooms, legal arguments, verdicts, and anything else that makes our flaws worse. We must also avoid physical exhaustion. Exhaustion drains away everything quiet and gentle within us and stirs up bitterness. For this reason, people who cannot trust their digestion always calm their stomach with food before they try to handle important business. Anger is especially provoked by exhaustion. This happens either because exhaustion pulls our vital heat toward the center of the body, spoiling the blood and blocking the veins, which disrupts circulation, or because a tired, weak body negatively affects the mind. This is exactly why people who are sick or weakened by old age get angry more easily than others.

For the same reason, hunger and thirst must be avoided. They irritate and provoke people's minds. There is an old saying that a tired person is quick to argue. The same is true for someone who is hungry, thirsty, or suffering for any reason at all. Just as a wound hurts at the slightest touch, and later even the fear of being touched causes pain, an unhealthy mind is offended by the smallest things. That is why some people explode in anger over a simple greeting, a letter, a speech, or even a single question.
