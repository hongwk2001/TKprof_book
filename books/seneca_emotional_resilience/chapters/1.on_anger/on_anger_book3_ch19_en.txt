[Chapter 19]
Some people might think we are wandering off-topic, but it is actually highly relevant for us to understand just how arrogantly his cruelty was displayed. This digression is directly tied to the abnormal explosions of anger. He beat senators with a whip. He did this so often that people could just shrug and say, "That is just how it is." He tortured them with ropes, a boot-shaped torture device, the rack, fire, and the most terrifying torture device of all—looking at his own face.

Even for this, we could answer: "Tearing three senators to pieces with whips and fire like they were criminal slaves was not a big deal for a man who wanted to massacre the entire senate. He always wished the Roman people only had one neck, so he could focus all the evil deeds he committed across different places and times into a single, fatal blow in one day."

A midnight execution was completely unheard of. Highway robbers look for the cover of darkness, but an execution carries much more power as an example and a lesson when it is done in public. Here, I might face this objection: "The thing that surprises you so much was just a daily habit for that monster. This is what he lived for, and this is why he stayed awake all night."

You will probably never find another person, besides him, who ordered sponges to be tightly stuffed into the mouths of everyone he sentenced to death, so they couldn't even make a sound. No one has ever forbidden a dying person to groan. He was afraid that during their final moments of agony, a voice might break free and say something he did not want to hear. Furthermore, he knew he had committed countless crimes that no one would dare criticize him for unless they were already dying.

When he couldn't find sponges, he ordered his men to tear the miserable victims' clothes and stuff the rags into their mouths. This was an unbelievably brutal act. You must let a person take their last breath and give their soul a way out. The soul should not be forced to escape through the wounds in the body.

It is exhausting to add that he sent centurions to the homes of the men executed that night to finish off their fathers as well. In his mind, he was being compassionate by freeing them from their grief. My goal here is not to describe the cruelty of Gaius Caesar, but to describe the cruelty of anger. Anger goes far beyond raging at an individual; it tears apart entire nations, and even lashes out at cities, rivers, and lifeless objects that can't even feel pain.
