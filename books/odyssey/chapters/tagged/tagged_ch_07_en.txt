Book 7

Odysseus Welcomed at King Alcinous's Palace

Odysseus waited and prayed. Meanwhile, the young princess drove her chariot towards the town. When she reached her father's house, she stopped at the main gate. Her brothers, handsome as gods, gathered around her. They unhitched the mules from the wagon and carried the clothes inside. The princess went to her own room. There, an old servant named Eurymedusa, from Apeira, was lighting a fire for her. This old woman had been brought across the sea from Apeira, given as a prize to Alcinous, the king of the Phaeacians. His people obeyed him as if he were a god. Eurymedusa had been Nausicaa's nurse. She had just lit the fire and brought supper to Nausicaa's room.

Soon after, Odysseus rose to head towards the town. The goddess Athena wrapped him in a thick mist. This was to prevent any proud Phaeacians he might meet from being rude or asking who he was. Just as Odysseus was about to enter the town, Athena approached him, disguised as a young girl carrying a water pitcher. She stood directly in front of him, and Odysseus spoke.

<odysseus>"My dear,"</odysseus> he asked, <odysseus>"could you please show me the way to King Alcinous's house? I am an unfortunate stranger, in great distress. I know no one in this town or country."</odysseus>

Athena replied, <others>"Yes, honored stranger, I will show you the house you're looking for. King Alcinous lives very close to my own father's home. I will lead the way. But as we go, don't say a word, and don't look at anyone or ask them questions. The people here don't like strangers, and they aren't fond of those from other places. They are a seafaring folk, and thanks to Poseidon's favor, their ships glide across the sea as swiftly as thought, or like birds flying through the air."</others>

She led the way, and Odysseus followed her steps. But as he passed through the heart of the city, not a single Phaeacian could see him. The great goddess Athena, in her kindness, had hidden him in a thick cloud of darkness. Odysseus marveled at their harbors, their ships, the bustling public squares, and the city's towering walls, which looked even grander with their sturdy palisades on top. When they reached the king's house, Athena spoke.

<others>"This is the house, honored stranger, that you asked me to show you. Inside, you'll find many important people seated at the table. But don't be afraid. Don't hesitate; go straight in. Even for a stranger, the bolder a person is, the more likely they are to achieve their goal. First, find the queen. Her name is Arete, and she comes from the same family as her husband, King Alcinous. Both of them are descended from Poseidon, who fathered Nausithous with the beautiful woman Periboea. Periboea was the youngest daughter of Eurymedon, who once ruled over the giants. But Eurymedon brought ruin upon his ill-fated people and lost his own life in the process.</others>

<others>"However, Poseidon lay with Periboea, and she bore him the great Nausithous, who then ruled the Phaeacians. Nausithous had two sons, Rhexenor and Alcinous. The god Apollo killed Rhexenor while he was still a newlywed and had no sons. But Rhexenor had a daughter, Arete, whom Alcinous married and took as his wife. Alcinous honors Arete more than any other woman who shares a home with her husband.</others>

<others>"She was, and still is, respected beyond measure by her children, by King Alcinous himself, and by all the people. The people regard her as a goddess, and they greet her whenever she walks through the city. This is because she is a truly excellent woman, both in mind and heart. If any women are her friends, she even helps their husbands settle disputes. If you can win her favor, you can have every hope of seeing your friends again and returning safely to your homeland."</others>

Athena then left Scheria and crossed the sea. She went to Marathon and the wide streets of Athens, entering the dwelling of Erechtheus. But Odysseus continued towards King Alcinous's house. He paused for a moment before reaching the bronze threshold, lost in deep thought. The palace's splendor was dazzling, like the sun or the moon. The walls on both sides were made of bronze from end to end, and the cornice gleamed with blue enamel. The doors were gold, hanging on silver pillars that rose from a bronze floor. The lintel above the door was silver, and the door handle was crafted from gold.

On either side of the palace stood fierce guard dogs, made of gold and silver. Hephaestus, with his unmatched skill, had specially crafted them to protect King Alcinous's palace. These dogs were immortal and would never grow old. Along the walls, from one end to the other, were rows of chairs covered with finely woven fabrics made by the women of the house. Here, the most important Phaeacians would sit to eat and drink, for there was always an abundance of food and drink, no matter the season. Golden statues of young men, holding torches, stood on pedestals, illuminating those seated at the tables by night. The house had fifty maidservants. Some were grinding rich yellow grain at the mill, while others worked at looms or sat spinning thread. Their shuttles flew back and forth like trembling aspen leaves, and the linen they wove was so tightly knit that it could repel oil. Just as the Phaeacians were the finest sailors in the world, their women surpassed all others in weaving skill. This was because Athena had taught them all sorts of useful arts, and they were exceptionally clever.

Outside the gate of the outer courtyard lay a large garden, about four acres in size, enclosed by a wall on all sides. It was filled with beautiful trees: pears, pomegranates, and the most delicious apples. There were also luscious figs and olive trees growing abundantly. The fruits never rotted or withered throughout the entire year, neither in winter nor summer. The air was so mild that new crops ripened before the old ones had even fallen. Pear grew on pear, apple on apple, and fig on fig, continuously bearing fruit. The excellent vineyard was just as remarkable. In one flat part of the vineyard, grapes were being made into raisins, while in another, the harvest was in full swing. Some grapes were being pressed in wine vats, others had just shed their blossoms and were beginning to form fruit, and still others were just changing color. In the deepest part of the garden were beautifully arranged flowerbeds, blooming all year long. Two streams flowed through the garden. One was channeled throughout the entire garden, while the other ran underground beneath the outer courtyard, leading to the house itself, where the townspeople drew water. Such were the astonishing splendors with which the gods had blessed King Alcinous's house.

Odysseus stood there for a while, taking in his surroundings. After he had looked long enough, he crossed the threshold and entered the house. Inside, he saw the most important Phaeacians performing their drink offerings to Hermes. They always did this just before going to bed for the night. Still hidden by the cloak of darkness Athena had wrapped around him, Odysseus walked straight across the courtyard towards Queen Arete and King Alcinous. He then placed his hands upon the queen's knees. At that very moment, the miraculous darkness lifted from him, revealing his presence. Everyone was stunned into silence, astonished to see a man standing there, but Odysseus immediately began his plea.

<odysseus>"Queen Arete, daughter of great Rhexenor!"</odysseus> Odysseus cried out. <odysseus>"In my distress, I humbly beg you, and your husband, and all these guests here. May the heavens grant you all long life and happiness, may you pass your wealth to your children, and enjoy all the honors bestowed upon you by the state. Please help me return to my homeland as quickly as possible. I have suffered for a long time and have been separated from my friends."</odysseus>

Odysseus then sat down on the hearth, among the ashes. Everyone fell silent. After a moment, the old hero Echeneus, a skilled speaker and an elder among the Phaeacians, spoke to them plainly and sincerely.

Echeneus addressed King Alcinous. <others>"King Alcinous, it is not right for a stranger to be sitting among the ashes of your hearth. Everyone is waiting to hear what you will say. Tell him to rise and take a seat on a silver-inlaid chair. Order your servants to mix wine and water so we can make a drink offering to Zeus, the god of thunder. Zeus protects all good-hearted suppliants. And have the housekeeper give him supper, whatever food there is in the house."</others>

Hearing this, King Alcinous took Odysseus by the hand and raised him from the hearth. He then told Odysseus to sit in the place of Laodamas, his favorite son, who had been sitting beside him. A maidservant brought water in a beautiful golden pitcher and poured it into a silver basin for Odysseus to wash his hands. She then placed a clean table next to him. A senior servant brought him bread and offered him many delicious dishes from what was in the house, and Odysseus ate and drank. Then King Alcinous spoke to one of his servants. <others>"Pontonous, mix the wine and pass it around. Let us make drink offerings to Zeus, the god of thunder, who protects all good-hearted suppliants."</others>

Pontonous mixed the wine and water, then passed the cups around after everyone had made their drink offering. After they had made their offerings and drunk as much as they wished, King Alcinous spoke.

<others>"Elders and councilors of Phaeacia, listen to my words. You have finished your supper, so now go home to bed. Tomorrow morning, I will invite even more elders, and we will hold a sacrificial banquet in honor of our guest. Then we will discuss the matter of his escort and decide how we can send him back to his homeland immediately and joyfully, without any trouble or inconvenience, no matter how distant it may be. We must ensure he comes to no harm on his journey home. However, once he is home, he will have to accept the fate he was born with, for better or worse, just like everyone else. It is possible, though, that this stranger is one of the immortal gods who has come down from heaven to visit us. If so, the gods are departing from their usual custom. Up until now, whenever we offered them hecatombs, they have always revealed themselves clearly. They would come and sit at our feasts, just like one of us. And if any solitary traveler happened to meet one of them, they never hid themselves. This is because we are as closely related to the gods as the Cyclopes and the savage giants."</others>

Odysseus said, <odysseus>"Please, King Alcinous, don't imagine such a thing. I am not immortal, not in body or spirit. In fact, I am more like the most suffering among you. If I told you all the hardships the gods have put me through, you'd probably think I'm even more miserable than anyone here. Still, I must eat, even in my sorrow. A hungry stomach is a truly stubborn thing. It demands a man's attention, no matter how terrible his pain. I am in great trouble, yet my stomach insists I eat and drink. It commands me to forget my sorrows and focus only on filling it. As for yourselves, please do as you've promised and help me return home at daybreak. If I could just see my home, my wealth, my servants, and all the glory of my house one more time, I would die a happy man."</odysseus>

Odysseus finished speaking, and everyone agreed with his words. They all said he had spoken so wisely, and that he deserved their escort. They poured out libations, and after drinking as much as they wished, they all went home to their beds. Odysseus remained in the hall with Queen Arete and King Alcinous while the servants cleared away the supper dishes. Queen Arete spoke first. She recognized the fine shirt, cloak, and clothes Odysseus wore as her own handiwork, made by her and her maids. <others>"Stranger,"</others> she said, <others>"before we go any further, I have a question for you. Who are you, and where do you come from? Who gave you these clothes? Didn't you say you came from across the sea?"</others>

Odysseus answered, <odysseus>"Madam, it would take too long to tell you my whole sad story, for the gods have been very harsh with me. But as for your question, there is an island far away in the sea called Ogygia. There lives the clever and powerful goddess Calypso, daughter of the Titan Atlas. She lives alone, far from any other gods or mortals. But fate, in its cruel way, led me, desolate and alone, to her home. Zeus struck my ship with his thunderbolts, shattering it in the middle of the vast ocean. All my brave comrades drowned, every single one. But I clung to the ship's keel and drifted for nine days. Finally, on the tenth night, in the darkness, the gods brought me to Ogygia, where the great goddess Calypso lives. She welcomed me and cared for me with great kindness. She even wanted to make me immortal so I would never grow old, but I couldn't bring myself to agree."</odysseus>

<odysseus>"I stayed with Calypso for seven long years, soaking the fine clothes she gave me with my tears the entire time. But at last, when the eighth year came, she finally agreed to let me go. Whether it was Zeus's command or simply a change of heart, I don't know. She sent me from her island on a raft, stocked with plenty of bread and wine. She also gave me strong, warm clothes and sent a wind that blew both warm and fair. I sailed for seventeen days across the sea. On the eighteenth day, I finally saw the first faint outlines of the mountains on your coast. I was overjoyed! Nevertheless, much trouble still awaited me. At that point, Poseidon, the sea god, would not let me continue. He unleashed a terrible storm against me. The waves were so monstrous that I couldn't hold onto my raft any longer. The furious gale tore it to pieces, and I had no choice but to swim, until the wind and currents finally carried me to your shores."</odysseus>

<odysseus>"There I tried to land, but I couldn't. It was a treacherous spot where the waves crashed me against the rocks. So I took to the sea again and swam on until I came to a river. It seemed the most likely place to land, as there were no rocks, and it was sheltered from the wind. Here, I finally got out of the water and regained my composure. Night was coming on, so I left the river and went into a thicket. I covered myself all over with leaves, and soon the gods sent me into a deep, healing sleep. Exhausted and in pain, I slept among the leaves all night and through the next day, waking only in the late afternoon as the sun began to set. On the beach, I saw your daughter's maidservants playing, and among them, your daughter, looking like a goddess herself. I begged her for help, and she showed a truly excellent nature, far beyond what one might expect from someone so young, as young people are often thoughtless. She gave me plenty of bread and wine, and after she had me wash in the river, she also gave me the clothes you see me wearing now. So, though it pains me to relive it, I have told you the whole truth."</odysseus>

King Alcinous said, <others>"Stranger, my daughter was quite wrong not to bring you straight to my house with her maids, especially since you asked her for help first."</others>

Odysseus replied, <odysseus>"Please, do not scold her. It's not her fault. She certainly told me to come with her and the maids. But I was ashamed and afraid. I worried you might be displeased if you saw me in such a state. All people can be a little suspicious and sensitive at times."</odysseus>

<others>"Stranger,"</others> King Alcinous replied, <others>"I am not a man who gets angry over trifles; it's always best to be reasonable. But by Father Zeus, Athena, and Apollo, now that I understand what kind of man you are, and how much our thoughts align, I wish you would stay here, marry my daughter, and become my son-in-law. If you choose to stay, I will give you a house and an estate. But no one, may the gods forbid it, will keep you here against your will. To assure you of this, I will arrange your escort tomorrow. You can sleep soundly for the entire journey if you wish. My sailors will carry you over calm waters, either to your own home or wherever else you desire, even if it's much further than Euboea. My people who took golden-haired Rhadamanthus to see Tityus, son of Gaia, say Euboea is the furthest place in the world. Yet, they completed the entire voyage there and back in a single day, without any trouble. This shows you how much my ships surpass all others, and what magnificent sailors my men are."</others>

Odysseus was filled with joy and prayed aloud: <odysseus>"Father Zeus, grant that King Alcinous may fulfill all he has promised. For then he will earn an everlasting name among mortals, and I, too, will finally return to my homeland."</odysseus>

Their conversation ended. Then Queen Arete told her maids to prepare a bed in the gatehouse room. She told them to lay down soft red rugs and spread warm woolen cloaks for Odysseus to sleep under. The maids went out, carrying torches. Once the bed was ready, they approached Odysseus and said, <others>"Rise, sir stranger, and come with us. Your bed is prepared."</others> Odysseus was more than happy to go to his rest.

So Odysseus slept in a bed placed in a room above the grand gateway. King Alcinous, meanwhile, went to his own chamber deep within the palace, with his queen by his side.
