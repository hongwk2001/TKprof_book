Chapter 22
Concerning the Secretaries of Princes

Choosing the right ministers is extremely important for a prince. Their quality depends entirely on the prince's good judgment. The very first impression people form of a ruler and his intelligence comes from observing the men he surrounds himself with. If they are capable and loyal, the prince is always considered wise, because he knew how to identify talent and keep it faithful. But if they are incompetent or disloyal, people will judge the prince poorly. His first major mistake was choosing them.

Anyone who knew Antonio da Venafro as the minister for Pandolfo Petrucci, the Prince of Siena, immediately considered Pandolfo to be a highly intelligent man just for hiring him. There are three types of human intellect. The first type understands things independently. The second type understands things when they are explained by others. The third type understands neither by itself nor through others. The first kind is excellent, the second is good, and the third is entirely useless. Therefore, even if Pandolfo did not have the first type of intellect, he certainly had the second. Whenever a ruler has the judgment to distinguish the good and bad in what others say and do, he can evaluate his minister's actions. Even if he lacks originality himself, he can still praise the good and correct the bad. This way, the minister knows he cannot deceive his prince, and he is forced to stay honest.

How can a prince judge a minister? There is one test that never fails. If you see a minister thinking more about his own interests than yours, and constantly looking for his own personal profit in everything he does, that man will never be a good minister. You will never be able to trust him. A man who holds another person's state in his hands must never think of himself. He must always think of his prince, and he must never pay attention to matters that do not concern the prince.

On the other hand, the prince must also do his part to keep his minister honest. He should reward him, honor him, enrich him, and treat him with kindness. The prince should share the honors and the heavy responsibilities of ruling with him. At the same time, the prince must make it clear that the minister cannot stand alone without the prince. By doing this, the minister's abundance of honors and wealth will stop him from desiring more. And his many responsibilities will make him fear any sudden political changes. When princes and ministers treat each other this way, they can trust each other completely. When it is any other way, the result will always be disastrous for one or the other.
