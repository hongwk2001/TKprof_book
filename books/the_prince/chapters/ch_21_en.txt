Chapter 21
How A Prince Should Conduct Himself So As To Gain Renown

Nothing earns a prince as much respect as great achievements and setting a remarkable example. Consider Ferdinand of Aragon, the current King of Spain. He can practically be called a new prince, because he rose from being an insignificant king to becoming the most prominent monarch in Christendom through fame and glory. If you examine his actions, you will find them all great, and some of them truly extraordinary. At the start of his reign, he attacked Granada. This campaign became the foundation of his empire. He launched it quietly at first, without fear of interference. He kept the minds of the Castilian barons occupied with the war, preventing them from anticipating any sudden political changes. As a result, they didn't even realize he was slowly gaining power and authority over them. Using money from the Church and the people, he was able to maintain his armies. That long war laid the groundwork for the military expertise that has distinguished his rule ever since. Furthermore, he consistently used religion as an excuse to undertake even more ambitious schemes. With a kind of pious cruelty, he dedicated himself to driving the Moors out of his kingdom and cleansing his lands of them. There could not be a more admirable or rare example of this strategy. Using that same religious cloak, he invaded Africa, descended upon Italy, and has now finally attacked France. His achievements and designs have always been massive. They have kept his people in a constant state of suspense, admiration, and distraction. Because his campaigns rolled out one after another in rapid succession, his enemies were never given the time or space to organize a steady resistance against him.

It also greatly helps a prince to set unusual examples in domestic affairs, much like the stories told of Messer Bernabò of Milan. Whenever someone in civil life did something extraordinary, whether good or bad, Bernabò would find a highly public way to reward or punish them. Above all else, a prince should always strive in every action to build a reputation as a great and remarkable man.

A prince is also highly respected when he is either a true friend or a straightforward enemy. This means declaring yourself purely in favor of one side against another, without any hesitation or reservation. This approach will always be more beneficial than remaining neutral. If two of your powerful neighbors go to war, they are either strong enough that you must fear the winner, or they are not. In either case, it is always to your advantage to pick a side and fight fiercely. If you refuse to declare yourself in the first scenario, you will inevitably become the prey of the winner. The defeated side will be pleased and satisfied by your ruin, and you will have no excuses to offer, and no one to protect or shelter you. The winner does not want unreliable friends who refuse to help in difficult times. The loser will not harbor you, because you were unwilling to draw your sword and share his fate.

Antiochus entered Greece after being invited by the Aetolians to drive out the Romans. He sent envoys to the Achaeans, who were friends of the Romans, urging them to stay neutral. The Romans, however, pressured the Achaeans to take up arms. When the Achaean council debated the issue, Antiochus's ambassador advised them to remain neutral. The Roman ambassador replied: "As for the claim that it is better and more advantageous for your state not to interfere in our war, nothing could be more wrong. By not interfering, you will be left without favor or consideration, entirely at the mercy of the conqueror." It will always be the case that your enemies will demand your neutrality, while your friends will beg you to take up arms and declare your support. Irresolute princes usually choose the path of neutrality to avoid immediate danger, and they are almost always ruined as a result. But if a prince boldly declares himself for one side, and his ally wins, the victor is indebted to him. Even if the victor is powerful and has the prince at his mercy, a bond of friendship has been established. Men are rarely so shameless as to show monumental ingratitude by turning on an ally. Besides, victories are never so absolute that the winner can ignore all standards of justice. On the other hand, if your ally loses, he may still shelter you. As long as he is able, he will help you, and you become partners in a fortune that might rise again.

In the second scenario, where the warring parties are not strong enough to threaten you, it is even smarter to take a side. By doing so, you are helping to destroy one party with the help of another, when that second party should have been trying to save the first if they had any sense. When your ally inevitably wins with your help, he remains entirely at your mercy. Here, it is crucial to note that a prince must never ally with someone more powerful than himself for the purpose of attacking others, unless absolutely necessary. If you win, you are entirely at the mercy of your more powerful ally, and princes must do everything they can to avoid being at the mercy of anyone. The Venetians allied with France against the Duke of Milan. This alliance caused their ruin, and it could easily have been avoided. But when such an alliance cannot be avoided, as happened to the Florentines when the Pope and Spain sent armies to attack Lombardy, the prince must choose a side for the reasons explained above.

No government should ever imagine that it can always choose perfectly safe policies. Instead, you must expect to face highly uncertain options. In ordinary affairs, trying to avoid one problem usually means running straight into another. True prudence consists of knowing how to evaluate the nature of these problems and choosing the lesser evil.

A prince should also present himself as a patron of talent, honoring those who excel in any profession. At the same time, he must encourage his citizens to practice their trades in peace, whether in commerce, agriculture, or any other field. A man should not hesitate to improve his property for fear of it being confiscated, and another should not be afraid to open a business for fear of excessive taxes. Instead, the prince should offer rewards to anyone willing to take on these ventures, or to anyone who seeks to enrich his city or state in any way.

Finally, a prince should entertain his people with festivals and public spectacles at appropriate times of the year. Since every city is divided into guilds or neighborhoods, he should show respect to these groups, attend their gatherings occasionally, and serve as an example of courtesy and generosity. However, in doing so, he must always maintain the absolute majesty and dignity of his position. He can never allow his status to be diminished in any way.
