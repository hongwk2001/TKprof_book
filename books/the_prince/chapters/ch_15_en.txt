Chapter 15
Concerning Things For Which Men, And Especially Princes, Are Praised Or Blamed


Now we must look at the rules of conduct a prince should follow with his subjects and friends. I know many people have already written about this. Because of that, I expect I will be called arrogant for bringing it up again, especially since my approach will be completely different from everyone else's. However, my goal is to write something genuinely useful for anyone who understands it. Because of this, I think it is better to focus on the real truth of the matter, rather than imaginary ideas. Many people have dreamed up republics and principalities that have never actually existed in the real world. The way people actually live is so far removed from the way they ought to live. Therefore, anyone who ignores reality in favor of some ideal will only bring about their own destruction. A man who tries to be good all the time is bound to be ruined among the great number of people who are not good.

Therefore, if a prince wants to keep his power, he must learn how not to be good. He must use this knowledge, or not use it, exactly as necessity requires. Let us set aside all these imaginary ideas about a prince and discuss what is real. Whenever men are discussed, and especially princes because they sit higher up, they are noted for specific qualities that bring them either praise or blame. One is considered generous, while another is called miserly. In our Tuscan language, a greedy person wants to take things by robbery, while a miserly person simply refuses to spend his own money. One man is known to be a giver, another a taker. One is cruel, another is compassionate. One breaks his promises, another keeps his word. One is weak and cowardly, another is fierce and brave. One is friendly, another is arrogant. One is lustful, another is pure. One is honest, another is sneaky. One is hard, another is easygoing. One is serious, another is silly. One is religious, another is an unbeliever, and so on. I know everyone will admit that it would be highly praised if a prince had all of these good qualities. However, human nature simply does not allow a person to have them all, or to practice them perfectly. Therefore, a prince must be smart enough to avoid the bad reputation of those vices that would cost him his state. If possible, he should also avoid the lesser vices that would not cost him his power. But if he cannot avoid those lesser vices, he can indulge in them with less worry. Furthermore, he should not lose any sleep over getting a bad reputation for the vices that are absolutely necessary to save his state. If you look at everything carefully, you will find that some things that look like virtue will actually ruin you. On the other hand, some things that look like vice will bring you safety and prosperity if you follow through with them.
