Chapter 5
The Five Laws of Gold

Arkad sat in his courtyard, reviewing a map of the trade routes that stretched north from Babylon toward Damascus. When Tarkad entered, Arkad rolled up the papyrus and gestured for the young man to sit. 

"Tarkad," Arkad said, looking at him with approval. "You have done well. Mathon told me how you refused to lend your savings for the risky shield deal. You have proven that you can save gold, and you have proven that you can guard it from loss. 

But saving is only the first step. To become a true merchant of Babylon, you must learn how to make your gold grow in the wider world. The time has come to test you."

Tarkad felt a mix of excitement and anxiety. "How will you test me, Arkad?"

"Next week, my largest trade caravan departs for Damascus," Arkad explained. "It carries fine linen, wool, and olive oil. I want you to accompany the caravan. I am placing a small portion of the cargo under your direct management. I will also give you a strong horse and a bag of gold to trade on your own account. If you succeed, you will keep a share of the profits."

Before Tarkad could speak, Arkad reached onto the table and picked up a heavy object wrapped in a faded blue silk cloth. He carefully unwrapped it, revealing a rectangular clay tablet inscribed with neat rows of cuneiform writing. 

"This," Arkad said, placing the tablet in Tarkad’s hands, "is the most valuable thing I own. It is not gold, but it is the key to all the gold you will ever desire. It contains the Five Laws of Gold. 

I did not write these laws. They were taught to me by Algamish, and they have guided my entire life. Years ago, when my own son Nomasir came of age, I wanted to test him before letting him inherit my estate. I gave him a bag of gold and this very tablet, and I sent him north to the city of Nineveh. 

Nomasir was young and foolish, much like you were when you first arrived in Babylon. He ignored the tablet. He met men who promised him quick riches through horse racing. He fell for their lies and lost most of his gold. Then, desperate to recover his losses, he invested his remaining silver in a shop selling cheap pottery, which quickly failed because he did not understand the trade. 

Within a few months, Nomasir had sold his extra clothes and his slave just to buy bread. He was starving, shivering in the streets of Nineveh. 

It was only then, at his lowest point, that he remembered this tablet. He unwrapped the silk, read the Five Laws, and resolved to follow them. 

He found a job managing a crew of workers who were building the new outer wall of Nineveh. He saved his coppers. He sought the advice of the chief builder on how to invest. Over the next ten years, he used the Five Laws to build a massive fortune. When he finally returned to Babylon, he presented me with three bags of gold in place of the one I had given him. He had proven that wisdom is worth far more than gold."

Arkad pointed to the lines of writing on the clay. 

"Listen closely, Tarkad. These are the Five Laws of Gold:

The First Law: Gold comes gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who puts aside no less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that of his family.

The Second Law: Gold works diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.

The Third Law: Gold clings to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.

The Fourth Law: Gold slips away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar, or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.

The Fifth Law: Gold flees from the man who forces it to impossible earnings, or who follows the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers, or who trusts it to his own inexperienced and romantic desires in investment."

Arkad wrapped the tablet back in the blue silk and handed it to Tarkad. 

"Take this with you on the trail to Damascus. Keep it close. On your journey, you will meet men who will try to trick you. You will be tempted by schemes that promise easy wealth. When that happens, read these laws. 

If you respect them, you will return to Babylon with a full purse and a proud heart. If you ignore them, the desert sands will swallow your gold, your horse, and your future. Do we have an agreement, Tarkad?"

Tarkad held the heavy tablet against his chest. He looked into Arkad's eyes and felt the weight of the trust being placed in him. 

"We have an agreement, Arkad," Tarkad said. "I will bring this tablet back to you, and I will bring the gold to prove I followed it."

"Good," Arkad said, standing up. "Prepare your gear. The caravan leaves at sunrise on the third day."
