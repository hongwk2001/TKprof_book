Chapter 9
The Legacy of Babylon

The sun was setting over Babylon, casting a warm, golden glow across the sprawling city. 

High upon the rooftop terrace of Arkad's estate, the old man sat in a comfortable chair, wrapped in a soft woolen blanket. His hair was now completely white, and his hands were thin and frail, but his eyes remained as bright and clear as they had been decades ago. 

Standing beside him, looking out over the busy harbor and the grand ziggurats, was Tarkad. 

"The years have flown by like the desert wind, Tarkad," Arkad said, his voice soft but steady. "It seems like only yesterday you were standing in the shadows of Mathon's shop, begging for three silver pieces."

Tarkad smiled, a deep feeling of gratitude in his heart. "If you had given me those three silver pieces, Arkad, I would have eaten for a day and remained a beggar. Instead, you gave me a shovel, a seat in the Temple, and a clay tablet. You gave me a future."

Arkad nodded, looking at the city. "You have used the wisdom well. You cleared your debts, you built your caravans, and today, you are a pillar of Babylon’s trade. But more importantly, you have passed the wisdom on. Mathon told me how you took young Hadan Gula into your warehouses to teach him how to work."

"He is working hard, Arkad," Tarkad said. "He has his grandfather's strength in him. He only needed a path."

"That is the true secret of Babylon," Arkad said, gesturing to the city below. "Look at our canals, our markets, our great brass gates. Why are we the wealthiest city in the world? It is not because of the gold in the King's treasury. It is because our citizens know how to build and preserve wealth. 

If only a few men hold all the gold, a city is poor, for the rest of the people have nothing to spend. But when the weaver, the potter, and the builder know the laws of gold, wealth flows through every street like water. A city's prosperity is only as strong as the financial wisdom of its humblest citizen."

Arkad reached onto the table beside him and picked up the heavy clay tablet, wrapped in its faded blue silk cloth. He placed it in Tarkad's hands. 

"I am old, Tarkad, and my time is drawing to a close. I am passing this scribe’s legacy to you. Keep these tablets safe. Teach the seven cures to the young. Write the five laws on the hearts of the willing. Let the world know that the rules of money do not change with the kings or the seasons."

Tarkad held the heavy tablet against his chest, just as he had on the trail to Damascus so many years ago. 

"I will protect them, Arkad," Tarkad promised. "And I will teach them to anyone who has the determination to find the way."

"Then I am content," Arkad said, closing his eyes with a peaceful smile. "Go now, Tarkad. Go build your caravans. The future belongs to those who work, save, and grow."

Tarkad bowed respectfully to his mentor and walked down the stone stairs of the estate. 

As he stepped out into the streets, the bells of the evening caravan began to ring, their clear notes echoing through the city. Tarkad walked with a firm, confident stride, ready to meet the tomorrow he had built. 

And high above, the golden city of Babylon stood strong, a monument to the timeless wisdom of the men who walked its streets.
