Chapter 10

I spent the next day exploring the valley. I stood near a massive glacier that was slowly moving down the mountain. The icy walls towered over me, and the solemn silence was only broken by crashing waves, falling rocks, or the thunder of an avalanche. These magnificent scenes comforted me. They didn't erase my grief, but they calmed my mind. I went to sleep that night feeling peaceful, dreaming of snowy peaks and soaring eagles.

But the next morning, the peace was gone. It was pouring rain, and thick fog hid the mountains. My dark, depressing thoughts returned. I decided I didn't care about the rain, so I took my mule and started climbing to the top of Montanvert. I remembered how seeing the giant glacier had thrilled me when I was younger. Nature always helped me forget my troubles. I went without a guide because I wanted to be totally alone.

The path was steep, zigzagging up the side of the mountain. It was a beautiful but desolate place. I saw where winter avalanches had destroyed whole forests, snapping trees in half. As I climbed higher, stones occasionally rolled down the snowy slopes. One area was so dangerous that even speaking loudly could cause an avalanche. The dark pine trees made the scene feel serious and gloomy. Looking down into the valley, I saw thick fog rising from the rivers while the rain poured down. It made me feel incredibly sad. 

By noon, I reached the top. I sat on a rock looking out over the "sea of ice." The fog cleared, and I walked down onto the glacier. The ice was rough, with deep cracks, and it took me nearly two hours to cross it. From there, I stared at the amazing view. Mont Blanc rose above everything in terrifying majesty. My heart swelled with a strange mix of joy and sadness. "Wandering spirits," I called out to the air, "if you are real, please let me have this small bit of happiness. Or just take my life right now."

Just as I said that, I saw a figure in the distance running toward me with impossible speed. He leaped easily over the dangerous cracks in the ice. As he got closer, I saw that he was incredibly tall. I suddenly felt faint and dizzy, but the cold mountain wind woke me up. When he got closer, I realized with horror who it was. 

It was the monster I had created! 

I shook with rage and prepared to fight him to the death. He approached me. His horribly ugly face was twisted with pain, hatred, and anger. But I didn't care. I was so angry I could barely speak, but I finally yelled at him.

"Devil!" I screamed. "How dare you come near me? Aren't you afraid I will kill you? Get away from me, you vile insect! Actually, stay here so I can crush you into dust! I wish killing you could bring back the innocent people you murdered!"

"I expected you to greet me like this," the monster said calmly. "All men hate wretched creatures. So of course everyone hates me, because I am the most miserable creature alive. Even you, my creator, hate me. You want to kill me. How dare you play with life like that? Do your duty to me, and I will do my duty to you and the rest of the world. If you listen to my conditions, I will leave you alone. But if you refuse, I will kill every last one of your friends until I am satisfied."

"You horrible monster!" I yelled. "Hell is too good for you! You blame me for creating you? Come here so I can snuff out the life I gave you!"

I leaped at him, fully intending to kill him. 

He easily dodged me. "Calm down!" he said. "Listen to me before you attack. Haven't I suffered enough? You made me taller and stronger than you. I could easily destroy you. But I won't. I am your creature. I will obey you, my lord and creator, if you just do what you owe me. You are fair to everyone else, but you treat me like garbage! I should be your Adam, but instead, I am a fallen angel, cast out for doing nothing wrong. I see happiness everywhere, but I am not allowed to have any. I used to be kind and good, but misery made me a monster. Make me happy, and I will be good again."

"Get away from me!" I said. "We are enemies. Leave now, or we will fight to the death."

"How can I convince you to listen?" he asked. "I am so miserably alone. You, my creator, hate me. Humans hate me. I have to live in these freezing ice caves just to be safe from them. If humans knew I existed, they would try to kill me just like you are doing. Why shouldn't I hate the people who hate me? You have the power to save your family from a terrible fate. Just listen to my story. Even the worst criminals are allowed to speak before they are condemned. You accuse me of murder, but you want to murder me right now! Please, just listen to me. After you hear my story, you can decide to destroy me if you still want to."

"Why are you reminding me that I created you?" I said. "I curse the day I made you! I curse my own hands for building you! You have ruined my life. Get out of my sight!"

The monster covered my eyes with his huge hands. I angrily swatted them away. 

"I am taking my ugly face out of your sight," he said. "But you can still listen to me. I beg you. My story is long, and it's too cold out here for you. Come with me to a hut on the mountain. You can hear my story before the sun goes down. Then, you can decide if I should disappear forever and live a harmless life, or if I will become the monster that destroys you and your world."

He turned and started walking across the ice. I followed him. I didn't say a word, but I thought about what he had said. I was curious, and I did feel a tiny spark of pity for him. I also wanted to know for sure if he had killed my brother. And, for the first time, I realized that as his creator, I had some responsibility for him. I shouldn't just complain about his evil without trying to make him happy first. 

We crossed the ice and climbed up to a small hut. The air was freezing and it had started to rain again. The monster looked triumphant, but I felt heavy and depressed. We sat by a fire he had built, and he began to tell me his story.
