Chapter 13

"I now come to a deeply moving part of my story. The events that followed made me the miserable creature I am today.

"Spring arrived. The weather grew warm and beautiful. One day, while the old man played his guitar, Felix looked deeply sad. Suddenly, someone knocked at the door.

"A beautiful lady arrived on horseback. She wore a dark veil. When she saw Felix, she lifted her veil, revealing a stunning face with dark hair, fair skin, and bright eyes. Felix was overjoyed. His sadness vanished instantly. He kissed her hand and called her his sweet Arabian. She didn't speak his language, but she smiled happily.

"Her name was Safie. Her arrival brought immense joy to the cottage. She began learning the villagers' language, and I decided to learn alongside her. I listened carefully to every lesson from my hiding spot. Safie learned twenty words on her first day.

"By summer, my friends were always happy. Safie and I both learned the language quickly, but I learned faster because I didn't have her distractions. I also learned how to read. Felix taught Safie from a history book called 'Ruins of Empires.' Through this book, I learned about different countries, wars, religions, and human society.

"These stories confused me. Humans seemed capable of incredible goodness, but also terrible cruelty. I learned about rich and poor people, and how society judges those without money or a noble family. 

"This made me think about myself. I had no money, no property, and no family. I was horribly ugly and didn't even belong to the human race. I was stronger and taller, but completely alone. Was I a monster that everyone hated?

"This knowledge brought me agony. I wished I had stayed in the forest, knowing nothing but hunger and cold. Knowledge is strange—once it enters your mind, it never leaves. I admired the villagers' kindness, but I was shut out of their world. I was a miserable, unhappy wretch!

"I learned about families, mothers, and fathers. I heard how parents loved their babies and guided them as they grew. But where was my family? No father watched over me; no mother smiled at me. I had never seen anyone who looked like me. What was I? I answered my own question with groans of despair. 

"Yet, despite my pain, I still loved my protectors in the cottage."
