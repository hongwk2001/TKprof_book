Chapter 11

"It is hard to remember my early days. Everything was confusing. I saw, felt, heard, and smelled all at once. It took a long time to learn to tell my senses apart.
Gradually, a strong light hurt my eyes, so I closed them. Darkness covered me, and I felt scared. But when I opened my eyes, the light poured back in. I walked and found that I could move freely without bumping into things. The light and heat became too much, so I looked for shade in the forest near Ingolstadt. I rested by a stream until hunger and thirst woke me. I ate berries, drank water, and went back to sleep.

"When I woke up, it was dark and cold. I felt lonely and frightened. I covered myself with clothes, but I was still cold. I felt pain everywhere, so I sat down and cried.

"Soon, the moon rose and gave me light. I found a huge cloak under a tree and wrapped it around myself. I was confused and had no clear thoughts, but the bright moon brought me comfort.

"Days and nights passed. I learned to understand my senses better. I saw the clear stream, the trees, and the birds. I tried to sing like them, but only harsh noises came out, which frightened me into silence.

"As time went on, my mind grew clearer. I learned to tell herbs apart and enjoyed the sweet songs of birds. One day, I found an abandoned fire left by beggars. I was thrilled by the warmth but burned my hand when I touched the glowing embers. It surprised me that the same fire could bring both pleasure and pain! I soon learned how to gather dry wood to keep it burning. I even discovered that roasted food tasted much better than raw berries.

"Eventually, food became scarce. I packed up and left the forest, wrapping myself in my cloak. After three days, it snowed heavily. I found a small shepherd's hut. I went inside and saw an old man. He screamed, ran away in terror, and left his breakfast behind. I ate his bread, cheese, and milk, then fell asleep.

"The next day, I arrived at a village. It was wonderful! I went into a nice cottage, but the children screamed, and a woman fainted. The whole village attacked me with stones and weapons. I ran away in fear and hid in a low, empty wooden shed attached to a cottage. It was small, but it protected me from the snow and rain, and from the cruelty of humans.

"I was happy to have shelter. I covered the gaps with stones and wood so nobody could see me. I stole some bread and a cup to drink water. The floor was dry, and the nearby chimney kept the room warm.

"I decided to stay hidden. I looked through a small crack in the wall and saw a young, gentle girl carrying a pail of milk. A young man looked sad as he helped her carry it. Later, I looked into the cottage through the crack and saw a clean, simple room. An old man sat near a fire. He played beautiful music that brought tears to the girl's eyes. The old man smiled kindly, and I felt a strange mix of joy and sadness that I had never felt before. I stepped away, overwhelmed.

"Later, the young man returned with firewood. They shared a small meal of bread, cheese, and vegetables. They seemed so beautiful to me—the old man was loving and gentle, and the young man was graceful but deeply sad.

"When night fell, I was surprised to see them light candles. The old man played music again, and the young man read aloud. I did not yet understand words or letters. Finally, they blew out their candles and went to sleep."
