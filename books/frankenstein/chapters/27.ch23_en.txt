Chapter 23

We landed at eight o'clock. After walking along the shore for a while to enjoy the fading light, we went back to the inn. From there, we took in the beautiful scenery of the water, woods, and mountains. The darkness made it hard to see clearly, but we could still make out their dark silhouettes.

The southerly wind had died down, only to be replaced by a violent gale blowing from the west. The moon had crested in the sky and was starting to set. Clouds raced across it faster than a bird of prey, blocking out its light. The lake mirrored the chaotic sky above, looking even more turbulent as the waves began to swell. Without warning, a torrential downpour began.

I had stayed calm throughout the day, but once darkness fell, my mind was flooded with a thousand fears. I grew anxious and hyper-aware, keeping my right hand on a pistol hidden inside my coat. Every little noise terrified me. I promised myself I wouldn't go down without a fight. I wouldn't back away from the confrontation until either I or my enemy was dead.

Elizabeth watched my restless pacing in nervous silence for a while. Then, noticing a terrified look in my eyes, she began to tremble. "What's upsetting you, Victor?" she asked. "What are you so afraid of?"

"Please try to relax, my love," I replied. "Everything will be fine tonight. But this night is truly awful."

I spent the next hour in this panicked state. Then I realized how terrifying it would be for my wife to witness the fight I knew was coming. I pleaded with her to go to bed, deciding I wouldn't join her until I had tracked down my enemy.

She left the room, and I spent a while pacing the hallways. I checked every corner where my enemy could be hiding, but I found no sign of him. I started to hope that some stroke of luck had stopped him from carrying out his threats. Suddenly, a piercing, horrifying scream echoed through the house. It came from Elizabeth's bedroom. The moment I heard it, the horrible truth hit me. My arms fell to my sides, and my body froze completely. I could feel the blood rushing through my veins, tingling all the way down to my fingertips. My shock only lasted a second. When the scream rang out again, I sprinted into the room.

My God! Why didn't I just die right then? Why am I even alive to talk about the murder of the most wonderful and pure person on earth? She was lying across the bed, completely lifeless. Her head hung off the edge, and her pale, twisted face was partially hidden by her hair. Everywhere I look, I still see her—her pale arms and limp body tossed onto the bed by her killer. How could I see something like that and survive? Unfortunately, life is stubborn. It hangs on the tightest when you want it to end the most. I blacked out for just a second, collapsing onto the floor.

When I came to, I was surrounded by the people staying at the inn. They looked terrified, but their horror felt like a joke compared to the crushing grief I was experiencing. I pushed past them and went back to the room where Elizabeth's body was. My love, my wife—she had been alive just moments ago, so precious and wonderful. Someone had moved her since I last saw her. Now, lying with her head on her arm and a cloth draped over her face and neck, she looked like she was just sleeping. I ran over and hugged her tightly. But the stiff, icy coldness of her body made it clear that she was gone. The deadly bruises from the monster's hands were still visible on her neck, and she was no longer breathing.

While I was crying over her body, I happened to glance up. The windows in the room had been dark earlier, so I panicked when I noticed the pale yellow moonlight flooding in. The shutters were wide open. To my absolute horror, I saw that disgusting monster standing at the window. He was grinning and mocking me, pointing his twisted finger at my wife's body. I lunged at the window, pulled the pistol from my coat, and took a shot. But he dodged it, jumped down, and sprinted away with lightning speed before diving into the lake.

The gunshot drew a crowd into the room. I pointed to where the monster had vanished, and we chased after him in boats. We dragged nets through the water, but it was useless. After searching for hours, we gave up and turned back. Most of the men thought I had just imagined the whole thing. Once we hit land, they split into groups to search the surrounding woods and vineyards.

I tried to go with them, but I didn't get far before I started feeling incredibly dizzy. I stumbled around like a drunk before finally collapsing from sheer exhaustion. My vision blurred, and my skin burned with a fever. They had to carry me back and put me in bed. I barely knew what was going on. I just kept looking around the room, feeling like I had lost something important.

A little while later, I got out of bed. Acting purely on instinct, I dragged myself into the room where my wife's body was lying. Several women were standing around crying, and I leaned over her and wept right alongside them. My mind was completely blank, save for a random stream of disjointed thoughts. I tried to make sense of everything that had happened and what had caused it all, feeling totally overwhelmed by the shock and horror of it. William's death, Justine's execution, Clerval's murder, and now my wife. Even then, I wasn't sure if the rest of my friends and family were safe from the monster's wrath. He could be strangling my father right now, or standing over Ernest's dead body. The thought sent chills down my spine and jolted me into action. I jumped up and decided to get back to Geneva as fast as I could.

I couldn't find any horses to rent, so I had to travel by boat across the lake. The wind was against us, and it was pouring rain. Still, it was early in the morning, and I hoped to make it back by nightfall. I hired some men to row and grabbed an oar to help out. Physical labor used to help calm my mind, but my overwhelming grief and anxiety made it impossible to keep going. I dropped the oar, buried my face in my hands, and let all my dark thoughts wash over me. Whenever I looked up, I saw places that reminded me of happier times. I had just been admiring them the day before with my wife, who was now nothing more than a memory. Tears streamed down my face. The rain stopped briefly, and I watched the fish swimming in the water, just as Elizabeth and I had seen them doing a few hours earlier. There's nothing more painful than a sudden, dramatic change. Regardless of the weather, nothing would ever look the same to me again. A monster had stolen any chance I had at a happy future. No one had ever been as miserable as I was in that moment. It was a tragedy unlike anything in human history.

But why should I obsess over what happened next? My story is already full of horrors. I've reached the absolute worst part, and the rest is just exhausting to talk about. To put it simply, my friends were taken from me one by one, leaving me completely alone. I'm too tired to keep going into detail, so I'll just finish the rest of this awful story as quickly as possible.

I made it to Geneva and found my father and Ernest still alive. Unfortunately, the news I brought completely destroyed my father. I can still picture him as the wonderful, respected old man he was. His eyes went blank, losing all their warmth and joy. Elizabeth was like a daughter to him, and he loved her more than words could say. As he got older, he held on tightly to the few people he still cared about. Damn that monster for torturing my poor father and condemning him to such a miserable end! He couldn't handle the horrors that kept piling up. His body just gave out. He couldn't even get out of bed, and a few days later, he died in my arms.

What happened to me next? I don't really know. I just kind of went numb. All I could feel were chains and darkness. I would dream about walking through beautiful meadows and valleys with my old friends, only to wake up and realize I was in a dungeon. The depression hit hard after that. Over time, I started to understand my situation and everything I'd lost, and eventually, they let me out. People said I'd gone crazy, and from what I gathered, I spent several months locked in solitary confinement.

Freedom wouldn't have meant a thing to me if I hadn't realized I wanted revenge at the exact same time I got my sanity back. As I started remembering everything that went wrong, I thought about what had caused it all. I thought about the monster I created, that miserable demon I let loose on the world to destroy my life. Thinking about him made me absolutely furious. I prayed that I would finally get my hands on him so I could get the revenge he deserved.

I didn't just sit around wishing for revenge for very long. I started trying to figure out the best way to track him down. About a month after I got out, I went to see a local criminal judge. I told him I needed to report a crime, explaining that I knew who had murdered my family. I demanded that he use his power to arrest the killer.

The judge listened to me carefully and kindly. "Don't worry, sir," he told me. "I will do absolutely everything in my power to find this man."

"Thank you," I said. "Please listen to my statement. It's a strange story, and I wouldn't expect you to believe it if it weren't so detailed. It's too cohesive to be just a dream, and I have no reason to lie to you." I spoke to him in a serious but calm manner. I had already decided I was going to hunt down my enemy until he was dead, and that goal gave me a temporary sense of peace. I gave him a brief, straightforward account of everything that had happened. I provided exact dates and never let myself get emotional or angry.

The judge looked completely skeptical at first. But as I kept talking, he became much more interested and attentive. I even saw him shudder in horror a few times. Sometimes he just looked genuinely surprised, without a hint of doubt.

When I finally finished my story, I said, "This is the creature I'm accusing. I expect you to use all of your authority to capture and punish him. It's your job as a judge. I hope your personal feelings won't stop you from doing what needs to be done."

His expression completely changed after I said that. He had been listening to my story like it was some kind of ghost story, but the moment I asked him to take official action, all his doubts came rushing back. He gave a gentle response, though. "I would gladly help you hunt him down, but this creature you're talking about seems to have powers that would make it impossible to catch him. How can we track down something that can cross frozen oceans and hide in caves where no human would ever go? Plus, it's been months since he committed those crimes. There's no telling where he's wandered off to by now."

"I have no doubt he's lingering close to my home. If he really is hiding in the Alps, we can hunt him down and kill him like an animal. But I know what you're thinking. You don't believe my story, and you have no intention of punishing him for what he did."

My eyes were practically burning with rage as I spoke, and the judge looked intimidated. "You've misunderstood," he said. "I will do everything I can. If it's possible to capture this monster, you can rest assured he'll be punished for his crimes. But based on how you've described him, I think it's going to be impossible. We'll do everything we can, but you should prepare yourself for disappointment."

"That's unacceptable. But I know nothing I say is going to change your mind. You don't care about my need for revenge. I know it's not a healthy obsession, but it's the only thing keeping me going. I get so angry when I remember that I unleashed a murderer on the world and he's still out there. If you're going to refuse to help me, I have no choice. I'm dedicating the rest of my life—and my death, if it comes to it—to destroying him."

I was shaking with anger as I spoke. I was acting completely unhinged, like one of those intense, self-righteous martyrs from the old days. But to a judge in Geneva, who was far more concerned with practical matters than heroism or devotion, my intensity just looked like sheer insanity. He tried to calm me down like a nurse coddling a child, dismissing my entire story as a fever dream.

"You're a fool!" I yelled. "You think you're so smart, but you have no idea what you're talking about! Just stop talking."

I stormed out of the house, absolutely furious. I went back home to try and come up with a new plan.
