Chapter 12

"I lay on my straw bed, but I could not sleep. I thought about the gentle manners of the people in the cottage. I longed to join them, but I was too scared. The villagers had beaten me the night before, so I decided to stay hidden in my shed and quietly observe them.

"The next morning, the family woke up early. The young woman cleaned the house and cooked, while the young man went out to work. The old man, who I realized was blind, played his instrument or sat quietly. The young people treated him with deep love and respect. He smiled kindly at them in return.

"But they were not completely happy. The young man and woman often cried in secret. If such beautiful people were sad, it made sense that a lonely monster like me was miserable. But why were they sad? They had a warm fire, delicious food, and good clothes. Most importantly, they had each other. It took me a long time to understand their sorrow.

"Eventually, I discovered they were very poor. They survived on vegetables from their garden and a little milk from their only cow. During the winter, they often went hungry so the old man could eat. This kindness moved me. I had been stealing their food at night, but when I realized it hurt them, I stopped. Instead, I ate berries and nuts from the forest.

"I found another way to help. The young man spent hours chopping wood. At night, I started taking his tools to cut enough firewood for several days. One morning, the young woman opened the door and gasped at the huge pile of wood. The young man was amazed. That day, he didn't have to work in the forest, so he fixed the cottage and tended the garden instead.

"Slowly, I made a huge discovery: these people communicated through sounds. Their words caused smiles, sadness, or joy. I desperately wanted to learn their language. I listened closely for months. Eventually, I learned simple words like 'fire,' 'milk,' 'bread,' and 'wood.' I learned their names too. The old man was 'father.' The girl was 'Agatha' or 'sister.' The young man was 'Felix' or 'brother.' I felt incredible joy when I finally understood and could say these words.

"I spent the winter this way. I grew to love the villagers. When they were sad, I was sad. When they were happy, I felt joy. The old man often cheered up his children, especially Agatha. But Felix was always the saddest.

"Even in their poverty, they were kind. Felix brought Agatha the first white winter flower. He shoveled the snow and drew water before she woke up. He was always amazed to find his chores finished by my invisible hand.

"Felix often read to the others. Slowly, I realized the marks on the paper matched the sounds he spoke. I wanted to read, too, but I couldn't even speak properly yet. I decided I must master their language before showing myself to them. If I could speak well, maybe they would overlook my terrible appearance.

"I admired their beautiful faces and graceful bodies. But one day, I saw my own reflection in a clear pool. At first, I refused to believe it was me. When I realized I truly was a monster, I felt bitter despair. I had no idea how much trouble my ugliness would cause.

"As spring approached, the snow melted. The earth grew green, and the family found more food. The old man took walks in the sun. My routine stayed the same: I watched them during the day and gathered food and wood at night. They called me their 'good spirit.'

"I longed to know why Felix and Agatha were sad. I dreamed of making them happy. I imagined stepping out and speaking to them kindly, so they would look past my face and love me. These hopes motivated me to study their language even harder. My voice was rough, but I learned to speak the words I knew.

"Spring brought warm showers and blooming trees. The world looked beautiful. My sadness disappeared, and my future felt bright with hope and joy."
