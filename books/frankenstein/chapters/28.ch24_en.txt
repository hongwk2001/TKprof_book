Chapter 24

My current situation was one where all conscious thought was swallowed up and lost. I was driven away by fury. Revenge alone gave me strength and composure. It molded my feelings and allowed me to be calculating and calm at times when delirium or death would have been my fate.

My first decision was to leave Geneva forever. My country was dear to me when I was happy and loved, but it became hateful in my suffering. I took some money and a few of my mother's jewels, and I left.

Now my wanderings began, which will only end when my life does. I have traveled across a vast part of the world. I have endured all the hardships that travelers typically face in deserts and wild lands. I barely know how I have survived. Many times I stretched my failing limbs on the sandy plain and prayed for death. But revenge kept me alive. I dared not die and leave my enemy alive.

When I left Geneva, my first task was to find some clue to track my fiendish enemy. My plans were unsettled. I wandered around the edge of the town for hours, unsure of what path to take. As night approached, I found myself at the entrance of the cemetery where William, Elizabeth, and my father rested. I went inside and walked up to the tomb that marked their graves. Everything was silent except the leaves on the trees gently shaking in the wind. The night was almost completely dark. The scene would have been solemn and moving even to a stranger. The spirits of the departed seemed to flit around. They cast a shadow around the head of the mourner, felt but not seen.

The deep grief that this scene initially brought quickly gave way to rage and despair. They were dead, and I lived. Their murderer also lived. I had to drag out my weary life just to destroy him. I knelt on the grass and kissed the ground. With quivering lips, I exclaimed, "By the sacred earth on which I kneel, by the shades that wander near me, and by the deep and eternal grief that I feel, I swear. And by you, O Night, and the spirits that preside over you, I swear to pursue the demon who caused this misery until he or I perish in mortal conflict. For this purpose, I will preserve my life. To carry out this revenge, I will again look at the sun and walk on the green grass of the earth, which otherwise should vanish from my eyes forever. I call on you, spirits of the dead, and on you, wandering ministers of vengeance, to help and guide me in my work. Let the cursed and hellish monster drink deeply of agony. Let him feel the despair that torments me now."

I began my plea with a solemnity and awe that almost convinced me my murdered friends heard and approved. But the furies possessed me as I finished, and rage choked my words.

I was answered through the stillness of the night by a loud, fiendish laugh. It rang in my ears long and heavily. The mountains echoed it. I felt as if all hell surrounded me with mockery and laughter. Surely in that moment I should have gone mad and destroyed my own miserable life, but my vow was heard, and I was saved for vengeance. The laughter faded. A well-known and hated voice, seemingly right next to my ear, whispered loudly, "I am satisfied, miserable wretch! You have decided to live, and I am satisfied."

I ran toward the spot where the sound came from, but the devil slipped from my grasp. Suddenly, the broad disk of the moon rose. It shone directly on his ghastly and distorted shape as he fled with superhuman speed.

I chased him, and this has been my task for many months. Guided by a slight clue, I followed the curves of the Rhone River, but with no luck. The blue Mediterranean appeared. By a strange chance, I saw the fiend sneak into a vessel bound for the Black Sea at night. I booked passage on the same ship, but he somehow escaped.

I continually followed his tracks through the wilds of Tartary and Russia, though he still managed to avoid me. Sometimes the peasants were frightened by this horrifying sight and told me where he went. Other times, he left a mark to guide me. He did this because he feared that if I lost his trail entirely, I might give up and die. The snow fell on my head, and I saw his huge footprints on the white plain. You are just beginning your life. Care is new to you, and you have not known agony. How can you understand what I have felt and still feel? Cold, hunger, and exhaustion were the least of the pains I was meant to endure. I was cursed by some devil, and I carried my personal hell with me everywhere. Yet a good spirit still followed and guided my steps. When I complained the most, it would suddenly rescue me from impossible difficulties. Sometimes nature gave out and I collapsed from exhaustion and hunger. Then I would find a meal prepared for me in the desert, which restored my strength and spirits. The food was rough, just what the local peasants ate. But I don't doubt that it was left there by the spirits I called upon to help me. Often everything was dry and the sky was cloudless. I would be parched by thirst, and a small cloud would darken the sky. It shed just a few drops to revive me and then vanished.

I followed the rivers whenever possible. The demon usually avoided them because people gathered around the water. In other places, humans were rarely seen. I generally survived on the wild animals that crossed my path. I carried money and won the friendship of the villagers by sharing it. Alternatively, I brought meat that I had hunted. I ate a small portion and always gave the rest to those who provided me with fire and cooking tools.

My life, spent in this way, was hateful to me. I only tasted joy when I slept. Oh, blessed sleep! Often, when I was most miserable, I sank into rest, and my dreams soothed me into absolute happiness. The spirits guarding me provided these moments, or rather hours, of joy so I could keep my strength to finish my journey. Without this break, I would have collapsed under my hardships. During the day, the hope of night kept me going. In my sleep, I saw my friends, my wife, and my beloved country. I saw my father's kind face again, heard the sweet tones of Elizabeth's voice, and watched Clerval enjoying his youth and health. Often, when I was exhausted by a difficult march, I convinced myself I was just dreaming. I believed that when night came, I would finally wake up to the real world in the arms of my dearest friends. I felt such agonizing love for them! I clung to their dear images. Sometimes they haunted my waking hours, and I convinced myself they were still alive. In those moments, the burning vengeance died in my heart. I chased the demon's destruction more as a duty ordered by heaven, like a mechanical reflex I wasn't even aware of, rather than a burning desire from my soul.

I cannot know what his feelings were. Sometimes he left written marks on the bark of trees or carved them into stone. These guided me and fueled my fury. "My reign is not yet over"—these words were clear in one inscription—"you live, and my power is complete. Follow me. I seek the everlasting ice of the north. There you will feel the misery of cold and frost, to which I am immune. You will find a dead hare near here, if you don't follow too slowly. Eat and be refreshed. Come on, my enemy. We still have to fight for our lives, but you must suffer through many hard and miserable hours before that time comes."

Mocking devil! I vow vengeance all over again. I dedicate you to torture and death once more, you miserable fiend. I will never stop searching until one of us is dead. And then, what joy it will be to join my Elizabeth and my departed friends. Even now, they are preparing the reward for my tedious work and horrible journey!

As I kept traveling north, the snow deepened. The cold became almost too severe to endure. The peasants locked themselves in their shacks. Only a few of the toughest went out to hunt animals that starvation forced from their hiding places. The rivers were covered in ice, and I couldn't catch any fish. My main source of food was completely cut off.

My enemy's triumph grew along with the difficulty of my struggles. He left one inscription that read: "Prepare! Your struggles have only just begun. Wrap yourself in furs and gather food. We will soon start a journey where your suffering will finally satisfy my eternal hatred."

My courage and perseverance were renewed by his mocking words. I swore not to fail in my goal. I called on heaven to support me and pushed on with unstoppable passion across massive deserts. Finally, the ocean appeared in the distance, marking the edge of the horizon. Oh! It looked nothing like the blue seas of the south. It was covered in ice and only looked different from the land because it was wilder and more rugged. The Greeks cried tears of joy when they saw the Mediterranean from the hills of Asia, celebrating the end of their struggles. I did not cry, but I knelt down. With a full heart, I thanked my guiding spirit for leading me safely to the place where I hoped to fight him, despite his mocking words.

A few weeks earlier, I had bought a sled and dogs. I traveled across the snow with unbelievable speed. I don't know if the fiend had the same advantages. Before, I lost ground every day, but now I realized I was catching up to him. When I first saw the ocean, he was only one day's journey ahead of me. I hoped to cut him off before he reached the beach. Filled with new courage, I hurried on. Two days later, I arrived at a miserable little village on the seashore. I asked the locals about the fiend and got precise details. They said a gigantic monster had arrived the night before. He carried a gun and many pistols, and his terrifying appearance scared the people out of a lonely cottage. He stole their winter food supply and loaded it onto a sled. To pull it, he grabbed a large pack of trained dogs and harnessed them up. That same night, to the relief of the terrified villagers, he headed out across the frozen sea, heading in a direction that led to no land at all. They guessed the ice would quickly break and drown him, or the endless freezing cold would kill him.

Hearing this gave me a temporary fit of despair. He had escaped me. I had to start a destructive and nearly endless journey across the mountainous ice of the ocean. The cold was something very few people could endure for long, and as someone from a warm, sunny climate, I had almost no hope of surviving. But the thought of the fiend living on in triumph brought back my rage and vengeance. It washed over every other feeling like a massive wave. After a short rest, during which the spirits of the dead hovered around me and pushed me to work and take my revenge, I got ready for my journey.

I traded my land sled for one designed for the rough surface of the frozen ocean. I bought a large supply of food and left the land behind.

I cannot guess how many days have passed since then. I have endured suffering that I could only survive because of the eternal desire for justice burning in my heart. Massive, jagged mountains of ice often blocked my way. I frequently heard the thunder of the shifting sea ice below, which threatened to destroy me. But the frost always returned and made the ocean paths safe again.

Judging by the food I ate, I guessed I had been traveling for three weeks. The constant teasing of hope, returning again and again to my heart, often forced bitter tears of sadness and grief from my eyes. Despair had nearly caught me, and I was close to collapsing under the misery. Once, my poor dogs dragged me up a sloping ice mountain with incredible effort. One of them collapsed from exhaustion and died. I looked out over the vast expanse ahead of me with pure agony. Suddenly, I noticed a dark spot on the dim plain. I strained my eyes to see what it was, and I let out a wild scream of joy when I recognized a sled and the distorted shape of a familiar figure inside. Oh! Hope rushed back into my heart with a burning surge! Warm tears filled my eyes. I wiped them away quickly so they wouldn't block my view of the demon. But my sight kept blurring from the burning tears until I finally gave in to my overwhelming emotions and wept out loud.

This was no time to delay. I unharnessed the dead dog, gave the others plenty of food, and took an hour's rest. It was absolutely necessary, but deeply annoying to me. Then I continued on my way. The sled was still in sight. I never lost track of it again, except when some icy crags temporarily hid it. I was clearly gaining on him. After nearly two more days of traveling, I saw my enemy less than a mile away, and my heart leaped.

Just as I seemed ready to grab my enemy, my hopes were suddenly destroyed. I lost all track of him more completely than ever before. The sea began to shift underneath the ice. The thunderous sound of the water rolling and swelling became more terrifying and ominous every second. I pushed forward, but it was useless. The wind picked up. The sea roared. With the massive shock of an earthquake, the ice split and cracked with a deafening, overwhelming noise. The destruction happened fast. In a matter of minutes, a chaotic, rolling sea stood between me and my enemy. I was left drifting on a broken piece of ice that kept shrinking, preparing me for a horrible death.

Many terrifying hours passed like this. Several of my dogs died. I was about to collapse under the crushing weight of it all when I saw your ship at anchor. It offered me a chance at rescue and life. I had no idea ships ever traveled this far north, and I was shocked to see it. I quickly broke apart some of my sled to make oars. With exhausting effort, I managed to row my ice raft toward your ship. If you had been heading south, I was determined to take my chances with the ocean rather than abandon my mission. I had hoped to talk you into giving me a boat so I could chase my enemy. But you were heading north. You brought me on board when my energy was completely gone. I soon would have died from my many hardships—a death I still fear, because my task is not finished.

Oh! When will my guiding spirit, which led me to the demon, finally let me rest? Or must I die while he still lives? If I do, swear to me, Walton, that he will not escape. Swear that you will hunt him down and satisfy my vengeance by killing him. Do I dare ask you to take on my journey and endure the hardships I have faced? No, I am not that selfish. But when I am dead, if he shows up—if the spirits of vengeance lead him to you—swear that he will not live. Swear that he will not triumph over my endless suffering and survive to add to his list of dark crimes. He is eloquent and persuasive, and his words once moved even my heart. Do not trust him. His soul is as hellish as his body, full of treachery and pure evil. Do not listen to him. Call on the names of William, Justine, Clerval, Elizabeth, my father, and the wretched Victor, and drive your sword into his heart. I will hover nearby and guide your blade straight.

Walton, in continuation.

August 26th, 17--.

You have read this strange and terrifying story, Margaret. Doesn't your blood freeze with horror, just as mine does right now? Sometimes, gripped by sudden agony, he couldn't even continue his tale. Other times, his voice, broken but piercing, struggled to speak words filled with pure anguish. His fine, lovely eyes would light up with anger one moment, and then drop into deep sorrow and infinite misery the next. Occasionally he controlled his face and voice, telling the most horrifying details calmly and hiding any sign of emotion. Then, like an erupting volcano, his face would suddenly twist into the wildest rage, and he would scream curses at his tormentor.

His story connects well and sounds like the simple truth. But I have to admit, the letters from Felix and Safie that he showed me, along with seeing the monster from our ship, convinced me more than his own earnest words. So, a monster like that actually exists! I can't doubt it, yet I am completely lost in shock and amazement. Sometimes I tried to get Frankenstein to tell me exactly how he made the creature, but he absolutely refused to talk about it.

"Are you crazy, my friend?" he asked. "Where is this pointless curiosity leading you? Do you want to create a demonic enemy for yourself and the world? Peace, peace! Learn from my suffering and don't look for ways to increase your own."

Frankenstein noticed I was taking notes on his story. He asked to see them, and then he personally corrected and expanded them in many places. Mostly, he added life and energy to the conversations he had with his enemy. "Since you have saved my story," he said, "I don't want a broken version to be passed down to the future."

A week has gone by while I listened to the strangest story anyone has ever imagined. My thoughts and feelings have been totally consumed by the interest I have in my guest. His story and his noble, gentle manners created this fascination. I want to comfort him, but how can I tell someone so completely miserable, with absolutely no hope, to keep living? Oh, no! The only joy he will ever know again is when his shattered spirit finally finds peace in death. He does have one comfort, born from his loneliness and delirium. He believes that his dreams of talking to his friends are real. He thinks they aren't just his imagination, but the actual spirits visiting him from another world to bring him comfort or push him toward revenge. This belief makes his visions so serious that they feel almost as real and interesting to me as the truth.

We don't always just talk about his past and his tragedies. He shows an incredible amount of knowledge and sharp understanding on every topic in literature. His speaking is powerful and moving. I can't even listen to him tell a sad story or try to evoke pity or love without crying. What an amazing person he must have been back when he was successful! Even now, in ruin, he is noble and godlike. He clearly knows his own worth and understands how far he has fallen.

"When I was younger," he said, "I thought I was meant for something great. My feelings are deep, but I always kept a cool head, which made me perfect for big achievements. Believing in my own potential kept me going when others would have given up. I thought it was a crime to waste my useful talents on pointless sadness. When I thought about what I had done—creating a thinking, feeling being—I couldn't just lump myself in with ordinary inventors. But the very thought that drove me at the start of my career only drags me further down into the dirt now. All my ideas and hopes mean nothing. Like the archangel who wanted ultimate power, I am locked in an endless hell. My imagination was vivid, but my ability to analyze and work was intense. By combining these traits, I came up with the idea and actually built a man. Even now, I can't remember my daydreams from when the work was unfinished without feeling passionate. I walked through heaven in my mind, sometimes thrilled by my skills, sometimes burning with thoughts of what I could achieve. From childhood, I was filled with high hopes and grand ambition. But look how far I've sunk! Oh, my friend, if you had known me back then, you wouldn't even recognize me in this degraded state. Sadness rarely touched my heart. I felt like a great destiny was carrying me forward, until I fell, never to rise again."

Do I really have to lose this wonderful man? I have wanted a friend for so long. I looked for someone who would understand and love me. And here, on these empty seas, I finally found one. But I am afraid I only met him to learn how great he is before losing him. I want to make him want to live, but he rejects the whole idea.

"I appreciate your kindness, Walton," he said. "But when you talk about making new friends and finding new people to care about, do you really think anyone can replace the ones I lost? Can any man be what Clerval was to me, or any woman take Elizabeth's place? Even if we don't deeply admire them, our childhood friends always have a hold on us that no new friend can ever match. They know how we were as kids, and those early traits never really disappear, no matter how much we change. They can judge our motives and actions more accurately. A brother or sister won't suspect you of lying or cheating unless you showed those signs early on. A new friend, no matter how close, might still look at you with suspicion without meaning to. But my friends were dear to me because of who they were, not just because I was used to them. Wherever I go, I will always hear Elizabeth's soothing voice and Clerval's conversation in my ear. They are dead, and only one thing in all this loneliness can convince me to stay alive. If I were working on some great project that would help humanity, I could live to finish it. But that isn't my fate. I have to hunt down and destroy the creature I brought to life. Once that is done, my purpose on earth will be complete, and I can finally die."

My beloved Sister,

September 2nd.

I am writing to you surrounded by danger. I don't know if I will ever see dear England or my beloved friends there again. Mountains of ice trap me on all sides, offering no way out and threatening to crush my ship at any second. The brave men I convinced to join me look to me for help, but I have nothing to give them. Our situation is terrifying, but my courage and hope haven't left me yet. Still, it breaks my heart to think that all these men are risking their lives because of me. If we die, my crazy plans are the reason.

And Margaret, what will happen to you? You won't hear about my death, and you will wait anxiously for me to come back. Years will go by. You will suffer moments of despair and be constantly tortured by hope. Oh, my dear sister, thinking about your broken heart hurts me more than the thought of my own death. But you have a husband and lovely children. You can still be happy. May heaven bless you and make sure of it!

My unfortunate guest looks at me with such tender pity. He tries to give me hope and talks about life like it's something he actually values. He reminds me that other sailors have faced these same accidents in this sea before. Without meaning to, he makes me feel optimistic. Even the sailors feel the power of his words. When he speaks, they stop despairing. He wakes up their energy. While they listen to him, they believe these massive ice mountains are just molehills that will melt away through sheer human willpower. But those feelings don't last. Every day we spend waiting fills them with fear. I am terrified that their despair will cause a mutiny.

September 5th.

Something so incredible just happened that I have to write it down, even though you will probably never read this.

We are still surrounded by mountains of ice. We are still in constant danger of being crushed as the ice shifts. The cold is extreme, and many of my poor crewmates have already died in this desolate place. Frankenstein's health has gotten worse every day. A feverish fire still burns in his eyes, but he is completely exhausted. If he suddenly pushes himself to do anything, he immediately collapses back into apparent lifelessness.

I mentioned in my last letter that I was afraid of a mutiny. This morning, I sat watching my friend's pale face. His eyes were half closed, and his limbs hung weakly. Suddenly, half a dozen sailors demanded to come into the cabin. They walked in, and their leader spoke to me. He said the other sailors had chosen them to come and make a demand that I couldn't fairly refuse. We were trapped in the ice and would probably never escape. But they worried that if the ice broke and cleared a path, I would be foolish enough to keep sailing north. They feared I would drag them into new dangers right after they survived this one. They insisted I make a solemn promise: if the ship broke free, I would instantly turn around and head south.

This speech bothered me. I hadn't lost hope, and I hadn't even thought about turning back if we got free. But could I really refuse this demand, fairly or practically? I paused before answering. Then Frankenstein, who had been quiet and seemed too weak to even notice, suddenly woke up. His eyes sparkled, and his cheeks flushed with a burst of energy. He turned to the men and said,

"What do you mean? What are you demanding from your captain? Are you giving up on your goal that easily? Didn't you call this a glorious expedition? Why was it glorious? Not because the water was smooth and calm like a southern sea, but because it was full of danger and terror. It was glorious because every new challenge tested your strength and showed your courage. Danger and death surrounded you, and you were supposed to face them and win. That is what made it a noble and honorable mission. You were going to be heroes, adored by people for being brave men who risked death for honor and the good of humanity. But look at you now. At the very first hint of danger—or, let's call it your first real test of courage—you shrink away. You are fine with being remembered as men who couldn't handle the cold and danger. 'Oh, poor things, they got chilly and ran back to their warm fireplaces.' You didn't need to prepare for that. You didn't need to travel this far and drag your captain into a shameful defeat just to prove you are cowards. Be men, or be better than men! Stick to your goals and stand firm as a rock. This ice isn't made of the same weak stuff as your hearts. It shifts and breaks. It can't stop you if you refuse to let it. Don't go back to your families with disgrace stamped on your foreheads. Return as heroes who fought and won, and who have no idea what it means to run away from an enemy."

He spoke with a voice that perfectly matched the emotions of his speech. His eyes were full of grand purpose and heroism. It is no surprise the men were moved. They looked at each other and couldn't say a word. I spoke up. I told them to leave and think about what they had just heard. I promised I wouldn't take them further north if they strongly opposed it, but I hoped that, after thinking it over, they would find their courage again.

They left, and I turned back to my friend, but he had slumped over, completely exhausted and barely alive.

I don't know how this will end, but I would rather die than return in shame without finishing what I started. Still, I am afraid that will be my fate. Without a sense of glory and honor to keep them going, the men will never willingly endure these hardships for much longer.

September 7th.

The decision is made. I agreed to turn back if we survive. My hopes have been destroyed by cowardice and hesitation. I am going back, ignorant and disappointed. I need more wisdom than I have to handle this unfairness patiently.

September 12th.

It is over. I am returning to England. I have lost my chance at glory and doing something useful. I have lost my friend. But I will try to explain these bitter events to you, my dear sister. And while I am sailing back to England and to you, I will not lose hope.

On September 9th, the ice started to shift. We heard roaring that sounded like distant thunder as the ice islands split and cracked everywhere. We were in extreme danger. Since we couldn't do anything but wait, I focused all my attention on my unfortunate guest. His illness had gotten so bad that he was completely stuck in bed. The ice cracked behind us and was pushed hard toward the north. A breeze started blowing from the west, and by the 11th, the path to the south was completely clear. When the sailors saw this, and realized they were definitely going home, they let out a loud, long shout of wild joy. Frankenstein, who had been dozing, woke up and asked what the noise was. "They are cheering," I said, "because they are finally heading back to England."

"Are you really going back, then?"

"Sadly, yes. I can't fight their demands. I can't drag them into danger against their will, so I must return."

"Do what you must. But I will not. You can give up your mission, but heaven gave me mine, and I don't dare abandon it. I am weak, but surely the spirits helping me get my revenge will give me the strength I need." Saying this, he tried to jump out of bed, but it was too much for him. He fell back and passed out.

It took a long time to revive him. I often thought he was completely dead. Finally, he opened his eyes. He was breathing heavily and couldn't speak. The doctor gave him a calming drink and told us to leave him alone to rest. He also quietly told me that my friend definitely only had a few hours left to live.

The final verdict was in, and all I could do was grieve and wait. I sat by his bed, watching him. His eyes were closed, and I thought he was sleeping. But then he called me over with a weak voice. He told me to come closer and said, "Sadly, the strength I counted on is gone. I feel that I will die soon, and my enemy and tormentor might still be alive. Don't think, Walton, that in my final moments I still feel that burning hatred and intense desire for revenge that I talked about before. But I do believe I am right to want my enemy dead. Over the past few days, I have thought deeply about my past actions, and I don't think I am to blame. In a burst of passionate madness, I created a thinking being. I owed it to him to ensure his happiness and well-being as much as I could. That was my duty, but I had an even bigger one. My duty to my fellow humans mattered more, because it involved a much larger scale of happiness or misery. Driven by that idea, I refused to build a companion for the first creature, and I was right to refuse. He showed unmatched evil and extreme selfishness. He murdered my friends. He destroyed living beings capable of feeling deep joy, happiness, and wisdom. I have no idea when his thirst for revenge will stop. He is miserable himself, so to keep him from making others miserable, he has to die. It was my job to destroy him, but I failed. Back when I was driven by selfish and dark motives, I asked you to finish my work. I am asking you again now, but this time I am driven only by reason and virtue.

"However, I can't ask you to abandon your country and friends to do this. And since you are heading back to England, you probably won't run into him anyway. I leave it to you to weigh these points and figure out your actual duties. My judgment and mind are already clouded by the fact that I am dying. I don't dare ask you to do what I think is right, because my anger might still be misleading me.

"The thought that he might survive to cause more harm bothers me. Otherwise, right now, as I wait for my release from life, I am happier than I have been in years. The faces of the loved ones I lost are flashing before my eyes, and I am rushing to hold them. Goodbye, Walton! Find happiness in a peaceful life and stay away from ambition. Avoid it even if it seems innocent, like trying to make a name for yourself in science and discovery. But why am I even saying this? My own hopes were destroyed, but maybe someone else will succeed."

His voice grew quieter as he spoke. Finally, worn out by the effort, he fell silent. About half an hour later, he tried to speak again but couldn't. He weakly squeezed my hand, and his eyes closed forever as a gentle smile faded from his lips.

What can I say, Margaret, about the early death of this incredible spirit? What can I say to help you understand how deeply sad I am? Any words I use would be weak and useless. My tears are flowing. My mind is clouded by a dark shadow of disappointment. But I am heading home to England, and maybe I will find some comfort there.

I am being interrupted. What do these sounds mean? It is midnight. The breeze is steady, and the watchmen on deck are barely moving. I hear a sound like a human voice again, but rougher. It is coming from the cabin where Frankenstein's body still lies. I need to get up and check. Good night, my sister.

Great God! What a scene just happened! My head is still spinning from the memory of it. I barely know if I have the strength to write it down. But the story I have been recording wouldn't be complete without this final, unbelievable disaster.

I walked into the cabin where the body of my tragic, wonderful friend was resting. A figure hung over him that I can't even find the words to describe. It was gigantic, but its body was awkward and misshapen. As he leaned over the coffin, thick, messy hair hid his face. One massive hand reached out, looking and feeling like the skin of a mummy. When he heard me walking in, he stopped screaming in grief and horror and jumped toward the window. I have never seen a face as horrifying as his—it was terrifyingly ugly and sickening. I closed my eyes without meaning to and tried to remember what my duties were regarding this monster. I ordered him to stay.

He stopped and stared at me in shock. Then he turned back to the dead body of his creator and seemed to completely forget I was there. Every feature on his face and every movement he made seemed driven by the wildest rage and some uncontrollable emotion.

"That is my victim too!" he yelled. "By murdering him, my crimes are finally finished. The miserable story of my life is coming to an end! Oh, Frankenstein! You generous, dedicated man! What good does it do to ask you to forgive me now? I ruined you completely by destroying everyone you loved. Sadly! He is cold. He can't answer me."

He sounded like he was choking. My first instinct was to follow my friend's dying wish and destroy his enemy, but a mix of curiosity and pity stopped me. I stepped closer to this massive creature. I didn't dare look at his face again because his ugliness was so frightening and unnatural. I tried to talk, but the words just faded on my lips. The monster kept rambling, blaming himself wildly and incoherently. Finally, I gathered the courage to speak to him during a break in his violent outburst.

"Your regret is useless now," I said. "If you had listened to your conscience and felt this guilt before pushing your evil revenge this far, Frankenstein would still be alive."

"Are you dreaming?" the demon replied. "Do you think I didn't feel agony and guilt back then? He," he continued, pointing at the dead body, "he didn't suffer when the deed was finally done. Oh! He didn't feel even a fraction of the agony I felt while I was slowly carrying out my plan. A terrifying selfishness pushed me forward, even as my heart was poisoned by guilt. Do you think I enjoyed hearing Clerval groan? My heart was made to feel love and sympathy. When misery twisted it into vice and hatred, the pain of that change was a torture you can't even imagine.

"After murdering Clerval, I went back to Switzerland, broken and exhausted. I pitied Frankenstein. My pity turned into pure horror. I hated myself. But when I found out that he—the man who created me and caused my unspeakable suffering—dared to hope for his own happiness, I was filled with bitter anger and helpless jealousy. It gave me an endless thirst for revenge. While he piled misery and despair onto me, he was out looking for joy and love—things I was locked out of forever. I remembered my threat and decided to make it happen. I knew I was setting myself up for deadly torture, but I was a slave to an urge I hated but couldn't fight. Yet when she died! No, I wasn't miserable then. I threw away all my feelings, shoved down the pain, and let myself run wild in my despair. From then on, evil became my only goal. Pushed this far, I had no choice but to adjust to the life I had chosen. Finishing my demonic plan became an obsession. And now it is done. There lies my last victim!"

At first, his words of suffering actually touched me. But then I remembered what Frankenstein had told me about how well the monster could speak and manipulate people. When I looked down at my dead friend again, my anger flared back up. "You wretch!" I said. "It's real nice of you to come here and cry over the ruin you caused. You throw a torch into a building, and when it burns down, you sit in the ashes and cry about it. You hypocritical fiend! If the man you are crying over were still alive, you would just hunt him down and make him your victim all over again. You don't actually feel pity. You are only crying because the target of your evil is finally out of your reach."

"Oh, that isn't true. That isn't true at all," the creature interrupted. "But I guess my actions make it look that way to you. I am not looking for someone to share my misery. I know I will never find sympathy. When I first wanted it, I wanted to share my love for goodness and the overwhelming happiness and affection I felt. But now that goodness is just a memory to me, and my happiness and affection have turned into bitter, sickening despair, what kind of sympathy could I even ask for? I am fine suffering alone as long as my pain lasts. When I die, I am perfectly happy to let people remember me with disgust and hatred. I used to comfort myself with dreams of being good, being known, and enjoying life. I used to foolishly hope I would find people who would look past my ugly body and love me for the good qualities I had inside. I fed my mind with noble thoughts of honor and loyalty. But now, my crimes have dragged me lower than the worst animal on earth. No guilt, no destruction, no evil, and no misery can compare to mine. When I think back on the horrible list of my sins, I can't even believe I am the same creature who used to have such beautiful, grand visions of goodness and majesty. But it is true. The fallen angel turns into an evil devil. Even the devil had friends and followers in his ruin, though. I am completely alone.

"You call Frankenstein your friend, so you seem to know about my crimes and his tragedies. But in the stories he told you, he couldn't possibly explain the months and years of misery I spent wasting away in helpless anger. Because while I destroyed his hopes, I never got what I wanted either. My desires were always burning and desperate. I just wanted love and companionship, and I was constantly rejected. Wasn't that unfair? Why am I the only one treated like a criminal when all of humanity sinned against me? Why don't you hate Felix, who kicked his friend out the door with insults? Why don't you curse the farmer who tried to kill the man who just saved his child's life? No, they are considered pure and virtuous! But me, the miserable, abandoned creature—I am an abomination meant to be rejected, kicked, and stepped on. Even now, my blood boils when I remember how unfair it all was.

"But it is true that I am a monster. I murdered the beautiful and the helpless. I strangled innocent people in their sleep, and I choked a man to death who never hurt me or anyone else. I forced my creator—the absolute best example of someone worthy of love and respect—into endless misery. I chased him all the way to this final ruin. There he is, pale and cold in death. You hate me, but your hatred is nothing compared to how much I hate myself. I look at the hands that did the killing. I think about the mind that planned it all out, and I can't wait for the moment when my hands cover my eyes and my mind finally stops torturing me.

"Don't worry about me causing any more trouble. My work is almost done. I don't need to kill you or anyone else to finish my story and do what needs to be done. It just requires my own death. Don't think I'll hesitate to make this sacrifice. I will leave your ship on the same ice raft that brought me here and head to the absolute furthest north point of the world. I will build my own funeral pyre and burn this miserable body to ashes, so there won't be anything left for some curious, unholy fool to find and use to make another creature like me. I will die. I won't feel this agonizing pain anymore, and I won't be tortured by desires I can never satisfy. The man who made me is dead. When I am gone too, the memory of both of us will quickly fade away. I won't see the sun or the stars anymore, and I won't feel the wind on my face. Light, feeling, and consciousness will all be gone. And I have to find my happiness in that. Years ago, when I first discovered the beauty of the world, when I felt the warm summer sun, heard the leaves rustling, and listened to the birds singing—when those things were my whole world—I would have cried if I had to die. But now, it's my only comfort. Soiled by crimes and ripped apart by the most bitter guilt, where else can I find peace except in death?

"Goodbye! I am leaving you, and you are the last human being I will ever see. Goodbye, Frankenstein! If you were still alive and still wanted revenge against me, you'd get a lot more of it by letting me live than by killing me. But that isn't what happened. You wanted me dead just so I couldn't cause any more pain. If somehow you are still able to think and feel, you wouldn't want any worse revenge than what I am already feeling. Your life was destroyed, but my agony was always worse than yours, because the bitter sting of guilt will never stop rotting my soul until death finally closes my wounds forever.

"But soon," he cried with a sad, intense passion, "I will die, and all this pain will disappear. Soon, these burning miseries will be gone. I will climb onto my funeral pyre in triumph and celebrate the agony of the flames. The light of that fire will fade. The wind will blow my ashes into the sea. My spirit will sleep in peace. If it still thinks, it definitely won't think like this anymore. Goodbye."

He jumped from the cabin window as he said this, landing on the ice raft floating right next to the ship. The waves quickly carried him away, and he vanished into the darkness and the distance.
