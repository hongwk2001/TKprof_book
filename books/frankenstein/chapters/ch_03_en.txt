Letter 3

To Mrs. Saville, England.

July 7, 17—.

My dear Sister,

I am writing a quick note to say that I am safe and making good progress. This letter will reach England on a merchant ship heading home from Archangel. Those sailors are luckier than I am, as I might not see my home country for many years. However, I am in good spirits. My crew is brave and focused. The floating sheets of ice we constantly pass don't seem to scare them, even though they show how dangerous this region is. We are already very far north. Since it is midsummer, the southern winds blow us quickly toward the shores I want to reach. The weather is not as warm as England, but the wind brings an unexpected, refreshing warmth.

Nothing worth mentioning has happened so far. Experienced sailors barely remember to write down a couple of strong storms or a small leak, and I will be happy if nothing worse happens on this trip.

Goodbye, my dear Margaret. I promise to be careful for both our sakes. I will stay calm, persistent, and smart.

But I am sure I will succeed. Why shouldn't I? I have made it this far, carving a safe path across the empty ocean. The stars themselves are watching my victory. Why shouldn't I keep going across this wild but manageable sea? What can stop a determined human heart?

My proud heart can't help but spill over like this. But I must finish now. May heaven bless my beloved sister!

R.W.
