Chapter 17

The creature finished his story and stared at me, waiting for an answer. I was completely overwhelmed and confused. He continued:

"You must create a female for me. I need someone to share my life and feelings. Only you can do this, and I demand it as my right."

Hearing the end of his story brought back all my anger. "I refuse," I replied firmly. "No torture will ever make me agree to this. You can make me miserable, but I will not do something so evil. What if I create another monster like you, and together you destroy the world? Get away from me! My answer is no."

"You are wrong," the fiend replied calmly. "I will not threaten you; I will reason with you. I am evil because I am miserable. Every human being hates me. Even you, my creator, would gladly tear me to pieces. Why should I show mercy to humans when they show none to me? If I could live with someone who showed me kindness, I would gladly give up my revenge and weep with gratitude. But human eyes will always see me as a monster. So, I will get my revenge. If I cannot have love, I will cause fear. I swear to destroy your life until you curse the day you were born."

His face twisted into a terrifying scowl. But he quickly calmed down and continued.

"I promised to reason with you. If anyone showed me kindness, I would return it a hundred times over. But I know that is impossible. What I ask is reasonable: make a female creature just as ugly as I am. We will be monsters, cut off from the world, but we will have each other. We won't be completely happy, but we will be harmless and free from this agonizing loneliness. Oh, my creator, give me this one chance at happiness! Do not deny my request."

I was moved. I shuddered at what might happen if I agreed, but I saw the justice in his argument. As his creator, I owed him whatever happiness I could provide.

"If you consent," he continued, seeing my hesitation, "no human will ever see us again. We will go to the vast jungles of South America. I do not eat meat; we will survive on berries and acorns. We will sleep on dried leaves under the sun. The life I am offering is peaceful. You only refuse out of cruelty. I see compassion in your eyes—please promise to give me what I desire."

"You say you will live in the wild," I argued, "but you long for human sympathy. Eventually, you will return to human cities. They will hate you again, your anger will return, and you will have a partner to help you destroy them. I cannot consent."

"How quickly you change your mind!" he cried. "I swear by the earth and the sky that if you give me a companion, I will leave the human world forever. My evil passions will disappear because I will finally have a friend. My life will pass peacefully, and I will not curse you when I die."

His words deeply affected me. I felt a surge of pity, but when I looked at his hideous, filthy body, my stomach turned with horror. Still, I realized that since I could never love him or accept him, I had no right to deny him a companion. I also knew that if I refused, this superhuman monster could easily hide in the ice caves and destroy my family.

After a long silence, I decided it was my duty to him and to humanity to agree.

"I consent to your demand," I finally said. "But you must swear to leave Europe and all human settlements forever, the moment I deliver the female to you."

"I swear by the sun, the blue sky, and the fire in my heart!" he shouted joyfully. "As long as you grant my request, you will never see me again. Go home and start your work. I will watch your progress anxiously. Do not worry—when you are ready, I will appear."

With that, he turned and ran down the mountain, moving faster than an eagle. He quickly vanished into the sea of ice.

It was sunset. My heart felt incredibly heavy. I slowly hiked down the dark mountain paths. When I stopped to rest, the dark pines and cold stars filled me with a terrifying dread. I wept bitterly. "Oh, stars and winds," I cried out, "if you pity me, crush my memory and let me disappear. Otherwise, leave me alone in the dark."

I hiked all night and returned to Geneva the next morning. I couldn't explain my feelings to my family. The secret weighed on me like a mountain. My haggard, terrified face worried everyone, but I barely spoke. I felt like a cursed man, unworthy of their love. 

Yet, because I loved them so deeply, I knew I had to complete this horrible task to save them. The thought of building another monster became my only reality, while everything else felt like a fading dream.
