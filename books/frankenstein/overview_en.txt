Overview of Frankenstein

"Frankenstein" is structured into Captain Walton's framing letters followed by Victor Frankenstein's narrative. This edition is divided into seven parts:

Part I: Letters I–IV — Captain Robert Walton's letters from the Arctic. These document his expedition and his dramatic rescue of Victor Frankenstein.
Part II: Chapters 1–4 — Victor's youth and family life. This section covers his obsessive studies at Ingolstadt and the animation of the creature.
Part III: Chapters 5–8 — The immediate aftermath of creation and the creature's escape. It includes the murder of William and the trial of Justine.
Part IV: Chapters 9–12 — Victor's retreat to Mont Blanc and his encounter with the creature. It details the creature's early solitary survival.
Part V: Chapters 13–16 — The creature's education observing the De Lacey family. It covers his despair leading to vows of vengeance.
Part VI: Chapters 17–20 — The creature's demand for a female mate. Victor journeys to England and Scotland, culminating in his ultimate refusal.
Part VII: Chapters 21–24 — The tragic deaths of Henry and Elizabeth. Victor relentlessly pursues the creature to the Arctic. The story ends with Captain Walton's final log entries.
