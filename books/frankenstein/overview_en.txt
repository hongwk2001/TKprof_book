Overview of Frankenstein

"Frankenstein" is structured into Captain Walton's framing letters followed by Victor Frankenstein's narrative, divided in this edition into seven parts:

Part I: Letters I–IV — Captain Robert Walton's letters from the Arctic documenting his expedition and his dramatic rescue of Victor Frankenstein.
Part II: Chapters 1–4 — Victor's youth, family life, his obsessive studies at Ingolstadt, and the animation of the creature.
Part III: Chapters 5–8 — The immediate aftermath of creation, the creature's escape, the murder of William, and the trial of Justine.
Part IV: Chapters 9–12 — Victor's retreat to Mont Blanc, his encounter with the creature, and the creature's early solitary survival.
Part V: Chapters 13–16 — The creature's education observing the De Lacey family, and his despair leading to vows of vengeance.
Part VI: Chapters 17–20 — The creature's demand for a female mate, Victor's journey to England and Scotland, and his ultimate refusal.
Part VII: Chapters 21–24 — The tragic deaths of Henry and Elizabeth, Victor's relentless pursuit of the creature to the Arctic, and Captain Walton's final log entries.
