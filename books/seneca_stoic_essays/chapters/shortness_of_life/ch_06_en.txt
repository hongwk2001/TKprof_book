Chapter VI: Livius Drusus and Restless Ambition

Consider Livius Drusus, a fiercely driven and bold man who, backed by a massive crowd from across Italy, aggressively pushed new laws and revived the destructive policies of the Gracchi. When he realized his political agenda was hopelessly trapped—impossible to fulfill yet too far gone to abandon—he is said to have bitterly cursed the life of relentless turmoil he had endured since the cradle. He cried out that he was the only person who had never known a single day of rest, even as a boy. 

Indeed, while still a minor wearing a boy's tunic, he had the sheer audacity to sway juries in favor of the accused and exert such overwhelming influence in the law courts that he was known to have outright forced favorable verdicts. Where else could such premature ambition lead? One could easily foresee that such reckless, precocious audacity would end in massive public and private catastrophe. Therefore, it was far too late for him to complain about never having a holiday, given that he had been a political agitator and a menace in the forum since childhood. Whether he died by his own hand remains debated; he collapsed from a sudden stab wound to his groin. While some doubted if his death was suicide, no one doubted that it was long overdue.

It would be pointless to list the many others who, though the world deemed them incredibly fortunate, openly despised every waking moment of their lives and offered genuine testimony against themselves. Yet, these complaints changed nothing—neither for themselves nor for anyone else. Once they vented their frustrations in words, they simply slipped back into their usual, exhausting routines. 

"By heaven, even if you had a thousand years to live, your life would shrink into a fleeting moment—your vices will devour any amount of time."

The time you are given—which reason could easily extend, even though it naturally flies by—escapes you inevitably. 

"You do not seize it, you do not hold it back, nor do you delay the swiftest thing in the universe. Instead, you let it slip away as if it were expendable and easily replaced."
