Chapter XIV: Friendship with Great Minds

"Of all people, only those who make time for philosophy are truly at leisure. Only they truly live." They are not content merely to guard their own lifetime; they annex every past age to their own. All the years that have come before are added to their store. Unless we are profoundly ungrateful, we must recognize that these glorious architects of sacred thought were born for us—they have paved the way for our lives. Through the labor of others, we are led to the most beautiful truths, wrested from darkness and brought into the light. We are excluded from no era; every age is open to us. If we choose to expand our minds beyond the narrow limits of human frailty, there is a vast expanse of time through which we may roam. We can argue with Socrates, navigate doubt with Carneades, find peace with Epicurus, transcend human nature with the Stoics, and defy it with the Cynics. Since nature allows us to enter into fellowship with every age, why shouldn't we turn away from this petty, fleeting span of life and surrender our souls to the boundless, eternal past that we share with our betters?

Consider those who exhaust themselves rushing from one social obligation to the next, denying rest to themselves and others. When they have fully indulged this madness, crossing every threshold and knocking on every open door across a sprawling, chaotic city—how many people will they actually manage to see? How many patrons will shut them out out of sheer laziness, self-indulgence, or rudeness? How many will make them wait for hours, only to rush past them pretending to be in a hurry? How many will avoid their crowded main halls entirely, sneaking out through hidden doors—as if deceiving a guest were somehow more polite than simply turning them away? And how many, still groggy and hungover from the night before, will bestow a half-hearted, insolent yawn upon these poor wretches who sacrificed their own sleep to wait on someone else, only acknowledging them after having their names whispered to them a thousand times?

"In truth, the only ones fulfilling the true duties of life are those who cultivate daily, intimate friendships with great minds: Zeno, Pythagoras, Democritus, Aristotle, Theophrastus, and the other high priests of liberal studies." Not one of them will ever be "not at home." Not one will fail to leave a visitor happier and more devoted than when they arrived. Not one will send you away empty-handed. Any person, by day or by night, can meet with them.
