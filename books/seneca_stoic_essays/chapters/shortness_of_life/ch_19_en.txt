Chapter 19: The Dignity of Philosophy

Withdraw yourself to these quieter, safer, and infinitely greater pursuits! Do you truly believe it is all the same whether you spend your days managing grain shipments—ensuring the cargo isn't stolen by corrupt transporters, ruined by moisture, or shorted in weight—or whether you dedicate yourself to sacred and lofty studies? Philosophy asks the ultimate questions: What is the nature and form of God? What fate awaits your soul? Where does Nature lay us to rest when we are freed from the physical body? What invisible force anchors the heaviest matter at the center of the universe, suspends light on high, drives fire to the peaks, and commands the stars through their eternal cycles? "You really must leave the ground and turn your mind's eye upon these things!" Now, while your blood is still hot and you have the strength, step briskly onto this better path. In this philosophical life, immense rewards await: the love and practice of virtue, freedom from the passions, the true knowledge of how to live and how to die, and a life of profound peace.

The condition of all who are consumed by busywork is wretched, but the most wretched of all are those who labor at preoccupations that are not even their own. They regulate their sleep by another man's schedule, match their walk to another's pace, and take orders in the freest matters in the world: who to love and who to hate. "If these people wish to know how short their life is, let them reflect on how small a part of it is truly their own."
