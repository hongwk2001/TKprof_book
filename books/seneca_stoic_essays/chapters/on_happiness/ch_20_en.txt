[Chapter 20]
You say philosophers do not put everything they teach into practice. That is true. But they do many good things through the noble thoughts and teachings they keep in their minds. It would be great if they could truly act as they speak. Who would be happier than them? But in the meantime, you have no right to despise a mind full of good words and good thoughts. People deserve praise just for joining useful studies. They deserve this even if they produce no results and stop in the middle. Why should you be surprised if people who start climbing a steep path do not succeed in climbing very high? Still, look with respect at the people who try great things even if they fail. It is an act of a generous spirit to match one's efforts to the power of human nature instead of one's own power. It is noble to hold a high goal and make plans that are too vast to put into practice. This is true even for people born with huge intellects. Such people set the following rules for themselves. "I will look at death or a comedy with the exact same face. I will support the strength of the body with the strength of the mind and gladly endure any great hard work. I will despise wealth equally whether I have it or not. I will not be less sad if wealth is somewhere else. I will not be more active than usual if it shines around me. I will not care about fortune whether she comes or leaves. I will treat all lands as if they were my own. I will treat my own land as if it belonged to all humanity. I will live remembering that I was born for other people. I will thank nature for this. What better thing could she have done for me? Nature gave me alone to everyone, and gave everyone to me alone. Whatever I own, I will not greedily gather it or carelessly waste it. I will think that there is no truer property than what I give to people who deserve it. I will not count the favors I give by size, frequency, or the value the receiver puts on them. If I give a gift to a worthy person, I will never think the gift is too big. I will not do anything because of public opinion. I will do everything only for my conscience. Whatever I do alone, I will believe the eyes of the people watch me while I do it. When I eat and drink, my goal will be to satisfy the needs of nature, not to fill and empty my belly. I will be kind to my friends and gentle to my enemies. I will forgive before someone asks for forgiveness. I will gladly go halfway to meet the wishes of honorable people. I will keep in mind that this world is my home city and its rulers are the gods. I will remember that they stand above and around me. They judge everything I do or say. Whenever nature asks for my breath back or Reason orders me to put down my life, I will leave this life. I will let everyone testify that I have loved a good conscience and good pursuits. I will declare that I never ruined the freedom of anyone, especially my own." A person who sets these things as the rules of his life will try to fly high and reach the gods. Truly, even if he fails, he fails in a noble adventure. But you, who hate Virtue and the people who practice Virtue, do not surprise us at all. Sickly lights cannot stand the sun. Night creatures avoid the brightness of the day and get confused by the first light of dawn. They all hide in their caves together. Creatures that fear the light naturally hide in cracks. So keep croaking like a frog. Move your miserable tongue and criticize good people. Open your jaws wide and bite hard. Your teeth will break a lot before you leave any mark.
