[Chapter 19]
People say that Diodorus did not cut his own throat according to the teachings of Epicurus. He was the Epicurean philosopher who killed himself with his own hands a few days ago. Some people view this action as a result of madness. Some people view it as a result of recklessness. But he left proof about himself through the way he left life. He was happy and full of awareness of his own goodness. He praised the peace of a life anchored in a safe harbor. He left words that you hate to hear because you also have to do the same thing. He said he lived and finished the race fortune set for him. You argue about the lives and deaths of other people. You bark at the names of people who became great because of some special noble quality. You bark like small puppies barking when a stranger approaches. You do this because it benefits you if no one looks good. It is as if the Virtue of other people is a criticism of all your crimes. You jealously compare the glory of other people with your own dirty actions. But you do not realize how bad this is for you. If those people seeking Virtue are greedy, lustful, and power-hungry, what kind of person must you be? You hate the very name of Virtue. You say no one acts according to his own confession and no one lives by the standards set in his speech. Their words are brave and great. They can survive all the storms that ruin humanity. But they themselves struggle to escape from the crosses where each of you nails his own nails. Why is this a surprise? Crucified people hang on a single post. But these people who punish themselves hang divided on as many crosses as the desires they have. Still, they enjoy saying bad things and despise the vices of other people so well. I would think they had no vices if some criminals did not spit on the crowd from the gallows.
