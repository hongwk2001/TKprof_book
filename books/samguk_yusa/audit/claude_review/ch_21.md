# ch_21 — Claude review (5 equivalence + 28 quality = 33 items)

This chapter (the Gyeon Hwon / Later Baekje annals, mostly diplomatic-letter exchanges
between Gyeon Hwon and Wang Geon) has an unusually high rate of a specific failure mode:
a classical **honorific or insult term treated as a literal proper name**. 虜足下 ("you,
wretch" — an insult, not a name) became "Nojok/Lo" twice; 王之族弟 conflated with a
fabricated "Jurchen"; 束手星山 likely conflates a verb ("pinned down") with a place name.

## REVISE (8)

- **P0004 n1** — 嬖豎在側 describes generic "favored minions" at court, not a named
  individual — current invents "Sinbaek," and the audit's fix substitutes "Hyeon"
  (甄萱/Gyeon Hwon), who is a rebel general, not a court minion — neither fits the
  sentence. Fix: KO "총애받는 간신들이 곁에 머물며 몰래 나라의 권력을 휘둘렀다." EN:
  "Favored minions stayed by the king's side and secretly wielded state power."
- **P0004 n3** — 西南都 romanizes as "Seonamdo," not "Saengnado" (current) or "Saenamdo"
  (the audit's fix, still one syllable off).
- **P0008 n1** — beyond the flagged "king's brother" fix, this sentence has a bigger
  uncaught error: 虜王弟孝廉。宰相英景 names **two captured individuals** — Prince
  Hyoryeom and Chief Minister Yeonggyeong — but current text (and the fix) treats this
  as "the Jurchen's younger brother obtained the official ranks of Hyoryeom and
  Yeonggyeong," inventing a "Jurchen" that appears nowhere in the hanmun. Fix: KO
  "그런 다음 왕의 아우 효렴과 재상 영경을 사로잡았다." EN: "He then captured the king's
  brother Hyoryeom and the chief minister Yeonggyeong."
- **P0009 n3** — 下枝 romanizes as "Haji," not "Haju" (current) or "Haje" (the audit's
  fix) — KO already correctly has "하지현"; only EN drifted, both before and after the
  "fix."
- **P0010 n2** — 百寮 means "the hundred officials" (a set phrase for the whole court),
  not "houses" or "households" — both current and the audit's fix keep this literal
  misreading. Fix: KO "나는 모든 관리들이 밝은 해처럼 빛나게 하겠다고 다짐했다." EN:
  "I vowed to be as bright as the clear sun to all the officials."
- **P0011 n1** — 束手星山 more likely reads as a verb + place: "[General] Saksang was
  pinned down (束手) at his camp below Mt. Seongsan," not a compound place name
  "Soksu-seong(san)" (both current and the audit's fix). Fix: KO "초겨울에 도두 색상이
  성산 아래 진영에서 꼼짝 못 하게 되었다." EN: "In early winter, the commander Saksang
  was pinned down at his camp below Mt. Seongsan."
- **P0012 n1, n3** — 虜足下 is a classical insult + polite address ("you, wretch" — 虜
  = captive/barbarian, a taunt; 足下 = "you," standard epistolary 2nd person), not a
  surname. Both current ("노족"/"Nojok") and the audit's fix ("Lo") treat it as a name.
  Fix n1: KO "그러나 그대, 이 미천한 자여, 그만두려 해도 그만두지 못하고 여전히 다투고
  있다." EN: "But you, wretch, though wishing to stop, cannot, and still you fight on."
  Fix n3: KO "또한 그대가 예의를 갖추어 보낸 긴 편지로 사건을 알린 것을 함께 받았다."
  EN: "I also received the long letter in which you courteously reported the matter."

## REJECT (3)

- **P0003 n2** — this passage is set in 936 CE (天福元年), decades after Wang Geon
  founded Goryeo — "高麗" here is the actual Goryeo dynasty (referenced as "太祖"
  throughout this chapter), not ancient Goguryeo. The audit's fix wrongly applies the
  Three-Kingdoms-era 高麗=Goguryeo convention out of its proper temporal context. Keep
  "Goryeo."
- **P0005 n3** — fix is identical to the current second sentence and would, if applied
  as a wholesale replacement, drop the first sentence about joining Tang forces. No
  actionable change.
- **P0014 n4** — 大德 here reads as the abstract phrase "a great virtue/kindness [I
  showed]," describing Wang Geon's own generous conduct toward defeated enemies — not
  an invocation of the Buddhist monastic title "Daedeok" (a king wouldn't claim a
  monk's honorific for himself). Current text is already correct; the fix introduces a
  wrong reading.
