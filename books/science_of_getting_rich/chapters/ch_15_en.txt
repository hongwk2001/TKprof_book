Chapter 14
The Impression of Increase

Whether you change your career or not, your current actions must focus on the business you are engaged in right now.

You can move into the business you want by making constructive use of the business you're already in—by doing your daily work in a Certain Way.

And as far as your business involves dealing with other people, whether in person or by message, the key thought behind all your efforts must be to give them the impression of increase.

Increase is what everyone is seeking. It is the drive of the Formless Intelligence within them, seeking fuller expression.

The desire for increase is built into all of nature. It's the fundamental impulse of the universe. All human activities are based on the desire for increase. People are always seeking more food, more clothes, better shelter, more luxury, more beauty, more knowledge, more pleasure—more of something, more life.

Every living thing is driven by this need for continuous advancement. When the increase of life stops, decay and death set in immediately.

People instinctively know this, and so they are forever seeking more. This law of perpetual increase was explained by Jesus in the parable of the talents: only those who gain more keep what they have; from the one who has nothing, even what little they have will be taken away.

The normal desire for increased wealth isn't evil or wrong. It's simply the desire for a more abundant life. It is aspiration.

And because it is the deepest instinct in human nature, all people are drawn to those who can give them more of the means of life.

By following the Certain Way described in these pages, you are securing continuous increase for yourself, and you are also giving it to everyone you deal with.

You are a creative center from which increase radiates to everyone.

Be absolutely sure of this, and convey that assurance to every man, woman, and child you interact with. No matter how small the transaction—even if it's just selling a piece of candy to a child—put the thought of increase into it, and make sure the customer feels that thought.

Convey the impression of advancement in everything you do, so that everyone gets the feeling that you are a person who is advancing, and that you advance everyone who deals with you. Even to people you meet socially, with no thought of business and to whom you aren't trying to sell anything, give the thought of increase.

You can convey this impression by holding the unshakable faith that you, yourself, are on the path of increase, and by letting this faith inspire, fill, and permeate every action you take.

Do everything you do with the firm conviction that you are an advancing personality, and that you are giving advancement to everyone else.

Feel that you are getting rich, and that by doing so, you are making others rich and bringing benefits to everyone.

Don't boast or brag about your success, or talk about it unnecessarily. True faith is never boastful.

Whenever you find a boastful person, you'll find someone who is secretly doubtful and afraid. Simply feel the faith, and let it show in every transaction. Let every act, tone of voice, and look express the quiet confidence that you are getting rich—that you are already rich. Words won't be necessary to communicate this feeling to others. They will feel the sense of increase when they are around you, and they will be drawn to you again.

You must impress others so strongly that they feel associating with you brings increase for themselves. Make sure you give them more in use value than the cash value you are taking from them.

Take honest pride in doing this, and let everyone know it. You will never lack customers. People will always go where they are given increase. And the Supreme Power, which desires increase for all and knows everything, will bring people to you who have never even heard of you. Your business will grow rapidly, and you will be surprised by the unexpected benefits that come your way. You will be able, day by day, to put together larger deals, secure greater advantages, and move into a more fulfilling career if you choose to.

But while doing all this, you must never lose sight of your vision of what you want, or your faith and purpose in getting it.

Let me give you another word of caution regarding your motives.

Beware of the hidden temptation to seek power over others.

Nothing feels as good to an unformed or partially developed mind as exercising power or control over others. The desire to rule for selfish pleasure has been the curse of the world. For countless ages, kings and lords have drenched the earth in blood during battles to expand their empires—not to seek more life for all, but to gain more power for themselves.

Today, the main motive in the business and industrial world is exactly the same. People marshal their armies of dollars and ruin the lives and hearts of millions in the same mad scramble for power over others. Commercial kings, just like political kings, are driven by a lust for power.

Jesus saw this desire for mastery as the driving force of the evil world he sought to overthrow. Read the twenty-third chapter of Matthew and see how he describes the desire of the Pharisees to be called "Master," to sit in places of honor, to dominate others, and to place heavy burdens on the backs of the less fortunate. Notice how he contrasts this lust for control with the brotherly pursuit of the Common Good that he called his disciples toward.

Watch out for the temptation to seek authority, to become a "master," to be seen as someone above the common crowd, to impress others with lavish displays, and so on.

The mind that seeks control over others is a competitive mind, and a competitive mind is not a creative one. To master your environment and your destiny, it is completely unnecessary to rule over your fellow humans. In fact, when you fall into the world's struggle for high positions, you begin to be conquered by fate and environment, and getting rich becomes just a matter of chance and speculation.

Beware of the competitive mind! No better statement of the principle of creative action can be made than the favorite saying of the late "Golden Rule" Jones of Toledo: "What I want for myself, I want for everybody."
