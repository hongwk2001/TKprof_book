Chapter 2. There Is a Science of Getting Rich.

There is a science to getting rich, and it's an exact science, just like algebra or arithmetic. Certain laws control how people get rich. If anyone learns and follows these laws, they will get rich with mathematical certainty.

You get money and property by doing things in a specific way. People who do things in this Certain Way get rich, whether they mean to or not. But those who don't follow this Certain Way stay poor, no matter how hard they work or how capable they are.

It's a natural law that similar actions always lead to similar results. So, any man or woman who learns to do things in this Certain Way will definitely get rich.

The following facts prove that this statement is true:

Getting rich isn't about your environment. If it were, everyone in certain neighborhoods would be wealthy. Everyone in one city would be rich, while everyone in other towns would be poor. Or, people in one state would be rolling in money, while those in a neighboring state would be living in poverty.

But everywhere you look, rich and poor people live side by side, in the same environment, often doing the same jobs. If two people are in the same area, doing the same business, and one gets rich while the other stays poor, it proves that getting rich isn't mainly about your environment. Some environments might be better than others. But if two people in the same business, in the same neighborhood, have one get rich and the other fail, it shows that getting rich comes from doing things in a Certain Way.

Also, the ability to do things in this Certain Way isn't just about having talent. Many very talented people stay poor, while others with very little talent get rich.

When we study people who have gotten rich, we find they're pretty average in every way. They don't have more talents or abilities than anyone else. It's clear they don't get rich because they have unique talents or abilities. They get rich because they happen to do things in a Certain Way.

Getting rich isn't about saving money or being "thrifty." Many extremely frugal people are poor, while those who spend freely often get rich.

Nor is getting rich about doing things that others can't or won't do. For example, two people in the same business often do almost exactly the same things. Yet, one gets rich while the other stays poor or goes bankrupt.

Considering all this, we have to conclude that getting rich comes from doing things in a Certain Way.

If getting rich is about doing things in a Certain Way, and if similar actions always lead to similar results, then any man or woman who can do things that way can become rich. This means the entire process falls within the realm of exact science.

This brings up a question: Is this Certain Way so difficult that only a few people can follow it? That can't be true, as we've already seen, at least when it comes to natural ability. Talented people get rich, and so do blockheads. Intellectually brilliant people get rich, and so do very stupid people. Physically strong people get rich, and so do weak and sickly people.

Of course, you need some ability to think and understand. But when it comes to natural ability, any man or woman with enough sense to read and understand these words can certainly get rich.

Also, we've seen that it's not about your environment. Location does matter to some extent. You wouldn't go to the middle of the Sahara Desert and expect to run a successful business, for example.

Getting rich requires dealing with people, and being in a place where there are people to deal with. It's even better if these people are open to dealing with you the way you want. But that's pretty much the extent of how much environment matters.

If anyone else in your town can get rich, you can too. And if anyone else in your state can get rich, so can you.

Again, it's not about choosing a specific business or profession. People get rich in every kind of business and profession. Meanwhile, their next-door neighbors, doing the exact same job, stay poor.

It's true that you'll do best in a business you enjoy and feel comfortable with. And if you have well-developed talents, you'll do best in a business that uses those talents.

Also, you'll do best in a business that suits your location. For example, an ice cream parlor would do better in a warm climate than in Greenland. And a salmon fishery will succeed better in the Northwest than in Florida, where there aren't any salmon.

But apart from these general points, getting rich doesn't depend on you being in a specific business. It depends on you learning to do things in a Certain Way. If you're in business now, and someone else in your area is getting rich in the same business, but you are not getting rich, it's because you're not doing things the same Way that person is.

Lack of capital doesn't stop anyone from getting rich. It's true that as you acquire capital, your wealth grows more easily and quickly. But someone who already has capital is already rich, so they don't need to figure out how to become rich. No matter how poor you might be, if you start doing things in the Certain Way, you will begin to get rich. And you will begin to acquire capital. Acquiring capital is part of the process of getting rich. It's also part of the result that always follows when you do things in the Certain Way.

You might be the poorest person on the continent, deeply in debt. You might have no friends, no influence, and no resources. But if you start doing things in this Way, you will definitely begin to get rich. This is because similar actions always produce similar results. If you have no capital, you can get capital. If you're in the wrong business, you can get into the right one. If you're in the wrong location, you can move to the right one. And you can do all of this by starting right now, in your current business and location, to do things in the Certain Way that leads to success.
