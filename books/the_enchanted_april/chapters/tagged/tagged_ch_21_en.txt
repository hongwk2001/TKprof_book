Chapter 21

Frederick wasn't the type to hurt anyone if he could avoid it, but he was completely baffled. Not only was his wife here, in this remote place, but she was hugging him like she hadn't in years, whispering sweet words and welcoming him. If she welcomed him, she must have been expecting him. Bizarre as it was, that was the only clear thing—that, and the softness of her cheek against his and her sweet, familiar scent.

Frederick was confused, but not wanting to hurt her, he put his arms around her and kissed her. Soon, he was kissing her almost as tenderly as she kissed him; then just as tenderly; and then even more tenderly, as if they had never been apart.

He was confused, but he could still kiss her. It felt surprisingly natural. It made him feel thirty again instead of forty, and made Rose feel like the twenty-year-old he had adored before she began judging his actions against her strict morals. Back then, she had judged him and found him wanting, turning cold, distant, and constantly shocked. He couldn't reach her at all; she refused to understand. She judged everything by "how it looked in the eyes of God." Her sad face—her strict principles certainly didn't make her happy—and her strained efforts to be patient had eventually driven him away. She should never have been raised by that narrow-minded minister of a father; she wasn't built to handle such a rigid upbringing.

How she got here and why she was acting like his sweet Rose again was beyond him, but until he figured it out, he kept kissing her. In fact, he couldn't stop. Now he was the one whispering sweet things in her ear, burying his face in her sweet-scented hair that tickled him just as it used to.

As he held her close and felt her arms soft around his neck, a wonderful feeling came over him—a gentle, spreading warmth he slowly recognized as security. Yes, security. He didn't have to feel self-conscious about his weight or make self-deprecating jokes to beat others to it. He didn't have to feel embarrassed about getting sweaty walking uphill, or worry about how he looked to beautiful young women—how middle-aged and foolish he must seem. Rose didn't care about any of that. With her, he was safe. To her, he was still the same lover he had always been. She would never care about the changes aging had made in him.

So Frederick continued to kiss his wife with growing affection, and holding her made him forget everything else. How could he think of Lady Caroline, to name just one of his problems, when his sweet wife was back in his arms, whispering how much she loved and missed him? For just a split second—since even lovers have moments of clear thought—he realized how much more powerful a woman in your arms is compared to a beautiful one who is somewhere else. But that was as far as he got to thinking about Scrap; she was like a dream fading in the morning sun.

<Rose>"When did you leave?"</Rose> Rose whispered, her lips near his ear. She couldn't let go of him, not even to talk.

<Frederick>"Yesterday morning,"</Frederick> Frederick whispered, holding her tight. He couldn't let go of her either.

<Rose>"Oh, the very moment you got my letter,"</Rose> Rose whispered.

This was a bit confusing, but Frederick agreed, <Frederick>"Yes, the very moment,"</Frederick> and kissed her neck.

<Rose>"How fast my letter reached you,"</Rose> Rose murmured, her eyes closed in pure happiness.

<Frederick>"It really did,"</Frederick> Frederick said, feeling like closing his own eyes.

So there had been a letter. Soon things would make sense, but for now, holding his Rose again after all these years was so sweet that he didn't care to figure it out. He had been happy during their years apart, because he was naturally a cheerful man. He had a busy life, success, friends, and plenty of other women happy to make him forget his sad, rigid wife at home. She had refused to spend his money, hated his books, and drifted away. Whenever he tried to talk to her, she stubbornly asked how his writing looked in the eyes of God. <Rose>"No one should write a book God wouldn't want to read. That's the test, Frederick,"</Rose> she had said once. He had laughed hysterically, yelled, and run out of the house to escape her solemn, pathetic little face.

But this Rose represented his youth again—the best part of his life, when he still had dreams and hopes. How they had dreamed together before he started writing memoirs! They had planned, laughed, and loved. For a time, they had lived a beautiful, poetic life. After happy days came happy nights, sleeping side-by-side. When he woke up, she was still pressed close to him. It was wonderful to feel all of that return at her touch, to have her give him back his youth.

<Frederick>"Sweetheart, sweetheart,"</Frederick> he whispered, overcome by the memories, holding onto her tightly now.

<Rose>"My beloved husband,"</Rose> she whispered, feeling absolute bliss.

Briggs, walking in a few minutes before dinner hoping to find Lady Caroline, was shocked. He had assumed Rose Arbuthnot was a widow, and seeing her in a passionate embrace left him stunned.

"Well, I'll be damned," Briggs thought clearly. The shock of what he saw by the window snapped him right out of his own thoughts.

Blushing, he said aloud, <Briggs>"Oh, sorry—excuse me."</Briggs> He stood there awkwardly, wondering if he should go back to his room.

If he had stayed quiet, they wouldn't have noticed him. But when he apologized, Rose looked at him blankly, and Frederick stared at him too, not quite registering who he was.

They didn't seem embarrassed at all, Briggs thought. He couldn't be her brother; no brother ever looked at a sister like that. It was very awkward. Even if they weren't embarrassed, he was. It upset him to see his pure Madonna acting this way.

<Frederick>"Is this a friend of yours?"</Frederick> Frederick asked Rose after a moment. She made no move to introduce the awkward young man, just smiling at him with happy distraction.

<Rose>"It's Mr. Briggs,"</Rose> Rose said, coming back to earth. <Rose>"This is my husband,"</Rose> she added.

As Briggs shook hands, he barely had time to think how strange it was for a "widow" to suddenly have a husband before the dinner gong rang. Knowing Lady Caroline would arrive any second, he stopped thinking altogether and just stared at the door.

Immediately, a procession entered the room. First came Mrs. Fisher, looking very dignified in her lace shawl. She smiled warmly at Briggs, but stiffened when she saw the stranger. Next was Mr. Wilkins, looking cleaner and neater than anyone else on earth. Then came Lotty, hurriedly tying her dress as she walked in. And then, no one else.

Lady Caroline was late. Where was she? Had she heard the gong? Should they ring it again? What if she didn't show up for dinner at all?

Briggs went cold.

<Frederick>"Introduce me,"</Frederick> Frederick whispered, nudging Rose's elbow as Mrs. Fisher walked in.

<Rose>"My husband,"</Rose> Rose said, holding his hand, her face glowing.

"This must be the last of the husbands," Mrs. Fisher thought, "unless Lady Caroline pulls one out of her sleeve."

But she greeted him politely. He certainly looked like a proper husband, not like those men who pretended to be married when they weren't. She assumed he had come to escort his wife home at the end of the month, noting that the house would now be completely full. <Fisher>"So,"</Fisher> she added, smiling at Briggs, <Fisher>"we will finally get our money's worth."</Fisher>

Briggs forced a grin, realizing someone was teasing him, but he hadn't really heard her. He didn't look at her. He was completely focused on the door.

When introduced, Mr. Wilkins was very hospitable and called Frederick "sir."

<Mellersh>"Well, sir,"</Mellersh> Mr. Wilkins said heartily, <Mellersh>"here we are."</Mellersh> He gripped Frederick's hand with a knowing look that Frederick didn't understand, since he had no idea what kind of "trouble" Mr. Wilkins was anticipating. Mr. Wilkins looked him squarely in the eye, trying to project loyalty, reliability, and support. He noticed that Mrs. Arbuthnot was blushing deeply, which she never did. "Well, I'm their man," he thought.

Lotty's greeting was very enthusiastic. She took both of Frederick's hands and laughed to Rose, <Lotty>"Didn't I tell you?"</Lotty>

<Frederick>"What did you tell her?"</Frederick> Frederick asked just to say something. The warm welcome from everyone was confusing; they had clearly all expected him, not just Rose.

The sandy-haired woman didn't answer but looked thrilled to see him. Why was she so happy about his arrival?

<Frederick>"What a lovely place this is,"</Frederick> Frederick said, making the first comment he could think of.

<Lotty>"It's a hot tub of love,"</Lotty> Lotty said earnestly, which confused him even more.

His confusion turned to panic at the next words he heard from Mrs. Fisher: <Fisher>"We won't wait. Lady Caroline is always late."</Fisher> Hearing her name, he finally remembered Lady Caroline, and the thought of her filled him with dread.

He walked into the dining room like he was dreaming. He had come here specifically to see Lady Caroline, and had even told her so. In his foolishness, he had told her he couldn't help but come. She had no idea he was married. She thought his name was Arundel. In fact, everyone in London knew him as Ferdinand Arundel. He had used the pen name for so long that he almost thought of it as his real name. In the brief time since he sat with her in the garden, he had reunited with Rose, embraced her, and completely forgotten about Caroline. It would be a stroke of luck if Caroline decided to skip dinner. Then he could... no, he couldn't. He blushed even redder at the thought of running away. He couldn't just leave after dinner and take the train to Rome, unless Rose came with him. But that would be so cowardly. No, he had to face it.

When they reached the dining room, Mrs. Fisher sat at the head of the table. Was this her house? he wondered. He had no idea. Rose, who sat at the opposite end, led Frederick to the seat next to her. If only he could have had five more minutes alone with Rose to ask her... but he probably would have just kept kissing her.

He looked around. Lotty was telling Briggs to sit next to Mrs. Fisher. Was the house Lotty's then? He didn't know anything. Lotty sat next to Rose, opposite Frederick, and next to Mr. Wilkins, who was looking very satisfied.

The empty chair next to Frederick, between him and Briggs, was Lady Caroline's. Just as Caroline didn't know about Rose, Rose didn't know about Caroline. What would they both think? He had no idea. All he knew was that his wife had forgiven him—suddenly, miraculously, and beautifully. Beyond that, he was clueless. He couldn't handle the situation, so he decided to just let things happen and drift.

Frederick ate his soup in silence. He could feel Lotty's large, expressive eyes watching him with growing curiosity. They were intelligent, friendly eyes. She probably thought he should start a conversation, but she wouldn't think so if she knew the truth. Briggs was quiet too, looking very uneasy. What was wrong with Briggs? Rose was silent as well, which was normal for her. She looked so happy. How long would that look last once Lady Caroline walked in? He had no idea.

But Mr. Wilkins was talking enough for everyone. The man should have been a preacher; a voice like that would make him a bishop in no time. He was explaining to Briggs—who was fidgeting in his seat—that he must have taken the same train as Frederick. When Briggs silently disagreed, Mr. Wilkins proceeded to prove it with long, detailed arguments.

<Frederick>"Who's the man with the booming voice?"</Frederick> Frederick whispered to Rose. Lotty, who seemed to have super-hearing, answered from across the table, <Lotty>"He's my husband."</Lotty>

<Frederick>"Then by the rules,"</Frederick> Frederick said, trying to be polite, <Frederick>"you shouldn't be sitting next to him."</Frederick>

<Lotty>"But I want to. I like sitting next to him now. I didn't before we came here."</Lotty>

Frederick couldn't think of a response, so he just smiled.

<Lotty>"It's this place,"</Lotty> she said, nodding. <Lotty>"It makes you understand things. You have no idea how much you'll figure out before you leave."</Lotty>

<Frederick>"I certainly hope so,"</Frederick> Frederick said with genuine sincerity.

The soup bowls were cleared and the fish was served. Briggs, on the other side of the empty chair, looked increasingly nervous. What was his problem? Didn't he like fish?

Frederick wondered how much Briggs would fidget if he were in his shoes. Frederick kept wiping his mustache and stared down at his plate, trying to hide his nervousness.

Even without looking up, he knew Lotty was staring at him like a searchlight. Rose was looking at him too, but her gaze was sweet and unquestioning. How long would she look at him like that once Caroline arrived? He didn't know.

He wiped his mustache for the twentieth time. His hand was shaking slightly, and Lotty noticed it, staring even harder. Why was she studying him so closely? He had no idea.

Suddenly, Briggs jumped to his feet. Why? Oh, right—she had arrived.

Frederick wiped his mustache and stood up too. The moment of truth was here. What a ridiculous situation. Whatever happened, he would just have to deal with it. He would look like a fool and a liar to Lady Caroline, who might think he was mocking her in the garden when he said he had come because he couldn't help himself. And how would he look to Rose when Caroline introduced him as her friend whom she had invited to dinner? Only God knew.

He wiped his mustache one last time before the disaster struck.

But he hadn't counted on Scrap.

The quick-thinking young woman slipped into the chair Briggs held for her. Before anyone else could speak, Lotty leaned forward and said, <Lotty>"Just imagine, Caroline, Rose's husband got here so quickly!"</Lotty> Scrap turned to him without a trace of surprise. She held out her hand, smiled like an angel, and said, <Scrap>"And here I am, late on your very first evening."</Scrap>
