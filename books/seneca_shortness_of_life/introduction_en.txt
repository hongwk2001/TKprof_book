Introduction to On the Shortness of Life

Welcome to Lucius Annaeus Seneca's classic Stoic manifesto: "On the Shortness of Life" (De Brevitate Vitae).

Written in 49 AD to his friend Paulinus, Seneca addresses one of humanity's most universal complaints: that life is brief and fleeting. Seneca forcefully rejects this common grief, arguing that nature has given us ample time to accomplish noble things—provided we do not waste it on trivial pursuits, political posturing, or mindless distraction.

"It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a lot of it," Seneca writes. "Life is long enough, and a sufficiently generous amount has been given to us for the highest achievements if it were all well invested."

In this modernized edition, Seneca's urgent, conversational advice is rendered into clear, high-impact English prose for contemporary readers and listeners seeking focus, discipline, and purpose.
