Chapter IX: Living in the Present

9. Is there anything more foolish than the mindset of those who pride themselves on their foresight? They keep themselves frantically busy so they can live better—spending their entire lives just preparing to live! They fix their goals on the distant future, yet postponing life is the greatest waste of it. It strips away each day as it arrives, snatching the present from their grasp by promising a tomorrow. 

"The greatest obstacle to living is expectation, which hangs on tomorrow and wastes today."

You are surrendering what is in your own hands while banking on what lies in the hands of Fortune. What are you looking toward? What goal are you chasing? Everything that is yet to come is shrouded in uncertainty—so live immediately! Listen to how the greatest of poets cries out, singing a saving truth as if inspired by the divine:

"The fairest day in hapless mortals' life
Is ever first to flee."

"Why do you delay?" he asks. "Why are you idle? Unless you seize the day, it flees." And even if you do seize it, it will still flee. Therefore, you must match the sheer speed of time by using it swiftly. Like drinking from a rushing torrent that will not flow forever, you must drink quickly. 

Notice how perfectly the poet words his critique of endless delay: he does not say "the fairest age," but rather "the fairest day." Why do you confidently stretch out months and years before yourself in a long, leisurely line, indifferent to how fast time is actually flying? The poet is speaking to you about the day—and about this very day that is slipping away right now. 

Is there any doubt that for hapless mortals—that is, for people consumed by busywork—the fairest day is always the first to flee? Old age ambushes them while their minds are still childish. They arrive unprepared and unarmed, having made no provision for it. They stumble into it suddenly and unexpectedly, never noticing how it crept closer day by day. 

Just as a traveler might be so absorbed in conversation, reading, or deep thought that they reach their destination before realizing they were even getting close, so too with this relentless, high-speed journey of life. We travel at the exact same pace whether awake or asleep, but those who are distracted only realize it when they reach the end.
