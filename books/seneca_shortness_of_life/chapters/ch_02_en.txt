Chapter II: How Men Waste Time

Why do we complain about Nature? She has been generous; "Life is long, if you know how to use it." Yet one person is consumed by insatiable greed, another by exhausting devotion to pointless tasks. One is drowned in alcohol, another paralyzed by laziness. One is drained by an ambition that relies constantly on the opinions of others, while another, driven by the merchant's hustle, is chased across every land and sea by the hope of profit. Some are tormented by a passion for war, always intent on endangering others or worried about their own safety. Some are worn out by voluntary slavery—a thankless servitude to the elite. Many are kept busy either chasing other people's money or complaining about their own. Many, wandering without any fixed goal, inconsistent and dissatisfied, are thrown by their own fickleness into endless new schemes. Some have no guiding principles at all, letting Fate catch them yawning and asleep. 

It is so undeniable that I must agree with the greatest of poets, who spoke as if delivering an oracle: "The part of life we truly live is small." All the rest of existence is not living; it is merely time. 

Vices surround and assault us from every side. They refuse to let us stand up or lift our eyes to see the truth. Once they overwhelm us and chain us to our desires, they hold us down. Their victims are never allowed to return to their true selves. Even if they occasionally find some momentary peace, they are like the deep sea that continues to swell long after the storm has passed—they are tossed about, and no true rest from their cravings ever lasts.

Do you think I am only talking about the miserable people whose flaws are obvious? Look at those whose success everyone envies—they are suffocated by their own blessings. How many find their wealth to be a heavy burden! How many bleed themselves dry with daily efforts to show off their eloquence! How many turn pale from endless partying! How many are trapped by the swarms of clients and followers that leave them with zero freedom!

In short, look at the whole spectrum of society, from the lowest to the highest: this person needs a lawyer, that one answers the call; this one is on trial, that one defends him, and another passes the sentence. No one lays claim to their own life. Everyone's time is squandered for the sake of someone else. Look at the people whose names are famous, and you will see how they are connected: A caters to B, and B caters to C. No one is their own master. 

And then there is the most senseless outrage of all: people complain about the arrogance of their superiors, angry that the boss was too busy to see them. How can anyone have the nerve to complain about someone else's pride, when they themselves never have time for their own soul? Despite everything, that busy executive might sometimes glance your way, even with an arrogant expression; he might occasionally listen to you or let you stand by his side. But you? You never deign to look at yourself. You never listen to your own thoughts. You have no right to demand gratitude for serving others, because when you did it, you weren't looking for someone else's company—you were just running away from your own.
