Chapter 3: Guarding Your Life's Hours

3. Even if all the greatest minds in history focused on this single issue, they could never fully express their shock at the blindness of the human mind. People will not let anyone steal their property, and they will rush to take up arms over the slightest boundary dispute. Yet, they allow others to trespass on their lives—in fact, they personally invite in those who will eventually take it over. 

You won't find anyone willing to give away their money, yet how many people do we distribute our lives to! People are ruthlessly stingy when guarding their wealth, yet when it comes to wasting time—the one thing where it is right to be greedy—they are incredibly reckless. 

And so, I would like to grab hold of an older man and say: "I see you have reached the very limits of a human lifespan. You are pushing one hundred years old, or perhaps even older. Come now, audit your life and do the math. Calculate how much of your time was stolen by a creditor, how much by a lover, how much by a boss, how much by a client. Consider how much was lost arguing with your spouse, disciplining your staff, or rushing around the city fulfilling social obligations. Add the illnesses caused by your own bad habits, and the time that simply slipped away unused. You will realize that you have far fewer actual years to your credit than your age suggests.

Look back and ask yourself: When did you ever stick to a solid plan? How few days went exactly as you intended? When were you ever truly in control of yourself? When did your face ever show a natural, relaxed expression, and when was your mind ever truly at peace? What real work have you accomplished in such a long life? Think about how many people robbed you of your life while you were completely oblivious to what you were losing. Think of how much time was swallowed by pointless sorrow, foolish joy, greedy desire, and the distractions of society. Consider how little of yourself was actually left for yourself. You will realize that you are dying prematurely!"

What, then, is the reason for this? "You live as if you were destined to live forever." No thought of your fragility ever enters your head, and you pay no attention to how much time has already passed. You squander time as if drawing from an infinite supply, even though the very day you give away to some person or obligation might be your last. 

"You have all the fears of mortals, and all the desires of immortals."

You often hear people say: "When I turn fifty, I will retire and relax. At sixty, I will step away from my public duties." But what guarantee do you have that your life will last that long? Who will ensure that everything goes exactly as you planned? Aren't you ashamed to save only the leftovers of your life for yourself, reserving for wisdom only the time that is useless for business? 

"How late it is to begin living just when we must cease to live!" 

What a foolish forgetfulness of mortality it is to postpone meaningful plans until your fiftieth or sixtieth year, intending to finally begin your life at an age that very few people even reach!
