Chapter VIII: The Illusion of Endless Time

It never ceases to amaze me how people demand the time of others—and how willingly those others give it away. Both focus on the reason for the request, but completely ignore the time itself. It is as if what is asked for is nothing, and what is given is nothing. People trifle with the most precious commodity in the world. Because time is invisible to the eye, they are blind to its worth; they treat it as cheap, or even worthless. People fiercely protect their salaries and stipends, trading their labor and effort for every penny. Yet no one sets a value on time; everyone squanders it as if it were free.

But watch these same people when illness strikes. When the shadow of death draws near, they will clasp the knees of their doctors. If threatened with execution, they are ready to surrender every last possession just to live a little longer. Such is the massive inconsistency of the human mind! "If we could see the exact number of our remaining years as clearly as the years that have passed, those with only a few left would panic and guard them fiercely." It is easy enough to manage a limited supply when you know exactly how much you have. But you must be infinitely more careful in guarding what might run out at any given moment.

Still, do not assume they are completely ignorant of time's value. People often tell their dearest loved ones that they would gladly give them a portion of their own years. And they do give them away—without realizing it. But in doing so, they subtract from their own lives without adding a single second to the lives of those they love. They do not even realize they are taking a loss; they find it bearable because they never notice what is slipping away. 

"No one can return your years to you; no one can restore you to yourself." Life will relentlessly follow the path it started upon. It will never reverse or halt its course. It will make no noise, nor will it remind you of its terrifying swiftness. It will glide on in total silence. It will not pause at the command of a king, nor will it stop for the applause of a crowd. It will run exactly as it started on its first day, without a single detour or delay.

And what is the inevitable result? You squander your days engrossed in distractions as life speeds by. Meanwhile, death draws near—and for death, whether you want to or not, you will finally have to find the time.
