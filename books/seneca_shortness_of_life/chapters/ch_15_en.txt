Chapter XV: True Immortality

15. "None of these great thinkers will force you to die, but all will teach you how to die." None will diminish your years, but each will add their own years to yours. Conversations with them will bring no danger, their friendship will not put your life at risk, and courting them will not empty your wallet. You can take whatever you wish from them; if you do not draw the utmost that you desire, the fault will be yours alone. What profound happiness and what a beautiful old age await the person who has dedicated themselves as a client to these minds! They will have friends to seek counsel from on matters both great and small, to consult every day about their own lives, to hear the truth without insult and praise without flattery, and to use as models to fashion their own character.

We often say that we had no power to choose the parents given to us by chance, yet "we can be the children of whomever we choose." There are households composed of the noblest intellects; choose the one into which you wish to be adopted. You will inherit not just their name, but their vast wealth of knowledge, which you won't need to hoard in a petty or stingy spirit. In fact, "the more people you share it with, the greater it will become." These brilliant minds will open the path to true immortality for you, elevating you to a height from which no one can be cast down. "This is the only way to prolong our mortal lives—or rather, to transform mortality into immortality." 

Titles, monuments, and everything that ambition has mandated by decree or built from stone will quickly collapse into ruin; "there is nothing that the passage of time does not eventually tear down and erase." But the works consecrated by philosophy cannot be harmed. No era will destroy them; no age will diminish them. Each succeeding generation will only increase its reverence for them, because envy targets what is close at hand, while we are free to admire what stands at a distance. 

Therefore, the life of the philosopher has a vast range. "They are not confined by the same boundaries that trap everyone else." The philosopher alone is free from the limitations of the human race; all ages serve them as if they were a god. Has time passed by? They embrace it through memory. Is time present? They make use of it. Is it still to come? They anticipate it. "They make their life long by combining all times into one."
