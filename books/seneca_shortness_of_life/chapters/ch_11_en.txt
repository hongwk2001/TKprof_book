Chapter XI: The Fear of Death

11. Do you want the ultimate proof that people do not truly "live long"? Just watch how desperately they cling to life! Frail old men pray on their knees for just a few more years; they shave off their age and deceive themselves, as pleased with the lie as if they had somehow managed to deceive Fate itself. But when a sudden illness finally forces them to face their own mortality, they die in absolute terror—feeling not as though they are departing life, but being violently dragged out of it. They cry out that they have been utter fools, that they have never truly lived, and they swear to spend the rest of their days in peaceful leisure if only they can survive this sickness. Only then do they realize how pointlessly they chased things they never even enjoyed, and how all their exhausting labor amounted to nothing. 

But what about the person who guards their time and lives far removed from the frantic rush of the crowd? Why shouldn't their life feel expansive? "None of it is surrendered to others; none of it is scattered in a thousand directions." None of it is gambled on the whims of Fortune, lost to careless neglect, or squandered on wasteful generosity. None of it sits idle. Every single moment, so to speak, yields a return. And so, no matter how brief that life may be, it is more than enough. When the final day arrives, the wise man will not hesitate; "he will step forward to meet death with a steady stride."
