Chapter 12

He replies that they remain ill at ease because many things distract their thoughts. Conflicting opinions disturb their minds. I admit that this is true. Still, these very people receive great Pleasure. They act foolish, inconsistent, and certain to feel regret. We must allow that they remain as far from feeling any trouble as they remain from forming a right judgment. As happens with many people, a merry madness possesses them. They laugh while they rave. On the other hand, the pleasures of wise people remain mild, polite, and kept under restraint. They remain scarcely noticeable. Wise people neither invite them to come nor receive them with honor when they come of their own accord. They do not welcome them with any delight. Wise people simply mix them up with their lives. They fill empty spaces with them. It resembles an amusing show in the breaks of serious business. Let them no longer join mismatched matters together. Let them not connect Pleasure with Virtue. This mistake courts the worst of people. The reckless profligate believes that he lives with Virtue. He always drinks and burps up the fumes of wine. He believes this because he knows that he lives with Pleasure. He hears people say that Pleasure cannot exist apart from Virtue. Consequently, he calls his vices wisdom. He parades everything he ought to hide. Epicurus does not encourage men to run wild. But vicious people hide their excesses in the lap of philosophy. They flock to the schools where they hear the praises of Pleasure. They do not consider how sober and temperate that Pleasure of Epicurus really is. Instead, they rush at his mere name. They seek to obtain some protection and cover for their vices. They lose the one virtue which their evil life possessed. That virtue consisted of feeling shame when doing wrong. They praise what they used to blush at. They boast of their vices. Modesty can never return when people dignify shameful idleness with an honorable name. The praise which your school gives to Pleasure causes harm. This happens because the honorable part of its teaching passes unnoticed. At the same time, everyone sees the degrading part.
