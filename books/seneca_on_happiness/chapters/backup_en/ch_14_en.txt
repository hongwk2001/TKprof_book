Chapter 14

Let Virtue lead the way and bear the standard. We shall have Pleasure for all that. But we shall act as her masters and controllers. She may win some concessions from us. But she will not force us to do anything. On the contrary, some permit Pleasure to lead the way. They have neither one nor the other. They lose Virtue altogether. Yet they do not possess Pleasure. Instead, Pleasure possesses them. Its absence tortures them or its excess chokes them. They feel wretched if it deserts them. They feel yet more wretched if it overwhelms them. They resemble sailors caught in shallow waters. The tide leaves them on dry ground at one time. The flowing waves toss them at another. This arises from an exaggerated lack of self-control and a hidden love of evil. A person who seeks after evil instead of good faces danger when they attain their object. We hunt wild beasts with toil and peril. Even when we catch them, we find them an anxious possession. They often tear their keepers to pieces. Great pleasures act exactly the same. They turn out to be great evils. They take their owners prisoner. The more numerous and the greater they are, the more inferior that person becomes. They become the slave of more masters. Yet the public calls them a happy person. I may even press this comparison further. Consider the person who tracks wild animals to their lairs. They set great store on seeking the wandering beasts with traps. They make their hounds surround the spacious woods. In order to follow their tracks, that person neglects far more desirable things. They leave many duties unfulfilled. In the same way, the person who pursues Pleasure postpones everything to it. They disregard that first essential of liberty. They sacrifice it to their belly. They do not buy Pleasure for themselves. They sell themselves to Pleasure.
