Chapter 8

What answer should we make to the reflection that Pleasure belongs to good and bad people alike? What about the fact that bad people delight in their shame as much as good people delight in noble things? This is why the ancients told us to lead the highest life, not the most pleasant life. They wanted Pleasure to accompany a right-thinking and honorable mind, not guide it. We should let Nature guide us. Let our Reason watch her and take her advice. To live happily means to live according to Nature. I will explain this. We must guard the body's gifts and nature's advantages with care and fearlessness. We must treat them as things that will soon depart and belong to us only for a day. We must not fall under their power or allow ourselves to become slaves to things outside our own being. We must assign bodily pleasures and external delights the same position that light troops hold in a camp. We must make them our servants, not our masters. That is the only way they become valuable to our minds. A person should remain unbiased and unconquered by external things. They ought to admire themselves alone. They should feel confidence in their own spirit and order their life to stay ready for good or bad fortune. Let their confidence have knowledge. Let their knowledge have steadfastness. Let them always abide by their past decisions. Let them never erase their doctrines. Everyone will understand that such a person remains tranquil and composed in their behavior. They will act high-minded and courteous. Let the senses encourage Reason to seek the truth and draw its first principles from there. Indeed, it has no other base of operations or starting place to pursue truth. It must fall back upon itself. Even the universe, and God who guides it, reach out into the external world. Yet, God always returns completely back to Himself. Let our mind do the same thing. It follows its bodily senses and sends itself forth into the outward world's things. But let it still remain their master and its own master. By this means we shall obtain a united and allied strength and ability. We shall derive from it the Reason that never halts between two opinions. It will not form dull perceptions, beliefs, or convictions. Such a mind arranges itself in order. It makes its various parts agree together and harmonizes them. It attains the Highest Good. It leaves nothing evil or hazardous remaining. It has nothing to shake it or make it stumble. It will do everything under its own will's guidance. Nothing unexpected will befall it. Everything it does will turn out well. It will happen readily and easily without the doer using any underhand devices. Slow and hesitating actions show discord and a lack of settled purpose. You may then boldly declare that the Highest Good remains singleness of mind. Where agreement and unity exist, the virtues must also exist. The vices war with one another.
