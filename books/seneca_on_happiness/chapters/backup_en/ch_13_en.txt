Chapter 13

I myself believe that the teaching of Epicurus was upright and holy. My Stoic comrades would feel unwilling to hear me say so. If you examine it closely, it even appears stern. This much-talked-of Pleasure shrinks to a very narrow limit. He tells Pleasure to submit to the same law which we tell Virtue to obey. This law requires obedience to Nature. Luxury feels unsatisfied with what satisfies Nature. What is the result? A person might think that happiness consists in lazy sloth and alternations of gluttony and wild living. That person requires a good patron for a bad action. They become an Epicurean. The attractive name of that school leads them to do so. However, they follow not the Pleasure which they hear spoken of there. They follow the Pleasure they brought with them. They learn to think that their vices agree with the rules of that philosophy. Then they indulge in them no longer timidly and in dark corners. They indulge in them boldly in the light of day. I will not say that the sect of Epicurus teaches crime. Most of our school say this, but I disagree. What I say is that people speak ill of it and it has a bad reputation. Yet it does not deserve it. Who can know this without gaining admission to its inner mysteries? Its very outside gives an opportunity for scandal. It encourages people's baser desires. It resembles a brave man dressed in a woman's gown. Your chastity remains assured. Your manhood remains safe. You submit your body to nothing disgraceful. Yet your hand holds a drum. Choose some honorable sign for your school. Choose some writing which shall in itself arouse the mind. The vices invented the sign that currently stands over your door. The person who ranges themselves on the side of Virtue gives proof of a noble character. The person who follows Pleasure appears to be weak and worn out. They seem to degrade their manhood. They will likely fall into infamous vices unless someone chooses their pleasures for them. They need this help to know which pleasures remain within the bounds of natural desire. They must know which ones grow frantic and boundless. Those ones become all the more hungry the more a person satisfies them. But come! Let Virtue lead the way, and then every step will remain safe. Too much Pleasure causes harm. But with Virtue we need fear no excess of any kind. Moderation exists within Virtue herself. That which its own extent injures cannot be a good thing. Besides, what better guide can Reason be for beings endowed with a reasoning nature? So if this combination pleases you, you may proceed to a happy life thus accompanied. Let Virtue lead the way. Let Pleasure follow and hang about the body like a shadow. Only a mind incapable of great things hands over Virtue as a servant to Pleasure. Virtue remains the highest of all qualities.
