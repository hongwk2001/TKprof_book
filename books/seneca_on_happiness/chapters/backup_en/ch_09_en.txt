Chapter 9

Someone might argue that you only practice Virtue because you hope to obtain some Pleasure from it. First of all, Virtue may give us Pleasure. However, we still do not seek her for that reason. She does not bestow this as her primary goal. She gives this in addition. Nor does she work for this end. Her labor wins this also, although she directs it to another end. It is like a plowed field. When someone plows a field for corn, they find some flowers in it. These flowers may please the eye. Yet, the person did not spend all this labor to produce them. The person who planted the field had another object in mind. They gained the flowers as a bonus. Pleasure is not the reward or the cause of Virtue. It comes in addition to it. We do not choose Virtue because she gives us Pleasure. Instead, she gives us Pleasure also if we choose her. The Highest Good lies in the act of choosing her and in the attitude of the noblest minds. When a mind fulfills its function and establishes itself within its own limits, it attains the Highest Good. It needs nothing more. Nothing exists outside of the whole. Nothing exists beyond the end. You make a mistake when you ask me why I seek Virtue. You seek something above the highest. Do you ask what I seek from Virtue? I answer, Herself. She has nothing better. She acts as her own reward. Does this not appear great enough? I tell you that the Highest Good remains an unyielding strength of mind. It encompasses wisdom, greatness, sound judgment, freedom, harmony, and beauty. Do you still ask me for something greater? Why do you talk to me about pleasures? I seek to find what is good for a person, not what is good for their belly. Cattle and whales have larger bellies than we do.
