Chapter 10

They argue that I purposely misunderstand their words. They say that no one can live pleasantly unless they also live honorably. They claim this cannot be true for dumb animals who measure their happiness by their food. They publicly proclaim that a pleasant life cannot exist without Virtue. Yet everyone knows that the greatest fools drink the deepest of your pleasures. Vice overflows with enjoyments. The mind itself suggests many twisted, vicious forms of Pleasure to itself. Arrogance and excessive self-esteem come first among these. Then a swaggering dominance over other people follows. A shortsighted and blind devotion to one's own interests comes next. Then reckless luxury and excessive delight spring from childish causes. Talkativeness, a pride that takes pleasure when it insults others, sloth, and a dull mind's decay also exist. Virtue chases all these away. She plucks a person by the ear. She measures the value of pleasures before she permits their use. Nor does she highly value the ones she allows to pass current. She merely allows their use. Her cheerfulness does not come from her use of them, but from her moderation when she uses them. They say that when moderation lessens Pleasure, it hurts the Highest Good. You devote yourself to pleasures, but I check them. You indulge in Pleasure, but I use it. You think that it represents the Highest Good, but I do not even think it is good at all. I do nothing for the sake of Pleasure. You do everything for it.
