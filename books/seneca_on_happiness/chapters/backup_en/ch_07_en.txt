Chapter 7

Even the very people who declare the Highest Good to reside in the belly see the shameful position they assigned to it. Therefore, they say that they cannot part Pleasure from Virtue. They claim that no one can live honorably without living cheerfully. They also say that no one can live cheerfully without living honorably. I do not see how these very different matters connect with one another. What prevents Virtue from existing apart from Pleasure? Of course, the reason is that all good things come from Virtue. Therefore, even the things you cherish and seek originally come from its roots. Yet, if they were entirely inseparable, we would not see pleasant things that lack honor. We would not see highly honorable things that are hard and only attainable by suffering. Add to this that Pleasure visits the basest lives. Virtue cannot co-exist with an evil life. Yet some unhappy people experience Pleasure. In fact, Pleasure itself makes them unhappy. This could not happen if Pleasure connected with Virtue. Virtue often exists without Pleasure and never needs it. Why do you combine two things that are unlike and even incompatible with one another? Virtue is a lofty quality. It remains sublime, royal, unconquerable, and untiring. Pleasure remains low, slavish, weak, and perishable. It haunts and homes in the brothel and the tavern. You will meet Virtue in the temple, the marketplace, the senate house, and on the walls. It remains covered with dust, sunburnt, and horny-handed. You will find Pleasure hiding out of sight. It seeks shady nooks at the public baths, hot chambers, and places that dread the magistrate's visits. It remains soft, effeminate, and reeking of wine and perfumes. It remains pale, or perhaps painted and made up with cosmetics. The Highest Good remains immortal. It knows no ending and does not admit fullness or regret. A right-thinking mind never alters or hates itself. Nor do the best things ever undergo any change. But Pleasure dies at the very moment it charms us most. It has no great scope, so it soon cloys and wearies us. It fades away as soon as its first impulse passes. Indeed, we cannot depend upon anything that changes by nature. Consequently, it is impossible for solid substance to exist in something that comes and goes so swiftly. It perishes when it exercises its own functions. It arrives at a point where it ceases to exist. Even while it begins, it always keeps its end in view.
