Chapter 3

Let us search for a blessing that does not merely look attractive on the surface. We need something that is sound and good through and through. It should look most beautiful in the parts we least see. Let us unearth this treasure. It rests near us, and we can easily discover it. We only need to know where to reach out our hand. But as things stand, we act like we walk in the dark. We reach far beyond the things closest to us. We stumble against the very things we seek.

However, I will not drag you into long side stories. I will skip the opinions of other philosophers. Stating and refuting all of them takes too long. Instead, take our view. When I say our view, I do not bind myself strictly to any single leader of the Stoic school. I too have the right to form my own opinion. I will follow the authority of some Stoics. I will ask others to clarify their meaning. Perhaps when I report all their views and someone asks for my own, I will not argue with my predecessors. I will simply add something to their decisions.

Meanwhile, I follow nature. Every Stoic philosopher agrees upon this principle. True wisdom consists in never departing from nature. We must mold our conduct according to her laws and model. A happy life exists in harmony with its own nature. We cannot achieve this unless the mind first becomes sound. It must remain sound without interruption. Next, the mind must become bold and vigorous. It must endure all trials with admirable courage. It must adapt to the times. It must take proper care of the body and its needs without excessive anxiety.

The mind must also place true value on the things that enrich our lives. It should not overestimate any of them. It must be able to enjoy the gifts of Fortune without becoming her slave. You understand this even without my mention of it. Unbroken calm and freedom naturally follow when we drive away everything that agitates or alarms us. We replace sensual pleasures and petty matters with immense joy. This joy remains unchangeable and balanced. Peace, Tranquility, greatness of mind, and kindness accompany this joy. All cruelty and hostility merely show weakness.
