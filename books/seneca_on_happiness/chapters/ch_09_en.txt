[Chapter 9]
Our opponent says that we practice Virtue because we also hope to get some Pleasure from it. First, Virtue can give us Pleasure. But we do not pursue Virtue for that reason. We do not practice Virtue aiming for Pleasure. Pleasure is just a bonus that follows the results of Virtue. Also, Virtue does not work hard just for Pleasure. But its hard work also gains Pleasure even if Virtue moves toward another goal. This is like finding some flowers while plowing a field to harvest grain. These flowers might please the eyes, but all this hard work was not spent to bloom flowers. The person who plowed the field had another purpose in mind and just got this as a bonus. Like this, Pleasure is not the reward or cause of Virtue. It is an addition that follows Virtue. We do not choose Virtue to get Pleasure. Virtue also gives us Pleasure when we choose Virtue. The Highest Good lies in the act of choosing Virtue itself and the most noble attitude of the mind. The mind reaches the Highest Good when it fully does its job and stands firmly within its own limits. It does not need anything else anymore. Nothing exists beyond the end just as nothing exists outside the whole. Therefore, you make a mistake if you ask me why I pursue Virtue. You look for something beyond the Highest Good. Do you ask what I seek in Virtue? I answer. I seek Virtue itself. Nothing is better than Virtue. Virtue is its own reward. I say that the Highest Good is unbending mental strength, wisdom, generosity, sound judgment, freedom, harmony, and beauty. Does this not seem great enough? Do you still ask me for something more that could be considered a property of these things? Why do you talk to me about Pleasure? I look for what is good for a person instead of what is good for a person's belly. Cattle and whales have much bigger bellies than people if you want to talk about the belly.
