[Chapter 27]
Look at this fact. Socrates speaks to you from that prison which he cleansed of its shame and made more honorable than any government building when he entered it. "What exactly is this madness of yours? What is this nature that leads you to slander Virtue with mean criticism and insult holiness? It is a nature that wages war with both God and humanity. If you can, praise good people. If you cannot, pass by them in silence. If you really feel joy from these attacking insults, fight each other instead. When you jump around like crazy against heaven, I will not say you are committing blasphemy. You are just wasting your time. I once provided material for jokes to Aristophanes. Since then, the whole group of comedy poets made me the target of their poisonous wit. But my Virtue shone even brighter because of the exact blows aimed at it. Exposing Virtue to a test in front of the public benefits Virtue. No one understands the greatness of Virtue better than the people who tested its strength through attacks on Virtue. The hardness of a stone is best known to the person who strikes the stone. I put myself in front of all attacks like a rock standing alone in a shallow sea. The waves constantly hit the rock from all sides but can never move the rock from its place. They cannot wear the rock down no matter how constantly they crash against it for years. Jump over me and rush at me. I will beat you by enduring your attacks. Everything that hits something hard and invincible only hurts itself with its own violence. So find a soft and bending target that you can pierce with your arrows. But do you have the time to look into the bad deeds of other people and judge someone? Do you have time to ask why this philosopher has such a large house? Do you have time to ask why that philosopher eats such a great dinner? Are your own bodies covered in countless ulcers while you stare at the pimples of someone else? This is like a person eaten by a terrible skin disease pointing at a small mark on a very handsome man and despising him. Try blaming Plato for seeking money, Aristotle for getting money, Democritus for ignoring money, and Epicurus for wasting money. Try insulting Phaedrus and Alcibiades right in front of my eyes. You reach the peak of joy whenever you get a chance to copy our vices. Why do you not look around at the diseases tearing you apart from all sides? These diseases are attacking you from the outside or burning inside your own chests. No matter how much you do not know your own position, humanity has not reached a point where you have the time to move your tongues to criticize people better than yourselves."
