[Chapter 18]
Our opponent argues back. He says you say this but you act differently. You are the most evil creature among all creations. You always show the bitterest hate to the best people. This criticism was thrown at Plato, Epicurus, and Socrates too. All of them declared how they should live, not how they were living. I talk about Virtue, not about myself. When I criticize vices, I criticize my own vices first. When I have the strength, I will live the way I am supposed to live. No malice dyed deep in poison will stop my steps toward the best things. Even the poison you scatter on others and use to choke yourself will not stop me. It will not stop me from continuing to praise the life that I know I should live, even if I cannot live it yet. It will not stop me from loving Virtue and following behind Virtue, even if I am far behind and walking with a limp. I see that bad words did not even respect great men. How should I expect bad words to respect anything? Who cares if someone receives criticism for being too rich by people who thought even a great philosopher was not poor enough? That most active philosopher fought against all bodily desires and was poorer than any other philosopher. He gave up owning anything and even gave up asking for anything. But people criticized him saying he was not needy enough. Truly, it was as if he confessed his knowledge of poverty instead of Virtue.
