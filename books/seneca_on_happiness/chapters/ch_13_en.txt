[Chapter 13]
My fellow Stoics might hate to hear me say this. But I believe the teachings of Epicurus are honest, holy, and strict if you look closely. His famous Pleasure reduces to a very narrow range. The law he ordered Pleasure to follow is the same law we give to Virtue. It is the law to obey nature. But luxury is not satisfied with what nature finds enough. What is the result? The person who thinks happiness lies in lazy idleness and wild living needs a good sponsor to support his bad deeds. He becomes an Epicurean drawn by the attractive name of this school. But he does not follow the Pleasure he hears about there. He follows the Pleasure he brought with him in the first place. He starts to think his vices match the sayings of that philosophy. He is no longer scared and does not hide in dark corners to enjoy Pleasure. He boldly enjoys it in broad daylight. Therefore, I will not say the Epicurean school is a teacher of crime like most people in our school do. I am saying that school receives blame and gets a bad reputation, but it does not deserve it. Its outward appearance itself gives an excuse for scandal and encourages the low desires of people. It is like a brave man wearing the clothes of a woman. Your purity is guaranteed and your body does not surrender to anything shameful. But your hands hold a sacred musical instrument like a priest of the goddess Cybele. So choose some honorable phrase for your school that can wake up the mind. Vices invented what hangs over your door right now. A person who stands on the side of Virtue proves his noble nature just by doing that. But a person who follows Pleasure is weak and tired. He reduces his manhood and easily falls into famous vices. This happens unless someone separates Pleasures for him. Someone must tell him which ones stay within the limits of natural desires. Someone must tell him which ones are crazy and endless. The crazy ones become greedier the more they are filled. Now let Virtue lead the way. Then every step will be safe. Too much Pleasure is harmful. But with Virtue you never need to fear that anything will become too much. Virtue already contains moderation inside itself. Something that takes harm from its own size can never be a good thing. Besides, what better guide is there than Reason for beings given a rational nature? Let Virtue go first and let Pleasure follow the body like a shadow if you want to walk toward a happy life with this companion. Giving Virtue as a servant to Pleasure only belongs to a weak mind that cannot handle great things.
