[Chapter 10]
He says you misunderstand him on purpose. He also says that a person cannot live happily without living honorably. This cannot apply to dumb animals that measure the size of happiness by the amount of food. He loudly declares to the public that the pleasant life he talks about cannot exist without Virtue added to it. But everyone knows that the most foolish people in the world fall deepest into the Pleasure you mention. Everyone knows that vice is full of Pleasure and the mind itself encourages many twisted and evil forms of Pleasure. First, those Pleasures include arrogance, too much pride, looking down on others, shortsighted and blind devotion to one's own interests, wild luxury, and excessive joy from the most trivial and childish causes. They also include talkativeness, pride that finds joy in insulting others, laziness, and the decline of a dull mind that falls asleep inside itself. Virtue scatters all these things. Virtue pulls the ears of a person to wake him up. It measures the value of Pleasure before allowing its use. Virtue does not think much of the Pleasures it allows to pass. It only allows the use of Pleasure. Virtue is cheerful not because it uses Pleasure, but because it is moderate in using it. He says that if moderation reduces Pleasure, it ruins the Highest Good. You dedicate yourself to Pleasure, but I control Pleasure. You indulge in Pleasure, but I use Pleasure. You think it is the Highest Good, but I do not even think it is good. I do nothing for the sake of Pleasure, but you do everything.
