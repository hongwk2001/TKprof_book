[Chapter 26]
You ask what the difference is between a foolish person and a wise person. There is every difference in the world. Wealth is a slave in the house of a wise person. But wealth is a master in the house of a foolish person. You get used to wealth and cling to it as if someone promised it would be yours forever. But a wise person never thinks about poverty more than when he is surrounded by wealth. No great general completely relaxes just because peace is kept. He always prepares for a war that can be declared at any time even if it is not fought right away. You become proud as if your fine house will never burn or fall down. You boast thinking your wealth is out of reach of all dangers and even fortune does not have the power to swallow it. You are like barbarians who idly watch the hard work of an army without knowing the purpose of the siege weapons being built from far away. You just play idly with your wealth and do not foresee the danger coming to it. This is exactly what happens to you. You fall asleep on your property. You do not reflect at all on how many bad lucks are threateningly hovering around and will soon rob your precious loot. But a wise person still has everything he needs even if someone takes his wealth away. He lives happily in the present and has no fear for the future. The great Socrates or anyone with his superiority and endurance over the things of this life would have said this. "I have no firmer principle than the principle that I will not change the course of my life to fit your prejudice. You may pour your usual words on me from all sides. I will not think you are insulting me. I will just think you are crying like pitiful little babies." This is what a person who owns wisdom will say. His mind is free from vice. He criticizes others to improve them. He does not criticize them because he hates them. He will add this. "Your opinion about me causes pain for yourselves, not for me. Hating perfection and attacking Virtue itself is the same as giving up all hope of doing good. You can do no harm to me. Knocking over an altar does not harm the gods. But it is clear that your intentions are bad and you willingly try to do harm even where you cannot do harm. I endure your chatter just as the great Jupiter endures the empty stories of poets. Some poets say Jupiter has wings. Some say he has horns. Some call him an adulterer who stays out all night. Some describe him as a person who treats gods harshly. They call him a person who is unfair to people. They call him a seducer who kidnaps noble youths and even his own relatives. They call him a person who killed his family members and conquered the kingdom of someone else, even the kingdom of his own father. The only result these stories produce is that they make people feel less shame about sinning by making them believe the gods are guilty of such actions. These actions of yours do not hurt me, but I advise you for your own sake to respect Virtue. Follow Virtue for a long time. Believe the words of the people who shout loudly that what they follow is truly powerful and looks more powerful every day. Worship Virtue just as you worship gods. Respect the people who follow Virtue just as you respect the priests of gods. Please do us a favor with silence whenever someone mentions sacred texts. This saying does not mean a favor like people usually think. It orders silence so that sacred rituals can be held without being interrupted by words that become bad signs. It is much more necessary to give this order to you. You can listen carefully and in silence when the words are delivered by that oracle only if you do so. Whenever someone pretends it is an order from God and beats a sacred musical instrument, or a person skilled at making scratches on his skin makes small cuts and dyes his arms and shoulders with blood, or someone crawls on his knees crying along the street, or an old man wearing simple clothes comes out in broad daylight holding a lamp and a branch and shouts that one of the gods is angry, you surround him. You listen to his words and increase the wonder of each other by declaring that God inspired him."
