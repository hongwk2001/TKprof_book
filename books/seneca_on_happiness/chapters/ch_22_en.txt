[Chapter 22]
But who can doubt that a wise person has a wider field to use his abilities when he is rich than when he is poor? The only Virtue he can show when he is poor is not being twisted or crushed by poverty. But he will have a wide stage to show moderation, generosity, diligence, organization, and greatness if he has wealth. The wise person will not despise himself even if he is short. But he will still wish to be tall. He can still be healthy even if he is weak and has only one eye. But he would rather prefer to have physical strength. He will do this while always knowing that he has something much more powerful inside him. He will endure illness, but he will also hope for good health. Some things are just minor things compared to the sum of the whole. Losing them does not destroy the Highest Good. But they add something to the constant cheerfulness that comes from Virtue. Wealth encourages and brightens such a person. This is just as a sailor rejoices in a wind that guides him smoothly. Or it is as people enjoy a sunny and warm place on a clear or cold day. Who among the wise people of our Stoic school can deny that even these things we call neither good nor bad have some value in themselves? Virtue is the only good for us. Who can deny that some of them are preferred over others? We show some respect to some of them, and a lot of respect to others. So do not be mistaken. Wealth belongs to the class of desirable things. You ask why I laugh at you when you put wealth in the same position as I do. Do you want to know how the positions where we place wealth are different? If my wealth leaves me, it will take nothing away from me except itself. But you will panic and look as if you have lost yourself if your wealth disappears from you. Wealth only takes a certain place for me. But wealth takes the highest place of all things for you. In a word, my wealth belongs to me, but you belong to your wealth.
