[Chapter 8]
Pleasure belongs to both good and bad people. Bad people feel as much joy in their shameful deeds as good people feel in their noble deeds. How should we answer this thought? This is exactly why the ancients told us to live the most noble life instead of the most pleasant life. They wanted to make Pleasure a companion instead of the guide of an honorable mind that thinks correctly. Nature is what we must take as our guide. Let our Reason watch her and follow her advice. Therefore, living happily is the same as living according to nature. I will explain what this means. We might view our physical abilities and the advantages of nature as temporary gifts that will soon leave us. We might protect them carefully and without fear. We might refuse to fall under their control or become slaves to things that are not part of our true selves. We might give all bodily Pleasure and outward joys the same rank as helper troops or light soldiers in a military camp. We might make them our servants instead of our masters. Only then do they become valuable to our minds. A person must not have prejudice toward outside things and must not let them conquer him. He must only respect himself and have confidence in his own spirit. He must organize his life so he is equally ready for good or bad fortune. His confidence must not exist without knowledge. His knowledge must not exist without firmness. He must always keep what he decides once. There must be no eraser marks on his principles. I do not even have to add that this kind of person will be calm and peaceful in his attitude. He will be noble and polite in his actions. You will understand this fact. Let Reason receive encouragement from the senses to seek the truth. Let it draw its first principles from there. In fact, Reason has no other base to carry out its operations. It has no other starting point to pursue the truth. Reason must rely on itself. Even the all-encompassing universe and its guide extend themselves to outward things. But they return completely to themselves from all sides. Let our mind act exactly the same way. The mind might follow the bodily senses and send itself out to the things of the outside world through them. But let it still remain the master of them and the master of itself. Through this method, we will gain a combined and allied power and ability. We will bring out a Reason that never hesitates between two opinions. It will never be slow to form perception, belief, or conviction. Such a mind reaches the Highest Good only when it arranges itself neatly. It makes its parts agree with each other and makes them harmonious. Nothing evil or dangerous remains in that mind anymore. There is nothing to shake it or make it stumble. That mind will do everything under the guidance of its own will. Unexpected things will never happen. Whatever that mind does will produce good results. The person doing the action will finish it quickly and easily without relying on any secret tricks. Slow and hesitating actions are signs of disagreement and a lack of firm purpose. Therefore, we can boldly declare that the Highest Good is the unity of the mind. Virtue always exists where there is agreement and unity. Only vices wage war against each other.
