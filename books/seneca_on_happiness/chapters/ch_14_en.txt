[Chapter 14]
Let Virtue go first and carry the flag. We will still enjoy Pleasure, but we will be its master and controller. Pleasure might win some small arguments from us, but it will not force anything on us. On the other hand, the people who let Pleasure take the lead get neither. They completely lose Virtue. They do not even own Pleasure. Instead, Pleasure owns them. They suffer when Pleasure is gone and almost choke when Pleasure is too much. They become miserable when Pleasure leaves. They become more miserable when Pleasure attacks them. They are like a ship stuck on sandbanks. They are left on dry land one moment and tossed around by rushing waves the next. All of this comes from a terrible lack of self-control and a secret love for evil. Reaching the goal itself is dangerous for a person who chases evil instead of good. We hunt wild animals with hard work and danger. But once we catch them, they often tear their keepers to pieces and become a worrying possession. Huge Pleasure is exactly like this. It turns into a huge evil and captures its master. As Pleasure gets bigger and more massive, the person becomes more inferior. He becomes a slave serving more masters. I can push this comparison further. A person who tracks wild animals to their homes neglects much more valuable things and leaves many duties undone. A person who chases Pleasure is like a hunter who tries to choke wandering beasts with traps or surrounds wide clearings with hunting dogs. He delays everything for the sake of Pleasure. He ignores his most necessary value, freedom, and sacrifices it to his belly. He does not buy Pleasure for himself. He sells himself to Pleasure.
