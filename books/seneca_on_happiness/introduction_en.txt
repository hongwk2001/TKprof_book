Introduction to Of a Happy Life

Welcome to Lucius Annaeus Seneca's classic Stoic dialogue: "Of a Happy Life" (De Vita Beata).

Written around 58 AD and addressed to his older brother Gallio, Seneca explores the nature of happiness, virtue, and pleasure. In this dialogue, Seneca lays down the foundation of Stoic ethics, asserting that true happiness is achieved only by aligning one's life with reason and nature. He argues that pleasure should not lead virtue, but rather follow it as an incidental byproduct.

Crucially, in the second half of the work, Seneca defends himself against critics who accused him of hypocrisy for praising Stoic self-denial while accumulating vast wealth. His explanation remains one of history's most sophisticated arguments for the role of material goods in a philosophical life: the wise man is the master of his wealth, not its slave, using it as an instrument to exercise virtue and generosity.

This digital edition presents Aubrey Stewart's classic translation (1900), styled and structured into twenty-eight chapters for a premium modern reading experience.
