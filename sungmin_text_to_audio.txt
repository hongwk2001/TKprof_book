**AFFIDAVIT OF KAT LYNNWOOD**

After being duly sworn upon oath, Kat Lynnwood testifies as follows: I am over the age of 18 and competent to make this affidavit. I am testifying in this case voluntarily.

My name is Kat Lynnwood. It’s weird to be writing this on paper, since most of my work comes from speaking out loud, but here we are. I should probably clarify—I’m an influencer, the world just doesn’t know it yet. I am the founder, CEO, host, producer, editor, social media manager, and currently only employee of my talk show, “Kat’s Out of the Bag.” My company is big, the world just doesn’t know that either. The world doesn’t know a lot of things.

I should probably clarify something else—I more formally work as a server at the restaurant Good Day Chinese. I don’t like to think of it as just serving, though, although you and I both know I “serve” plenty. I like to think of my job as… boots-on-the-ground journalism. I should probably start from the beginning. See, this is why my calling is speaking out loud.

If I could describe my early years with one song title, it would probably be: “Born this Way.” It was clear from a young age that I was meant to communicate with the masses. I gave breaking news reports from my living room, hosted red carpet interviews with my stuffed animals, and once attempted to produce a six-part investigative documentary on why my third-grade teacher was being controlled by our classroom hamster, Humphrey. That project was shut down by the school administration, which I still consider my first experience with censorship.

Naturally, I went to the University of Southern California (Go Trojans!) to study journalism with a concentration in TikTok trends; for some reason, the university refuses to recognize that specialization. I learned many important things there: how to ask a follow-up question, how to frame a shot, even how to cage my rage—at society. I also learned that the media industry is not always kind to young visionaries with limited experience, limited connections, and what my therapist Brian called “a concerning attachment to cats.” To him I say: do you even know my name?

On April 18, 2025, I was working the morning shift at Good Day as per usual, witnessing what seemed like yet another Lin-and-El brunch. I noticed Lin’s car right away because they had parked it directly in front of the window for all to see. Let me tell you, Lin always drives this bright red car, and usually, it did not bother me; I love red just as much as any other Trojan alum out there. But that morning, it was so in-your-face it almost looked crimson. Blech.

The brunch went longer than usual. Normally, Lin and El stayed for about an hour, but that day they were still there past 10:00 A.M. Lin seemed like they were trying to leave, but El kept talking. Around 10:30 A.M., Lin got up quickly. When they stood, they spilled coffee on themself. I heard Lin say something about needing to clean up and get to work. Then Lin went to the bathroom.

While Lin was in the bathroom, I was still working. Good Day was busy, and brunch rush is not exactly the ideal environment for surveillance. From what I remember, El stayed at the table. I do not remember seeing El leave. But, I was the only waiter clocked in at the moment, and was simultaneously suffering from a cat allergy I did not know I had (darn you, Samarah!). So I cannot say I had eyes on El every second.

Eventually, Lin came back from the bathroom with their clothes a few shades darker than they were before. I remember thinking Lin seemed rushed, which made sense because they had already said they needed to get to work, and they sped out the door just a little before 10:50 A.M. As Lin pulled away, I saw through the window something strange near the underside of the car. It looked like a little smoke or haze, maybe even something dark and wet on the pavement where the car had been parked.
