And then the writhing and convulsing slowed down, the gnashing of the teeth weakened, and the trembling in the face faded. Finally, the body lay completely still. The horrific task was finished.

The hammer dropped from Arthur’s hands. He swayed on his feet, and if we hadn't caught him, he would have collapsed. Huge drops of sweat beaded on his forehead, and he was gasping for air. It had been an unimaginable emotional strain on him; if it hadn't been for a sense of duty that went beyond ordinary human limits, he never would have been able to go through with it. For a few minutes, we were so focused on tending to him that we didn't even look at the coffin. When we finally did, a murmur of shocked surprise rippled through us. We were staring so intently that Arthur, who had been sitting on the floor, stood up and came over to look as well. Then, a strange, joyful light broke across his face, completely washing away the dark horror that had covered it just moments before.

Lying in the coffin was no longer the foul, demonic creature we had dreaded and hated so much that we treated her destruction as a privilege given to the man who loved her most. Instead, it was Lucy, exactly as she had looked in life, with a face of unequaled sweetness and purity. Yes, the traces of worry, illness, and pain were still visible on her face, just as they had been before she died, but those marks were precious to us because they proved this was the real Lucy we knew. We all felt that the holy calm resting over her thin face and body like sunshine was an earthly symbol of the eternal peace she had now finally found.

Van Helsing walked over, put his hand on Arthur’s shoulder, and asked:

"And now, Arthur my friend, my dear boy, am I forgiven?"

The release of all that terrible tension finally hit Arthur. He took the old man's hand, pressed it to his lips, and said:

"Forgiven! God bless you for giving my darling her soul back, and for giving me peace." He put his hands on the Professor’s shoulders, rested his head against Van Helsing's chest, and cried silently for a few minutes while the rest of us stood completely still. When he lifted his head, Van Helsing told him:

"And now, my child, you may kiss her. Kiss her dead lips if you want to. It is what she would have wanted you to do, if she could choose. Because she is no longer a grinning devil—no longer a foul monster cursed for all eternity. She is no longer the devil’s vampire. She is God’s true dead, and her soul is with Him!"

Arthur leaned down and kissed her. After that, we sent him and Quincey outside. The Professor and I sawed off the top of the wooden stake, leaving the pointed end buried in her chest. Then, we cut off her head and stuffed her mouth with garlic. We soldered the lead coffin shut, screwed the wooden lid back on, gathered our tools, and left the tomb. Once the Professor locked the door, he handed the key to Arthur.

Outside, the air felt sweet, the sun was shining brightly, and the birds were singing. It felt as if all of nature had shifted to a happier, brighter tune. There was a sense of joy, lightness, and peace everywhere, because we had finally found closure on this one tragic chapter, and we felt a deep—if somewhat solemn—gladness.

Before we walked away, Van Helsing said:

"Now, my friends, one phase of our work is finished—the phase that was the most heartbreaking for us. But an even greater task remains: to track down the monster responsible for all our sorrow and destroy him. I have clues we can follow, but it will be a long, difficult mission, filled with danger and pain. Will you all help me? We have all learned to believe the unbelievable—isn't that true? And since we believe, don't we see what our duty is? Yes! So, do we promise to see this through to the bitter end?"

One by one, we took his hand and made the promise. Then, as we started walking away, the Professor added:

"Two nights from now, I want you all to meet me for dinner at seven o'clock at John's house. I will invite two other people—two people you haven't met yet—and I will be ready to lay out all my research and reveal our plan. Friend John, you need to come home with me now, because I have a lot to discuss with you, and I need your help. I am leaving for Amsterdam tonight, but I will be back tomorrow evening. And then, our great hunt begins. But first, I have a lot to explain so that you understand exactly what we have to do, and exactly what we are up against. After that, we will renew our promise to each other. We have a terrifying task ahead of us, and once we put our hands to the plow, there is no turning back."
